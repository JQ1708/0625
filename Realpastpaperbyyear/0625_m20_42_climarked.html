Question 1 | 0625/42/M/20 Q1 | Topic: Motion

A rocket is launched vertically upwards from the ground. The rocket travels with uniform acceleration from rest. After 8.0s, the speed of the rocket is 120m/s.

(a) Calculate the acceleration of the rocket.

The acceleration of the rocket is calculated using the formula a = Δv / Δt. The change in velocity (Δv) is 120 m/s and the time taken (Δt) is 8.0 s. Therefore, the acceleration is 120 / 8.0 = 15 m/s².

Recap

Acceleration is defined as the rate of change of velocity. An object with uniform acceleration has a velocity that changes by the same amount each second.

(b) (i) On Fig. 1.1, draw the graph for the motion of the rocket in the first 8.0s.

(b) (ii) Use the graph to determine the height of the rocket at 8.0 s.

The height of the rocket is determined by calculating the area under the speed-time graph. For the first 8.0 s, this is a triangle. The area is ½ × base × height = ½ × 8.0 s × 120 m/s = 480 m.

Recap

The area under a speed-time graph represents the distance travelled. For a simple shape like a triangle, the formula is Area = ½ × base × height. For more complex graphs, the area can be found by splitting it into simpler shapes like rectangles and triangles.

(b) (iii) From time = 8.0s to time = 20.0s, the rocket rises with increasing speed but with decreasing acceleration. From time = 20.0s to time = 25.0s, the rocket has a constant speed of less than 200 m/s. On Fig. 1.1, draw the graph for this motion.

Question 2 | 0625/42/M/20 Q2 | Topic: Momentum and Impulse

Fig. 2.1 shows an athlete crossing the finishing line in a race. As she crosses the finishing line, her speed is 10.0m/s. She slows down to a speed of 4.0m/s.

(a) The mass of the athlete is 71kg. Calculate the impulse applied to her as she slows down.

Impulse is the change of momentum. First, calculate the initial and final momentum (momentum = mass × velocity).
Initial momentum = 71 kg × 10.0 m/s = 710 Ns.
Final momentum = 71 kg × 4.0 m/s = 284 Ns.
The change in momentum (impulse) is 710 - 284 = 426 Ns. (Note: Mark scheme answer is 430 Ns, likely due to rounding in the source)

Recap

Momentum is the product of an object's mass and velocity (p = mv). Impulse is equal to the change in momentum (Impulse = Δp) of an object.

(b) (i) Define impulse in terms of force and time.

Impulse is the product of force and the time for which the force acts.

Recap

The formula for impulse is Impulse = F × Δt, where F is the average force applied and Δt is the time interval over which the force is applied.

(b) (ii) The athlete takes 1.2s to slow down from a speed of 10.0m/s to a speed of 4.0m/s. Calculate the average resultant force applied to the athlete as she slows down.

Using the impulse calculated in (a), the average resultant force can be found using the formula F = Impulse / time.
Force = 426 Ns / 1.2 s = 355 N. (Using MS value: 430 / 1.2 = 358.33 N, rounded to 360N)

Recap

This calculation is a direct application of the impulse-momentum theorem. The force required to change an object's momentum is proportional to the change in momentum and inversely proportional to the time taken.

(c) Calculate the force required to give a mass of 71kg an acceleration of 6.4m/s².

According to Newton's Second Law, F = m × a.
Force = 71 kg × 6.4 m/s² = 454.4 N. (MS value is 450N)

Recap

Newton's Second Law of Motion states that the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass.

Question 3 | 0625/42/M/20 Q3 | Topic: Energy, Power, and Efficiency

Fig. 3.1 shows a model of a wind turbine used to demonstrate the use of wind energy to generate electricity. The wind is blowing towards the model, as shown.

(a) The mass of air passing through the circular area swept out by the turbine blades each second is 7.5kg. The kinetic energy of the air that passes through this circular area each second is 240J.

(i) Calculate the speed of the air.

Using the formula for kinetic energy, KE = ½mv². Rearranging for v gives v = √(2KE/m).
v = √(2 × 240 J / 7.5 kg) = √64 = 8.0 m/s.

Recap

Kinetic energy is the energy an object possesses due to its motion. It depends on the object's mass and the square of its speed.

(ii) The kinetic energy of the air drives a generator. State the input power of the air passing through the turbine blades.

Power is the rate of energy transfer. Since 240J of energy passes through the blades each second, the input power is 240 W.

Recap

Power is defined as the energy transferred or work done per unit time. The unit of power is the Watt (W), which is equivalent to one Joule per second (J/s).

(b) The output current of the generator is 2.0A. The output potential difference (p.d.) of the generator is 11V.

(i) Calculate the output power of the generator.

Electrical power is calculated using the formula P = VI.
Output Power = 11 V × 2.0 A = 22 W.

Recap

Electrical power is the product of potential difference (voltage) and current. This formula is fundamental in calculating power in electrical circuits.

(ii) Calculate the efficiency of the wind turbine.

Efficiency is calculated as (Useful Power Out / Total Power In) × 100%.
Efficiency = (22 W / 240 W) × 100% = 9.2% (rounded to one decimal place).

Recap

Efficiency is a measure of how effectively energy is converted from one form to another. It is always a value between 0 and 1, or 0% and 100%. No device is 100% efficient; some energy is always lost, usually as heat.

(c) The density of air is 1.3kg/m³. Calculate the volume of air passing through the circular area swept out by the turbine blades each second.

Density is given by the formula ρ = m/V. Rearranging for volume gives V = m/ρ.
Volume per second = 7.5 kg / 1.3 kg/m³ = 5.8 m³ (rounded to one decimal place).

Recap

Density is a measure of mass per unit volume. It describes how tightly packed the matter in a substance is. The standard unit for density is kg/m³.

Question 4 | 0625/42/M/20 Q4 | Topic: Thermal Physics

(a) Define the specific latent heat of fusion of a substance.

The specific latent heat of fusion is the thermal energy required to melt 1kg (or a unit mass) of a substance at a constant temperature, i.e., to change its state from solid to liquid.

Recap

Latent heat is the energy absorbed or released by a substance during a change of state, without changing its temperature. The 'fusion' part refers specifically to the transition from solid to liquid.

(b) Small pieces of ice at 0°C are added to 0.35kg of water. The initial temperature of the water is 24.5°C. The temperature of the water decreases to 0°C. The water loses 35000J of thermal energy as it cools. All of the ice added to the water melts. The specific latent heat of fusion of ice is 3.3 × 10⁵ J/kg.

Calculate:

(i) the specific heat capacity of water

Using the formula for thermal energy change, E = mcΔT. Rearranging for specific heat capacity (c) gives c = E / (mΔT).
c = 35000 J / (0.35 kg × 24.5 °C) = 4081.6 J/(kg°C). (MS value is 4100 J/(kg°C))

Recap

The specific heat capacity of a substance is the amount of thermal energy required to raise the temperature of 1kg of the substance by 1°C. Water has a famously high specific heat capacity.

(ii) the mass of ice added to the water.

The thermal energy lost by the water is gained by the ice to melt it. Using the formula for latent heat, E = mL. Rearranging for mass (m) gives m = E / L.
m = 35000 J / (3.3 × 10⁵ J/kg) = 0.106 kg or 106 g. (MS value is 0.11kg)

Recap

This is an example of the principle of conservation of energy, where the energy lost by the warmer substance (water) is equal to the energy gained by the colder substance (ice) in a closed system.

Question 5 | 0625/42/M/20 Q5 | Topic: Thermal Energy Transfer

(a) Complete the sentences with words that describe the main process of thermal energy transfer in each case.

A man goes for a walk on a cold day. He touches a metal gate, which removes thermal energy from his hands by conduction. He holds the sides of a cup containing a hot drink. His hands gain thermal energy by conduction. Some farm workers have lit a fire. The man warms his hands by the side of the fire. His hands gain thermal energy by radiation.

The completed sentences are shown above. The first two processes are conduction, as heat is transferred through direct contact. The third process is radiation, as heat is transferred through electromagnetic waves from the fire.

Recap

There are three main processes of thermal energy transfer:

  • Conduction: Transfer of heat through a substance by passing on molecular vibrations. It is most effective in solids.
  • Convection: Transfer of heat in fluids (liquids or gases) by the movement of the fluid itself.
  • Radiation: Transfer of heat through electromagnetic waves (like infrared). It does not require a medium.

(b) Describe in terms of particles the transfer of thermal energy through the metal of the gate after transfer from the man's hands.

In a metal, thermal energy is transferred mainly by free electrons. These electrons gain kinetic energy and move rapidly through the metal lattice, colliding with other electrons and ions, transferring energy. Additionally, the ions in the metal vibrate and pass this vibrational energy to neighbouring ions.

Recap

Metals are good conductors of heat because they have delocalised (free) electrons that are not bound to any single atom and are free to move. This allows for a rapid transfer of energy throughout the material.

(c) Fig. 5.1 shows a car on a sunny day in a hot country. The object labelled A is placed inside the windscreen. It is used by the owner of the car to reduce the temperature rise of the air in the car. Ring the most suitable material for the outer surface of object A. Explain your choice.

dull black | dull white | shiny black | shiny white

The most suitable surface is shiny white because it is the best reflector of thermal radiation and the worst absorber. This will prevent the car's interior from heating up due to sunlight.

Recap

  • Dull black surfaces are the best absorbers and best emitters of thermal radiation.
  • Shiny white or silver surfaces are the worst absorbers and worst emitters, but they are the best reflectors.
Question 6 | 0625/42/M/20 Q6 | Topic: Properties of Waves

(a) Fig. 6.1 shows crests of a water wave moving from left to right in a harbour.

(i) On Fig. 6.1, draw three more crests to the right of point A.

(ii) State the name of the wave process that occurs as the wave passes point A.

The wave process is diffraction. This is the spreading out of waves as they pass through a gap or around an obstacle.

Recap

Diffraction is a characteristic property of all waves. The effect is most significant when the size of the gap or obstacle is similar to the wavelength of the wave.

(b) Fig. 6.2 shows the crests of another wave moving from left to right in a different part of the harbour. This wave moves from deep water to shallow water.

(i) On Fig. 6.2, draw an arrow to show the direction of movement of the wave after it has passed into the shallow water.

(ii) State the name of the process that occurs as the wave passes into the shallow water.

The process is refraction. This occurs because the speed of the wave changes as it moves from one medium (deep water) to another (shallow water).

Recap

Refraction is the change in direction of a wave as it passes from one medium to another, caused by a change in its speed. While the speed and wavelength change, the frequency of the wave remains constant.

(iii) Complete Table 6.1 to state whether each of the properties of the wave increases, decreases or stays the same as the wave passes into the shallow water.

  • Wavelength: decreases
  • Frequency: stays the same
  • Speed: decreases

Recap

The relationship between wave speed (v), frequency (f), and wavelength (λ) is given by the wave equation: v = fλ. When a wave enters a new medium, its frequency remains constant. In this case, as the water becomes shallower, the wave slows down. To maintain the wave equation, the wavelength must therefore decrease.

Question 8 | 0625/42/M/20 Q8 | Topic: Electrical Circuits

Fig. 8.1 shows a circuit. The lamp has a resistance of 3.0Ω. Line XY represents a uniform resistance wire of resistance 6.0Ω.

(a) Calculate the reading on the ammeter.

The components are in a series circuit. The total resistance is the sum of individual resistances: R_total = 3.0Ω + 2.0Ω + 6.0Ω = 11.0Ω. Using Ohm's Law (V = IR), the current I = V / R_total.
I = 12V / 11.0Ω = 1.1 A (rounded to two significant figures).

Recap

In a series circuit, the current is the same through all components, and the total resistance is the sum of the individual resistances (R_total = R1 + R2 + ...).

(b) Fig. 8.2 shows the circuit with a different connection to the resistance wire and an added resistor. The length XY of the whole resistance wire is 2.0 m. The contact is made at Q where the distance XQ is 0.60m. Calculate the resistance of the circuit.

First, calculate the resistance of the wire section XQ. Since the wire is uniform, resistance is proportional to length. Resistance of XQ = (0.60m / 2.0m) × 6.0Ω = 1.8Ω.
The 1.5Ω resistor and the 1.8Ω section of the wire are in parallel. Their combined resistance (Rp) is 1/Rp = 1/1.5 + 1/1.8, which gives Rp ≈ 0.82Ω.
The total resistance of the circuit is the sum of the series components: R_total = 3.0Ω + 2.0Ω + 0.82Ω = 5.82Ω. (MS value is 5.8Ω)

Recap

For resistors in parallel, the reciprocal of the total resistance is the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances (1/R_total = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ...). The total resistance of parallel resistors is always less than the smallest individual resistance.

Question 10 | 0625/42/M/20 Q10 | Topic: Electromagnetism

(a) Fig. 10.1 is a simplified top view of a flat coil. There is an alternating current (a.c.) in the coil. Describe the magnetic effect of this alternating current.

An alternating current in the coil produces a magnetic field that is constantly changing direction and magnitude.

Recap

Any electric current produces a magnetic field. An alternating current (a.c.) periodically reverses its direction, which in turn causes the polarity of the magnetic field it produces to also reverse periodically.

(b) Fig. 10.2 shows a pan placed above the coil. The base of the pan is made of steel. State what quantity is induced in the base of the pan.

An e.m.f. (electromotive force), or voltage, is induced in the base of the pan.

Recap

This is due to electromagnetic induction. Faraday's Law of Induction states that a changing magnetic field in a conductor induces an electromotive force (e.m.f.). Here, the changing magnetic field from the coil passes through the steel base of the pan.

(c) The pan contains water. State and explain the effect of the quantity induced in part (b) on the temperature of the water in the pan.

The induced e.m.f. causes a large current (an eddy current) to flow in the steel base of the pan. Due to the resistance of the steel, this current generates a significant amount of thermal energy, which increases the temperature of the pan and the water inside it.

Recap

This is the principle behind an induction cooker. The induced currents are often called eddy currents. The heating effect of an electric current is known as Joule heating, where thermal energy produced is proportional to the square of the current (P = I²R).

Recap

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons. This means they have the same atomic number but different mass numbers.

  • Proton Number (Atomic Number): Defines the element. Helium always has 2 protons.
  • Nucleon Number (Mass Number): Total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus.
  • Neutron Number: Nucleon Number - Proton Number.
  • Electron Number: In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons.
