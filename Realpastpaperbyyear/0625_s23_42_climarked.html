(a) Fig. 1.1 shows a helicopter which is stationary at a height of 1500 m above the ground.

(i) State the two conditions necessary for the helicopter to remain in equilibrium. [2]

condition 1

condition 2

Condition 1: There must be no resultant/net force acting on the helicopter.

Condition 2: There must be no resultant/net moment (or turning effect) about any pivot.

Recap

According to the reference textbook, for a system to be in equilibrium, two conditions must be met: there must be no resultant force and no resultant moment.

(ii) The mass of the helicopter is 3200 kg.

Calculate the change in the gravitational potential energy of the helicopter as it rises from the ground to 1500 m. [2]

The formula for change in gravitational potential energy (GPE) is:

ΔEp = m × g × Δh

ΔEp = 3200 kg × 9.8 N/kg × 1500 m

ΔEp = 47,040,000 J

Change in gravitational potential energy = 4.7 × 107 J (or 47 MJ)

Recap

The textbook provides the formula for calculating gravitational potential energy (Ep):

Ep = mass (kg) × gravitational field strength (N/kg) × height (m)

Ep = mgΔh

(b) Fig. 1.2 shows a vertical speed-time graph for a parachutist who jumps from a stationary hot-air balloon.

The parachutist jumps from the balloon at time = 0 and reaches the ground at B. The point A indicates when the parachute opens.

(i) On Fig. 1.2, label a point on the graph where the acceleration is:

  • zero with '1'
  • negative with '2'
  • decreasing with '3'. [3]

1. Zero acceleration: This is where the graph is horizontal (gradient is zero). A point should be labelled '1' on either of the horizontal sections of the graph (the first terminal velocity before A, or the second terminal velocity at B).

2. Negative acceleration: This is where the speed is rapidly decreasing (gradient is negative). A point should be labelled '2' on the steep downward curve immediately after point A.

3. Decreasing acceleration: This is where the graph is still curving upwards but becoming less steep (gradient is positive but decreasing). A point should be labelled '3' on the initial curved section between the origin and point A.

Recap

On a speed-time graph, the gradient of the line is equal to the acceleration.

  • A horizontal line (zero gradient) means zero acceleration (i.e., steady speed or terminal velocity).
  • A negative gradient means negative acceleration (deceleration).
  • A decreasing positive gradient (a curve that is flattening) means a decreasing acceleration.

(ii) Explain, in terms of forces, the changes in motion which occur from when the parachutist leaves the hot-air balloon until point A. [4]

Initially, the main downward force is the parachutist's weight (or gravitational force), causing acceleration.

As the parachutist's speed increases, air resistance increases, acting in the opposite (upward) direction.

This reduces the resultant downward force on the parachutist, which means the acceleration decreases (this is section '3' on the graph).

Eventually, the air resistance becomes equal in size to the weight. The resultant force is now zero, so the acceleration becomes zero, and the parachutist falls at a constant speed (terminal velocity), shown by the horizontal line leading up to point A.

Recap

The textbook describes the motion of a falling object (like a skydiver): "Initially, the skydiver accelerates, but the acceleration decreases with time, as air resistance increases... Finally... she reaches a steady velocity (zero gradient). This is the terminal velocity." This matches the motion from time = 0 to point A.

Paper: 0625/42/M/J/23 Q2 | Topics: Energy, Forces, Momentum

A student catches a cricket ball. The speed of the ball immediately before it is caught is 18 m/s. The mass of the cricket ball is 160 g.

(a) Calculate the kinetic energy stored in the cricket ball immediately before it is caught. [3]

First, convert the mass from grams to kilograms:

Mass (m) = 160 g = 0.16 kg

The formula for kinetic energy (KE) is:

Ek = 1/2 × m × v²

Ek = 1/2 × 0.16 kg × (18 m/s)²

Ek = 0.08 × 324

Ek = 25.92 J

Kinetic energy ≈ 26 J

Recap

The textbook defines the formula for kinetic energy (Ek):

Ek = 1/2 mv²

Kinetic energy (J) = 1/2 × mass (kg) × velocity² (m/s)²

(b) It takes 0.12 s to catch the ball and bring it to rest.

Calculate the average force exerted on the ball. [2]

Force is the rate of change of momentum (Impulse).

F = (mv - mu) / t

Where m = 0.16 kg, v (final velocity) = 0 m/s, u (initial velocity) = 18 m/s, and t = 0.12 s.

F = ( (0.16 × 0) - (0.16 × 18) ) / 0.12

F = ( 0 - 2.88 ) / 0.12

F = -24 N

The average force exerted is 24 N. (The negative sign indicates the force is in the opposite direction to the ball's initial motion).

Recap

The textbook links force, impulse, and momentum. The resultant force is equal to the change in momentum per second: F = Δp / Δt.

This can also be written as Ft = mv - mu, where Ft is the impulse.

(c) As the student catches the ball, she moves her hands backwards.

Explain the effect of this action on the student's hands. [1]

By moving her hands backwards, the student increases the time (t) it takes to stop the ball. Since the change in momentum (mv - mu) is the same, increasing the time decreases the average force (F) exerted on her hands (because F = Δp / t). This makes the catch hurt less.

Recap

From the impulse-momentum equation Ft = mv - mu, we can see that force (F) and time (t) are inversely related for a given change in momentum. If the time (t) is increased, the force (F) must decrease. This principle is also used in safety features like car crumple zones.

(a) Fig. 3.1 shows a person moving across an ice-covered pond to reach a ball on the ice.

Explain why this way of moving across the ice is safer than walking. Use your understanding of pressure in your answer. [3]

The formula for pressure is Pressure = Force / Area (p = F/A).

The person's weight (Force) remains the same. By lying flat, the person distributes their weight over a much greater area of the ice compared to standing.

Because the area is greater, the pressure exerted on the ice is much lower, making it less likely to crack.

Recap

The textbook defines pressure as the force per unit area. "The pressure on a surface due to a force is equal to the force on 1 m² of the surface."

pressure (N/m²) = force (N) / area (m²)

p = F/A

(b) Fig. 3.2 shows a side view of the pond with a layer of ice floating freely on the water.

The surface area of the pond is 5.0 m². The mass of the ice is 690 kg. The density of water is 1000 kg/m³. Point X is 0.45 m below the ice.

Calculate the pressure at point X due to the ice and the water. [4]

The total pressure at X is the pressure from the water PLUS the pressure from the ice.

1. Pressure from water (pwater):

pwater = ρ × g × h

pwater = 1000 kg/m³ × 9.8 N/kg × 0.45 m

pwater = 4410 Pa

2. Pressure from ice (pice):

First, find the weight (Force) of the ice: Fice = m × g

Fice = 690 kg × 9.8 N/kg = 6762 N

Now, find the pressure this force exerts: pice = F / A

pice = 6762 N / 5.0 m² = 1352.4 Pa

3. Total Pressure (ptotal):

ptotal = pwater + pice

ptotal = 4410 Pa + 1352.4 Pa = 5762.4 Pa

Total pressure ≈ 5.8 × 10³ Pa (or 5800 Pa)

Recap

This problem combines two pressure formulas.
1. Pressure in a liquid: Δp = ρgΔh. The pressure... increases directly proportionally with depth (h) and density (ρ).
2. Pressure from a solid: p = F/A. The pressure... is equal to the force (N) / area (m²).

(a) The temperature of a fixed mass of gas at constant volume is decreased.

State and explain, in terms of particles, how the pressure of the gas changes. [3]

State: The pressure decreases.

Explain: Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles. By decreasing the temperature, the particles move slower and have less kinetic energy. This means they collide with the walls of the container less frequently and with less force. Since pressure is caused by the total force of these collisions, the pressure decreases.

Recap

The textbook explains the link between temperature and pressure for a gas: "If the temperature of the gas increases, the average speed of the particles and the number of collisions per second increases. Hence the force... is greater and so the pressure increases." This question describes the reverse: decreasing temperature leads to slower speed, fewer/weaker collisions, and thus lower pressure.

(b) (i) State the value of absolute zero in °C. [1]

Value of absolute zero = -273 °C

Recap

The textbook states: "...there is a lowest theoretical temperature called absolute zero (-273 °C)."

(ii) Explain what is meant by the term absolute zero. Refer to particles in your answer. [2]

Absolute zero is the lowest possible temperature. At this temperature, the particles of a substance have their minimum (or least) kinetic energy; they would theoretically stop moving.

Recap

The concept of absolute zero is linked to the kinetic theory of matter. Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of particles, so the lowest possible temperature corresponds to the lowest possible kinetic energy.

(c) Cylinder 1 contains 350 cm³ of gas at a pressure of 9.0 × 10⁴ Pa. The gas is transferred to cylinder 2 and the pressure increases to 1.6 × 10⁵ Pa. The temperature remains constant.

Calculate the volume of cylinder 2. [3]

Since the temperature is constant, we can use Boyle's Law (the gas law):

p1V1 = p2V2

(9.0 × 10⁴ Pa) × (350 cm³) = (1.6 × 10⁵ Pa) × V2

31,500,000 = 160,000 × V2

V2 = 31,500,000 / 160,000

V2 = 196.875 cm³

Volume ≈ 200 cm³ (to 2 significant figures)

Recap

The textbook states the relationship for a gas at constant temperature: "This means that the volume is inversely proportional to the pressure. The relationship... can be summarized in the following equation: pV = constant."

Paper: 0625/42/M/J/23 Q5 | Topics: Thermal Physics, Density

(a) Fig. 5.1 shows an electric heater used to heat a room.

The dimensions of the room are 4.5 m × 6.1 m × 2.4 m. The density of air is 1.2 kg/m³.

(i) Show that the mass of air in the room is 79 kg. [2]

1. Calculate the volume of the room (V):

V = length × width × height

V = 4.5 m × 6.1 m × 2.4 m = 65.88 m³

2. Calculate the mass of the air (m) using the density (ρ):

m = ρ × V

m = 1.2 kg/m³ × 65.88 m³ = 79.056 kg

This shows that the mass of the air is approximately 79 kg.

Recap

The textbook provides the formula for density (ρ):

density (kg/m³) = mass (kg) / volume (m³)

ρ = m / V

This can be rearranged to find mass: m = ρ × V.

(ii) The power of the heater is 1100 W. The specific heat capacity of air is 1000 J/(kg °C).

Calculate the time taken to increase the temperature of the air in the room from 16.0 °C to 20.0 °C. [4]

1. Calculate the temperature change (Δθ):

Δθ = 20.0 °C - 16.0 °C = 4.0 °C

2. Calculate the thermal energy (ΔE) needed:

ΔE = m × c × Δθ

ΔE = 79 kg × 1000 J/(kg °C) × 4.0 °C = 316,000 J

3. Calculate the time (t) using power (P):

Power = Energy / time (P = ΔE / t), so t = ΔE / P

t = 316,000 J / 1100 W (or 1100 J/s)

t = 287.27 s

Time ≈ 290 s (to 2 significant figures)

Recap

This calculation uses two key formulas:
1. Thermal Energy: E = mcΔθ
(Energy transferred = mass × specific heat capacity × temperature change)
2. Power: P = ΔE / t
(Power = change in energy ÷ time)

(iii) Suggest one reason why the time calculated in (a)(ii) is the minimum time needed to increase the temperature of the air in the room from 16.0 °C to 20.0 °C. [1]

The calculation assumes all the heat energy from the heater goes into heating *only* the air. In reality, thermal energy is transferred (lost) to the furniture, walls, windows, or ceiling, or escapes from the room.

Recap

Energy transfer in a real-world scenario is rarely 100% efficient. The principle of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, but it can be transferred to other stores. In this case, energy is transferred to the internal energy of the surroundings (walls, windows) as well as the air, so more total energy (and thus more time) is needed.

(b) Fig. 5.2 shows a cross-section of a double-glazed window in the room.

State the main methods of thermal energy transfer from the room to outside which are reduced by this type of window. [1]

The narrow air gap reduces heat transfer by conduction (as air is a poor conductor) and convection (as the narrow gap prevents air from circulating easily).

Recap

The textbook explains methods of home insulation. "Double-glazed windows have air... between the two layers of glass to reduce energy exchange by conduction and convection." Air is a poor thermal conductor because its molecules are far apart. The trapped air cannot move easily, which reduces convection.

(b) Fig. 8.1 shows a negatively charged metal sphere.

The lines must touch the surface of the sphere.

(c) Fig. 8.2 shows a circuit.

The three cells are identical and have zero resistance. The resistors R₁, R₂ and R₃ are identical. The reading on the voltmeter is 6.0 V. When the diode is conducting, it has zero resistance and zero potential difference (p.d.) across it.

(i) Determine the e.m.f. of one cell. [1]

The voltmeter is measuring the total e.m.f. of the three cells in series.

e.m.f. of one cell = Total e.m.f. / Number of cells

e.m.f. of one cell = 6.0 V / 3 = 2.0 V

Recap

Cells connected in series provide a total e.m.f. (electromotive force) that is the sum of their individual e.m.f.s. The voltmeter in parallel measures the potential difference across the component(s) it is connected to.

(ii) Determine the ratio of the p.d. across R₂ to the p.d. across R₃. [1]

The diode is forward-biased and conducts. Let R be the resistance of each resistor.

The branch with R₁ and the diode has resistance R.

The branch with R₂ has resistance R.

These two branches are in parallel, so their combined resistance (Rp) is R/2.

This parallel combination is in series with R₃ (resistance R).

The total resistance of the circuit is Rtotal = R/2 + R = 1.5R.

The total current (Itotal) from the battery is V / Rtotal = 6.0 V / 1.5R = 4.0/R.

This total current flows through R₃. So, p.d. across R₃ (V₃) = Itotal × R₃ = (4.0/R) × R = 4.0 V.

The p.d. across the parallel section is 6.0 V - 4.0 V = 2.0 V. This is the p.d. across R₂.

So, V₂ = 2.0 V and V₃ = 4.0 V.

The ratio V₂ : V₃ is 2.0 : 4.0, which simplifies to 1 : 2.

Recap

This problem uses rules for series and parallel circuits.
1. In series, current is the same, and voltage divides (Vtotal = V₁ + V₂).
2. In parallel, voltage is the same, and current divides.
3. Ohm's Law: V = IR.

(iii) All the cells are reversed.

1. State and explain the change in current in R₁. [1]

State: The current in R₁ becomes zero.

Explain: Reversing the cells reverses the direction of the conventional current. The diode is now reverse-biased and acts as an open switch, blocking any current from flowing through that branch.

Recap

A diode is a component that allows current to flow easily in only one direction (the "forward" direction, indicated by its symbol's arrow). If voltage is applied in the reverse direction, it has a very high resistance and (ideally) no current flows.

2. Determine the new value of the ratio of the p.d. across R₂ to the p.d. across R₃. [1]

Since no current flows through the R₁-diode branch, the circuit is now a simple series circuit with R₂ and R₃.

In a series circuit, the total voltage (6.0 V) is split between the components in proportion to their resistance.

Since R₂ and R₃ are identical (have the same resistance), the voltage will split equally.

p.d. across R₂ (V₂) = 3.0 V

p.d. across R₃ (V₃) = 3.0 V

The ratio V₂ : V₃ is 3.0 : 3.0, which simplifies to 1 : 1.

Recap

In a series circuit, the potential difference (voltage) from the supply is divided between the components. If two components in series have equal resistance, they will share the voltage equally.

(b) State how β-decay changes the nucleus of an atom. [1]

(c) A radiation detector used in a laboratory detects a background count rate of 30 counts/min. A radioactive source is placed in front of the radiation detector. The initial reading on the detector is 550 counts/min. The half-life of the source is 25 minutes.

Calculate the expected reading on the detector after 75 minutes. [4]

1. Correct for background radiation:

Initial count rate from source = Total reading - Background reading

Initial count rate from source = 550 counts/min - 30 counts/min = 520 counts/min

2. Determine the number of half-lives:

Number of half-lives = Total time / Half-life

Number of half-lives = 75 minutes / 25 minutes = 3 half-lives

3. Calculate the final count rate from the source:

After 1 half-life: 520 / 2 = 260

After 2 half-lives: 260 / 2 = 130

After 3 half-lives: 130 / 2 = 65 counts/min

4. Calculate the expected detector reading:

Expected reading = Final source reading + Background reading

Expected reading = 65 counts/min + 30 counts/min = 95 counts/min

Recap

Background radiation is all around us from sources like radon gas, cosmic rays, and medical uses. It must be subtracted from a measurement to find the true activity of a source. Half-life is the time taken for the activity (or count rate) of a radioactive source to halve.

(d) State two safety precautions taken when moving, using or storing radioactive sources in a laboratory. [2]

Any two from:

  • Limit time of exposure.
  • Store sources in lead-lined boxes when not in use.
  • Keep distance from sources (use tongs to handle).
  • Avoid contact with skin (wear gloves).
  • Point sources away from people.

Recap

Radioactive particles are dangerous because they can ionise atoms in cells, which can lead to cell damage or death, and potentially cause cancer. People who work with radiation are monitored to check they have not been exposed to unsafe levels.

(a) State the equation that defines the average orbital speed v of a planet. State the meaning of any symbols you use. [2]

(b) Suggest why countries that are a significant distance from the Equator experience significant temperature variation throughout the year. [1]

This is due to the tilt of the Earth's axis. Throughout the year, this tilt means that the rays from the Sun strike the country at different angles, which changes the concentration of energy (insolation).

OR: The number of hours of daylight per day changes significantly throughout the year.

Recap

The Earth orbits the Sun. The tilt of the Earth's axis relative to its orbit is responsible for the seasons. When a hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, it receives more direct sunlight and experiences summer; when tilted away, it receives less direct sunlight and experiences winter.

(d) A galaxy is moving away from the Earth with a speed of 33 000 km/s. The value of the Hubble constant is 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second.

Calculate the distance from the galaxy to the Earth. Give your answer in light-years. [2]

1. State Hubble's Law:

v = H₀ × d

2. Convert speed (v) to m/s:

v = 33,000 km/s = 33,000 × 1000 m/s = 3.3 × 10⁷ m/s

3. Rearrange to find distance (d) in meters:

d = v / H₀

d = (3.3 × 10⁷ m/s) / (2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹)

d = 1.5 × 10²⁵ m

4. Convert meters to light-years (1 ly ≈ 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m):

d (ly) = (1.5 × 10²⁵ m) / (9.46 × 10¹⁵ m/ly)

d (ly) = 1.585 × 10⁹ light-years

Distance ≈ 1.6 × 10⁹ light-years

Recap

The Hubble constant (H₀) relates a galaxy's recessional velocity (v) to its distance (d) from us. This relationship, v = H₀d, is evidence for the expansion of the universe. Galaxies that are further away are moving away from us faster.
