6. Thermal Physics (Conduction, States)

Fig. 4.1 shows a metal pan on an electric hotplate. The pan contains 200 cm³ of water.

The pan is heated. The temperature of the water in the pan increases.

(a) Thermal energy is transferred through the metal pan by conduction.

State and explain the two ways that thermal energy is conducted in a metal. [3]

1. Lattice vibrations: The particles (ions) in the metal lattice vibrate more when heated. They collide with neighbouring particles, passing the kinetic energy along the metal.

2. Free electrons: Metals have delocalised/free electrons. These electrons gain kinetic energy at the hot end and move quickly through the metal, colliding with ions and other electrons, rapidly transferring energy to the colder parts.

Recap

Conduction is the transfer of thermal energy through a substance without the substance itself moving. In metals, this is efficient due to both lattice vibrations and the movement of free electrons. Non-metals are poor conductors as they lack free electrons. Liquids and gases are generally very poor conductors.

(b) (i) The water boils and leaves the liquid as a gas.

Explain, in terms of forces and distances between particles, why the gas occupies a much greater volume than it does as a liquid. [2]

In the liquid state, particles are close together with strong intermolecular forces of attraction holding them. In the gas state, the particles are very far apart and have negligible (almost zero) intermolecular forces between them, allowing them to expand and fill a much larger volume.

Recap

Matter exists in three states:

  • Solid: Particles are close together, in a regular pattern, vibrating in fixed positions. Strong forces.
  • Liquid: Particles are close together, in a random arrangement, able to move past one another. Weaker forces.
  • Gas: Particles are very far apart, in a random arrangement, moving randomly and quickly. Negligible forces.

(ii) State two ways in which boiling differs from evaporation. [2]

1 ...........................................................

2 ...........................................................

  1. Boiling occurs at a fixed temperature (the boiling point), whereas evaporation occurs at any temperature below the boiling point.
  2. Boiling takes place throughout the liquid, whereas evaporation only takes place from the surface of the liquid.

Recap

Evaporation is when the more energetic molecules escape from the surface of a liquid, which can happen at a range of temperatures. Boiling is a rapid change of state from liquid to gas that occurs at a specific boiling point and happens throughout the bulk of the liquid.

(a) Sound waves have compressions and rarefactions.

Explain what is meant by compression and rarefaction. [2]

compression ...........................................................

rarefaction ...........................................................

Compression: A region in a longitudinal wave (like sound) where the particles of the medium are closer together than normal, resulting in a region of higher pressure.

Rarefaction: A region in a longitudinal wave where the particles are further apart than normal, resulting in a region of lower pressure.

Recap

Sound is a longitudinal wave, meaning the particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of wave travel. This creates a series of compressions and rarefactions. Sound transfers energy without transferring matter.

(b) We can see light from the Sun but we cannot hear any sound from it.

State the reason for this. [1]

Sound requires a medium (like air or water) to travel through, as it is a vibration of particles. Light (an electromagnetic wave) does not require a medium and can travel through the vacuum of space.

Recap

Waves can be mechanical or electromagnetic. Mechanical waves (like sound) need a medium to propagate. Electromagnetic waves (like light and radio waves) can travel through a vacuum. Space is a vacuum, so light can reach us from the Sun, but sound cannot.

Question 7

11. Electrical Quantities / 13. Circuits

(a) Define potential difference (p.d.). [2]

Potential difference (p.d.), or voltage, is the work done (or energy transferred) per unit charge as the charge moves between two points in a circuit. It is measured in Volts (V).

Recap

The formula for potential difference (p.d.) is $V = WQ$, where $V$ is p.d. in Volts, $W$ is work done (energy transferred) in Joules, and $Q$ is charge in Coulombs. One Volt is equal to one Joule per Coulomb (1 V = 1 JC).

(b) (i) State the equation which defines electromotive force (e.m.f.) E. [1]

The equation is $E = WQ$.

Recap

Electromotive force (e.m.f.) is the work done by the energy source (like a battery) in moving a unit charge around a complete circuit. It is also measured in Volts (V). It represents the total energy supplied per coulomb of charge.

(ii) The e.m.f. of a battery is 9.0 V. The battery is in a circuit.

Calculate the work done by the battery when it moves a charge of 30 C around a complete circuit. [2]

work done = ...........................................................

Using the e.m.f. equation $E = WQ$, we rearrange for work done ($W$):

$W = E x Q$

$W = 9.0 \text{ V} x 30 \text{ C}$

$W = 270 \text{ J}$

Recap

Since e.m.f. ($E$) is the work done (W) per unit charge (Q) for a complete circuit, the total work done (total energy supplied) is the e.m.f. multiplied by the total charge that flows.

(c) A circuit consists of a d.c. power supply, a lamp and a thermistor.

(i) Draw a circuit diagram of these components connected in series. [2]

Recap

A series circuit is one where components are connected end-to-end, forming a single loop. The current is the same at all points in a series circuit. A thermistor is a resistor whose resistance changes with temperature.

(ii) Explain what happens in the circuit you have drawn in (c)(i) when the temperature of the thermistor is increased. [2]

When the temperature of the thermistor increases, its resistance decreases. This causes the total resistance of the series circuit to decrease. As a result, the current flowing through the entire circuit increases, and the lamp will glow brighter.

Recap

For a standard (NTC) thermistor, resistance decreases as temperature increases. In a series circuit, the total resistance is the sum of individual resistances. According to Ohm's Law ($V=IR$), if the total voltage ($V$) from the supply is constant, a decrease in total resistance ($R$) will cause the total current (I) to increase.

14. Electromagnetic Effects (Motor)

Fig. 8.1 shows a horizontal, flat coil in a magnetic field

The coil is connected to a cell. The coil rotates.

(a) Determine the direction of movement of the side AB relative to the plane of the coil. [1]

direction of movement = ...........................................................

The direction of movement is downwards (into the page).

Recap

This is determined using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule (the motor effect).

  • Thumb: Thrust / Force / Motion
  • Forefinger: Field (North to South)
  • Centre finger: Current (Positive to Negative)

(b) Explain how you determined the direction in (a). [2]

By using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule:

  1. The magnetic field (Forefinger) points from North to South (left to right).
  2. The conventional current (Centre finger) in side AB flows from A to B (away from the positive terminal, towards the negative).
  3. This results in the Thrust/Force (Thumb) pointing downwards.

Recap

The motor effect describes how a current-carrying wire experiences a force when placed in an external magnetic field. The force is maximum when the wire and field are at 90° and zero when they are parallel.

(c) State and explain what happens to the coil as it reaches the vertical position. [2]

As the coil reaches the vertical position, the turning effect (moment) becomes zero. This happens because the forces on sides AB and its opposite side are still acting up and down, but they are now acting in line with the axis, producing no turning moment. However, the coil's momentum causes it to overshoot this vertical position.

Recap

In a simple d.c. motor, the turning effect (moment) is maximum when the coil is horizontal (parallel to the field) and zero when the coil is vertical (perpendicular to the field).

(d) To operate as a motor, a split-ring commutator and brushes are added to the parts shown in Fig. 8.1.

Explain the effects of the split-ring commutator and the brushes on the action of the motor. [3]

Split-ring commutator: Its function is to reverse the direction of the current in the coil every half turn (each time the coil passes the vertical position). This ensures that the force on side AB is always downwards and the force on the opposite side is always upwards, allowing the coil to rotate continuously in the same direction.

Brushes: The brushes (usually made of graphite) maintain electrical contact between the power supply and the rotating commutator segments, allowing current to flow to the coil without the wires getting tangled.

Recap

A d.c. motor requires a split-ring commutator to reverse the current direction every 180° of rotation. This ensures the moment (turning force) always acts in the same direction, causing continuous rotation. The brushes provide a stationary connection to the moving coil.

15. Nuclear Physics (Fission & Fusion)

(a) A nuclear power station has a reactor where controlled nuclear fission of uranium-235 takes place.

(i) Explain what is meant by nuclear fission. [3]

Nuclear fission is the process where a large, unstable nucleus (like uranium-235) splits into two or more smaller nuclei (daughter nuclei). This process is typically triggered by the nucleus absorbing a neutron. It also releases a large amount of energy and more neutrons (usually 2 or 3), which can go on to cause a chain reaction.

Recap

Nuclear fission is the splitting of a heavy nucleus, releasing energy. This is different from nuclear fusion, which is the joining of light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, also releasing energy. Both processes convert a small amount of mass into a large amount of energy, following E = mc2.

(ii) State one advantage and one disadvantage of generating electrical power in nuclear power stations compared with electrical power generated using wind turbines. [2]

advantage ...........................................................

disadvantage ...........................................................

Advantage: Nuclear power provides a continuous, reliable supply of energy and is not dependent on the weather (like wind). It also produces a very large amount of energy from a small amount of fuel and does not produce greenhouse gases.

Disadvantage: Nuclear power produces hazardous/dangerous radioactive waste which is difficult and expensive to store safely for a long time. The cost of building and decommissioning is also very high, and there is a risk of catastrophic accidents.

Recap

All energy resources have advantages and disadvantages related to cost, reliability, scale, and environmental impact. Nuclear is reliable and high-output but produces dangerous waste. Wind is renewable and clean but is unreliable (not always windy) and has a lower power output per turbine.

(b) Deuterium is an isotope of hydrogen (H) with 1 proton and 1 neutron. Nuclear fusion occurs when two nuclei of deuterium combine. An isotope of helium (He) and a neutron are formed.

Use nuclide notation to write down the nuclide equation for this reaction. [3]

21H + 21H → 32He + 10n

  • Deuterium (21H): 1 proton + 1 neutron = mass number 2. Proton number = 1.
  • Helium isotope (32He): To balance the equation, mass = 2+2-1 = 3. Protons = 1+1-0 = 2.
  • Neutron (10n): 0 protons = atomic number 0. Mass number = 1.

Recap

Nuclide notation is written as AZX, where X is the element symbol, A is the mass number (protons + neutrons), and Z is the proton number (atomic number). In any nuclear equation, both the total mass number and the total proton number must be conserved (be equal) on both sides of the arrow.

(c) Light from a distant galaxy is redshifted.

(i) Explain what is meant by redshift. [2]

Redshift is the observation that the wavelength of light from distant galaxies has increased (or stretched), shifting it towards the red end of the electromagnetic spectrum. This happens because the galaxy is moving away from us (and the universe is expanding).

Recap

Redshift is an example of the Doppler effect for light. Light from objects moving away from an observer is redshifted (wavelength increases, frequency decreases). Light from objects moving towards an observer would be blueshifted (wavelength decreases, frequency increases). Nearly all distant galaxies show redshift, which is evidence for the expansion of the universe.

(ii) State the quantity that the redshift of a galaxy is used to calculate. [1]

Redshift is used to calculate the speed (or velocity) at which the galaxy is moving away from Earth.

Recap

The greater the redshift observed from a distant galaxy, the faster it is moving away from us. This relationship (Hubble's Law) also implies that galaxies with greater redshift are further away.
