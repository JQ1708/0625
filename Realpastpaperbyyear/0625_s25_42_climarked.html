Question Number: 1 | Paper Code: 0625/42/M/J/25 Q1 | Topic(s): Kinematics

Fig. 1.1 is a speed–time graph for an ice skater.

1 (a) Describe the motion of the skater between t = 0 and t = 3.0s.

The skater is moving at a constant speed or uniform speed.

Recap

On a speed-time graph, a horizontal line indicates that the speed is not changing, which means the object is moving at a constant speed.

1 (b) Calculate the distance travelled by the skater between t = 0 and t = 3.0s.

The distance travelled is 33 m. This is calculated by finding the area under the graph between t=0 and t=3.0s, which is a rectangle. The area is speed × time = 11 m/s × 3.0 s = 33 m.

Recap

The distance travelled is the area under a speed-time graph. For a constant speed, this is a rectangle with an area equal to speed multiplied by time.

1 (c) (i) State what is meant by deceleration.

Deceleration is the rate of decrease of velocity, or the decrease in velocity per unit time. It is also known as negative acceleration.

Recap

Acceleration is a vector quantity, so it has both magnitude and direction. Deceleration means the acceleration is in the opposite direction to the velocity.

(ii) Draw a tangent to the graph at t = 9.0 s. Use the tangent to calculate the deceleration of the skater at t = 9.0s.

A tangent should be drawn to the curve at t = 9.0s. The gradient of this tangent gives the deceleration. The value should be between 0.50 and 0.70 m/s².

Recap

The gradient of a speed-time graph represents acceleration. To find the instantaneous acceleration at a specific point on a curve, you need to draw a tangent to the curve at that point and calculate its gradient.

2 (a) Define the 'moment' of a force in words.

The moment of a force is the product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force.

A moment is a turning effect. The unit of a moment is the newton-metre (N m).

2 (b) On Fig. 2.1, mark the position of the centre of gravity of the metre ruler with a point labelled X. Label the distance of X from the 0 cm end of the ruler.

X should be marked at the 50 cm mark of the ruler, as it is a uniform ruler.

The centre of gravity of a uniform object is at its geometric center.

2 (c) (i) Show that the mass of the metre ruler is 0.081 kg.

By the principle of moments, the sum of clockwise moments equals the sum of anticlockwise moments. Let m be the mass of the ruler. Then (mg × 8) + (0.12 × 38) = 0.34 × 32. Solving for m gives approximately 0.081 kg.

Recap

The principle of moments is a condition for rotational equilibrium.

(ii) Calculate the density of the wood of the metre ruler.

The density is 520 kg/m³. First, calculate the volume of the ruler: Volume = 1.0 m × (2.6 × 10⁻² m) × (6.0 × 10⁻³ m) = 1.56 × 10⁻⁴ m³. Then, density = mass/volume = 0.081 kg / (1.56 × 10⁻⁴ m³) ≈ 520 kg/m³.

Recap

Density is mass per unit volume. The standard unit for density is kg/m³.

Question Number: 3 | Paper Code: 0625/42/M/J/25 Q3 | Topic(s): Momentum

3 (a) Define momentum.

Momentum is the product of an object's mass and its velocity.

Recap

Momentum is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction. Its unit is kg m/s.

Fig. 3.1 shows two toy trains, A and B, on a track. Train A travels towards train B and they collide. Immediately before the collision: the velocity of train A is 0.34 m/s to the right; the velocity of train B is 0.12 m/s to the left. The trains stick together when they collide.

3 (b) Calculate the velocity of the trains immediately after the collision.

The velocity of the trains immediately after the collision is 0.19 m/s to the right. Using the principle of conservation of momentum: (m_A * v_A) + (m_B * v_B) = (m_A + m_B) * v_final. Given m_A = 0.45kg, v_A = 0.34 m/s, m_B = 0.21kg, v_B = -0.12 m/s (since it's in the opposite direction). So, (0.45 * 0.34) + (0.21 * -0.12) = (0.45 + 0.21) * v_final. This gives 0.153 - 0.0252 = 0.66 * v_final, so 0.1278 = 0.66 * v_final, and v_final = 0.1936 m/s, which rounds to 0.19 m/s.

Recap

The principle of conservation of momentum states that in a closed system, the total momentum before a collision is equal to the total momentum after the collision.

3 (c) A different train is travelling with a momentum of 0.26kgm/s. The train slows down and stops after 2.1 s. Calculate the average force acting on the train during this time.

The average force acting on the train is -0.12 N. Using the formula F = Δp / Δt, where Δp is the change in momentum (0 - 0.26 kgm/s) and Δt is the time (2.1 s). So, F = -0.26 / 2.1 = -0.1238 N, which rounds to -0.12 N. The negative sign indicates the force is in the opposite direction to the initial momentum.

Recap

Force is the rate of change of momentum. A negative force indicates that the force is acting to slow the object down.

Container A will have the larger temperature change. This is because its dull black surface is a better emitter of infrared radiation compared to the shiny white surface of container B.

Dull, dark surfaces are good absorbers and emitters of thermal radiation, while shiny, light surfaces are poor absorbers and emitters.

(ii) After 30 minutes, the temperature of the water in container A remains constant. State, in terms of energy transfers, why the temperature remains constant.

The temperature remains constant because the rate of energy transfer from the container to the surroundings is equal to the rate of energy transfer from the surroundings to the container.

When an object reaches thermal equilibrium with its surroundings, there is no net transfer of thermal energy.

(iii) The experiment is repeated without the lids on the containers. State the name of the additional method of energy transfer that occurs.

The additional method of energy transfer that occurs is convection.

Recap

Convection involves the movement of fluid (in this case, air) to transfer heat. Without lids, air can move freely above the water surface.

Fig. 4.2 shows a metal pan with a wooden handle.

4 (b) Explain why there is a much greater rate of transfer of energy through the metal than through the wood.

Metal is a much better thermal conductor than wood. Metal has free electrons that can move and transfer thermal energy efficiently, whereas wood transfers energy mainly through lattice vibrations, which is less efficient.

Recap

Thermal conductivity is a measure of a material's ability to conduct heat. Metals are generally good conductors due to their free electrons.

Question Number: 5 | Paper Code: 0625/42/M/J/25 Q5 | Topic(s): Waves, Sound, Electromagnetic Spectrum

5 (a) Describe an experiment to determine the speed of sound in air. State the apparatus required, the measurements taken and how to calculate the speed of sound in air from the measurements. You may draw a labelled diagram as part of your answer.

An experiment to determine the speed of sound in air can be done using the echo method. Apparatus: two people, a stopwatch, a measuring tape, and a large wall. Procedure: One person stands a measured distance (e.g., 100m) from a wall. The person claps their hands loudly and starts the stopwatch simultaneously. The second person listens for the echo and stops the stopwatch when they hear it. The time taken for the sound to travel to the wall and back is recorded. Calculation: The speed of sound = (2 × distance to wall) / time taken.

Recap

The speed of sound depends on the medium it travels through and its temperature. Sound travels faster in warmer air.

5 (b) (i) Bluetooth is used to connect a mobile (cell) phone to headphones. State the name of the region of the electromagnetic spectrum used by Bluetooth.

Bluetooth uses the radio waves region of the electromagnetic spectrum.

Recap

Radio waves are the longest wavelength electromagnetic waves and are used for communication, broadcasting, and remote control.

(ii) State the speed of electromagnetic waves in air.

The speed of electromagnetic waves in air is approximately 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

Recap

All electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light in a vacuum, which is approximately 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Their speed in air is very close to this value.

(iii) The frequency of a Bluetooth network is 2.48 GHz. Calculate the wavelength of the waves used in the Bluetooth network.

The wavelength of the waves is 0.12 m. Using the wave equation v = fλ, where v = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s and f = 2.48 GHz = 2.48 × 10⁹ Hz. So, λ = v / f = (3.0 × 10⁸) / (2.48 × 10⁹) = 0.1209 m, which rounds to 0.12 m.

Recap

The wave equation applies to all types of waves, including electromagnetic waves.

Question Number: 7 | Paper Code: 0625/42/M/J/25 Q7 | Topic(s): Electricity, Electrostatics

7 (a) State what is meant by an electric field.

An electric field is a region where an electric charge experiences a force.

Recap

Electric fields are created by electric charges and can exert forces on other charges.

7 (b) (i) A plastic rod is rubbed with a cloth. The plastic rod becomes negatively charged and the cloth becomes positively charged. Explain why.

When the plastic rod is rubbed with a cloth, electrons are transferred from the cloth to the plastic rod. The plastic rod gains electrons and becomes negatively charged, while the cloth loses electrons and becomes positively charged.

Recap

Static electricity is caused by the transfer of electrons between materials due to friction.

(ii) The negatively charged plastic rod is suspended by an insulating thread. Another negatively charged plastic rod is brought close to the suspended rod. State what happens to the suspended plastic rod.

The suspended plastic rod will be repelled by the other negatively charged rod.

Recap

Like charges repel each other, while opposite charges attract.

7 (c) (i) Define the kilowatt-hour (kWh) in words.

The kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the energy transferred by an appliance with a power of 1 kilowatt operating for 1 hour.

Recap

The kilowatt-hour is a common unit of energy used for billing electricity consumption.

(ii) A small lamp illuminates an electric oven. The lamp has an output power of 25W and operates for 220 hours in one year. The p.d. across the lamp is 230 V.

1. Calculate the energy transferred by the lamp in one year. Give your answer in kWh.

The energy transferred is 5.5 kWh. Energy = Power × Time = 25 W × 220 hours = 5500 Wh = 5.5 kWh.

Recap

To convert from Watt-hours to kilowatt-hours, divide by 1000.

2. Calculate the current in the lamp.

The current in the lamp is 0.11 A. Using P = IV, I = P/V = 25W / 230V = 0.1086 A, which rounds to 0.11 A.

Recap

Ohm's Law and the power formula are fundamental for electrical calculations.

An e.m.f. is induced only when the coil is turning because this causes the coil to cut magnetic field lines, which is necessary for electromagnetic induction.

Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction states that an e.m.f. is induced in a conductor when it cuts magnetic field lines or when the magnetic flux linkage through it changes.

One possible change is to increase the strength of the magnetic field, increase the speed of rotation of the coil, or increase the number of turns in the coil.

The magnitude of the induced e.m.f. depends on the rate at which magnetic field lines are cut.

8 (b) (i) Resistors A, B, C and D are identical. The current in resistor A is 2.4A. State the value of the current in resistor B. Explain your answer.

The current in resistor B is 1.2 A. Resistors B and C are in parallel, and their combined resistance is in series with A and D. Since B and C are identical and in parallel, the current from the branch containing A splits equally between B and C. So, the current in B is half of the current in A, which is 2.4 A / 2 = 1.2 A.

In a parallel circuit, the current divides among the branches, and if the resistances are equal, the current divides equally.

The value of the current I is 6.0 A. This implies a specific circuit configuration where the total current is the sum of currents through different parallel branches, or a series combination where the current is constant. Based on the mark scheme, the total current is 6.0A.

The resistance of resistor A is 2.1 Ω. Using Ohm's Law, R = V/I = 5.0V / 2.4A = 2.083 Ω, which rounds to 2.1 Ω.

9 (a) (i) State what is meant by background radiation.

Background radiation is the radiation (always) present in the environment, originating from natural sources.

Recap

Sources of background radiation include cosmic rays, rocks, soil, and radon gas.

(ii) State one source that makes a significant contribution to background radiation.

One significant source of background radiation is radon gas.

Recap

Radon gas is a naturally occurring radioactive gas that seeps from the ground and can accumulate in buildings.

9 (b) (i) Radium-223 (²²³Ra) is an isotope of radium. Radium-223 decays by the emission of alpha (α) particles to an isotope of radon (Rn). Complete the nuclide equation for this decay.

The completed nuclide equation is: ²²³₈₈Ra → ²¹⁹₈₆Rn + ⁴₂α.

Recap

Alpha decay involves the emission of an alpha particle (a helium nucleus), which reduces the atomic number by 2 and the mass number by 4.

The source must be inside the body because alpha particles are easily absorbed by the skin and would not reach the target organ if the source were outside the body.

Gamma radiation is used because it is highly penetrating and can pass out of the body to be detected externally.

A source with a short half-life must be used so that the radioactivity quickly diminishes, minimizing the patient's exposure to radiation after the diagnostic procedure.

Medical isotopes often have short half-lives to reduce the long-term radiation dose to patients.
