Question 1 | 0625/42/O/N/23 Q1 | Topics: Kinematics, Forces, Work and Energy

A car accelerates uniformly in a straight line from rest at time t = 0. At t = 3.2s, the speed of the car is 13.0m/s.

(a) (i) Calculate the acceleration of the car. [2]

The acceleration of the car is 4.1 m/s². Acceleration is calculated as the change in velocity divided by the time taken (a = Δv / Δt). Since the car starts from rest, its initial velocity is 0 m/s. The final velocity is 13.0 m/s and the time taken is 3.2 s. So, the acceleration is 13.0 m/s / 3.2 s = 4.0625 m/s², which is approximately 4.1 m/s².

Recap

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction. Uniform acceleration means the velocity changes by the same amount in equal time intervals.

(a) (ii) Explain in words what is meant by the term acceleration. [1]

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. It describes how quickly an object's velocity changes over time.

Recap

Velocity is a vector quantity that describes both the speed and direction of an object. Acceleration can involve a change in speed, a change in direction, or both.

(b) The car travels at 13.0m/s from t = 3.2s to t = 12.0 s.

(i) Plot the speed-time graph for the car from t = 0 to t = 12.0s. [2]

The speed-time graph should show a straight line joining (0,0) and (3.2s, 13.0m/s), representing the uniform acceleration. From t = 3.2s to t = 12.0s, the graph should show a horizontal line at 13.0m/s, indicating constant speed.

Recap

Speed-time graphs are powerful tools for analyzing motion. The gradient of a speed-time graph represents acceleration, and the area under the graph represents the distance traveled.

(ii) Determine the distance travelled by the car between t = 0 and t = 3.2s. [2]

The distance travelled by the car between t = 0 and t = 3.2s is 21 m. This is calculated by finding the area under the speed-time graph for this interval, which is a triangle. The area of a triangle is ½ × base × height. So, the distance is ½ × 3.2 s × 13.0 m/s = 20.8 m, which is approximately 21 m.

Recap

For uniform acceleration from rest, the distance traveled can also be calculated using the formula s = ½at² or by finding the average velocity ((u+v)/2) and multiplying by time (s = ((u+v)/2)t).

(c) The car decelerates from 13.0m/s to 0m/s at a constant deceleration. The mass of the car is 1350 kg. The car travels 13m in 2.0s as it decelerates. Show that the work done by the car as it decelerates is approximately 1.1 × 105 J. [3]

The work done by the car as it decelerates is approximately 1.1 × 105 J. First, calculate the deceleration: a = (0 - 13.0 m/s) / 2.0 s = -6.5 m/s². Then, calculate the force: F = ma = 1350 kg × 6.5 m/s² = 8775 N. Finally, calculate the work done: Work Done = Force × distance = 8775 N × 13 m = 114075 J. This is approximately 1.1 × 105 J.

Recap

Work done is a measure of energy transfer. When a force acts on an object and causes a displacement, work is done. The work-energy theorem states that the net work done on an object is equal to the change in its kinetic energy.

(d) On another day, the car in (c) travels a longer distance while it decelerates from 13.0m/s to 0m/s. The deceleration is constant. Suggest and explain what causes the stopping distance to increase. [2]

A possible cause for the stopping distance to increase is worn tires. Worn tires would reduce the friction between the tires and the road surface. With less friction, the braking force would be smaller, and therefore, a longer distance would be required to bring the car to a stop from the same initial speed.

Recap

Stopping distance is influenced by both thinking distance (distance traveled during reaction time) and braking distance (distance traveled while braking). Factors like road conditions, tire condition, and vehicle mass can significantly affect braking distance.

Question 2 | 0625/42/O/N/23 Q2 | Topics: Thermal Physics, Forces, Environmental Science

Fig. 2.1 shows an electric tumble dryer used to dry wet clothes. Hot air blows into the drum. The air gains water vapour from the clothes and then leaves the drum. The moist air enters the condenser. Cool air leaves the condenser, passes through the heating element and enters the drum again.

(a) (i) State the process by which the hot air removes water from the wet clothes. [1]

The process by which hot air removes water from wet clothes is evaporation.

Recap

Evaporation is the process by which a liquid changes into a gas or vapor. It occurs at the surface of a liquid and is faster at higher temperatures, larger surface areas, and with air movement.

(a) (ii) The air is cooled as it passes through the condenser. Describe and explain one other way in which the air leaving the condenser is different from the air entering the condenser. [2]

The air leaving the condenser is drier than the air entering it. This is because the water vapour has condensed (turned back into liquid water) as the air was cooled in the condenser.

Recap

Condensation is the process by which water vapor in the air is changed into liquid water. It is crucial in the water cycle and is the opposite of evaporation. Cooling a gas below its dew point causes condensation.

(b) The drum of the tumble dryer rotates, lifting up the wet clothes which then fall down through the hot air.

(i) Name the force that causes the clothes to fall down. [1]

The force that causes the clothes to fall down is gravitational force (or weight).

Recap

Gravity is a fundamental force of attraction between any two objects with mass. On Earth, it causes objects to fall towards the center of the planet. Weight is the measure of this gravitational force acting on an object.

(ii) When the drum rotates too fast the clothes remain in contact with the wall of the drum. State the direction of the resultant force on the clothes during the circular motion. [1]

The resultant force on the clothes during circular motion is directed perpendicular to the motion (or towards the center of the circular path). This is the centripetal force.

Recap

For an object to move in a circular path, a resultant force, called the centripetal force, must act towards the center of the circle. This force is always perpendicular to the object's velocity.

(c) Suggest why using a clothesline to dry clothes in the open air is better for the environment than using an electric tumble dryer. [1]

Using a clothesline is better for the environment because it uses renewable energy sources (solar and wind energy) and does not require electricity generated from fossil fuels. This means it does not produce greenhouse gases or contribute to air pollution.

Recap

Energy conservation and reducing carbon footprint are important environmental considerations. Choosing energy-efficient methods or renewable energy sources helps mitigate climate change and resource depletion.

Question 3 | 0625/42/O/N/23 Q3 | Topics: Momentum, Impulse, Forces, Vectors

(a) A balloon of mass 15g is glued to a straw. The straw is threaded onto a horizontal string, as shown in Fig. 3.1. The balloon is filled with air and then the air is released. As the air leaves the balloon, the balloon experiences a force. The balloon accelerates from rest until it reaches a constant speed. It then travels 0.67 m in 0.18 s at this constant speed.

(i) Explain in words what is meant by the term impulse. [2]

Impulse is defined as the product of the force and the time for which the force acts. It is also equal to the change in momentum of an object.

Recap

Impulse is a vector quantity, measured in Newton-seconds (Ns) or kilogram-meters per second (kgm/s). It quantifies the overall effect of a force acting over time.

(ii) Calculate the resultant impulse on the balloon while it is accelerating. [3]

The resultant impulse on the balloon is 0.056 Ns. First, calculate the constant speed of the balloon: v = distance / time = 0.67 m / 0.18 s = 3.722 m/s. The mass of the balloon is 15g = 0.015 kg. Impulse is equal to the change in momentum (Δp = mv - mu). Since the balloon starts from rest (u=0), the impulse is 0.015 kg × 3.722 m/s = 0.05583 Ns, which is approximately 0.056 Ns.

Recap

The impulse-momentum theorem states that the impulse applied to an object is equal to the change in its momentum. This theorem is derived directly from Newton's second law of motion.

(iii) Explain how momentum is conserved as the balloon accelerates. [2]

As the air is released from the balloon, the air moves in the opposite direction to the balloon's acceleration. The momentum of the air expelled is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the momentum gained by the balloon. Therefore, the total momentum of the balloon-air system remains constant, demonstrating the conservation of momentum.

Recap

The principle of conservation of momentum states that in a closed system, the total momentum remains constant if no external forces act on it. This principle is fundamental to understanding collisions and explosions.

(b) Fig. 3.2 shows the directions of two forces acting on a different balloon as it moves. Determine the magnitude and direction of the resultant force on the balloon. [4]

The magnitude of the resultant force is 0.84 N, and its direction is 62° below the horizontal. The two forces are 0.40 N (horizontal) and 0.74 N (vertical). Using Pythagoras' theorem, the magnitude of the resultant force is √(0.40² + 0.74²) = √(0.16 + 0.5476) = √0.7076 = 0.841 N, which is approximately 0.84 N. Using trigonometry, the angle θ below the horizontal is given by tan θ = opposite / adjacent = 0.74 / 0.40 = 1.85. So, θ = tan⁻¹(1.85) = 61.6°, which is approximately 62° below the horizontal.

Recap

Forces are vector quantities and can be resolved into components or combined to find a resultant force. Vector addition can be done graphically (using a vector triangle or parallelogram) or analytically (using Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry).

Question 4 | 0625/42/O/N/23 Q4 | Topics: Kinetic Theory, Thermal Physics, Pressure in Liquids

Fig. 4.1 shows a bottle part-filled with water. The air inside the bottle is at the same pressure as the air outside the bottle. The bottle and its contents are at room temperature. The temperature of the bottle and its contents are increased.

(a) (i) Explain, in terms of particles, how the air pressure inside the bottle changes as the temperature increases. [3]

As the temperature of the air inside the bottle increases, the average kinetic energy and speed of the air particles increase. This leads to more frequent and more forceful collisions of the air particles with the walls of the bottle. Since the volume of the bottle is unchanged, this results in a greater force per unit area, hence the pressure inside the bottle increases.

Recap

The kinetic theory of gases explains gas pressure in terms of the collisions of gas particles with the container walls. Increasing temperature increases the kinetic energy of particles, leading to more frequent and energetic collisions, thus increasing pressure.

(a) (ii) The lid is removed from the bottle. State and explain how the air pressure inside the bottle changes. [2]

When the lid is removed, the air pressure inside the bottle decreases. This happens because air particles escape from the bottle into the surrounding atmosphere until the pressure inside the bottle becomes equal to the atmospheric pressure outside.

Recap

Gases naturally move from areas of higher pressure to areas of lower pressure. This diffusion continues until the pressure gradient is eliminated, reaching equilibrium with the surroundings.

(b) The mass of water in the bottle is 0.18kg. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/(kg°C). Calculate the thermal energy needed to increase the temperature of the water by 20°C. [2]

The thermal energy needed is 1.5 × 104 J. This is calculated using the formula ΔE = mcΔθ, where m is the mass (0.18 kg), c is the specific heat capacity (4200 J/(kg°C)), and Δθ is the change in temperature (20°C). So, the thermal energy is 0.18 kg × 4200 J/(kg°C) × 20°C = 15120 J, which is approximately 1.5 × 104 J.

Recap

Specific heat capacity is the amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C. Water has a high specific heat capacity, meaning it can store a lot of thermal energy.

(c) Another plastic bottle is filled to the top with water. The height of the bottle is 40.0 cm. The density of water is 1.0 × 103 kg/m³. Calculate the pressure difference between the top and bottom of the water. [2]

The pressure difference between the top and bottom of the water is 3900 Pa. This is calculated using the formula for pressure in a fluid, Δp = h⍴g, where h is the height (0.40 m), ⍴ is the density of water (1.0 × 103 kg/m³), and g is the acceleration of free fall (9.8 m/s²). So, the pressure difference is 0.40 m × 1.0 × 103 kg/m³ × 9.8 m/s² = 3920 Pa, which is approximately 3900 Pa.

Recap

The pressure in a fluid increases linearly with depth. This pressure difference is independent of the shape of the container, depending only on the height of the fluid column, its density, and the acceleration due to gravity.

Fig. 5.1 shows a road junction, a moving car and a stationary truck. The road has high walls on each side. The driver of the truck is at position X. The car moves around the corner.

Point Y should be labeled on Fig. 5.1 at the position along the straight line from X, above and to the left of the road at the junction, where the line of sight from the truck driver (at X) just clears the corner to see the car.

To show how the mirror works, an incident ray should be drawn from the car to the mirror, and a reflected ray should be drawn from the mirror to the truck driver's eye. The angle of incidence must be equal to the angle of reflection at the mirror surface.

Plane mirrors form virtual images by regular reflection. The law of reflection states that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, reflected ray, and normal all lie in the same plane.

Question 6 | 0625/42/O/N/23 Q6 | Topics: Electricity, Circuits, LEDs, Energy

Fig. 6.1 shows the circuit diagram for a flashlight (torch).

The electromotive force (e.m.f.) of the battery is 4.5V. The circuit contains a 60Ω fixed resistor. The current in the light-emitting diode (LED) is 0.020A.

(a) Calculate the potential difference (p.d.) across the LED. [2]

The potential difference across the LED is 3.3 V. In a series circuit, the sum of the potential differences across components equals the e.m.f. of the battery. First, calculate the p.d. across the 60Ω resistor using Ohm's Law (V = IR): V_resistor = 0.020 A × 60 Ω = 1.2 V. Then, subtract this from the battery's e.m.f.: V_LED = 4.5 V - 1.2 V = 3.3 V.

Recap

In a series circuit, the current is the same through all components, and the total voltage (e.m.f.) is divided among the components. Ohm's Law (V=IR) relates voltage, current, and resistance.

(b) Explain why the LED does not light up if the battery is reversed. [1]

An LED (Light Emitting Diode) is a type of diode. Diodes only allow current to flow in one direction. If the battery is reversed, the LED is reverse-biased, meaning it has a very high resistance and effectively blocks the current, so it does not light up.

Recap

Diodes are semiconductor devices that act as one-way valves for electric current. They have a low resistance when forward-biased and a very high resistance when reverse-biased.

(c) The chemical energy stored in the battery is 1050 J. Show that the flashlight operates for approximately 3h. [3]

The flashlight operates for approximately 3 hours. The power of the circuit is P = IV = 0.020 A × 4.5 V = 0.09 W. The energy stored in the battery is 1050 J. Time (t) can be calculated using E = Pt, so t = E/P = 1050 J / 0.09 W = 11666.67 s. To convert seconds to hours: 11666.67 s / 3600 s/h = 3.24 h, which is approximately 3 hours.

Recap

Electrical energy is converted into other forms of energy (light, heat) in a circuit. The relationship between energy (E), power (P), and time (t) is E = Pt. Power is the rate at which energy is transferred.

(d) Calculate the total charge that flows through the LED in 3600 s. [2]

The total charge that flows through the LED in 3600 s is 72 C. This is calculated using the formula Q = It, where I is the current (0.020 A) and t is the time (3600 s). So, the charge is 0.020 A × 3600 s = 72 C.

Recap

Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter. Current is the rate of flow of charge. One coulomb is the amount of charge transferred by a current of one ampere in one second.

The electromagnetic spectrum is a continuous range of all possible frequencies of electromagnetic radiation. Different regions have different wavelengths and frequencies, leading to different applications.

(b) State the speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum. [1]

The speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum is 3.0 × 108 m/s.

Recap

The speed of light in a vacuum (c) is a fundamental physical constant. All electromagnetic waves, regardless of their frequency or wavelength, travel at this speed in a vacuum.

(c) A Bluetooth headset can be used to listen to music on a mobile (cell) phone without the need for wires to connect the headset to the phone.

(i) The headset uses frequencies in the range 2.40–2.48GHz. Calculate the wavelength of the radio waves when the frequency is in the middle of the frequency range. [3]

The wavelength of the radio waves is 0.12 m. The middle of the frequency range is (2.40 GHz + 2.48 GHz) / 2 = 2.44 GHz = 2.44 × 109 Hz. Using the wave equation v = fλ, where v is the speed of electromagnetic waves (3.0 × 108 m/s), f is the frequency, and λ is the wavelength. Rearranging for wavelength: λ = v / f = (3.0 × 108 m/s) / (2.44 × 109 Hz) = 0.1229 m, which is approximately 0.12 m.

Recap

The wave equation (v = fλ) is a fundamental relationship between the speed, frequency, and wavelength of a wave. It applies to all types of waves, including electromagnetic waves.

(ii) Suggest why a Bluetooth headset only works well over short distances. [1]

Recap

Electromagnetic waves attenuate (lose energy) as they travel through a medium or over distance. This attenuation is influenced by factors such as the frequency of the wave, the properties of the medium, and the presence of obstacles.

Question 8 | 0625/42/O/N/23 Q8 | Topics: Nuclear Physics, Energy Generation

The isotope uranium-235 is represented by 23592U.

(a) State what the numbers 92 and 235 represent in this symbol. [2]

In the symbol 23592U:

  • The number 92 represents the proton number (or atomic number) of uranium, which is the number of protons in the nucleus.
  • The number 235 represents the nucleon number (or mass number), which is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus.

Recap

The atomic number (Z) defines the element and is equal to the number of protons. The mass number (A) is the total number of nucleons (protons + neutrons) in the nucleus. The number of neutrons can be found by A - Z.

(b) Uranium-235 is a fuel used in nuclear reactors.

(i) State the process by which energy is released from uranium-235 in a nuclear reactor. [1]

The process by which energy is released from uranium-235 in a nuclear reactor is nuclear fission.

Recap

Nuclear fission is a nuclear reaction in which the nucleus of an atom splits into two or more smaller, lighter nuclei, often producing gamma photons, and releasing a very large amount of energy.

(ii) A nuclide equation for this process is 23592U + 10n → 14054Xe + 9438Sr + 2 10n. Describe the mass and energy changes that take place during this process in a nuclear reactor. [2]

During nuclear fission, the total mass of the products is slightly less than the total mass of the reactants. This mass difference is converted into a large amount of energy, primarily in the form of kinetic energy of the fission products and thermal energy. This energy is released according to Einstein's mass-energy equivalence relation, E=mc².

Recap

In nuclear reactions, mass is not conserved in the classical sense. Instead, mass and energy are interconvertible. A small loss of mass (mass defect) results in a huge release of energy.

(c) (i) Describe how thermal energy from nuclear reactions is used to generate electricity in a power station. [3]

The thermal energy released from nuclear fission reactions is used to heat water, producing high-pressure steam. This steam then drives a turbine, which is connected to a generator. The generator contains coils of wire that rotate in a magnetic field, inducing an electric current and thus generating electricity.

Recap

Nuclear power stations operate on the same principle as conventional thermal power stations, but they use nuclear fission as the heat source instead of burning fossil fuels. The energy conversion chain is: Nuclear Energy → Thermal Energy → Kinetic Energy (steam) → Kinetic Energy (turbine) → Electrical Energy.

(c) (ii) State one advantage and one disadvantage of using nuclear fuels in a power station instead of using fossil fuels. [2]

Advantage: Nuclear fuels require a much smaller amount of fuel to produce the same amount of energy compared to fossil fuels. Also, nuclear power stations do not produce greenhouse gases or air pollutants during operation.

Disadvantage: Nuclear power plants produce hazardous radioactive waste that must be stored safely for a very long time. There is also a danger of radiation leaks or accidents, and the cost to build and decommission nuclear power plants is very high.

Recap

Nuclear energy offers a low-carbon alternative to fossil fuels but comes with significant challenges related to safety, waste disposal, and public perception.

(d) Show that the average orbital speed of the Earth is approximately 30km/s. [3]

To show that the average orbital speed of the Earth is approximately 30 km/s: The Earth's average distance from the Sun (radius of orbit, r) is 149.6 × 106 km = 149.6 × 109 m. The orbital period (T) is 365.2 days. Convert this to seconds: 365.2 days × 24 hours/day × 60 minutes/hour × 60 seconds/minute = 3.155 × 107 s. The orbital speed (v) can be calculated using the formula v = 2πr / T. So, v = (2 × π × 149.6 × 109 m) / (3.155 × 107 s) = 29785 m/s, which is approximately 29.8 km/s or 30 km/s.

Recap

Orbital speed is the speed at which an object revolves around another object. For a circular orbit, it can be calculated as the circumference of the orbit divided by the orbital period.

Question 10 | 0625/42/O/N/23 Q10 | Topics: Astrophysics, Stellar Evolution

(a) Protostars are formed from interstellar clouds of gas and dust (also known as a stellar nebula). [1]

Protostars are formed from interstellar clouds of gas and dust, often referred to as a stellar nebula.

Recap

A stellar nebula is a vast cloud of gas (mostly hydrogen) and dust in space. Gravity slowly pulls these particles together, causing the cloud to collapse and form a dense, hot core known as a protostar. This is the earliest stage in the formation of a star.

(b) A protostar becomes a stable star when the inward force of gravitational attraction is balanced by the outward force due to the high temperature in the centre of the star. [2]

A protostar becomes a stable star when the inward force of gravitational attraction is balanced by the outward force due to the high temperature and pressure from nuclear fusion in the core.

Recap

This state of balance is called hydrostatic equilibrium. The immense gravitational forces trying to collapse the star are counteracted by the thermal and radiation pressure generated by nuclear fusion reactions in the star's core. A star will remain in this stable, main-sequence phase for most of its life.

(c) The initial fuel used to power nuclear reactions in stars is hydrogen. [1]

The initial fuel for nuclear reactions in stars is hydrogen.

Recap

In the core of a main-sequence star, hydrogen nuclei undergo nuclear fusion to form helium nuclei. This process, known as the proton-proton chain or the CNO cycle, releases a tremendous amount of energy, which provides the outward pressure to counteract gravity.

(d) Stars that are approximately the same size as the Sun become red giant stars which then form a planetary nebula with a white dwarf star at its centre. [1]

Stars of a similar size to the Sun will become red giants and then form a planetary nebula with a white dwarf at the center.

Recap

After a Sun-like star exhausts the hydrogen fuel in its core, it expands into a red giant. Eventually, the outer layers of the red giant drift away into space, creating a beautiful, glowing shell of gas called a planetary nebula. The hot, dense core of the star that is left behind is a white dwarf.
