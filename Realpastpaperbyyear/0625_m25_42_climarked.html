Question 1 | 0625/42/M/25 Q1 | Topic: General Physics

Fig. 1.1 shows a force-extension graph for a spring.

(a) Calculate the spring constant k of the spring.

The spring constant k is calculated from the gradient of the force-extension graph. Using a point from the graph, for example, a force of 4000 N corresponds to an extension of 0.08 m.

k = Force / extension = 4000 N / 0.08 m = 50 000 N/m.

Recap

According to Hooke's Law, the force needed to stretch or compress a spring by some distance is directly proportional to that distance. The spring constant, k, is a measure of the stiffness of the spring. A higher value of k means a stiffer spring.

(b) A student states that the spring has not reached the limit of proportionality when a force of 4500 N is applied to it. State how the graph shows that this statement is true.

The graph shows that the statement is true because the line on the graph is straight up to and beyond 4500 N, which indicates that the force is proportional to the extension.

Recap

The limit of proportionality is the point beyond which Hooke's law is no longer true. Up to this point, the graph of force against extension is a straight line passing through the origin. If the force exceeds the elastic limit, the spring will be permanently stretched.

(c) Springs can be compressed by forces. The spring described by Fig. 1.1 is compressed by a force F and has an extension of –0.025 m. Determine F.

Using the spring constant k = 50 000 N/m calculated in part (a).

F = k × extension = 50 000 N/m × 0.025 m = 1250 N. The question implies compression, so the force is compressive.

Recap

The same linear relationship between force and displacement holds for both stretching and compression of a spring. The negative sign for the extension simply indicates a decrease in length (compression) rather than an increase (extension).

(d) State whether force is a scalar quantity or a vector quantity. Explain your answer.

Force is a vector quantity because it has both magnitude (size) and direction.

Recap

A scalar quantity has only magnitude (e.g., mass, time, speed), while a vector quantity has both magnitude and direction (e.g., velocity, acceleration, force). When dealing with vectors, the direction is as important as the numerical value.

Question 2 | 0625/42/M/25 Q2 | Topic: General Physics

Trolley A and trolley B are on a horizontal, frictionless bench. Trolley A moves to the right with a constant velocity u = 0.44 m/s. Trolley B is stationary. Fig. 2.1 shows trolley A before it collides with trolley B.

(a) State the momentum of trolley B before the collision. Explain your answer.

The momentum of trolley B before the collision is zero. This is because momentum is the product of mass and velocity (p = mv), and since trolley B is stationary, its velocity is zero.

Recap

Momentum is a measure of the motion of an object. The principle of conservation of momentum states that for a system of interacting objects, the total momentum remains constant, provided no external forces act on the system.

(b) After the collision, the two trolleys are joined together and travel with a constant velocity v = 0.18 m/s to the right. The mass of trolley A is 0.75 kg. Calculate the mass of trolley B.

According to the principle of conservation of momentum, the total momentum before the collision is equal to the total momentum after the collision.

Momentum before = (mass of A × velocity of A) + (mass of B × velocity of B) = (0.75 kg × 0.44 m/s) + (mass of B × 0) = 0.33 kg m/s.

Momentum after = (mass of A + mass of B) × final velocity = (0.75 kg + mass of B) × 0.18 m/s.

0.33 = (0.75 + mass of B) × 0.18

mass of B = (0.33 / 0.18) - 0.75 = 1.1 kg (to 2 s.f.)

Recap

In a collision, the total momentum of the objects before the collision is equal to the total momentum of the objects after the collision. This is a fundamental principle in physics and is essential for analysing interactions between objects.

(c) (i) The trolleys move onto a rough surface which exerts a constant force F on the trolleys and brings them to rest in 2.6 s. Calculate F.

The force is the rate of change of momentum.

Total mass = 0.75 kg + 1.1 kg = 1.85 kg.

Initial momentum = 1.85 kg × 0.18 m/s = 0.333 kg m/s.

Final momentum = 0.

F = (Final momentum - Initial momentum) / time = (0 - 0.333) / 2.6 = -0.128 N.

The resistive force is 0.13 N (to 2 s.f.).

Recap

Newton's second law of motion can be expressed in terms of momentum: the net force acting on an object is equal to the rate of change of its momentum. A resistive force, like friction, opposes the motion of an object.

(c) (ii) A different rough surface exerts a smaller resistive force on the trolleys. State how this affects the time taken to bring the trolleys to rest. Explain your answer.

The time taken to bring the trolleys to rest will increase. Since the change in momentum is the same, and Force = Change in momentum / time, a smaller force will require a longer time to produce the same change in momentum.

Recap

The relationship FΔt = Δp (impulse-momentum theorem) shows that for a constant change in momentum (Δp), the force (F) and the time interval (Δt) are inversely proportional. A smaller force requires a longer time to achieve the same impulse and thus the same change in momentum.

Question 3 | 0625/42/M/25 Q3 | Topic: Thermal Physics & Electricity

Fig. 3.1 shows a mains electric heater used to heat a small room.

(a) State the region of the electromagnetic spectrum which radiates thermal energy from the heater.

The region of the electromagnetic spectrum that radiates thermal energy from the heater is infrared.

Recap

All objects with a temperature above absolute zero emit thermal radiation. For objects at everyday temperatures, this radiation is mostly in the infrared part of the electromagnetic spectrum. Hotter objects, like the heating elements in a heater, emit more intense infrared radiation.

(b) Explain why the shiny metal surface behind the heating elements increases the thermal energy radiated into the room.

The shiny metal surface is a good reflector of thermal radiation and a poor emitter. It reflects the infrared radiation from the back of the heating elements and directs it into the room, increasing the overall amount of heat radiated forward.

Recap

Dull, black surfaces are good absorbers and emitters of thermal radiation, while shiny, light-coloured surfaces are poor absorbers and emitters but good reflectors. This principle is used in many applications, such as vacuum flasks and emergency blankets.

(c) The metal outer casing of the heater is earthed. State why this is an important safety feature.

Earthing is a safety feature that prevents electric shock. If a fault causes the live wire to touch the metal casing, the earth wire provides a low-resistance path for the current to flow to the ground, causing a large current to flow, which blows the fuse and disconnects the power supply.

Recap

The earth wire is a crucial safety component in mains electricity. It is connected to the metal casing of an appliance and provides a safe route for current to escape if a fault occurs, preventing the casing from becoming live and protecting the user from electric shock.

(d) (i) The mains voltage is 230 V. The two identical heating elements are connected in parallel. Each heating element has a resistance of 89 Ω. Calculate the current in one heating element.

Using Ohm's Law (V = IR), the current I can be calculated as I = V / R.

I = 230 V / 89 Ω = 2.6 A (to 2 s.f.).

Recap

Ohm's Law states that the current through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage across the two points, provided the temperature and other physical conditions remain unchanged. The constant of proportionality is the resistance (R).

(d) (ii) Show that the electrical power of the heater is approximately 1200 W. State any equation you use in words or symbols.

The total current is the sum of the currents in the parallel branches: 2.6 A + 2.6 A = 5.2 A.

Using the formula for electrical power, P = IV.

P = 5.2 A × 230 V = 1196 W, which is approximately 1200 W.

Alternatively, the power of one element is P = IV = 2.6 A * 230 V = 598 W. Total power = 2 * 598 W = 1196 W.

Recap

Electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is converted into another form, such as heat or light. It is calculated as the product of voltage and current (P = IV). The unit of power is the Watt (W).

(d) (iii) The heater is 95% efficient at converting electrical work done to thermal energy. Calculate the thermal energy emitted by the heater in (d)(ii) in 60 s. Give your answer to two significant figures.

Electrical energy input = Power × time = 1200 W × 60 s = 72 000 J.

The useful thermal energy output is 95% of the input energy.

Thermal energy = 0.95 × 72 000 J = 68 400 J.

To two significant figures, the thermal energy is 68 000 J or 68 kJ.

Recap

The efficiency of an energy conversion is the ratio of the useful energy output to the total energy input, often expressed as a percentage. No device is 100% efficient; some energy is always lost to the surroundings, usually as heat.

Question 4 | 0625/42/M/25 Q4 | Topic: Kinematics & Dynamics

A train has a maximum speed of 200 km/h. It accelerates from rest with constant acceleration of 0.70 m/s².

(a) (i) Define acceleration.

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

Recap

Acceleration is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction. An object is accelerating if its speed is changing or its direction of motion is changing. The unit of acceleration is meters per second squared (m/s²).

(a) (ii) Show that the maximum speed of the train is approximately 56 m/s.

To convert km/h to m/s, we convert kilometers to meters (× 1000) and hours to seconds (× 3600).

Speed in m/s = (200 × 1000) / 3600 = 55.6 m/s, which is approximately 56 m/s.

Recap

It is a common requirement in physics to convert units to the standard SI units before performing calculations. For speed, the SI unit is meters per second (m/s).

(a) (iii) Calculate the time taken for the train to reach its maximum speed.

Using the formula for acceleration, a = (v - u) / t, where v is the final velocity, u is the initial velocity, and t is the time taken.

t = (v - u) / a = (56 m/s - 0 m/s) / 0.70 m/s² = 80 s.

Recap

The equations of uniformly accelerated motion can be used to solve problems involving objects moving with constant acceleration. These equations relate initial velocity (u), final velocity (v), acceleration (a), time (t), and displacement (s).

(b) (i) The train has a total mass of 440 000 kg. Calculate the force which causes the acceleration of the train.

Using Newton's second law of motion, Force = mass × acceleration (F = ma).

F = 440 000 kg × 0.70 m/s² = 308 000 N or 3.1 × 10^5 N.

Recap

Newton's second law is a fundamental principle in dynamics. It states that the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass. The direction of the acceleration is in the direction of the net force.

(b) (ii) The train travels into a headwind. The force of this headwind opposes the motion of the train. State and explain the effect of this force on the motion of the train.

The headwind provides a resistive force that opposes the driving force of the train. This reduces the net forward force on the train. According to Newton's second law (F=ma), a smaller net force results in a smaller acceleration. If the train is at maximum speed, the headwind will cause it to slow down until the driving force again equals the total resistive forces.

Recap

The net force (or resultant force) is the vector sum of all the forces acting on an object. It is the net force that determines the object's acceleration. When forces are balanced (net force is zero), the object's velocity remains constant (it either stays at rest or moves at a constant velocity).

Question 5 | 0625/42/M/25 Q5 | Topic: Electricity

A light-dependent resistor (LDR) has a low resistance in high light intensity and a high resistance in the dark.

(a) Sketch a graph of resistance (y-axis) against light intensity (x-axis) for an LDR.

The graph should show resistance on the y-axis and light intensity on the x-axis. It will be a curve starting from a high resistance at low light intensity and decreasing as light intensity increases.

Recap

An LDR is a non-ohmic conductor. Its resistance changes significantly with light intensity. This property makes it useful as a light sensor in circuits for automatic lighting, alarms, and other light-sensitive applications.

(b) Fig. 5.1 shows part of the electric circuit used to turn on a light when it is dark.

(b) (i) Complete the circuit in Fig. 5.1 with the symbol for a light-dependent resistor (LDR).

The LDR symbol (a rectangle with two arrows pointing towards it, enclosed in a circle) should be drawn in the gap in the circuit, in series with the fixed resistor.

Recap

It is important to know the standard symbols for common electrical components to be able to read and draw circuit diagrams correctly.

(b) (ii) Explain why the lamp is off in the light and the lamp is on in the dark. Use ideas about potential difference (p.d.) in your answer.

The LDR and the fixed resistor form a potential divider. The voltage from the supply is shared between them.

In the light, the LDR has a low resistance. Therefore, it takes a small share of the supply voltage, and the potential difference across it is low. Since the lamp is in parallel with the LDR, the p.d. across the lamp is also low, so it is off.

In the dark, the LDR has a high resistance. It takes a larger share of the supply voltage, and the p.d. across it is high. This high p.d. is sufficient to turn the lamp on.

Recap

A potential divider circuit is used to provide a fraction of the voltage of a source. The voltage is divided between the components in series. The ratio of the voltages across the components is equal to the ratio of their resistances. V₁/V₂ = R₁/R₂.

Question 7 | 0625/42/M/25 Q7 | Topic: Waves

(a) Fig. 7.1 is a scale drawing of light waves approaching a narrow slit. SCALE 1.0 cm : 4.0 × 10⁻⁷ m

(a) (i) Name the wave effect produced by the narrow slit.

The wave effect produced by the narrow slit is diffraction.

Recap

Diffraction is the spreading out of waves as they pass through a gap or around an obstacle. The effect is most significant when the size of the gap or obstacle is similar to the wavelength of the waves.

(a) (ii) Using Fig. 7.1, determine the wavelength of the light. Give your answer to two significant figures.

By measuring the distance between two consecutive wavefronts on the diagram (e.g., 1.2 cm), we can use the scale to find the wavelength.

Wavelength = 1.2 cm × (4.0 × 10⁻⁷ m / 1.0 cm) = 4.8 × 10⁻⁷ m or 480 nm.

Recap

The wavelength of a wave is the spatial period of the wave – the distance over which the wave's shape repeats. It is the distance between consecutive corresponding points of the same phase, such as two adjacent crests, troughs, or zero crossings.

(a) (iii) On Fig. 7.1, draw three wavefronts that have passed through the narrow slit.

Three semi-circular wavefronts should be drawn spreading out from the slit. The wavefronts should be centered on the slit and have the same spacing (wavelength) as the incoming waves.

Recap

After passing through a narrow gap, plane waves become circular waves. The narrower the gap, the more the waves spread out. The wavelength of the waves remains unchanged after diffraction.

(b) A foghorn emits a sound with frequency 380 Hz. The sound is heard by a ship 2.5 km away from the foghorn. The speed of sound in air is 330 m/s.

(b) (i) Show that the wavelength of the sound is approximately 0.9 m. State any equation you use in words or symbols.

Using the wave equation: speed = frequency × wavelength (v = fλ).

Wavelength (λ) = speed (v) / frequency (f) = 330 m/s / 380 Hz = 0.87 m, which is approximately 0.9 m.

Recap

The wave equation relates the speed of a wave to its frequency and wavelength. This fundamental equation applies to all types of waves, including sound waves and electromagnetic waves.

(b) (ii) Calculate the time it takes for sound to travel to the ship from the foghorn.

Using the formula: time = distance / speed.

Distance = 2.5 km = 2500 m.

Time = 2500 m / 330 m/s = 7.6 s.

Recap

The speed of sound in a medium depends on the properties of the medium, such as its temperature and density. In air at sea level, the speed of sound is approximately 343 m/s at 20 °C, but it varies with conditions.

Question 8 | 0625/42/M/25 Q8 | Topic: Magnetism & Electromagnetism

Fig. 8.1 shows a metal rod suspended in the magnetic field produced by a pair of permanent magnets. The metal rod is connected to a cell and there is a current in the metal rod.

(a) State the direction of the force on the metal rod due to the current. Explain your answer.

The direction of the force on the metal rod is downwards. This is determined by Fleming's left-hand rule. The magnetic field runs from North to South (left to right), and the current flows into the page. Applying the rule, the thumb points downwards, indicating the direction of the force.

Recap

Fleming's left-hand rule is used to find the direction of the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. The first finger represents the direction of the magnetic field (N to S), the second finger represents the direction of the current (+ to -), and the thumb gives the direction of the force (the motion).

(b) The connections to the cell are reversed. State how this change affects the force on the metal rod.

If the connections to the cell are reversed, the direction of the current is reversed. This will cause the direction of the force on the rod to reverse, so it will now be directed upwards.

Recap

The direction of the force in the motor effect depends on both the direction of the magnetic field and the direction of the current. Reversing either one will reverse the direction of the force. Reversing both will result in the force being in the original direction.

(c) Two magnets and a cell are used to make a simple electric motor as shown in Fig. 8.2. Describe the function of parts J, K, L and M.

J (Carbon brushes): These maintain continuous electrical contact between the power supply and the commutator, allowing current to flow to the coil. They are typically made of carbon because it is a good conductor and is self-lubricating.

K (Coil): This is a loop of wire that rotates when a current passes through it while it is in a magnetic field. The forces on the two sides of the coil produce a turning effect.

L (Axle): This is the rod on which the coil is mounted, allowing it to rotate freely.

M (Split-ring commutator): This reverses the direction of the current in the coil every half turn. This ensures that the force on each side of the coil always acts to keep the coil rotating in the same direction.

Recap

A simple DC electric motor uses the principle of the motor effect to convert electrical energy into mechanical energy. The key components work together to produce continuous rotation of the coil.

Question 9 | 0625/42/M/25 Q9 | Topic: Atomic Physics

Strontium-90 is a radioactive isotope of strontium. The nuclide notation for strontium-90 is ⁹⁰₃₈Sr.

(a) (i) Explain what isotopes are.

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons in their nucleus.

Recap

Because they have the same number of protons and electrons, isotopes have the same chemical properties. However, their different number of neutrons gives them different physical properties, such as mass and stability (radioactivity).

(a) (ii) Complete Table 9.1 for strontium-90.

For Strontium-90 (⁹⁰₃₈Sr):

Number of protons = Atomic number = 38 (inside nucleus).

Number of electrons = Number of protons = 38 (outside nucleus).

Number of neutrons = Mass number - Atomic number = 90 - 38 = 52 (inside nucleus).

Recap

The atomic number (Z) represents the number of protons in an atom's nucleus, defining the element. The mass number (A) is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus.

(b) Strontium-90 is used to measure the thickness of metal sheets in industry. Strontium-90 decays by emitting beta (β) particles which pass through a metal sheet to a detector.

(b) (i) One metal sheet is 0.75 mm thick. Suggest why strontium-90 is a suitable radioactive source to measure the thickness of the metal sheets.

Strontium-90 is a beta emitter. Beta particles are moderately penetrating. They can pass through thin metal sheets like the 0.75 mm one, but the amount that gets through is sensitive to changes in thickness. This allows for precise measurement, as a thicker sheet will absorb more particles and lead to a lower count rate at the detector. Alpha particles would be completely stopped, and gamma rays would pass through with little change, making them unsuitable.

Recap

The choice of radioactive isotope for industrial applications depends on its penetrating power and half-life. Alpha (α) particles are easily stopped, beta (β) particles are more penetrating, and gamma (γ) rays are the most penetrating.

(b) (ii) The half-life of strontium-90 is approximately 27 years. Fig. 9.1 shows the shape of a decay curve. The strontium-90 source is replaced with a new source after 15 years. Using Fig. 9.1, suggest why a strontium-90 source that is more than 15 years old needs to be replaced with a new source.

After 15 years, the activity of the strontium-90 source has significantly decreased. From the decay curve, at 15 years, the percentage of strontium-90 remaining is around 65-70%. This means the count rate from the source is considerably lower. A lower count rate reduces the precision of the thickness measurement and can become too close to the background radiation level, making it difficult to detect variations in the metal sheet's thickness accurately.

Recap

The half-life of a radioactive isotope is the time taken for half of the unstable nuclei in a sample to decay, or for the activity of the sample to halve. For industrial gauges, a source needs a sufficiently long half-life to avoid frequent replacement, but its activity must remain high enough for reliable measurements.

Question 10 | 0625/42/M/25 Q10 | Topic: Earth & The Solar System

Fig. 10.1 shows the path of the Earth as it orbits the Sun. X is a position on the Earth where scientists observe the apparent motion of the Sun throughout the year.

(a) Determine how many days it takes the Earth to move around its orbit from F to G. Explain your answer.

The journey from F to G represents one-quarter of the Earth's total orbit around the Sun. Since a full orbit takes approximately 365 days (one year), the time taken to travel from F to G is 365 / 4 = 91.25 days, which is approximately 91 days.

Recap

The Earth completes one full orbit around the Sun in about 365.25 days. This period is what we define as a year. The orbit is not a perfect circle; it is slightly elliptical.

(b) Fig. 10.1 shows four positions E, F, G and H of the Earth in its orbit of the Sun.

(b) (i) Identify the position of the Earth when it is summer at X.

Position F. At this position, the Northern Hemisphere (where X is located) is tilted towards the Sun, receiving more direct sunlight and experiencing summer.

Recap

The seasons are caused by the tilt of the Earth's axis (about 23.5 degrees) relative to its orbital plane. When a hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, it experiences summer. When it is tilted away, it experiences winter.

(b) (ii) Identify the position of the Earth when it is winter at X.

Position H. At this position, the Northern Hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun, receiving less direct sunlight and experiencing winter.

Recap

At positions E and G (the equinoxes), the Earth's tilt is neither towards nor away from the Sun, resulting in spring and autumn.

(c) The orbital speed of the Earth around the Sun is approximately 3.0 × 10⁴ m/s. Calculate the average radius of the Earth's orbit.

We can model the Earth's orbit as a circle. The orbital speed is given by v = 2πr / T, where r is the radius and T is the orbital period (1 year).

First, convert the orbital period T into seconds: T = 365 days × 24 hours/day × 60 mins/hour × 60 secs/min ≈ 3.15 × 10⁷ s.

Rearranging the formula for r: r = vT / 2π.

r = (3.0 × 10⁴ m/s × 3.15 × 10⁷ s) / 2π ≈ 1.5 × 10¹¹ m.

Recap

The orbital speed of a planet depends on the mass of the star it orbits and the radius of its orbit. For a circular orbit, the gravitational force provides the necessary centripetal force.

(d) Earth is a planet in the Solar System. State one other type of naturally occurring object that is present in the Solar System.

Other naturally occurring objects in the Solar System include asteroids, comets, or moons (natural satellites).

Recap

Our Solar System consists of the Sun (a star) and everything that orbits it, including eight planets, their moons, dwarf planets, and countless asteroids, comets, and other small bodies.
