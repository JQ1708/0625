Question 1 | Topics: Forces, Vectors

A load is suspended from a thread. The vertical force on the thread due to the load is 0.75 N.

(a) Calculate the mass of the load.

The mass of the load is 0.077 kg or 77 g. This is calculated using the formula Weight = mass × gravitational field strength (W = mg), so m = W / g = 0.75 N / 9.8 N/kg.

Recap

Weight is the force of gravity on an object, while mass is the amount of matter in it. The gravitational field strength, g, is approximately 9.8 N/kg near the Earth's surface.

(b) By drawing a scale diagram or by calculation, determine the magnitude and direction of the resultant force at X.

The magnitude of the resultant force is 1.4 N and its direction is 58° relative to the vertical. This is found by treating the two forces as vectors at right angles and using Pythagoras' theorem (R² = 0.75² + 1.2²) and trigonometry (tan(θ) = 1.2 / 0.75).

Recap

The resultant force is the single force that has the same effect as all the individual forces acting on an object. When forces are perpendicular, they can be added using vector addition methods like the parallelogram rule or Pythagoras' theorem.

(c) State two other changes that forces may produce.

Forces can change an object's speed (acceleration/deceleration) and its direction of motion.

Recap

A force is a push or a pull that can cause an object with mass to change its velocity (including a change from a state of rest), i.e., to accelerate. Forces can also cause a non-rigid object to deform.

Question 2 | Topics: Kinematics, Dynamics

(a) Define acceleration.

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

Recap

Since velocity is a vector (speed in a given direction), you can accelerate by changing your speed, changing your direction, or both. The unit for acceleration is m/s².

(b) A train accelerates at 1.1 m/s².

(i) Calculate the time taken for the train to increase its speed from 15 m/s to 28 m/s.

The time taken is 12 s. This is calculated using the formula a = (v - u) / t, so t = (v - u) / a = (28 - 15) / 1.1.

Recap

The equations of motion for uniform acceleration link initial velocity (u), final velocity (v), acceleration (a), time (t), and displacement (s). The formula v = u + at is a rearrangement of the definition of acceleration.

(ii) Calculate the force required to produce an acceleration of 1.1 m/s² for this train (mass = 520,000 kg).

The force required is 570,000 N (or 5.7 x 10⁵ N). This is calculated using Newton's Second Law, F = ma = 520,000 kg × 1.1 m/s².

Recap

Newton's Second Law states that the resultant force on an object is equal to the product of its mass and acceleration. This force is the *net* force, also known as the resultant force.

(iii) Explain why the force on the train due to the motors is greater than the value calculated in (ii).

The calculated force is the resultant force. The motors must provide a larger force to overcome resistive forces like friction and air resistance (drag).

Recap

Driving Force = Resultant Force + Resistive Forces. To achieve a certain acceleration, the engine or motor must produce a force that not only provides the resultant force (F=ma) but also cancels out all opposing forces.

Question 3 | Topics: Energy

A student drops a heavy ball from a vertical height of 1.8 m above the ground.

(a) Describe the transfers of energy of the ball between stores from when the ball begins to fall to when it reaches the ground.

As the ball falls, its gravitational (potential) energy is transferred to kinetic energy. As it hits the ground, the kinetic energy is transferred to internal / thermal energy in the ball and the ground.

Recap

The principle of conservation of energy states that energy is not created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another. Gravitational Potential Energy (GPE) is energy stored due to an object's height (GPE=mgh), while Kinetic Energy (KE) is the energy of motion (KE=½mv²).

(b) Calculate the maximum speed of the ball. Ignore air resistance.

The maximum speed is 5.9 m/s. This is found by equating the loss in gravitational potential energy to the gain in kinetic energy: mgh = ½mv². The mass cancels out, giving v² = 2gh, so v = √(2 × 9.8 × 1.8).

Recap

When air resistance is ignored, the conversion between GPE and KE is considered to be 100% efficient. This calculation shows that for a falling object, the final speed depends only on the height it fell from, not its mass.

Question 4 | Topics: Thermal Physics

(a) State two ways that evaporation differs from boiling.

1. Evaporation occurs only at the surface of the liquid, while boiling occurs throughout the liquid.
2. Evaporation can occur at any temperature, while boiling occurs at a specific temperature (the boiling point).

Recap

Both evaporation and boiling are processes of vaporization (liquid to gas). Evaporation is a cooling process, as the most energetic particles escape, lowering the average kinetic energy of the remaining particles.

(b) Fig. 4.1 shows a container for liquid oxygen.

(i) Determine the temperature of the liquid oxygen (-160 °C) in K.

The temperature is 113 K. To convert from Celsius to Kelvin, you add 273. So, T(K) = T(°C) + 273 = -160 + 273.

Recap

The Kelvin scale is an absolute temperature scale where 0 K (absolute zero) is the point where particles have minimum internal energy. The size of one kelvin is the same as the size of one degree Celsius.

(ii) State two methods of thermal energy transfer that a vacuum prevents.

A vacuum prevents heat transfer by conduction and convection.

Recap

Conduction (transfer via particle collisions) and convection (transfer via fluid movement) both require a medium. A vacuum is a space with no particles, so these two methods are eliminated. Heat transfer by radiation (infrared waves) can still occur.

(c) Describe, in terms of particles, how a gas exerts a pressure on the walls of its container.

Gas particles collide with the walls of the container. Each collision produces a change in momentum, which exerts a small force on the wall. The pressure is the total force per unit area from the vast number of collisions occurring every second.

Recap

Gas pressure is defined as force per unit area (P = F/A). According to the kinetic theory of gases, this pressure arises from the constant, random motion and collisions of gas particles with the container surfaces.

Question 5 | Topics: Waves, Optics

(a) Explain why the ray does not change direction when it enters the prism at face AB.

The ray travels along the normal to the surface, meaning its angle of incidence is 0°.

Recap

Refraction is the change in direction of a wave passing from one medium to another. This change in direction is caused by a change in speed. If a wave enters a new medium at an angle of incidence of zero (i.e., perpendicular to the surface), it changes speed but does not change direction.

(b) The critical angle for the glass is 42°.

(i) Calculate the refractive index of the glass.

The refractive index is 1.5. The formula linking refractive index (n) and critical angle (c) is n = 1 / sin(c). So, n = 1 / sin(42°).

Recap

The critical angle is the angle of incidence beyond which total internal reflection occurs for light travelling from a denser medium to a less dense medium. At the critical angle, the angle of refraction is 90°.

(c) State two advantages of using optical fibres to transmit data.

1. They allow for high rates of data transmission (high bandwidth).
2. The signal suffers from little signal/data loss and is more secure from tapping.

Recap

Optical fibres work by the principle of total internal reflection, where light signals bounce along the inside of a very thin glass or plastic fibre. They are superior to copper wires for data transmission due to higher speed, less signal degradation, and immunity to electromagnetic interference.

Question 6 | Topics: Waves, Sound

(a) A sound wave travels through air. Fig. 6.1 shows a pressure-time graph.

(ii) Explain why this graph cannot be used to find the wavelength of the sound wave.

This is a pressure-time graph, not a displacement-distance graph. It shows the variation of pressure at a single point over time, not the variation with displacement or distance at a single instant.

Recap

Wavelength (λ) is the spatial period of a wave – the distance over which the wave's shape repeats. To measure it, you need a "snapshot" of the wave in space. A time-based graph can give you the period (T) and frequency (f), but not the wavelength directly.

(iii) The sound becomes louder and of lower pitch. State what happens to the amplitude and frequency.

The amplitude increases (making it louder), and the frequency decreases (making the pitch lower).

Recap

For sound waves, loudness is determined by amplitude, and pitch is determined by frequency. A larger amplitude means more energy and a louder sound. A higher frequency means more vibrations per second and a higher-pitched sound.

(b) A sound of frequency 13 kHz is transmitted through water at 1500 m/s. Calculate the wavelength.

The wavelength is 0.12 m. This is calculated using the wave equation v = fλ, so λ = v / f = 1500 m/s / 13,000 Hz.

Recap

The wave equation (speed = frequency × wavelength) is a fundamental relationship that applies to all types of waves. Remember to convert kHz to Hz (1 kHz = 1000 Hz) before calculating.

(c) State the approximate speed of sound in air.

The speed of sound in air is approximately 330-350 m/s.

Recap

The speed of sound varies depending on the medium it travels through, as well as the temperature and pressure of the medium. It travels fastest in solids, slower in liquids, and slowest in gases.

Question 7 | Topics: Magnetism, Electromagnetism

(a) Describe and explain how to determine which of three steel bars (A, B, C) is unmagnetised, given two are magnets.

Bring the end of one bar (e.g., A) close to one end of another bar (e.g., B), then bring it to the other end of B. The definitive test for magnetism is repulsion. If two bars repel each other, they are both magnets. If a bar only ever attracts the others (at both ends), it is the unmagnetised piece.

Recap

While magnets attract ferromagnetic materials and other magnets (with opposite poles), only two magnets can repel each other (when like poles face each other). Therefore, repulsion is the only conclusive test to identify a permanent magnet.

(b) Fig. 7.2 shows a step-down transformer.

(i) Calculate the input current if the input voltage is 240V, output power is 45W, and it is 100% efficient.

The input current is 0.19 A. For a 100% efficient transformer, input power = output power. Using the formula P = IV, the input current is I = P / V = 45 W / 240 V.

Recap

A transformer changes AC voltages. For an ideal (100% efficient) transformer, the power is conserved: P_in = P_out, which means I_p * V_p = I_s * V_s. A step-down transformer decreases voltage and increases current.

Question 8 | Topics: Electronics

(a) A circuit is designed to switch on a night light when it is dark.

(ii) The surroundings change from light to dark.

1. State the effect on the resistance of the LDR.

The resistance of the LDR increases.

Recap

An LDR (Light-Dependent Resistor) is a component whose resistance changes with light intensity. In bright light, its resistance is low; in darkness, its resistance is very high.

2. State and explain the effect on the p.d. across the LED.

The potential difference (p.d.) across the LED increases. The LDR and LED are in a parallel combination, which is part of a potential divider circuit. As the LDR's resistance increases, the resistance of the parallel combination increases. This gives the parallel part a greater proportion of the total supply voltage.

Recap

In a potential divider circuit, the supply voltage is shared between components in series. The ratio of the p.d. across each component is equal to the ratio of their resistances. V₁/V₂ = R₁/R₂.

(b) Fig. 8.2 shows another circuit with two identical lamps.

Calculate the resistance of lamp A if the supply is 240V and the total current is 0.50A.

The resistance of lamp A is 960 Ω. Since the lamps are identical and in parallel, the total current of 0.50 A splits equally, so the current in each bulb is 0.25 A. The voltage across each parallel branch is the same as the supply, 240 V. Using Ohm's Law, R = V / I = 240 V / 0.25 A.

Recap

For components in parallel, the voltage across each is the same. The total current is the sum of the currents in each branch. For identical components, the current divides equally among them.

Question 9 | Topics: Radioactivity

(a) Radioactive isotopes are used in hospitals.

(i) State and explain two safety precautions.

1. Reduce exposure time: This lowers the total amount of radiation absorbed.
2. Use shielding (e.g., lead aprons): The shielding absorbs the radiation before it reaches the body.

Recap

Other precautions include increasing distance from the source (as radiation intensity follows an inverse square law) and using isotopes with a short half-life to minimize the duration of exposure.

(ii) Give two reasons why alpha-emitters are not used as radioactive tracers inside the body.

1. Alpha radiation has high ionisation ability, which is very damaging to cells inside the body.
2. Alpha radiation is not very penetrating, so it would not reach a detector placed outside the body.

Recap

Ideal medical tracers use gamma emitters. Gamma rays are weakly ionising (less damaging) and highly penetrating, allowing them to be easily detected outside the body. They should also have a short half-life to decay quickly after the procedure.

(b) Write the nuclide equation for the beta-decay of Sodium-24 (¹¹Na) to Magnesium (Mg).

2411Na → 2412Mg + 0-1β

Recap

In beta-decay, a neutron in the nucleus turns into a proton and an electron. The proton stays, increasing the proton number by one (11 → 12), while the nucleon number remains the same. The electron is ejected as a beta particle.

Question 10 | Topics: Space Physics

(a) State two other types of natural object that orbit the Sun.

Any two from: comets, asteroids, or dwarf planets.

Recap

Our Solar System consists of the Sun and everything that orbits it, including planets, dwarf planets (like Pluto), moons, asteroids (mainly in the asteroid belt), and comets (which have highly elliptical orbits).

(b) State the shape of the orbits of the planets.

The orbits are elliptical (though nearly circular).

Recap

Kepler's First Law of Planetary Motion states that planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus. For most planets in our solar system, these ellipses are very close to being circles.

(c) State and explain the energy transfer as the object travels from point A (closest) to point B (furthest).

As the object moves from A to B, its kinetic energy decreases and its potential energy increases. This is because the object slows down as it moves further away from the Sun, and its height in the gravitational field increases. The total energy is conserved.

Recap

In an elliptical orbit, an object moves fastest when it is closest to the body it is orbiting (perihelion) and slowest when it is furthest away (aphelion). This is a consequence of the conservation of energy: as GPE increases, KE must decrease, and vice versa.

(d) Calculate the time taken for light from the Sun to reach Jupiter (7.8 x 10¹¹ m away).

The time taken is 2.6 x 10³ s (or 2600 s). This is calculated using time = distance / speed = (7.8 × 10¹¹ m) / (3.0 × 10⁸ m/s).

Recap

The speed of light in a vacuum (c ≈ 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s) is the ultimate speed limit in the universe. This calculation shows that even at this incredible speed, it takes a significant amount of time for light to travel across the vast distances of the solar system.

Question 11 | 0625/42/M/J/24 Q11 | Topics: Cosmology

(a) Name the galaxy that contains the Sun.

The Milky Way.

Recap

A galaxy is a massive, gravitationally bound system of stars, stellar remnants, interstellar gas, dust, and dark matter. Our solar system is located in the Orion Arm of the Milky Way, a spiral galaxy.

(b) Light observed from distant galaxies is redshifted. State the theory of the Universe that this observation supports.

This supports the Big Bang Theory, which states that the Universe is expanding.

Recap

Redshift is the stretching of light waves to longer wavelengths as the source of the light moves away from the observer. The observation that light from almost all distant galaxies is redshifted implies that they are moving away from us, providing key evidence for the expansion of the Universe.

(c) Cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR) is observed at all points in space.

(i) State when this radiation was produced.

It was produced shortly after the Universe was formed (or shortly after the Big Bang).

Recap

The CMBR dates to about 380,000 years after the Big Bang, when the Universe cooled enough for protons and electrons to combine into neutral hydrogen atoms. This "decoupling" allowed photons to travel freely through space for the first time.

(ii) Explain why this radiation is now in the microwave region of the electromagnetic spectrum.

As the Universe has expanded, the wavelength of this radiation has been stretched out (or redshifted), moving it from high-energy gamma or X-rays into the lower-energy microwave region.

Recap

The CMBR is considered the "afterglow" of the Big Bang. Its discovery was another crucial piece of evidence supporting the Big Bang theory over other models. The expansion of space itself stretches the photons, increasing their wavelength and decreasing their energy.
