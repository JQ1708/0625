Question 1 | 0625/42/M/J/20 Q1 | Mechanics

Fig. 1.1 shows the speed–time graph of a person on a journey. On the journey, he walks and then waits for a bus. He then travels by bus. He gets off the bus and waits for two minutes. He then walks again. His journey takes 74 minutes.

(a) For the whole journey calculate:

(i) the distance travelled [3]

The distance travelled is the area under the speed-time graph. The journey consists of three parts with movement.
1. Walking: Area = (20/60)h * 5 km/h = 1.67 km.
2. Bus: Area = (20/60)h * 40 km/h = 13.33 km.
3. Walking: Area = (22/60)h * 6 km/h = 2.2 km.
Total distance = 1.67 + 13.33 + 2.2 = 17 km.

Recap

On a speed-time graph, the distance travelled by an object is represented by the area under the graph. This area can be calculated by dividing the graph into regular shapes like rectangles and triangles.

(ii) the average speed. [2]

Average speed is calculated as Total Distance / Total Time.
Total Distance = 17 km.
Total Time = 74 minutes = 74/60 hours.
Average Speed = 17 km / (74/60 h) = (17 * 60) / 74 km/h ≈ 14 km/h.

Recap

Average speed is defined as the total distance travelled divided by the total time taken for the journey. It is a scalar quantity and does not account for changes in direction.

(b) State and explain which feature of a speed–time graph shows acceleration. [2]

The gradient (or slope) of a speed-time graph represents acceleration. This is because acceleration is the rate of change of speed over time, which is what the gradient calculates.

Recap

The gradient (or slope) of a speed-time graph represents acceleration. A positive gradient indicates acceleration, a negative gradient indicates deceleration, and a zero gradient (a horizontal line) indicates constant speed.

(c) State and explain the acceleration of the person at time = 40 minutes. [2]

At time = 40 minutes, the graph is a horizontal line, which means the speed is constant. The gradient of the line is zero. Therefore, the acceleration is 0 m/s².

Recap

When an object moves at a constant speed, its acceleration is zero. This is shown on a speed-time graph as a horizontal line, which has a gradient of zero.

Question 2 | 0625/42/M/J/20 Q2 | Mechanics

Fig. 2.1 shows a train.

The total mass of the train and its passengers is 750 000 kg. The train is travelling at a speed of 84 m/s. The driver applies the brakes and the train takes 80 s to slow down to a speed of 42 m/s.

(a) Calculate the impulse applied to the train as it slows down. [3]

Impulse is the change in momentum (Δp = m(v-u)).
m = 750 000 kg, u = 84 m/s, v = 42 m/s.
Impulse = 750 000 kg × (42 m/s - 84 m/s) = 750 000 × (-42) = -31 500 000 Ns.
The impulse applied is 3.2 × 10⁷ Ns (or kg m/s), in the direction opposite to motion.

Recap

Impulse is the product of a force and the time for which it acts (Impulse = F × t). It is also equal to the change in momentum (Δp) of an object. The unit of impulse is the Newton-second (Ns), which is equivalent to kg m/s.

(b) Calculate the average resultant force applied to the train as it slows down. [2]

The average resultant force is the impulse divided by the time over which it is applied (F = Δp / t).
Impulse = 3.15 × 10⁷ Ns, t = 80 s.
Force = (3.15 × 10⁷ Ns) / 80 s = 393 750 N.
The average resultant force is approximately 3.9 × 10⁵ N.

Recap

From the impulse-momentum theorem, the average resultant force is the rate of change of momentum. It can be calculated by dividing the impulse (change in momentum) by the time over which the force is applied (F = Δp / t).

(c) Suggest how the shape of the train helps it to travel at high speeds. [1]

The train has a streamlined shape. This design reduces drag or air resistance, allowing it to travel more efficiently at high speeds with less energy wasted overcoming resistive forces.

Recap

Streamlining is the process of designing an object with a shape that reduces air resistance or drag. This allows the object to move more efficiently through a fluid (like air or water), especially at high speeds.

(d) The train took 80 s to reduce its speed from 84 m/s to 42 m/s. Explain why, with the same braking force, the train takes more than 80 s to reduce its speed from 42 m/s to zero. [1]

The total resultant force causing deceleration is the braking force plus air resistance. Air resistance is much less at lower speeds. With the same braking force, the total decelerating force is smaller at lower speeds, resulting in a smaller deceleration. Therefore, it takes more time to slow down from 42 m/s to 0 than from 84 m/s to 42 m/s.

Recap

Air resistance is a form of friction that depends on the speed of an object. It increases as the object's speed increases. Therefore, the net decelerating force is greater at higher speeds than at lower speeds for the same braking force.

(e) On a wet day, the train travels a greater distance before it stops along the same track. The train has the same speed of 84 m/s before the brakes are applied. Suggest a reason for this. [1]

On a wet day, the friction between the train's wheels and the track is reduced. This reduces the maximum possible braking force that can be applied without the wheels sliding, leading to a smaller deceleration and a longer stopping distance.

Recap

Friction is a force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact. The presence of water can act as a lubricant, reducing the coefficient of friction between the wheels and the track, which in turn reduces the maximum braking force.

Question 3 | 0625/42/M/J/20 Q3 | Thermal Physics & Energy

(a) A solar panel receives energy from the Sun at a rate of 5.0 kW. Thermal energy is transferred from the solar panel to water with an efficiency of 20%. Cold water of mass 15 kg enters the solar panel every hour. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/(kg °C). Calculate the temperature increase of the water. [4]

1. Calculate the total energy input from the sun per hour.
Energy Input = Power × Time = 5.0 kW × 1 hour = 5000 W × 3600 s = 18,000,000 J.
2. Calculate the useful thermal energy transferred to the water (output).
Energy Output = Efficiency × Energy Input = 0.20 × 18,000,000 J = 3,600,000 J.
3. Calculate the temperature increase using E = mcΔT.
ΔT = E / (mc) = 3,600,000 J / (15 kg × 4200 J/(kg °C)) = 3,600,000 / 63,000 = 57.14 °C.
The temperature increase of the water is 57 °C.

Recap

Efficiency is the ratio of useful energy output to total energy input, often expressed as a percentage. The energy required to change an object's temperature is given by E = mcΔT, where 'c' is the specific heat capacity.

(b) State and explain one advantage and one disadvantage of heating the water in a solar panel compared with heating the water in a coal-burning boiler. [4]

Advantage: Solar energy is a renewable resource.
Explanation: The sun's energy will not run out, unlike coal which is a finite fossil fuel. It also has very low running costs and produces no air pollution or greenhouse gases during operation.
Disadvantage: Solar panels are not available at night or on very cloudy days.
Explanation: Their operation is dependent on weather conditions and the time of day, making them unreliable as a sole source of heat without a storage system. They also have a high initial installation cost.

Recap

Renewable energy sources, like solar, are naturally replenished. They are generally better for the environment but can be unreliable. Non-renewable sources, like coal, are finite and produce pollutants, but are often more reliable and have lower initial costs.

Question 4 | 0625/42/M/J/20 Q4 | Thermal Physics

Fig. 4.1 shows a liquid-in-glass thermometer without a temperature scale. The liquid inside the thermometer has a melting point of –39 °C.

(a) Describe simple experiments to mark the positions of the fixed points on this liquid-in-glass thermometer. [4]

To mark the fixed points, you need to establish the temperatures for the freezing and boiling points of water.
1. Lower Fixed Point (0 °C): Place the thermometer into a funnel containing pure melting ice. Wait for the liquid level to become stationary. Mark this level as the 0 °C point.
2. Upper Fixed Point (100 °C): Place the thermometer in the steam just above the surface of pure boiling water at standard atmospheric pressure. Wait for the liquid level to become stationary. Mark this level as the 100 °C point.

Recap

A thermometer is calibrated by using two fixed points, which are easily reproducible and constant temperatures. For the Celsius scale, these are the ice point (the temperature of pure melting ice, 0 °C) and the steam point (the temperature of steam above pure boiling water at standard pressure, 100 °C).

(b) A scientist is measuring temperatures at the South Pole. These temperatures have a minimum value of –90 °C. State why the liquid used in the thermometer in Fig. 4.1 would not be suitable for this scientist. [1]

The liquid in the thermometer has a melting point of -39 °C, which means it will freeze (become solid) at this temperature. Since the temperatures at the South Pole can reach -90 °C, the liquid would be frozen and unable to expand or contract to indicate the temperature.

Recap

A liquid-in-glass thermometer is only useful for measuring temperatures between the freezing point and boiling point of the liquid inside it. If the external temperature drops below the liquid's freezing point, the liquid will solidify and the thermometer will not work.

(c) State a design change that:

(i) increases the sensitivity of a liquid-in-glass thermometer [1]

To increase sensitivity, you could use a thinner bore (capillary tube) or a larger bulb. A thinner bore means a small change in volume results in a larger change in length, and a larger bulb contains more liquid to expand.

Recap

The sensitivity of a thermometer is how much the reading changes for a small change in temperature. It can be increased by using a narrower capillary bore or a larger bulb, both of which cause the liquid to move a greater distance for the same temperature change.

(ii) increases the range of a liquid-in-glass thermometer. [1]

To increase the range, you could use a longer tube or a liquid that expands less for each degree change in temperature.

Recap

The range of a thermometer is the difference between the maximum and minimum temperatures it can measure. A longer stem or a liquid that expands less per degree (lower thermal expansivity) will increase the range.

(d) State the property of the liquid which ensures that the scale on a liquid-in-glass thermometer is linear. [1]

The liquid must expand uniformly with temperature. This means it expands by the same amount for each degree rise in temperature, ensuring the degree markings on the scale can be spaced equally.

Recap

For a thermometer to have a linear scale (evenly spaced markings), the physical property being measured must change uniformly with temperature. For a liquid-in-glass thermometer, this means the liquid should expand by the same amount for each degree rise in temperature.

Fig. 5.1 shows crests of a wave approaching a barrier where the wave is reflected.

(a) On Fig. 5.1, draw three crests of the reflected wave. [3]

The reflected wave should be drawn showing three crests that are parallel to each other. The angle of reflection (angle between the reflected crests and the barrier) must be equal to the angle of incidence. The wavelength of the reflected waves should be the same as the incident waves. The reflected crests should appear to originate from the point of impact on the barrier.

Recap

The Law of Reflection states that the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection. For waves, the wavelength and frequency do not change upon reflection.

(b) The wave has a wavelength of 36 cm and a speed of 1.2 m/s. Calculate the frequency of the wave. [3]

The wave equation is v = fλ.
v = 1.2 m/s.
λ = 36 cm = 0.36 m.
f = v / λ = 1.2 m/s / 0.36 m = 3.33... Hz.
The frequency of the wave is approximately 3.3 Hz.

Recap

The wave equation, v = fλ, connects the speed (v), frequency (f), and wavelength (λ) of a wave. Ensure all units are consistent (e.g., meters for wavelength and m/s for speed) before calculating.

(c) Complete the following sentences. [2]

An echo is the name for a reflected .................................................................. wave.

The waves that form an echo are a type of longitudinal wave. Longitudinal waves are made up of .................................................................. and rarefactions.

An echo is the name for a reflected sound wave (or ultrasound wave).
Longitudinal waves are made up of compressions and rarefactions.

Recap

An echo is a reflection of a sound wave. Sound is a longitudinal wave, meaning its vibrations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer. These waves propagate as a series of compressions (high pressure regions) and rarefactions (low pressure regions).

Question 6 | 0625/42/M/J/20 Q6 | Optics

(a) Fig. 6.1 shows an arrangement of glass prisms inside a box. The angles of the prisms are 45°, 45° and 90°. This is a device used to view objects that are behind a wall. The incident ray of light undergoes total internal reflection in the prisms. On Fig. 6.1, complete the path of the ray through the device and show the ray as it emerges from the box. [3]

The path of the light ray should be drawn as follows:
1. The incident ray enters prism 1 without deviation as it strikes the face at a 90° angle.
2. It then strikes the hypotenuse (the sloping face) of prism 1 at an angle of 45°. Since this is the critical angle, it undergoes total internal reflection and is reflected downwards by 90°.
3. The ray travels vertically down to prism 2, entering it without deviation.
4. It strikes the hypotenuse of prism 2, again at 45°, undergoes another total internal reflection, and is reflected by 90° horizontally towards the eye.
5. The final ray emerges from the box travelling horizontally to the right.

Recap

Total Internal Reflection (TIR) occurs when light travels from a denser to a less dense medium at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle. In a 45-90-45 prism, light entering perpendicular to one face will strike the hypotenuse at 45°, causing TIR if the critical angle is less than 45°.

(b) Show that the refractive index of glass with a critical angle of 45° is 1.41. [2]

The relationship between refractive index (n) and the critical angle (c) is given by the formula n = 1 / sin(c).
Given the critical angle c = 45°.
n = 1 / sin(45°) = 1 / 0.7071... ≈ 1.414.
Thus, the refractive index n is shown to be 1.41.

Recap

The refractive index (n) of a medium is related to the critical angle (c) by the formula n = 1 / sin(c). This relationship applies when light is travelling from the medium into a vacuum or air.

Question 7 | 0625/42/M/J/20 Q7 | Electricity and Magnetism

(a) A student makes a transformer that uses an alternating current (a.c.) supply with an electromotive force (e.m.f.) of 12.0 V to induce an output potential difference (p.d.) of 2.0 V. The student is provided with two lengths of insulated wire and the U-shaped piece of iron shown in Fig. 7.1.

(i) Complete and label Fig. 7.1 to show the transformer connected to the supply and the output from the transformer. [3]

1. A primary coil should be wound around one side of the U-shaped iron core and connected to the 12.0 V a.c. supply.
2. A secondary coil should be wound around the other side of the core, with its ends representing the 2.0 V output.
3. To step down the voltage from 12V to 2V (a ratio of 6:1), there must be significantly more coils on the primary winding than on the secondary winding.

Recap

A transformer consists of a primary coil and a secondary coil wound on a soft iron core. The ratio of voltages is approximately equal to the ratio of the number of turns on the coils (Vp/Vs = Np/Ns). To step down voltage, the primary coil must have more turns than the secondary.

(ii) Explain the function of the piece of iron in the transformer. [2]

The soft iron core serves to concentrate the magnetic field produced by the primary coil and efficiently link the magnetic flux to the secondary coil. This ensures a stronger magnetic field passes through the secondary coil, leading to a more efficient induction of e.m.f.

Recap

The soft iron core in a transformer becomes easily magnetised. Its function is to concentrate the magnetic field lines and guide the changing magnetic flux from the primary coil through the secondary coil, ensuring efficient energy transfer.

(iii) The output of the transformer is connected to a lamp. The current in the lamp is 100 mA. The transformer is 100% efficient. Calculate the input current to the transformer. [2]

For a 100% efficient transformer, Input Power = Output Power (V₁I₁ = V₂I₂).
V₁ = 12.0 V, V₂ = 2.0 V, I₂ = 100 mA = 0.100 A.
I₁ = (V₂I₂) / V₁ = (2.0 V × 0.100 A) / 12.0 V = 0.20 / 12.0 A ≈ 0.0167 A.
The input current is approximately 0.017 A (or 17 mA).

Recap

For a 100% efficient transformer, the input power equals the output power (Vp × Ip = Vs × Is). This means that if the voltage is stepped down, the current must be stepped up by the same ratio to conserve energy.

(b) Another transformer is used in a school laboratory to step down a mains supply with a p.d. of 110 V to 12 V. This transformer is mounted in a metal case. State and explain an essential safety feature required for this arrangement. [2]

An essential safety feature is to earth the metal case.
Explanation: If the insulation on the live wire were to fail and it touched the metal case, the case would become live at 110V. The earth wire provides a low-resistance path to the ground, causing a very large current to flow, which blows the fuse or trips the circuit breaker, disconnecting the supply and preventing electric shock.

Recap

An essential safety feature for appliances with metal cases is an earth wire. If a live wire touches the case, the earth wire provides a very low resistance path to the ground, causing a large surge of current that blows the fuse or trips the circuit breaker, preventing electric shock.

Question 8 | 0625/42/M/J/20 Q8 | Electricity and Magnetism

(a) A light-emitting diode (LED) is a diode that emits light when there is a current in it. Draw a circuit diagram showing an LED, connected so that it is lit, in series with a battery and a fixed resistor. Use standard electrical symbols. [4]

The circuit diagram should show the following standard symbols connected in a single loop (series):
1. The symbol for a battery (or a single cell).
2. The symbol for a fixed resistor (a rectangle).
3. The symbol for a diode (a triangle pointing to a vertical line) with two arrows pointing away from it to indicate it is an LED.
The LED must be connected the correct way round, with the triangle pointing in the direction of conventional current (from the positive terminal of the battery to the negative).

Recap

A diode is a component that allows current to flow in only one direction (the 'forward bias' direction). An LED is a special type of diode that emits light when current flows through it. A series resistor is needed to limit the current to a safe level.

(b) The p.d. across the LED when lit is 3.1 V and the current in the LED is 0.030 A. Calculate the value of the resistance of the LED when lit. [2]

Using Ohm's Law, R = V / I.
V = 3.1 V, I = 0.030 A.
R = 3.1 V / 0.030 A = 103.33... Ω.
The resistance of the LED is approximately 100 Ω.

Recap

Ohm's Law states that the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it, provided the temperature is constant. The relationship is V = IR, where R is the resistance. This can be rearranged to calculate any of the three quantities.

(c) Fig. 8.1 shows a power supply of e.m.f. 10.5 V connected in series with a lamp and a heater. The p.d. across the lamp is 2.1 V and the current in the lamp is 1.5 A.

(i) Calculate the resistance of the heater. [2]

In a series circuit, the total voltage is shared between components.
Voltage across heater = Total e.m.f. - Voltage across lamp = 10.5 V - 2.1 V = 8.4 V.
The current is the same everywhere in a series circuit, so the current in the heater is also 1.5 A.
Resistance of heater = V / I = 8.4 V / 1.5 A = 5.6 Ω.
The resistance of the heater is 5.6 Ω.

Recap

In a series circuit, the total voltage from the supply is divided among the components (Kirchhoff's Voltage Law). The current, however, is the same through each component.

(ii) the power of the heater. [2]

Power is calculated using P = VI.
V (across heater) = 8.4 V, I = 1.5 A.
P = 8.4 V × 1.5 A = 12.6 W.
The power of the heater is 12.6 W.

Recap

Electrical power is the rate of energy conversion. It can be calculated in several ways, including P = VI, P = I²R, and P = V²/R. The choice of formula depends on the quantities that are known.

Question 10 | 0625/42/M/J/20 Q10 | Atomic Physics

Fig. 10.1 shows a vacuum tube with a radioactive source. The radioactive source emits α-particles, β-particles and γ-rays. There is a very strong magnetic field between the N pole and the S pole of the magnet.

(a) The lead cylinder has a narrow central hole. State and explain the effect of the lead cylinder. [2]

The lead cylinder is used to produce a narrow, straight beam of radiation. Lead is very dense and absorbs the radiation, so only the particles and rays travelling in the desired direction can pass through the hole.

Recap

A collimator is a device that narrows a beam of particles or waves. In nuclear physics, a block of lead with a narrow hole is used to absorb all radiation except for a fine, parallel beam that passes through the hole.

(b) Describe the paths of the α-particles, β-particles and γ-rays as they pass through the magnetic field. Explain your answers.

(i) α-particles [2]

α-particles are positively charged. Using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule (with the conventional current in the direction of the beam), they will be deflected by the magnetic field. The path will be a curve out of the page (or towards the viewer). The deflection is less than that of beta particles due to their larger mass.

Recap

Charged particles moving through a magnetic field experience a force (motor effect). Alpha particles are positively charged, so they are deflected. The direction of deflection can be found using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule. Due to their large mass, their path is a gentle curve.

(ii) β-particles [2]

β-particles are negatively charged (electrons). Their path will be curved in the opposite direction to the α-particles, so they will be deflected into the page. As they have a much smaller mass than alpha particles, they are deflected more strongly and follow a tighter curve.

Recap

Beta particles are negatively charged (electrons). They are deflected in the opposite direction to alpha particles in the same magnetic field. Because they have a very small mass, they are deflected much more easily and follow a much tighter curve.

(iii) γ-rays [2]

γ-rays are a form of electromagnetic radiation and have no charge. Therefore, they are not affected by the magnetic field and will pass straight through with no deflection.

Recap

Gamma rays are high-energy electromagnetic waves. They have no mass and no charge. Because they are uncharged, they do not interact with magnetic fields and are therefore undeflected.
