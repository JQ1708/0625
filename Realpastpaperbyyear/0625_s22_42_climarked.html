Question 1

0625/42/M/J/22 Q1 | Topics: Energy, Work, Power, Energy Resources

1 Fig. 1.1 shows an electrically powered bicycle.

When fully charged, the battery can deliver a power of 600W for 60 min.

(a) (i) Calculate the energy, in joules, stored in the battery when fully charged. [3]

First, convert the time from minutes to seconds: 60 min = 60 × 60 s = 3600 s.

Next, use the formula for energy transferred: Energy = Power × time (E = Pt).

E = 600 W × 3600 s

E = 2,160,000 J (or 2.16 × 106 J)

Recap

Work, Energy, and Power: Power (measured in watts, W) is the rate of doing work or the energy transferred per second.

The relationship is given by the equation: Power (W) = Energy (J) ÷ time (s), or P = ΔE ÷ t.

Therefore, to find the total energy transferred, you can rearrange the equation to: Energy = Power × time.

(a) (ii) State the form of energy stored by the battery. [1]

The form of energy stored in the battery is chemical energy.

Recap

Energy Stores: Chemical energy is a type of stored energy that can be released in a chemical reaction. Examples include a battery, fuel such as coal, or food.

(b) The bicycle has a motor with an electrical input power of 250 W. Calculate the time for which the battery can power the bicycle. [2]

Using the energy calculated in (a)(i) (2,160,000 J) and the rearranged power formula: time = Energy ÷ Power (t = E ÷ P).

t = 2,160,000 J / 250 W

t = 8640 s (Note: The mark scheme allows 8600s or 8800s due to rounding variations).

Recap

Work, Energy, and Power: The equation P = ΔE ÷ t can be rearranged to solve for any of the three variables. To find the time, the equation becomes time = Energy ÷ Power.

(c) Consider this bicycle compared to a small motorcycle. State two environmental benefits of the electrically powered bicycle. [2]

1. The bicycle produces less noise (or no noise) pollution.

2. The bicycle itself does not produce air/gaseous pollution (like carbon dioxide or sulfur dioxide) during use, unlike a motorcycle which burns fossil fuel.

Recap

Energy Resources: Many conventional vehicles use fossil fuels (non-renewable) which produce gaseous pollution when burned. This pollution includes carbon dioxide (a greenhouse gas contributing to global warming) and sulfur dioxide (which causes acid rain). Electric vehicles do not produce these emissions directly, which is a major environmental advantage.

0625/42/M/J/22 Q2 | Topics: Forces and Motion

2 Fig. 2.1 shows an object of mass 2.0 kg on a bench. This object is connected by a cord, passing over a pulley, to an object of mass 3.0 kg.

The 2.0 kg object is released from rest and accelerates at 4.0 m/s2.

(a) Calculate the resultant force acting on the 2.0 kg object. [2]

Using Newton's Second Law: Resultant Force = mass × acceleration (F = ma).

F = 2.0 kg × 4.0 m/s2

F = 8.0 N

Recap

Newton's Second Law: The relationship between force, mass, and acceleration is given by the equation F = ma.

A resultant force acting on an object will cause it to accelerate (change its velocity). This acceleration is directly proportional to the force and inversely proportional to the mass.

(b) Calculate the upward force F exerted by the cord on the 3.0 kg object. [3]

First, find the weight of the 3.0 kg object: Weight = mass × g (W = mg).

W = 3.0 kg × 10 N/kg = 30 N (downwards)

Next, find the resultant force on the 3.0 kg object: F = ma.

F_resultant = 3.0 kg × 4.0 m/s² = 12 N (downwards, in the direction of acceleration)

The resultant force is the difference between the weight (down) and the tension F (up): F_resultant = Weight - F.

12 N = 30 N - F

F = 30 N - 12 N = 18 N

Recap

Weight: Weight is the force on a mass due to a gravitational field. It is calculated using W = mg, where g on Earth is approximately 10 N/kg.

Resultant Force: The resultant force is the overall force on an object when all forces are combined, taking their directions into account.

(c) The objects have a constant acceleration.

(i) Show that the speed of the objects 0.80 s after release is 3.2 m/s. [2]

Use the equation for acceleration: acceleration = change in velocity / time (a = (v - u) ÷ t).

The object starts from rest, so initial velocity (u) = 0 m/s.

Rearranging for final velocity (v): v = u + at.

v = 0 + (4.0 m/s2 × 0.80 s)

v = 3.2 m/s

Recap

Acceleration: Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. For an object with constant acceleration, we can use the equation a = (v - u) ÷ t, where v is final velocity, u is initial velocity, and t is time.

(ii) A card, of width 2.0 cm, is fixed to the 2.0 kg object. As the 2.0 kg object moves to the left, the card passes through a beam of light that is perpendicular to the card. Using the speed given in (c)(i), calculate the time taken for the card to pass through the beam of light. [2]

First, convert the distance (card width) to metres: 2.0 cm = 0.020 m.

Use the equation for speed: speed = distance / time (v = s ÷ t).

Rearrange for time: t = s ÷ v.

t = 0.020 m ÷ 3.2 m/s

t = 0.00625 s (or 6.3 × 10-3 s)

Recap

Speed: Speed is the distance travelled per unit of time. The equation is v = s ÷ t. This can be rearranged to find time (t = s ÷ v) or distance (s = v × t).

0625/42/M/J/22 Q3 | Topics: Motion, Vectors, Kinetic Energy

(a) Fig. 3.1 shows water in a river moving parallel to the river bank at 4.0 m/s and a canoe travelling in the river.

The canoe travels at 2.5 m/s relative to the water and heads at an angle of 38° to the river bank. Draw a scale diagram to determine the canoe's resultant velocity and state the scale you used. [4]

This question requires drawing a vector diagram. We add the two velocity vectors ('nose to tail') to find the resultant.

1. Scale: A clear scale must be stated (e.g., 1 cm = 1.0 m/s).

2. Draw Vectors: - Draw the river vector: a line 4.0 cm long, representing 4.0 m/s (downstream). - From the end of the river vector, draw the canoe vector: a line 2.5 cm long, at an angle of 38° to the river bank line, representing 2.5 m/s.

3. Draw Resultant: Draw a line from the start of the first vector (river) to the end of the second vector (canoe). This is the resultant velocity.

4. Measure: - Measure the length of the resultant vector. Based on the MS, this should be 2.3-2.8 cm, corresponding to a magnitude of 2.3 – 2.8 m/s. - Measure the angle of the resultant vector relative to the river bank. This should be 35° – 40°.

Recap

Vectors and Scalars: Scalar quantities have magnitude (size) only (e.g., speed, distance). Vector quantities have both magnitude and direction (e.g., velocity, force).

Vectors can be added using a scale diagram. They are drawn one after another, 'nose to tail'. The resultant is the vector drawn from the start of the first vector to the end of the last vector.

(b) The mass of the canoeist is 65 kg. Calculate her kinetic energy when travelling on still water at 2.5 m/s. [2]

Use the formula for kinetic energy: Kinetic Energy = ½ × mass × velocity2 (Eₖ = ½mv2).

Eₖ = ½ × 65 kg × (2.5 m/s)2

Eₖ = 0.5 × 65 × 6.25

Eₖ = 203 J (or 200 J)

Recap

Kinetic Energy (Eₖ): This is the energy an object has due to its movement. Objects with greater mass or higher velocity have more kinetic energy.

The formula is Eₖ = ½mv2, where m is mass in kg and v is velocity in m/s.

0625/42/M/J/22 Q4 | Topics: Thermal Physics

(a) State and explain the two features of a liquid-in-glass thermometer that are necessary for linearity. [4]

Statement 1: The bore (narrow tube) must have a constant (cross-sectional) area.

Explanation 1: This ensures that for the same increase in volume (expansion) of the liquid, the liquid thread moves the same distance up the tube.

Statement 2: The liquid must have constant thermal expansion (it expands uniformly).

Explanation 2: This ensures that the liquid moves the same distance for each °C temperature rise, making the scale linear.

Recap

Liquid-in-Glass Thermometers: These work on the principle of thermal expansion. When the liquid (e.g., alcohol) is heated, it expands and is forced up the narrow tube (bore).

For the scale to be linear (evenly spaced), the expansion must be uniform. This requires the liquid to expand by the same amount for each degree rise in temperature and the tube to have a constant width so that a given change in volume always results in the same change in length.

(b) The value of the heat capacity of the hot junction of a thermocouple thermometer is important in ensuring that it can measure temperature changes very rapidly. Explain why. [2]

The hot junction should have a small heat capacity.

This means that it only needs a small amount of (thermal) energy to raise its temperature. This allows its temperature to change very quickly to match the temperature of the object it is measuring.

Recap

Heat Capacity: This is the amount of energy required to raise the temperature of an object by 1°C. (This is different from *specific* heat capacity, which is per kg).

An object with a low heat capacity (like the junction of a thermocouple) heats up and cools down very quickly, making it responsive to rapid temperature changes.

(c) The hot junction of a thermocouple thermometer has a heat capacity of 0.11 J/°C. Calculate the thermal energy required to increase the temperature of the hot junction from 20 °C to 345 °C. [3]

First, find the change in temperature (ΔT):

ΔT = Final Temperature - Initial Temperature

ΔT = 345 °C - 20 °C = 325 °C

Next, use the formula for thermal energy (E) and heat capacity (C): E = C × ΔT.

E = 0.11 J/°C × 325 °C

E = 35.75 J (or 36 J)

Recap

Calculating Energy Change: When using heat capacity (C, in J/°C), the formula for energy change (ΔE) is:

ΔE = C × ΔT

When using specific heat capacity (c, in J/kg°C), the formula is:

ΔE = m × c × ΔT

This question provides heat capacity (C), so the mass (m) is not needed.

0625/42/M/J/22 Q5 | Topics: Waves (Sound and EM Spectrum)

Sound waves are longitudinal and electromagnetic waves are transverse.

(a) A sound wave used for a medical examination has a frequency of 1.5 MHz.

(i) State and explain what type of sound wave this is. [2]

This is an ultrasound wave.

This is because its frequency (1.5 MHz = 1,500,000 Hz) is above the range of human hearing (which is typically 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz or 20 kHz).

Recap

Sound Waves: Sound is a longitudinal wave. The range of frequencies for human hearing is approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz).

  • Sound with frequencies *below* 20 Hz is called infrasound.
  • Sound with frequencies *above* 20,000 Hz is called ultrasound.

(ii) The wave travels through soft human tissue at a speed of 1.3 km/s. Calculate the wavelength of the wave in soft human tissue. [3]

First, convert all units to standard units (m/s and Hz):

Speed (v) = 1.3 km/s = 1300 m/s

Frequency (f) = 1.5 MHz = 1,500,000 Hz

Use the wave equation: speed = frequency × wavelength (v = fλ).

Rearrange for wavelength (λ): λ = v ÷ f.

λ = 1300 m/s ÷ 1,500,000 Hz

λ = 0.000867 m (or 8.7 × 10-4 m)

Recap

The Wave Equation: This equation links the three key properties of a wave:

v = f × λ

  • v = velocity (or speed) in m/s
  • f = frequency in Hertz (Hz)
  • λ = wavelength in metres (m)

(b) Describe one use of X-rays in medicine. [2]

Use: X-rays are used for detecting broken bones.

Detail: This works because X-rays pass through soft tissue (like skin and muscle) but are absorbed by bone. This creates a shadow image on a detector, allowing doctors to see the skeleton.

Recap

Uses of X-rays:

  • Medical imaging for bones and teeth.
  • CT scans in hospitals.
  • Airport luggage scanners (security).
  • Industrial checks for cracks in metal structures.

X-rays are ionizing radiation, which can damage cells, so exposure should be limited.

0625/42/M/J/22 Q6 | Topics: Light (Lenses)

6 Fig. 6.1 is a full-size ray diagram showing the formation of an image by a thin glass lens.

(a) Determine the focal length of the lens. [1]

The focal length is the distance from the centre of the lens to the principal focus. The diagram shows the ray that travels parallel to the principal axis is refracted through a point. Measuring this distance on the full-size diagram gives:

Focal length = 2.0 cm (Mark scheme allows 1.9–2.1 cm)

Recap

Converging (Convex) Lens: A converging lens is thicker in the middle.

The principal axis is the line passing through the centre of the lens.

The principal focus (F) is the point on the principal axis where rays travelling parallel to the axis converge (meet) after passing through the lens.

The focal length (f) is the distance from the centre of the lens to the principal focus.

(b) Circle three items in the list which describe the nature of the image formed. [3]

enlarged     same size     diminished

upright     inverted

real     virtual

The correct items are:

  • enlarged (the image arrow is taller than the object arrow)
  • inverted (the image arrow points downwards, opposite to the object)
  • real (the image is formed by the actual intersection of light rays and can be projected on a screen)

Recap

Image Characteristics:

  • Size: Enlarged (magnified), diminished (smaller), or same size.
  • Orientation: Upright (same way up) or inverted (upside down).
  • Type: Real (formed by converging rays, can be projected) or Virtual (formed where rays *appear* to come from, cannot be projected).

In this diagram, the object is between F and 2F, which always produces a real, inverted, and enlarged image.

(c) State one feature of a virtual image. [1]

A virtual image cannot be formed on a screen.

(Alternatively: It is formed where light rays *appear* to diverge from, not where they actually meet).

Recap

A virtual image is one that cannot be projected onto a screen. Examples include the image seen in a plane mirror, or the image seen when looking through a magnifying glass (a converging lens with the object inside the focal length).

0625/42/M/J/22 Q7 | Topics: Magnetism, Electromagnetism

7 Fig. 7.1 shows a small plotting compass which is aligned with the magnetic field between magnetic poles A and B of a U-shaped magnet.

(a) State the polarity of the poles. [1]

Pole A: N (North)

Pole B: S (South)

(The North pole of the compass needle points towards the South pole of the magnet).

Recap

Magnetic Fields: Magnetic field lines point away from a North pole and towards a South pole. A plotting compass will align itself with the magnetic field lines, with its North pole pointing in the direction of the field (i.e., towards the magnetic South pole).

(b) Fig. 7.2 shows a wire, placed between two poles, carrying a current in the direction of the arrow.

On Fig. 7.2, draw an arrow to show the direction of the force on the wire due to the magnetic field. [2]

The arrow should be drawn on the wire, pointing vertically up.

This is determined by Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: - First finger (Field): N to S (left to right) - Second finger (Current): Into the page (away from viewer) - Thumb (Force): Points upwards.

Recap

The Motor Effect: When a current-carrying wire is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a force. The direction of this force is given by Fleming's Left-Hand Rule.

  • Thumb = Thrust / Force
  • First finger = Field (N to S)
  • Second finger = Current (Conventional, + to -)

(c) Fig. 7.3 shows a β-particle moving in the direction of the arrow between the same two poles.

On Fig. 7.3, draw an arrow to show the direction of the force on the β-particle due to the magnetic field. [2]

The arrow should be drawn on the β-particle, pointing vertically down.

A β-particle is a high-speed electron, which has a negative charge. We use Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, but because the charge is negative, the force is in the *opposite* direction to the thumb.

- Field: N to S (left to right) - Current: A negative charge moving *into* the page is equivalent to a conventional current *out of* the page. - Thumb (Force): Points downwards.

Recap

Force on Charged Particles: A moving charge in a magnetic field experiences a force. We can still use Fleming's Left-Hand Rule.

  • For positive charges (like an alpha particle), the "Current" (C) is the direction of motion, and the force is in the direction of the thumb.
  • For negative charges (like a beta particle or electron), the "Current" (C) is *opposite* to the direction of motion. (Alternatively, use the Left-Hand Rule as normal and then reverse the direction of the force given by the thumb).

0625/42/M/J/22 Q8 | Topics: Electricity, AC/DC, Circuits

8 Fig. 8.1 shows how the electromotive force (e.m.f.) of a 60 Hz alternating current (a.c.) power supply varies with time.

(a) Calculate the time period of the a.c. [1]

The time period (T) is the inverse of the frequency (f). T = 1 ÷ f.

T = 1 ÷ 60 Hz

T = 0.0167 s (or 0.017 s)

Recap

Frequency and Period:

  • Frequency (f): The number of complete waves (or cycles) passing a point per second. Measured in Hertz (Hz).
  • Time Period (T): The time taken for one complete wave (or cycle) to pass. Measured in seconds (s).

The relationship is T = 1 ÷ f and f = 1 ÷ T.

(b) Fig. 8.2 shows this power supply connected in a circuit.

(i) State the name of component A. [1]

Component A is a diode.

Recap

A diode is an electrical component that allows current to flow easily in one direction only (the direction of the triangle). It has a very high resistance in the reverse direction. This process is called rectification.

(ii) In each time period of the a.c., 1.5 × 1017 electrons pass through component A. The charge on an electron is 1.6 × 10-19 C. Calculate the average current in the circuit during one time period. [3]

First, find the total charge (Q) that passes in one time period:

Q = Number of electrons × Charge per electron

Q = 1.5 × 1017 × 1.6 × 10-19 C = 0.024 C

Next, use the time period (t) from part (a): t = 0.0167 s

Now, calculate the average current (I): Current = Charge / time (I = Q ÷ t).

I = 0.024 C ÷ 0.0167 s

I = 1.44 A (or 1.4 A)

Recap

Electric Current: Current (I) is the rate of flow of charge (Q). It is measured in Amperes (A).

The equation is I = Q ÷ t, where Q is the charge in Coulombs (C) and t is the time in seconds (s).

Electric charge is carried by particles, most commonly electrons. The charge on one electron is 1.6 × 10-19 C.

(c) On Fig. 8.3: 1. mark, with an arrow labelled E, the direction of the electron flow through component B 2. mark, with an arrow labelled I, the direction of the conventional current in component C.

The diode (A) only allows current to flow from left to right.

1. Arrow E (Electron flow): Drawn on component B, pointing clockwise (from - to +).

2. Arrow I (Conventional current): Drawn on component C, pointing anti-clockwise (from + to -).

Recap

Conventional Current vs. Electron Flow:

  • Conventional Current (I): Assumed to flow from the positive (+) terminal to the negative (-) terminal. This is the standard direction used in circuit diagrams.
  • Electron Flow (E): The *actual* flow of electrons, which are negatively charged. They flow from the negative (-) terminal to the positive (+) terminal.

(d) Fig. 8.4 shows a circuit with components B and C connected to a direct current (d.c.) power supply of e.m.f. 12 V. The current in the circuit is 0.35 A.

Calculate the power delivered by the power supply to the circuit. [2]

Use the formula for electrical power: Power = Current × Voltage (P = IV).

P = 0.35 A × 12 V

P = 4.2 W

Recap

Electrical Power: Power (P) is the rate at which energy is transferred in a circuit. It is measured in Watts (W).

The formula is P = I × V, where I is current in Amps (A) and V is voltage in Volts (V).

Other forms of the equation (using Ohm's Law) are P = I2R and P = V2 ÷ R.

0625/42/M/J/22 Q9 | Topics: Electricity, Resistance, Circuits

9 Fig. 9.1 shows a circuit with a 3-position switch. The moving part of the switch is always connected to point Y... The other end, labelled X, can be connected to one of the points A, B or C.

The resistance of the motor is 2.0 Ω and the resistance of the resistor is 3.0 Ω.

(a) Determine the current in the motor when the switch is connected to:

(i) point A [1]

When connected to point A, the circuit is open. No current can flow.

Current = 0 A

Recap

An open circuit (or open switch) creates a break in the path, so charge cannot flow. The resistance is effectively infinite, and the current is zero.

(ii) point B [2]

When connected to point B, only the motor (M) is in the circuit.

Use Ohm's Law: Voltage = Current × Resistance (V = IR), rearranged to I = V ÷ R.

I = 12 V ÷ 2.0 Ω

I = 6.0 A

Recap

Ohm's Law: This describes the relationship between voltage (V), current (I), and resistance (R). The formula is V = IR.

It can be rearranged to find current (I = V ÷ R) or resistance (R = V ÷ I).

(iii) point C [2]

When connected to point C, the resistor (3.0 Ω) and the motor (2.0 Ω) are in series.

First, find the total resistance: R_total = R₁ + R₂.

R_total = 3.0 Ω + 2.0 Ω = 5.0 Ω

Now, use Ohm's Law (I = V ÷ R_total):

I = 12 V ÷ 5.0 Ω

I = 2.4 A (This is the current flowing through both the motor and the resistor).

Recap

Resistors in Series: When components are connected in series, they are in a single loop.

  • The current is the same through all components.
  • The total resistance is the sum of the individual resistances: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + ...
  • The total voltage is shared between the components.

(b) Two resistors of resistance 2.0 Ω and 3.0 Ω are connected in parallel. Calculate the combined resistance of the resistors in this arrangement. [3]

Use the formula for resistors in parallel: 1⁄Rp = 1⁄R₁ + 1⁄R₂.

1⁄Rp = 1⁄2.0 Ω + 1⁄3.0 Ω

1⁄Rp = 3⁄6 + 2⁄6 = 5⁄6

Now, invert both sides to find R_p: R_p = 6 ÷ 5

R_p = 1.2 Ω

Recap

Resistors in Parallel: When components are connected in parallel, they are on separate branches.

  • The voltage is the same across all branches.
  • The total current is the sum of the currents in the branches.
  • The total resistance (R_p) is found using the formula: 1⁄Rp = 1⁄R₁ + 1⁄R₂ + ...
  • Note: The total resistance in parallel is *always* less than the smallest individual resistor.

10 Fig. 10.1 is a simplified diagram of a digital circuit. The output of logic gate Y controls a buzzer.

(a) Complete Table 10.1, the truth table for the circuit. [3]

Logic gate X is a NOT gate. Logic gate Y is a NOR gate.

The 'output of X' column is the inverse of 'input A'.

The 'output of Y' column is the NOR of ('output of X') and ('input B'). (Output is 1 ONLY IF *both* inputs are 0).

input A input B output of X output of Y
0 0 1 0

Logic Gates:

(b) Input A is the output of a humidity sensor which gives logic 1 when the humidity is high... Input B is the output of a light sensor which gives logic 1 in bright light... The buzzer sounds when the output of Y is logic 1. State the conditions of humidity and light when the buzzer is on. [1]

From the truth table, the output of Y is 1 (buzzer on) only in the third row:

  • Input A = 1
  • Input B = 0

Therefore, the conditions are:

High humidity (A=1) AND Darkness (B=0).

Recap

Logic circuits are used to make decisions based on inputs from sensors. By combining logic gates, a circuit can be designed to perform a specific task (like sounding an alarm) only when a precise set of conditions is met.

(c) The output of the digital circuit alone is not able to operate the buzzer. Ring the component from the list that must be connected between the output of the digital circuit and the buzzer. [3]

fuse     heater     relay     resistor     thermistor

Explain your answer.

Component: relay

Explanation: Logic gates produce a low voltage and small current output. This is not enough to power a high-current device like a buzzer. A relay uses this small current to operate an electromagnet, which closes a switch in a separate, high-current circuit to power the buzzer.

Recap

A relay is an electromagnetic switch. It allows a low-power circuit (like the output of a logic gate) to control a high-power circuit (like a motor, heater, or buzzer) without the two circuits being electrically connected.

11 (a) Fig. 11.1 shows the paths of three α-particles moving towards a thin gold foil. Four gold nuclei are shown.

(i) On Fig. 11.1, complete the paths of the three α-particles. [3]

1. Top particle: Passes close to a nucleus and is deflected strongly (e.g., to the left/upwards).

Recap

Rutherford's Alpha-Scattering Experiment:

  • Most alpha particles passed straight through, showing the atom is mostly empty space.
  • Some were deflected by small angles, showing they were repelled by a concentration of positive charge (the nucleus).
  • A tiny few were deflected by large angles (>90°), showing the nucleus is very small and dense.

(ii) State the sign of the charge on the α-particles. [1]

Positive (or +)

Recap

An alpha (α) particle is identical to a helium nucleus. It consists of 2 protons and 2 neutrons, giving it a charge of +2. It is positive.

(b) The nuclide notation for a nucleus of gold-198 is 19879Au. State the numbers of electrons, neutrons and protons in a neutral atom of gold-198. [3]

  • Number of electrons = 79
  • Number of neutrons = 119
  • Number of protons = 79

Recap

Nuclide Notation (AZX):

  • A (Mass Number): The top number. It is the total number of protons + neutrons. (Here, A = 198)
  • Z (Proton/Atomic Number): The bottom number. It is the number of protons. (Here, Z = 79)
  • Number of Protons = Z = 79
  • Number of Neutrons = A - Z = 198 - 79 = 119
  • Number of Electrons: In a neutral atom, the number of electrons is equal to the number of protons to balance the charge. So, electrons = 79.
