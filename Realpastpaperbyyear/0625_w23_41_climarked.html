Question 1 | 0625/41/O/N/23 Q1 | Topics: Mechanics, Energy, Momentum, Forces

A girl holds a rubber ball out of a window of a tall building. The mass of the ball is 0.20 kg. The ball is at rest 10m above a concrete path.

(a) Calculate the gravitational potential energy of the ball relative to the concrete path.

The gravitational potential energy is 20 J. This is calculated using the formula GPE = mgh, where m is the mass (0.20 kg), g is the acceleration of free fall (9.8 m/s²), and h is the height (10 m). So, the gravitational potential energy is 0.20 kg × 9.8 m/s² × 10 m = 19.6 J, which is approximately 20 J.

Recap

Gravitational potential energy is the energy an object has due to its position in a gravitational field. The higher the object, the more gravitational potential energy it has. It is a form of stored energy.

(b) The girl releases the ball and it falls towards the path. The ball strikes the path and bounces vertically upwards.

The speed of the ball immediately before it strikes the path is 14m/s.

The speed of the ball immediately after it strikes the path is 12m/s.

(i) Calculate the kinetic energy of the ball immediately after it strikes the concrete path.

The kinetic energy of the ball immediately after it strikes the path is 14 J. This is calculated using the formula KE = ½mv², where m is the mass (0.20 kg) and v is the velocity (12 m/s). So, the kinetic energy is ½ × 0.20 kg × (12 m/s)² = 14.4 J, which is approximately 14 J.

Recap

Kinetic energy is the energy an object has due to its motion. The faster an object moves, the more kinetic energy it has. It is directly proportional to the mass and the square of the velocity.

(ii) Show that the change in momentum of the ball when it bounces off the path is 5.2 kgm/s.

The change in momentum is 5.2 kgm/s. Momentum is calculated as p = mv. The initial momentum (downwards) is 0.20 kg × 14 m/s = 2.8 kgm/s. The final momentum (upwards) is 0.20 kg × -12 m/s = -2.4 kgm/s. The change in momentum is the final momentum minus the initial momentum, so it is -2.4 kgm/s - 2.8 kgm/s = -5.2 kgm/s. The magnitude of this change is 5.2 kgm/s.

Recap

Momentum is a measure of the mass in motion. It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction. The principle of conservation of momentum states that the total momentum of a closed system remains constant.

(iii) The ball is in contact with the path for 0.25s. Calculate the average resultant force on the ball when it is in contact with the path.

The average resultant force on the ball is 21 N. Force is the rate of change of momentum, calculated as F = ∆p / ∆t. The change in momentum is 5.2 kgm/s and the time is 0.25 s. So, the force is 5.2 kgm/s / 0.25 s = 20.8 N, which is approximately 21 N.

Recap

Force is an interaction that can change the motion of an object. According to Newton's second law, the force acting on an object is equal to the rate at which its momentum changes. The unit of force is the Newton (N).

Question 2 | 0625/41/O/N/23 Q2 | Topics: Thermal Physics, Conduction, Convection, Radiation

A copper cooking pan contains water. Fig. 2.1 shows the pan on a hotplate of a cooker.

Copper is a metal.

(a) Thermal energy is conducted through all solids by lattice vibrations. Describe one other way in which thermal energy is conducted through the copper.

In metals like copper, thermal energy is also conducted by the movement of free electrons. These electrons gain thermal energy from the hotplate and, being mobile, they move through the metal, colliding with other particles and transferring the energy to cooler parts of the pan.

Recap

Conduction is the transfer of thermal energy through direct contact. In metals, it happens through both lattice vibrations and the movement of delocalised electrons, which makes them excellent thermal conductors.

(b) The outside surface of the cooking pan is kept clean by regular polishing. Explain one other advantage of keeping the surface of the pan shiny.

A shiny surface is a poor emitter of thermal radiation. This reduces energy loss from the sides of the pan to the surroundings, helping to keep the water hot more efficiently.

Recap

All objects emit thermal radiation. The rate of emission depends on the surface temperature and its nature. Dull, dark surfaces are good emitters and absorbers, while shiny, light surfaces are poor emitters and good reflectors of thermal radiation.

(c) The thermal energy passes into the water through the base of the pan. Identify the main method by which thermal energy is transferred throughout the water.

The main method of thermal energy transfer throughout the water is convection.

Recap

Convection is the transfer of heat through fluids (liquids or gases) by the movement of the fluid itself. When water at the bottom of the pan is heated, it expands, becomes less dense, and rises. Cooler, denser water from the top sinks to take its place, creating a convection current that distributes heat.

Question 3 | 0625/41/O/N/23 Q3 | Topics: States of Matter, Pressure, Buoyancy

(a) Liquids are difficult to compress whereas gases can be compressed easily. Explain, in terms of particles, why it is difficult to compress liquids.

It is difficult to compress liquids because the particles in a liquid are already touching or are very close to each other. Therefore, there are very large repulsive forces between the particles that oppose them being pushed any closer together.

Recap

In the particle model, solids have particles in fixed positions, liquids have particles that are close together but can move past each other, and gases have particles that are far apart and move randomly. The incompressibility of liquids is a key difference from gases.

(b) Fig. 3.1 shows a rectangular block floating in water. The density of the water is 1000kg/m³.

The area of the base of the block is 0.014m². The base of the block is at a depth of 0.087 m below the surface of the water.

(i) Show that the pressure due to the water at the base of the block is approximately 850 Pa.

The pressure at the base of the block is approximately 850 Pa. This is calculated using the formula for pressure in a fluid, p = h⍴g, where h is the depth (0.087 m), ⍴ is the density of the water (1000 kg/m³), and g is the acceleration of free fall (9.8 m/s²). So, the pressure is 0.087 m × 1000 kg/m³ × 9.8 m/s² = 852.6 Pa, which is approximately 850 Pa.

Recap

The pressure in a fluid increases with depth. This is because the weight of the fluid above exerts a force on the area below. The formula p = h⍴g is fundamental for calculating pressure in liquids.

(ii) Calculate the force F on the base of the block caused by the pressure given in (b)(i).

The force on the base of the block is 12 N. This is calculated using the formula F = pA, where p is the pressure (850 Pa) and A is the area (0.014 m²). So, the force is 850 Pa × 0.014 m² = 11.9 N, which is approximately 12 N.

Recap

Pressure is defined as force per unit area (p = F/A). This relationship allows you to calculate the total force exerted on a surface if you know the pressure and the area over which it acts.

(iii) Force F is equal to the weight of the block. Calculate the mass of the block.

The mass of the block is 1.2 kg. Since the force F (the upthrust) is equal to the weight of the block, we can use the formula W = mg, where W is the weight (12 N) and g is the acceleration of free fall (9.8 m/s²). Rearranging for mass gives m = W/g. So, the mass is 12 N / 9.8 m/s² = 1.22 kg, which is approximately 1.2 kg.

Recap

Archimedes' principle states that the upward buoyant force that is exerted on a body immersed in a fluid, whether fully or partially submerged, is equal to the weight of the fluid that the body displaces. For a floating object, the buoyant force is equal to the object's own weight.

Question 4 | 0625/41/O/N/23 Q4 | Topics: Mechanics, Stability, Moments, Waves

A radio transmitter is a very tall, thin cylinder. It is prevented from falling over by wires which have one end fixed to the transmitter and the other end fixed in the ground. The ends of the wires in the ground are a long distance from the transmitter. Fig. 4.1 shows the transmitter and two of the wires.

The centre of gravity G is shown on Fig. 4.1.

(a) (i) State what is meant by centre of gravity.

The centre of gravity is the single point where the entire weight of an object can be considered to act.

Recap

The centre of gravity is an important concept for understanding stability. It is the average location of the weight of an object. For a uniform object, the centre of gravity is at its geometric center.

(a) (ii) Explain why the radio transmitter without the wires is a very unstable structure.

The transmitter is very tall and thin, meaning it has a high centre of gravity and a narrow base. A small tilt would cause the vertical line through its centre of gravity G to fall outside the base. This would create a turning moment that would cause the transmitter to topple over.

Recap

An object is stable if its centre of gravity is low and its base is wide. It becomes unstable if a small displacement causes its centre of gravity to fall outside its base, leading to a resultant moment that causes it to topple.

(b) Wire W is under tension and it exerts a force T on the transmitter.

(i) On Fig. 4.1, mark an arrow to show the force T exerted by wire W on the transmitter.

An arrow should be drawn on the diagram along the wire W, pointing from the transmitter towards the ground, to indicate the direction of the tension force.

Recap

Tension is a pulling force transmitted axially by the means of a string, a cable, chain, or similar one-dimensional continuous object.

(ii) The force T produces a moment on the transmitter about its base. Describe how the moment produced by T is calculated and indicate on Fig. 4.1 what is meant by any other terms in the description.

The moment of a force is calculated by multiplying the force by the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. On the diagram, the force is T, and the perpendicular distance would be a line drawn from the base of the transmitter (the pivot) to the line of action of force T, meeting it at a right angle.

Recap

A moment is the turning effect of a force. The principle of moments states that for an object to be in rotational equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point must be equal to the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point.

(c) The radio transmitter uses radio waves to transmit radio and television programmes. State one other use of radio waves.

Other uses of radio waves include Wi-Fi, Bluetooth communications, RADAR systems, and astronomy (radio astronomy).

Recap

Radio waves are a type of electromagnetic radiation with the longest wavelengths in the electromagnetic spectrum. They are used for various forms of communication over long distances.

Question 5 | 0625/41/O/N/23 Q5 | Topics: Energy Resources, Power Generation

(a) Many methods of generating electrical power involve the use of water. Describe one method of generating electrical power from energy stored in water.

One method is hydroelectric power. Water is stored at a height in a reservoir, giving it gravitational potential energy. This water is then released and flows down through pipes, converting its potential energy into kinetic energy. The moving water turns a turbine, which is connected to a generator that produces electricity.

Recap

Hydroelectric power is a renewable energy source that relies on the water cycle. The basic principle is the conversion of energy from one form to another: Potential Energy → Kinetic Energy → Mechanical Energy → Electrical Energy.

(b) For the method you chose in (a), state one advantage and one disadvantage of generating electricity this way.

Advantage: Hydroelectric power is a renewable energy source and does not produce greenhouse gases during operation, making it a clean form of energy.

Disadvantage: Building a dam and reservoir can be very expensive and requires flooding large areas of land, which can destroy habitats and displace communities.

Recap

When evaluating energy sources, it's important to consider their environmental impact, cost, reliability, and sustainability. All methods of power generation have both pros and cons.

(c) State two methods of generating electrical power for which the main source of energy is not the Sun.

Two such methods are:

  1. Geothermal energy, which uses heat from the Earth's core.
  2. Nuclear energy, which is derived from the energy stored in the nucleus of an atom.

Recap

While most energy sources on Earth, including fossil fuels and wind, ultimately derive their energy from the Sun, there are a few that do not. Geothermal, nuclear, and tidal power (driven by the Moon's gravity) are notable exceptions.

Question 6 | 0625/41/O/N/23 Q6 | Topics: Optics, Lenses, Ray Diagrams, Vision

A page of printed text is placed 18cm from a converging lens of focal length 35 cm. Fig. 6.1 is a scale diagram of the arrangement with each of the two principal focuses (focal points) of the lens labelled F. A length of 1.0cm on the scale diagram represents an actual length of 5.0cm.

(a) (i) By drawing on Fig. 6.1, locate the image of the page produced by the lens and label it I.

To locate the image, at least two rays must be drawn from the top of the object. One ray travels parallel to the principal axis and is then refracted through the principal focus (F) on the other side. A second ray travels straight through the optical centre of the lens without deviation. Since the object is placed within the focal length, these rays will diverge after passing through the lens. They must be traced back to a point of intersection on the same side of the lens as the object. This intersection point is where the virtual image 'I' is formed.

Recap

Ray diagrams are used to determine the position and nature of an image formed by a lens. For a converging lens, an object placed inside the focal length produces a virtual, upright, and magnified image.

(a) (ii) Using Fig. 6.1, determine the actual distance of image I from the lens.

The distance of the image from the lens on the diagram is measured to be between 7.1 cm and 7.7 cm. Since the scale is 1.0 cm : 5.0 cm, the actual distance is between 35.5 cm and 38.5 cm.

Recap

Scale diagrams are a useful tool in optics. It is important to measure accurately on the diagram and then convert the measurement to the actual distance using the given scale.

(b) Converging lenses can be used as magnifying glasses. State whether the image produced when a lens is used as a magnifying glass is real or virtual. Explain why.

The image is virtual. This is because the light rays do not actually converge at the image location; they only appear to come from that point. A virtual image cannot be projected onto a screen.

Recap

A real image is formed where light rays actually intersect, and it can be projected onto a screen. A virtual image is formed where light rays only appear to diverge from, and it cannot be projected.

(c) Suggest how someone who is long-sighted may benefit from using a converging lens.

In a long-sighted eye, the lens is not powerful enough to focus light from nearby objects onto the retina; instead, it focuses the image behind the retina. A converging lens helps by converging the light rays before they enter the eye. This reduces the overall focal length and allows the eye to focus the image correctly on the retina, producing a sharp image of close objects.

Recap

Long-sightedness (hyperopia) is a common vision defect where distant objects can be seen clearly but close ones cannot. It is corrected by using a converging lens, which increases the overall refractive power of the eye's lens system.

Question 7 | 0625/41/O/N/23 Q7 | Topics: Electrostatics, Electric Fields

(a) A plastic rod is uncharged. When the rod is rubbed with a woollen cloth, the rod becomes negatively charged. Explain, in terms of particles, why the rod becomes negatively charged.

When the plastic rod is rubbed with the woollen cloth, friction causes electrons, which are negatively charged particles, to move from the cloth to the rod. The rod therefore gains an excess of electrons and becomes negatively charged.

Recap

Charging by friction is a process where electrons are transferred from one object to another when they are rubbed together. The object that gains electrons becomes negatively charged, and the object that loses electrons becomes positively charged.

(b) Fig. 7.1 shows a negatively charged metal sphere S. There is an electric field surrounding S.

(i) State what is meant by an electric field.

An electric field is a region of space where an electric charge will experience a force.

Recap

An electric field is a vector field that surrounds an electric charge. The direction of the field is defined as the direction of the force that would be exerted on a small positive test charge placed in the field.

(ii) On Fig. 7.1, draw the pattern of the electric field surrounding sphere S and indicate its direction.

The electric field lines should be drawn as radial lines pointing towards the center of the negatively charged sphere S. The lines should be evenly distributed around the sphere and should touch its surface.

Recap

Electric field lines are used to visualize electric fields. They always point from positive to negative. For a single negative point charge, the field lines are radial and point inwards.

(c) Fig. 7.2 shows a small negative charge Z placed near to sphere S. Charge Z experiences a force due to the electric field surrounding S. On Fig. 7.2, draw an arrow to show the direction of this force on Z.

An arrow should be drawn on charge Z pointing directly away from the center of the negatively charged sphere S. This is because like charges repel each other.

Recap

The basic law of electrostatics states that like charges repel and opposite charges attract. A negative charge placed in an electric field will experience a force in the opposite direction to the field lines.

Question 8 | 0625/41/O/N/23 Q8 | Topics: Electricity, Circuits, Resistance

A cylinder is made of modelling clay. The modelling clay is an electrical conductor. Fig. 8.1 shows the cylinder.

The cylinder is connected into a circuit. Fig. 8.2 shows that the circuit also includes a battery of electromotive force (e.m.f.) 9.0V and a resistor P.

The resistance of P is 4.0Ω. The current in P is 1.5A.

(a) (i) Calculate the magnitude X of the charge that flows through P in 600 s.

The magnitude of the charge X is 900 C. This is calculated using the formula Q = It, where I is the current (1.5 A) and t is the time (600 s). So, the charge is 1.5 A × 600 s = 900 C.

Recap

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge. One ampere is defined as one coulomb of charge flowing per second. The relationship is given by the formula Q = It.

(a) (ii) Calculate the resistance of the cylinder of modelling clay.

The resistance of the clay cylinder is 2.0 Ω. First, the total resistance of the circuit is calculated using Ohm's Law, R = V/I, where V is the e.m.f. (9.0 V) and I is the current (1.5 A). The total resistance is 9.0 V / 1.5 A = 6.0 Ω. Since the resistor P and the clay cylinder are in series, the total resistance is the sum of their individual resistances. Therefore, the resistance of the cylinder is 6.0 Ω - 4.0 Ω = 2.0 Ω.

Recap

For components in a series circuit, the total resistance is the sum of the individual resistances (R_total = R1 + R2 + ...). The current is the same through all components, and the total voltage is shared between them.

(b) The cylinder is removed from the circuit and replaced with a new cylinder made of the same modelling clay. The new cylinder is twice the length and has half the cross-sectional area of the first cylinder. Calculate the time that it now takes for a charge of magnitude X to flow through resistor P.

The time taken is 1200 s. The resistance of a conductor is directly proportional to its length (l) and inversely proportional to its cross-sectional area (A). The new cylinder has twice the length, which doubles the resistance, and half the area, which also doubles the resistance. Therefore, the new resistance is 2 × 2 = 4 times the original resistance of the cylinder, so it is 4 × 2.0 Ω = 8.0 Ω. The new total resistance of the circuit is 8.0 Ω + 4.0 Ω = 12.0 Ω. The new current is I = V/R = 9.0 V / 12.0 Ω = 0.75 A. The time taken for the same charge (900 C) to flow is t = Q/I = 900 C / 0.75 A = 1200 s.

Recap

The resistance of a material is determined by its resistivity (⍴), length (l), and cross-sectional area (A), according to the formula R = ⍴l/A. Understanding this relationship is key to predicting how changes in a conductor's geometry will affect its resistance.

Question 9 | 0625/41/O/N/23 Q9 | Topics: Atomic Physics, Radioactivity

Many household smoke alarms contain a sample of the radioactive isotope americium-241 (Am). Americium-241 is the isotope of the element americium that has the nucleon number (mass number) 241.

(a) (i) State how the composition of a nucleus of americium-241 differs from that of a nucleus of americium-242.

A nucleus of americium-241 has one fewer neutron than a nucleus of americium-242. Both are isotopes of americium, so they have the same number of protons.

Recap

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons. This means they have the same atomic number but different mass numbers.

(a) (ii) An atom of a different element has a nucleon number of 241. State two differences between the composition of a nucleus of this atom and a nucleus of americium-241.

Compared to a nucleus of americium-241, the nucleus of this different element will have a different number of protons and a different number of neutrons. Because it is a different element, the proton number must be different. Since the total nucleon number is the same (241), the neutron number must also be different to compensate.

Recap

The number of protons in a nucleus (the atomic number) defines the element. Atoms with the same mass number but different atomic numbers are called isobars.

(b) Americium-241 decays to an isotope of neptunium (Np) by alpha-particle (α-particle) emission.

(i) Complete the equation for this decay: 241Am → ...93Np + ......α

The completed equation is: 24195Am → 23793Np + 42α. An alpha particle consists of 2 protons and 2 neutrons. Therefore, the mass number of Americium decreases by 4 and the atomic number decreases by 2.

Recap

In alpha decay, a nucleus emits an alpha particle (a helium nucleus, 42He). This decreases the mass number of the parent nucleus by 4 and the atomic number by 2, resulting in a new element.

(ii) One reason for using an isotope that emits α-particles in a smoke detector is that α-particles are more strongly ionising than beta-particles (β-particles). Explain why α-particles are more strongly ionising than β-particles.

Alpha particles are more strongly ionising than beta particles for two main reasons: they have a greater charge (+2e compared to -1e for a beta particle) and they generally have more kinetic energy. This allows them to knock electrons off atoms more effectively as they pass through a medium.

Recap

Ionising power is the ability of radiation to remove electrons from atoms or molecules. Alpha particles are highly ionising but have low penetration power. Beta particles are less ionising but more penetrating. Gamma rays are weakly ionising but highly penetrating.

(iii) The isotope of neptunium produced by americium-241 is also radioactive... The half-life of neptunium is longer than two million years. Using this information, explain the advantage of this long half-life for the use and safe disposal of a household smoke alarm.

A very long half-life means that the neptunium has a very low activity, meaning it emits radiation at a very slow rate. This makes it not hazardous to humans during the lifetime of the smoke detector and also makes disposal cheap and easy, as it does not require special handling as high-level radioactive waste.

Recap

Half-life is the time taken for the activity of a radioactive sample to decrease by half. A long half-life corresponds to low activity, while a short half-life corresponds to high activity. The activity of a source is the number of decays per second, measured in becquerels (Bq).

Question 10 | 0625/41/O/N/23 Q10 | Topics: Astrophysics, Stars, Cosmology

The Milky Way is one of many billions of galaxies. Each galaxy contains many billions of stable stars.

(a) Stable stars transfer energy into space by emitting electromagnetic radiation from their surfaces. Describe what happens in the core of a stable star to release energy that is eventually transferred into space.

In the core of a stable star, immense pressure and temperature allow for nuclear fusion to occur. In this process, hydrogen nuclei fuse together to form helium nuclei. This reaction releases a vast amount of energy, which is then radiated outwards to the star's surface and into space.

Recap

Nuclear fusion is the process that powers stars. It involves light atomic nuclei combining to form heavier nuclei, releasing energy in the process. This is the same principle behind hydrogen bombs.

(b) On the Earth, light from a distant galaxy is observed and analysed by astronomers. This information is used to determine the speed at which the galaxy is moving away from the Earth.

(i) Describe how the observed light is different from when it was emitted.

The observed light from distant galaxies has a longer wavelength than when it was emitted. This phenomenon is known as redshift, as the light is shifted towards the red end of the electromagnetic spectrum.

Recap

Redshift is a key piece of evidence for the expansion of the universe. It is an example of the Doppler effect for light. The further away a galaxy is, the greater its redshift, which means it is moving away from us faster.

(ii) State the quantity that astronomers use to determine the speed at which the galaxy is moving away.

Astronomers use the change in wavelength (or the magnitude of the redshift) of the starlight to determine the recessional speed of the galaxy.

Recap

The change in wavelength (Δλ) compared to the original wavelength (λ₀) gives the redshift (z), where z = Δλ/λ₀. This value is then used in Hubble's Law to calculate the galaxy's speed.

(c) The Hubble constant Ho is equal to 2.2 × 10-18 per second.

(i) Calculate the distance from the Earth of a galaxy that is moving away at a speed of 1.3 x 107 m/s.

The distance to the galaxy is 5.9 x 1024 m. This is calculated using Hubble's Law, v = Hod. Rearranging for distance gives d = v / Ho. So, the distance is (1.3 x 107 m/s) / (2.2 × 10-18 s-1) = 5.9 x 1024 m.

Recap

Hubble's Law is a fundamental concept in cosmology. It states that the recessional velocity of a galaxy is directly proportional to its distance from us. The constant of proportionality is the Hubble constant, Ho.

(ii) Calculate an estimate for the age of the Universe. Give your answer in years.

The age of the Universe is estimated to be 1.4 × 1010 years. The age of the Universe can be estimated as the reciprocal of the Hubble constant, Age = 1 / Ho. So, the age in seconds is 1 / (2.2 × 10-18 s-1) = 4.5 × 1017 s. To convert this to years, we divide by the number of seconds in a year (3.15 x 107), giving approximately 1.4 × 1010 years.

Recap

The inverse of the Hubble constant provides a simple estimate for the age of the Universe, assuming a constant rate of expansion. This calculation gives us a timescale for how long the universe has been expanding since the Big Bang.
