Question 1 | 0625/43/O/N/24 Q1 | Topic: General Physics

(a) (i) State the difference between a scalar quantity and a vector quantity.

A scalar quantity has only magnitude, whereas a vector quantity has both magnitude and direction.

Recap

Examples of scalar quantities include mass, time, and temperature. Examples of vector quantities include force, velocity, and acceleration.

(a) (ii) Define momentum.

Momentum is defined as the product of an object's mass and its velocity (p = mv).

Recap

Momentum is a vector quantity. The principle of conservation of momentum states that in a closed system, the total momentum before a collision or explosion is equal to the total momentum after.

(b) A test car crashes into a barrier to test the safety features. The test car has a total mass of 950 kg. It is moving with constant velocity from time t = 0 for 4.0 s. At t = 4.0 s, the car hits the barrier.

(i) During the test crash, the resultant force acting on the car is 27000 N. The car takes 1.5 s to come to rest. The deceleration is uniform. Calculate the initial velocity of the car.

Using the impulse-momentum theorem, FΔt = Δ(mv). Since the final velocity is 0, FΔt = -mu. So, 27000 N × 1.5 s = 950 kg × u. Rearranging for u, u = (27000 × 1.5) / 950 = 42.6 m/s (or 43 m/s to 2 s.f.).

Recap

Impulse is the change in momentum of an object. It is equal to the force applied multiplied by the time over which it acts. This principle is fundamental in understanding collisions.

(ii) On Fig. 1.2, sketch a speed-time graph to show the motion of the car from time t = 0 until the car becomes stationary.

The graph should show a straight horizontal line from t = 0 to t = 4.0 s (representing constant velocity). From t = 4.0 s, there should be a straight line with a negative gradient, reaching the x-axis (zero speed) at t = 5.5 s (4.0 s + 1.5 s).

Recap

On a speed-time graph, a horizontal line indicates constant velocity, and a straight line with a negative gradient indicates constant deceleration. The area under the graph represents the distance traveled.

Question 2 | 0625/43/O/N/24 Q2 | Topic: General Physics

(a) Describe an experiment to determine the spring constant of a spring. State: the apparatus you need; details of how to take measurements; how to calculate the spring constant. You may use the space below to draw a labelled diagram as part of your answer.

Apparatus needed: spring, retort stand, clamp, ruler/metre rule, set of slotted masses/weights. Procedure: Hang the spring from the clamp. Measure its initial length. Add known masses to the spring and measure the new length for each mass. Calculate the extension (new length - initial length). Calculate the force (F = mg). Plot a load-extension graph. The gradient of this graph is the spring constant (k = F/x).

Recap

This experiment is designed to verify Hooke's Law and determine the spring constant, which is a measure of the stiffness of the spring. A stiffer spring has a higher spring constant.

(b) Fig. 2.1 shows a baby in a baby bouncer. The baby bouncer consists of a holder suspended from a spring. The baby pushes his feet on the ground and bounces gently up and down.

(i) Two springs Q and R are tested to determine their spring constants. Each spring is tested up to its limit of proportionality. Define 'limit of proportionality'.

The limit of proportionality is the maximum load that can be applied to a spring for which its extension is directly proportional to the load (force).

Recap

Beyond the limit of proportionality, the spring no longer obeys Hooke's Law. If the force is increased further, it may reach the elastic limit, beyond which the spring will be permanently deformed.

Question 3 | 0625/43/O/N/24 Q3 | Topic: Thermal Physics

Fig. 3.1 shows a portable shower used on a campsite. The bag is filled with water. The water is heated using infrared radiation from the Sun.

(a) (i) Explain why the shower bag is painted black.

The shower bag is painted black because black surfaces are good absorbers of infrared radiation (heat) from the Sun, allowing the water to heat up more efficiently.

Recap

Dark, dull surfaces are generally good absorbers and emitters of thermal radiation, while light, shiny surfaces are poor absorbers and emitters, making them good reflectors.

(a) (ii) Explain a disadvantage of radiation from the Sun being the only source to heat the water.

A disadvantage is that the Sun has a variable output. The water will not heat up at night, or on cloudy days, or when there is no sun, making the shower unreliable.

Recap

Solar energy is a renewable resource, but its availability is intermittent. This variability is a key challenge for integrating solar power into energy grids.

(b) Solar energy is a renewable energy resource. State two other renewable energy resources.

Two other renewable energy resources are hydroelectric power and tidal energy.

Recap

Other renewable energy sources include wind, geothermal, and biomass. These sources are sustainable and produce little to no greenhouse gas emissions.

(c) During the day, the Sun shines on the shower bag and some of the energy in the infrared radiation from the Sun transfers to the thermal energy stores of the water. The water absorbs 60% of the energy incident on the bag. The temperature of the water rises from 10°C to 43°C. The mass of the water in the bag is 40 kg. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/(kg°C). Calculate the energy incident on the shower bag during the day.

First, calculate the energy absorbed by the water: E_absorbed = mcΔθ = 40 kg × 4200 J/(kg°C) × (43°C - 10°C) = 40 × 4200 × 33 = 5,544,000 J. Since this is 60% of the incident energy, E_incident = E_absorbed / 0.60 = 5,544,000 J / 0.60 = 9,240,000 J (or 9.2 × 10⁶ J).

Recap

This calculation involves specific heat capacity and energy transfer. The efficiency of energy absorption is also a key factor in practical applications like solar water heaters.

Question 4 | 0625/43/O/N/24 Q4 | Topic: Waves

(a) Fig. 4.1 shows a ray of light as it enters the side of a plastic block. The ray of light passes from air into the plastic.

(i) State how the speed, wavelength and frequency of the wave in the plastic block compare with their values in the air.

In the plastic block, the speed decreases, the wavelength decreases, but the frequency remains unchanged.

Recap

When light passes from one medium to another, its speed and wavelength change, but its frequency remains constant. This is because the frequency is determined by the source of the light.

(a) (ii) Show that the refractive index of the plastic is 1.4.

Using Snell's Law, n = sin(i) / sin(r). Given i = 45° and r = 30°, n = sin(45°) / sin(30°) = 0.707 / 0.500 = 1.414, which rounds to 1.4.

Recap

Refractive index is a measure of how much a medium slows down light. A higher refractive index means light travels slower in that medium and bends more when entering it from a less dense medium.

(a) (iii) Calculate the critical angle for the plastic.

The critical angle (c) is calculated using sin(c) = 1/n. So, sin(c) = 1 / 1.414 = 0.707. Therefore, c = arcsin(0.707) = 45°.

Recap

The critical angle is the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the less dense medium is 90°. Beyond this angle, total internal reflection occurs.

(b) Fig. 4.2 shows the same plastic as in (a) used to make an optical fibre. A ray of light is passing along the fibre.

(i) Carefully continue the ray of light P until it reaches the other end of the fibre.

The ray of light P should be drawn undergoing total internal reflection at the boundaries of the optical fibre, reflecting back into the fibre each time, until it reaches the other end.

Recap

Total internal reflection is the phenomenon where light traveling in a denser medium strikes the boundary with a less dense medium at an angle greater than the critical angle, and all the light is reflected back into the denser medium.

(ii) State two uses for optical fibres.

Two uses for optical fibres are internet transmission (or high-speed broadband/telecommunications) and endoscopes (for medical imaging).

Recap

Optical fibres are thin strands of glass or plastic that transmit light signals over long distances with minimal loss. They are widely used in telecommunications, medicine, and sensing.

Question 5 | 0625/43/O/N/24 Q5 | Topic: Electricity and Magnetism

Fig. 5.1 shows a metal sphere S. The sphere has been charged with a negative charge.

(a) (i) There is an electric field around sphere S. On Fig. 5.1, draw four field lines to show the pattern of the field and indicate the direction of the field with arrows on the lines.

The diagram should show four evenly spaced radial lines touching the sphere S, with arrows pointing towards the sphere (since it is negatively charged). There should be no lines inside the sphere.

Recap

Electric field lines represent the direction of the force that a positive test charge would experience. They originate from positive charges and terminate on negative charges.

(a) (ii) Fig. 5.2 shows a position X next to sphere S. A small negatively charged particle is placed at position X. State the direction of the force on the negatively charged particle at X due to the electric field around sphere S.

The force on the negatively charged particle at X will be to the left (or away from the sphere S), because like charges repel.

Recap

Coulomb's Law describes the electrostatic force between charged particles. Like charges repel each other, while opposite charges attract.

(a) (iii) The negatively charged particle at X is released from rest. Describe the motion of the small negatively charged particle due to the electric field around sphere S.

The negatively charged particle will accelerate away from the sphere S due to the repulsive force. As it moves further away, the electric field strength decreases, so its acceleration will also decrease.

Recap

The motion of a charged particle in an electric field is governed by the electric force acting on it. If the force is constant, the acceleration is constant; if the force changes, the acceleration changes.

(b) Fig. 5.3 shows sphere S being spray painted. Sphere S is negatively charged. As the paint particles exit the wide nozzle of the paint sprayer, they become charged with a positive charge.

(i) Explain why the paint particles spread out when they leave the nozzle.

The paint particles spread out because they are all charged with a positive charge. Since like charges repel, the particles push each other away, causing them to spread out.

Recap

This principle is used in electrostatic spraying to achieve an even coating. The repulsion between charged particles helps create a fine mist and prevents clumping.

(ii) Suggest and explain one advantage to using charged paint from a spray gun to paint sphere S.

One advantage is that it provides a more even coat and less paint is wasted. This is because the positively charged paint particles are attracted to all parts of the negatively charged sphere, ensuring they spread out uniformly and adhere effectively.

Recap

The electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged objects ensures that the paint particles are drawn to the entire surface of the object, even areas not directly in the spray path, leading to a more efficient and uniform coating.

Question 6 | 0625/43/O/N/24 Q6 | Topic: Electricity and Magnetism

(a) A car windscreen is covered in condensation (small droplets of water). Thermal energy is used to remove the droplets of water. The thermal energy is provided by three resistors on the windscreen. Fig. 6.1 shows two possible circuits for the three resistors. The three resistors are identical.

(i) Describe two advantages of using Circuit B.

Two advantages of using Circuit B (parallel circuit) are: 1. If one resistor fails, the others will still work. 2. Each resistor receives the full voltage (12 V), resulting in higher power output for heating.

Recap

Parallel circuits offer redundancy and allow components to operate independently at the full supply voltage. In contrast, series circuits divide the voltage among components, and a break in one component breaks the entire circuit.

(ii) Describe, in terms of the water particles, the process by which the water droplets are removed from the car windscreen using the heater.

The heater transfers thermal energy to the water particles. These particles gain kinetic energy, causing the more energetic particles to escape from the surface of the water droplets (evaporation), thus removing the condensation.

Recap

Evaporation is a cooling process where liquid molecules with sufficient kinetic energy overcome intermolecular forces and transition into the gaseous phase. Heating increases the rate of evaporation.

(b) Fig. 6.2 shows a circuit containing two resistors, P and Q. The circuit is powered by a 12 V battery.

(i) Calculate the current in resistor Q.

Resistor Q is in parallel with the battery, so the voltage across it is 12 V. Using Ohm's Law, I = V/R = 12 V / 70 Ω = 0.17 A.

Recap

In a parallel circuit, the voltage across each branch is the same. Ohm's Law (V=IR) is fundamental for calculating current, voltage, or resistance in a circuit.

(ii) Calculate the energy transferred electrically when the current calculated in (b)(i) is present in resistor Q for 5 minutes.

First, convert time to seconds: 5 minutes × 60 s/minute = 300 s. Using the formula for electrical energy, E = VIt = 12 V × 0.17 A × 300 s = 612 J (or 610 J to 2 s.f.).

Recap

The electrical energy transferred is the product of voltage, current, and time. This energy is often converted into other forms, such as heat or light, by the component.

(iii) Energy is transferred from the battery by the electrical current. State the energy store in the battery.

The energy store in the battery is chemical energy.

Recap

Batteries store chemical energy, which is converted into electrical energy through chemical reactions. This process is an example of energy transformation.

(iv) Calculate the total resistance of the circuit.

For parallel resistors, 1/R_total = 1/R_P + 1/R_Q = 1/90 Ω + 1/70 Ω = (7 + 9) / 630 = 16 / 630. So, R_total = 630 / 16 = 39.375 Ω (or 39 Ω to 2 s.f.).

Recap

The formula for total resistance in a parallel circuit is 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + .... The total resistance in a parallel circuit is always less than the smallest individual resistance.

Question 7 | 0625/43/O/N/24 Q7 | Topic: Electricity and Magnetism

Fig. 7.1 shows a barrier at the entrance to a car park. The wooden barrier arm has a weight of 60 N which acts through the centre of gravity at the position shown on Fig. 7.1.

(a) Initially the wooden barrier arm is horizontal.

(i) Using Fig. 7.1, calculate the clockwise moment of the weight of the wooden arm about the pivot.

The clockwise moment is calculated as Force × Perpendicular Distance. From Fig. 7.1, the weight is 60 N and the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the center of gravity is 1.7 m. So, Moment = 60 N × 1.7 m = 102 Nm.

Recap

A moment is the turning effect of a force. It is a vector quantity and its direction (clockwise or anticlockwise) is important in equilibrium calculations.

(a) (ii) The wooden barrier arm is in equilibrium. The mass of the soft iron bar A is 23 kg. Calculate the distance d between the pivot and the joint holding the soft iron bar A.

For equilibrium, the total clockwise moment must equal the total anticlockwise moment. The weight of bar A is 23 kg × 9.8 N/kg = 225.4 N. So, 102 Nm = 225.4 N × d. Rearranging for d, d = 102 Nm / 225.4 N = 0.45 m.

Recap

The principle of moments states that for an object to be in rotational equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point must equal the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point.

(b) Fig. 7.2 shows a coil attached to a power supply placed below the soft iron bar A.

(i) State and explain what happens to the wooden barrier arm when the switch in the coil circuit is closed.

When the switch is closed, the coil produces a magnetic field, making the coil and its soft iron core an electromagnet. This electromagnet attracts the soft iron bar A downwards, causing the wooden barrier arm to rotate anticlockwise and move upwards.

Recap

An electromagnet is a temporary magnet created by passing an electric current through a coil of wire. The strength of the magnetic field can be controlled by varying the current.

(b) (ii) The switch is opened. An operator decreases the potential difference across the coil and the switch is closed. State and explain how the effect on the wooden barrier arm compares with the effect in (b)(i).

The barrier arm will move slower or go up less high. This is because decreasing the potential difference across the coil decreases the current, which in turn decreases the strength of the magnetic field and thus the attractive force on the soft iron bar.

Recap

The strength of an electromagnet is directly proportional to the current flowing through its coil. A weaker magnetic field results in a weaker attractive force.

(b) (iii) A student suggests that the soft iron bar A is replaced by a steel bar. Explain why a steel bar is less effective than a soft iron bar in the barrier.

A steel bar is less effective because steel is a hard magnetic material, meaning it would become permanently magnetised. This would cause the barrier arm to stay attracted to the electromagnet even when the switch is opened, preventing the barrier from returning to its original position.

Recap

Soft magnetic materials (like soft iron) are easily magnetized and demagnetized, making them suitable for electromagnets. Hard magnetic materials (like steel) retain their magnetism, making them suitable for permanent magnets.

Question 8 | 0625/43/O/N/24 Q8 | Topic: Atomic Physics

An isotope of boron is used in the treatment of cancer in the brain. Boron sticks to cancer cells in the brain.

(a) The isotope of boron is bombarded with neutrons then undergoes fission to form lithium and alpha-particles.

(i) Describe one difference between fission and fusion.

Fission is the splitting of a heavy nucleus into two or more lighter nuclei, while fusion is the joining of two or more light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus.

Recap

Both nuclear fission and nuclear fusion release enormous amounts of energy. Fission is used in nuclear power plants, while fusion powers the Sun and other stars.

(a) (ii) A nucleus of boron (B) contains 5 protons and 5 neutrons. Complete the nuclide equation for this fission reaction.

¹⁰₅B + ¹₀n → ⁷₃Li + ⁴₂α

The completed equation is: ¹⁰₅B + ¹₀n → ⁷₃Li + ⁴₂α. This shows that Boron (B) has 5 protons and 5 neutrons (nucleon number 10), Lithium (Li) has 3 protons and 4 neutrons (nucleon number 7), and the alpha particle (α) has 2 protons and 2 neutrons (nucleon number 4).

Recap

In a nuclear reaction, both the total mass number (sum of superscripts) and the total atomic number (sum of subscripts) must be conserved on both sides of the equation.

(b) The alpha-particles destroy the cancer cells. Suggest and explain one reason why alpha particles are more suitable than gamma radiation for use in this treatment of brain cancer.

Alpha particles are more suitable because they are highly ionising and have a shorter range (less penetrating) than gamma radiation. This means they will deposit all their energy within the cancer cells, effectively destroying them, but will not travel beyond the cancer cells to damage healthy brain tissue.

Recap

The ionising power and penetrating power of different types of radiation vary significantly. Alpha particles are highly ionising but have low penetrating power, making them suitable for targeted internal radiation therapy.

(c) Other cancers are treated with gamma radiation. Describe one safety precaution a nurse or radiologist takes during this treatment.

One safety precaution is to reduce exposure time to the radiation source. This can be achieved by working quickly or by using automated systems.

Recap

The three main principles of radiation protection are: time (minimize exposure time), distance (maximize distance from the source), and shielding (use appropriate barriers).

Question 10 | 0625/43/O/N/24 Q10 | Topic: Astrophysics

(a) Stars more massive than the Sun can eventually form black holes. Describe how a black hole can be formed from a more massive star.

For a star much more massive than the Sun, after it runs out of fuel, the hydrogen is converted to helium and heavier elements. The star expands to form a red supergiant. Eventually, it explodes as a supernova. If the remaining core is massive enough, it collapses under its own gravity to form a black hole.

Recap

The life cycle of a star is determined by its initial mass. Very massive stars end their lives in spectacular supernova explosions, leaving behind either a neutron star or a black hole.

(b) The star system V404 Cygni contains a black hole. The system is approximately 7800 light-years from the Earth.

(i) Describe what is meant by a light-year.

A light-year is the distance travelled by light in one year in the vacuum of space.

Recap

A light-year is a unit of distance, not time. It is used to measure vast distances in astronomy. One light-year is approximately 9.46 trillion kilometers.

(ii) Calculate the approximate distance from V404 Cygni to the Earth in km.

1 light-year ≈ 9.46 × 10¹² km. So, 7800 light-years × 9.46 × 10¹² km/light-year ≈ 7.38 × 10¹⁶ km (or 7.4 × 10¹⁶ km to 2 s.f.).

Recap

Converting between light-years and kilometers is essential for understanding the scale of the universe. These large numbers highlight the immense distances involved in astrophysics.
