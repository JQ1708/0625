Paper: 0625/42/O/N/22 Q1 | Topics: Density, Pressure, GPE

Fig. 1.1 shows sea water flowing down a channel into a tank without splashing. The water is flowing at a rate of 800 kg/min. The length and width of the tank are 3.10 m and 1.20 m. The density of the sea water is 1020 kg/m³.

(a) Initially, the tank is empty.

Calculate the depth of water in the tank after 1.00 minute. Give your answer to three significant figures.

depth = ...................................................... m [3]

First, calculate the volume (V) of water that flows into the tank in 1.00 minute using the formula ρ = m / V (density = mass / volume).

Rearranging for volume: V = m / ρ

V = 800 kg / 1020 kg/m³ = 0.7843 m³

Next, calculate the base area (A) of the tank:

A = length × width = 3.10 m × 1.20 m = 3.72 m²

Finally, calculate the depth (d) using the formula V = A × d.

Rearranging for depth: d = V / A

d = 0.7843 m³ / 3.72 m² = 0.2108 m

To three significant figures, the depth is 0.211 m.

Recap

Density is defined as the mass per unit volume of a material. The equation is ρ = m / V. The standard unit for density is kg/m³, though g/cm³ is also used (1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³).

(b) The height of the water decreases by 0.420 m as it flows down the channel.

Calculate the decrease in gravitational potential energy of the water each second.

decrease in gravitational potential energy = ...................................................... J [3]

First, find the mass (m) of water flowing per second. The rate is 800 kg/min.

m (per second) = 800 kg / 60 s = 13.33 kg/s

The formula for the change in gravitational potential energy (GPE) is ΔE_p = mgΔh, using the acceleration of free fall g = 10 m/s² (or 10 N/kg).

ΔE_p = (13.33 kg) × (10 N/kg) × (0.420 m)

ΔE_p = 56.0 J

The decrease in GPE each second is 56.0 J.

Recap

Gravitational potential energy (E_p) is the energy an object has due to its position in a gravitational field, e.g., energy gained when lifted away from the Earth. The equation for the *change* in GPE is ΔE_p = mgΔh, where m is mass, g is the gravitational field strength, and Δh is the change in height.

(c) The water stops flowing. The depth of water in the tank is 0.800 m.

Calculate the pressure at the bottom of the tank due to the water.

pressure = ...................................................... Pa [3]

The formula for the pressure (p) at a certain depth (h) in a liquid is p = hρg (or p = ρgΔh).

Given:

  • h = 0.800 m
  • ρ = 1020 kg/m³
  • g = 10 N/kg (or 10 m/s²)

p = 0.800 m × 1020 kg/m³ × 10 N/kg = 8160 Pa

The mark scheme answer is 8200 Pa (which may be due to rounding or a specific convention).

Recap

The pressure beneath a liquid surface increases directly proportionally with depth and density. The equation to calculate this pressure difference is Δp = ρgΔh. The unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa), which is equal to one newton per square metre (N/m²).

(a) A pendulum swings with a time period of approximately one second.

Describe how to use a stop-watch to determine the time period of the pendulum.

[3]

To determine the time period accurately, you should time multiple oscillations, for example 20 complete swings. Start the stop-watch as the pendulum bob passes a central, fixed point (a fiduciary aid) in one direction. Count 20 complete swings (e.g., from the centre, to the right, to the left, and back to the centre) and stop the watch when the bob passes the fiduciary aid for the 20th time.

The time period (T) is then calculated by dividing the total time recorded by the number of oscillations (20).

T = Total time / 20

Recap

A stopwatch or stopclock is used to measure time. Measuring multiple oscillations and then dividing is a standard technique to reduce human reaction time error, thereby increasing the accuracy of the measurement for a single time period.

Question 4

Paper: 0625/42/O/N/22 Q4 | Topics: Kinetic Theory, Gas Pressure, Boyle's Law

(a) Explain, in terms of the momentum of particles, how a gas exerts a pressure.

[3]

Gas particles are in constant, random motion and possess momentum. When these moving particles hit the walls of the container, they rebound. This collision causes a change in momentum for the particle. According to Newton's laws, this change in momentum exerts a force on the walls. Pressure is defined as this total force exerted per unit area of the container's walls.

Recap

Gas pressure is caused by gas particles moving and hitting the sides of the container. When they rebound, their momentum changes, which creates a force on the wall. Pressure is the total force on a specific area. This is evidence for the kinetic theory of matter.

(b) The temperature of a sample of gas is increased at constant volume.

State and explain any change in the pressure of the gas.

[2]

The pressure increases. When the temperature is increased, the gas particles absorb thermal energy and their average kinetic energy increases, so they move faster. This results in more frequent collisions with the walls and a greater change of momentum (a greater force) per collision. Since pressure is force per unit area, the total pressure increases.

Recap

As the temperature of a gas increases, the average kinetic energy of the particles increases, and so the average speed of the particles increases. If the volume is constant, this increases the pressure. Temperature in Kelvin (K) is directly proportional to the average kinetic energy. K = °C + 273.

Calculate the new pressure of the gas.

  • Initial volume (V₁): 170 cm³
  • Final volume (V₂): 170 cm³ - 70 cm³ = 100 cm³
  • Final pressure (p₂): ?

(9.0 × 10⁴ Pa) × (170 cm³) = p₂ × (100 cm³)

Recap

For a fixed mass of gas at a constant temperature, the pressure (p) is inversely proportional to the volume (V). Doubling the pressure halves the volume. This relationship is summarised by the equation pV = constant or p₁V₁ = p₂V₂.

Fig. 5.1 shows an aluminium block after leaving a furnace in a factory.

(a) The mass of the block is 1200 kg and it is heated in the furnace from 20 °C to 380 °C. The aluminium block does not melt.

The specific heat capacity of aluminium is 960 J/(kg °C).

Calculate the thermal energy gained by the block in the furnace.

thermal energy = ...................................................... J [3]

First, calculate the change in temperature (ΔT or Δθ):

ΔT = 380 °C - 20 °C = 360 °C

The formula for thermal energy gained is ΔE = mcΔT, where m is mass, c is specific heat capacity, and ΔT is the temperature change.

ΔE = (1200 kg) × (960 J/(kg °C)) × (360 °C)

ΔE = 414 720 000 J

This can be written as 4.1 × 10⁸ J or 410 MJ (megajoules), as in the mark scheme.

Recap

The specific heat capacity (c) of a material is the energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of that material by 1 °C. The equation for the energy transferred is E = mcΔθ (or mcΔT).

(b) Fig. 5.1 shows a factory worker standing 3 m from the block.

State and explain the main process by which thermal energy is transferred to the worker.

[3]

The main process is thermal radiation (or heat radiation).

The hot aluminium block emits thermal energy as infrared (IR) radiation, which is a type of electromagnetic wave. This radiation can travel through the air (even a vacuum) and is then absorbed by the worker, transferring thermal energy.

Recap

Infrared radiation is thermal radiation emitted by all objects. Unlike conduction or convection, it can travel through a vacuum. The colour and texture of a surface affect its ability to emit, absorb, and reflect radiation. Hot objects emit infrared radiation, which can be absorbed by other objects, causing them to heat up.

(c) State and explain the main process by which thermal energy is transferred from the outer surface of the solid metal rollers to their interior.

[3]

The main process is conduction.

Metals are good conductors because they have free, delocalised electrons. These electrons at the hot outer surface gain high kinetic energy and move (diffuse) through the metal lattice. They collide with the metal ions and other electrons, rapidly transferring this energy to the interior of the roller.

Recap

Thermal conduction in solids occurs through particle vibrations. In metals, conduction is much more efficient because free electrons gain kinetic energy and move through the metal, transferring energy via collisions with ions. Liquids and gases are generally poor conductors because their particles are not in a fixed lattice (liquids) or are very far apart (gases).

1. .......................................................................................................................................................

2. ....................................................................................................................................................... [2]

(b) Fig. 6.2 shows a ray of green light passing into, through and out of a glass prism.

A ray of blue light is incident on the prism on the same path as the incident ray of green light.

On Fig. 6.2, draw the path of the blue light through and out of the prism.

[3]

Blue light has a shorter wavelength and higher frequency than green light. In glass, blue light travels slower than green light, so it has a higher refractive index and is refracted (bent) more.

The path would be:

  1. On entering the prism, the blue ray refracts closer to the normal than the green ray did.
  2. On exiting the prism, the blue ray refracts away from the normal, but by a greater angle than the green ray.

The overall result is that the blue light ray is deviated (bent from its original path) more than the green light ray.

Recap

This effect is called dispersion. When white light passes through a prism, it splits into a spectrum (Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet). This happens because the refractive index of glass is slightly different for each colour. Violet light travels the slowest in glass and is deviated the most, while red light travels the fastest and is deviated the least.

Paper: 0625/42/O/N/22 Q8 | Topics: Electrical Circuits, LDR, Power

(a) Fig. 8.1 shows an electrical circuit.

The light intensity at the circuit increases from dark to bright.

State any effect on the resistance of component Y.

State and explain any effect on the reading of the voltmeter.

[3]

Effect on resistance of Y: Component Y is a Light-Dependent Resistor (LDR). When light intensity increases, its resistance decreases.

Effect on voltmeter reading: The voltmeter reading decreases.

Explanation: The circuit is a potential divider. The total voltage from the battery is shared between the fixed resistor and the LDR (Y). When the resistance of Y decreases (due to bright light), its share (or proportion) of the total voltage also decreases. Since the voltmeter is measuring the voltage across Y, its reading goes down.

Recap

A Light-Dependent Resistor (LDR) is a component whose resistance changes with light level. In the dark, it has high resistance. In bright light, its resistance is low. In a potential divider circuit, the total voltage (p.d.) is shared between components in series in direct proportion to their resistance (V₁/V₂ = R₁/R₂).

(b) The circuit shown in Fig. 8.2 is switched on for 2.0 min.

The current in the 4.0 Ω resistor is 3.0 A and the magnitude of the charge on an electron is 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

(i) Calculate the number of electrons that pass through the resistor each second.

number = ...................................................... [3]

So, total charge (Q) passing per second is 3.0 C.

To find the number of electrons (n), divide the total charge by the charge on one electron (e).

n = Total Charge / Charge per electron

n = 3.0 C / (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)

n = 1.875 × 10¹⁹

The number of electrons per second is 1.9 × 10¹⁹ (to 2 s.f.).

Recap

Electric current (I) is the rate of flow of charge (Q), given by the equation I = Q / t. The unit of current is the ampere (A), and the unit of charge is the coulomb (C). One ampere is one coulomb per second. The charge on a single electron is e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

power = ...................................................... W [2]

The formula for electrical power (P) is P = I × V, where I is current and V is voltage.

  • V = 12 V

Alternatively, using P = I²R: P = (3.0 A)² × 4.0 Ω = 9.0 × 4.0 = 36 W.

Recap

Electrical power (P) is the rate at which energy is transferred. It is measured in watts (W). It can be calculated using P = IV, P = I²R, or P = V²/R. These equations are derived from P = E / t, V = IR (Ohm's Law), and V = E / Q.

(b) A nucleus of radium-230 consists of 88 protons and 142 neutrons. Radium-230 is radioactive and decays by β (beta)-emission to an isotope of actinium. The symbol for radium is Ra and the symbol for actinium is Ac.

Write down the nuclide equation for this decay.

[3]

In beta-decay, a neutron in the nucleus turns into a proton (so the proton number increases by 1) and an electron (the beta-particle), which is emitted. The nucleon number (mass number) remains unchanged.

  • Radium (Ra): Proton number = 88, Nucleon number = 230
  • Actinium (Ac): Proton number = 88 + 1 = 89, Nucleon number = 230
  • Beta-particle (β): Proton number = -1, Nucleon number = 0

The equation is: ${}^{230}_{88}Ra \rightarrow {}^{230}_{89}Ac + {}^{0}_{-1}\beta$

Recap

In all nuclear decay equations, both the nucleon number (top) and the proton number (bottom) must be conserved (balanced) on both sides of the arrow.

  • Alpha decay: ${}^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A-4}_{Z-2}Y + {}^{4}_{2}\alpha$
  • Beta decay: ${}^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z+1}Y + {}^{0}_{-1}\beta$

(c) The half-life of radium-230 is 93 min. A sample contains 9.6 × 10⁻¹² g of radium-230.

Calculate the mass of radium in the sample after 279 min.

mass = ...................................................... g [2]

First, determine the number of half-lives that have passed.

Number of half-lives = Total time / Half-life

Number of half-lives = 279 min / 93 min = 3 half-lives

Now, reduce the initial mass by half for each half-life:

  • After 1 half-life (93 min): 9.6 × 10⁻¹² g / 2 = 4.8 × 10⁻¹² g
  • After 2 half-lives (186 min): 4.8 × 10⁻¹² g / 2 = 2.4 × 10⁻¹² g
  • After 3 half-lives (279 min): 2.4 × 10⁻¹² g / 2 = 1.2 × 10⁻¹² g

The final mass is 1.2 × 10⁻¹² g.

Recap

The half-life of a radioactive isotope is the time taken for half of the unstable nuclei in a sample to decay (or the time taken for the activity to halve). This process is random and is not affected by temperature or pressure.
