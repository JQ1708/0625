Paper: 0625/43/M/J/23 Q1 | Topics: Forces, Mass & Weight, Motion

Fig. 1.1 shows a balloon filled with helium gas. The mass of the balloon is 120kg.

(a)

Calculate the weight of the balloon. Show your working. [1]

The relationship between weight, mass, and gravitational field strength (g) is W = mg. The front of the exam paper states to take g = 9.8 N/kg.

W = 120 kg × 9.8 N/kg

W = 1176 N

(Note: The mark scheme shows 1200 N, which implies using g = 10 N/kg is also acceptable, but 9.8 N/kg is specified on the paper.)

Recap

Mass and Weight (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 21)

Mass is a quantity related to the total number of particles in an object. It is measured in kg.

Weight is the force, in N, on a mass due to a gravitational field. It is measured in newtons (N).

The equation is: weight (N) = mass (kg) × gravitational field strength (N/kg), or W = m × g.

(b)

The resultant force on the balloon is 54 N. Show that the acceleration of the balloon is 0.45 m/s2. [2]

Newton's second law states that resultant force (F) is equal to mass (m) times acceleration (a), or F = ma.

This can be rearranged to find acceleration: a = F⁄m.

Substituting the given values:

a = 54 N⁄120 kg

a = 0.45 m/s2

Recap

Force and Acceleration (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 25)

A resultant force can speed up (accelerate), slow down (decelerate), or change the direction of an object.

The relationship between force, mass, and acceleration is given by the equation: Force (N) = mass (kg) × acceleration (m/s2), or F = m × a.

(c)

The balloon accelerates upwards from rest at 0.45 m/s2 for 8.0s. Calculate the velocity of the balloon after 8.0s. [2]

Acceleration is the change in velocity (Δv) per unit time (t), or a = Δv⁄t.

To find the change in velocity, we rearrange the formula: Δv = a × t.

Since the balloon starts from rest, its initial velocity is 0, so the change in velocity is its final velocity.

Δv = 0.45 m/s2 × 8.0 s

Δv = 3.6 m/s

Recap

Calculating Acceleration (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 13)

Acceleration is a vector quantity. It is equal to the change in velocity per second.

The equation is: acceleration (m/s2) = change in velocity (m/s)⁄time (s), or a = Δv⁄t.

This can be rearranged to v = u + at, where v is final velocity, u is initial velocity (0 in this case), a is acceleration, and t is time.

(d)

Calculate the distance travelled by the balloon in the first 8.0 s. [2]

For an object accelerating uniformly from rest, the distance travelled can be found using the average speed.

Average speed = (Initial velocity + Final velocity)⁄2 = (0 m/s + 3.6 m/s)⁄2 = 1.8 m/s.

Distance = Average speed × time

Distance = 1.8 m/s × 8.0 s

Distance = 14.4 m

(Alternatively, using s = ut + 1⁄2at2: s = (0 × 8.0) + 1⁄2(0.45)(8.0)2 = 0 + 0.225 × 64 = 14.4 m. The mark scheme allows 14m.)

Recap

Motion Graphs (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 12)

On a speed-time graph, the distance travelled can be calculated from the area under the graph.

For this motion, the graph is a triangle, so the area is 1⁄2 × base × height.

Area = 1⁄2 × time × final velocity = 1⁄2 × 8.0 s × 3.6 m/s = 14.4 m.

(a) (i)

Define pressure. [1]

Pressure is defined as the force acting per unit area.

Recap

Pressure, Force, and Area (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 53)

The pressure on a surface due to a force is equal to the force on 1 m2 of the surface.

The equation is: pressure (N/m2) = force (N)⁄area (m2), or p = F⁄A.

The unit of pressure, N/m2, is also known as the pascal (Pa).

(a) (ii)

Describe how pressure in a liquid varies with its depth and with its density. [2]

Variation with depth: The pressure in a liquid increases as the depth increases.

Variation with density: The pressure in a liquid increases as the density of the liquid increases.

Recap

Pressure in a Liquid (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 54)

The pressure below the surface of all liquids increases directly proportionally with depth and directly proportionally with density. Increasing the depth and the density of a liquid increases the weight of liquid above, and hence increases the pressure.

The equation for the pressure change is: Δp = ρgΔh (where ρ is density, g is gravitational field strength, and Δh is change in depth).

(b)

State two energy resources for which the Sun is not the main source. [2]

Two such energy resources are:

  1. Geothermal energy
  2. Nuclear energy

(Tidal energy is also a correct answer).

Recap

The Sun as the Source (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 47)

The Sun is the source of energy for all energy resources *except* geothermal, nuclear, and tidal.

  • The source of tidal energy is the gravitational attraction between the Moon and the Earth's oceans.
  • The source of nuclear energy is stored in the nuclei of elements (e.g., uranium).
  • Geothermal energy has its origins in radioactive elements, which release heat energy when they decay in the Earth's crust.

(a) (i)

State which state of matter, solid, liquid or gas, has the greatest thermal expansion and which has the least. [2]

Greatest expansion: gas

Least expansion: solid

Recap

Thermal Expansion (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 63-64)

When heated, solids expand only a little. Liquids expand more than solids. Gases expand more than liquids (i.e., the most).

This is because the expansion depends on the space between molecules and their motion. In solids, particles are held in fixed positions and expand the least. In gases, particles have the largest separation and move fastest, so they expand the most.

(a) (ii)

Describe, in terms of the motion and arrangement of particles, the structures of solids and gases. [3]

Solids:

  • Arrangement: Particles are arranged in a regular repeating pattern (lattice) and are close together in fixed positions.
  • Motion: Particles vibrate about their fixed positions.

Gases:

  • Arrangement: Particles are randomly arranged and are very far apart (have a wide separation).
  • Motion: Particles move freely, quickly, and randomly in all directions.

Recap

Solids, Liquids, and Gases (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 55)

Solid: Particles are arranged in a regular repeating pattern. Particles are close together, vibrating in fixed positions.

Gas: Particles are arranged randomly. Particles are very far apart compared with the size of the particles themselves, and are moving randomly in all directions.

Forces between particles are strong in solids, weak in liquids, and negligible (practically zero) in gases.

(b) (i)

Define specific heat capacity. [2]

Specific heat capacity is the energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg (or unit mass) of a substance by 1°C (or 1 Kelvin / unit temperature).

Recap

Specific Heat Capacity (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 65)

The specific heat capacity (c) of a material is the energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of the material by 1°C.

The equation is: Energy transferred (J) = mass (kg) × specific heat capacity (J/kg°C) × temperature change (°C), or E = mcΔθ.

(b) (ii)

  2. The initial temperature and the final temperature of the metal (e.g., using a thermometer) to calculate the temperature change.

Experiment (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 66)

The experiment to measure specific heat capacity (shown for a liquid, but the principle is the same for a solid block) requires measurements to be used in the equation E = mcΔθ.

  • m (mass): The mass of the substance must be measured.
  • Δθ (temp. change): The initial temperature and final temperature are measured with a thermometer.
  • E (energy): For an electrical heater, E = P × t. Since power (P) is known (12W), the student must measure the time (t) with a stopwatch.

Recap

Electrical Quantities (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 135-137)

There are several equations for electrical power (P), which is the energy transferred per second.

  • P = V × I (Power = Voltage × Current)
  • Using Ohm's Law (V = IR), we can substitute for V to get: P = (IR) × I = P = I2R

You must choose the correct equation based on the quantities given (Power and Resistance in this case).

(b)

The resistance wire of the heater has a length of 1.2m and a cross-sectional area of 7.9 × 10-7 m2. A new heater is designed using wire of the same material with length 1.8m and cross-sectional area 5.8 × 10-7 m2. Calculate the resistance of this new wire. [3]

Resistance (R) is directly proportional to length (l) (R ∝ l) and inversely proportional to cross-sectional area (A) (R ∝ 1⁄A). The constant of proportionality is resistivity (ρ), which is constant as it's the "same material".

We can use ratios: Rnew⁄Rold = (lnew⁄lold) × (Aold⁄Anew)

Rnew⁄26 Ω = (1.8 m⁄1.2 m) × (7.9 × 10-7 m2⁄5.8 × 10-7 m2)

Rnew⁄26 Ω = (1.5) × (1.362)

Rnew⁄26 Ω = 2.043

Rnew = 26 Ω × 2.043 = 53.118 Ω

Rnew ≈ 53 Ω

Recap

Resistance (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 138-139)

The resistance of a wire depends on several factors:

  • Length (l): Resistance is directly proportional to length. (Longer wire = more resistance).
  • Cross-sectional area (A): Resistance is inversely proportional to area. (Thicker wire = less resistance).
  • Material: Different materials have different resistivity (e.g., copper has low resistivity).
  • Temperature: Resistance in metals usually increases with temperature.

Energy (kWh) = 2.5 kW × 48 h = 120 kWh.

(a) (i)

During β-decay, one of the neutrons in the nucleus changes. (i) State what happens to this neutron. [1]

The neutron becomes a proton and an electron (the β-particle).

Recap

Beta (β) particle (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 182)

A beta particle is a high-speed electron. It is emitted from the nucleus of an atom when a neutron decays into a proton and an electron.

This process increases the proton number (Atomic Number) by 1, but the nucleon number (Mass Number) stays the same.

(a) (ii)

(ii) Explain how charge is conserved during this change. [2]

Charge is conserved because the initial particle (the neutron) has charge = 0.

The products are a proton (charge = +1) and an electron (charge = -1).

The total charge of the products is (+1) + (-1) = 0. Therefore, the total charge before (0) and after (0) is the same.

Recap

Conservation Laws (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 187)

In all nuclear equations, both mass number (nucleon number) and proton number (atomic number) must be conserved (i.e., the total must be the same on both sides of the equation).

Conserving the proton number is the same as conserving charge. A neutron (10n) decays into a proton (11p) and an electron (0-1e).

Charge: 0 → (+1) + (-1) = 0. (Conserved)

Mass number: 1 → 1 + 0 = 1. (Conserved)

(b)

Complete the nuclide equation for the α-decay of radon-212 to form an isotope of polonium, symbol Po. [3]
   21286Rn →

The completed equation is:

21286Rn → 20884Po + 42α

This is because an alpha particle (42α or 42He) has 2 protons and 2 neutrons. The mass number (top) must balance (212 = 208 + 4) and the proton number (bottom) must balance (86 = 84 + 2).

Recap

Alpha (α) particle (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 182 & 187)

An alpha particle consists of two protons and two neutrons; it is identical to a helium nucleus. It has a charge of +2.

Volume in m3 = (1.4 × 1015) × (109 m3) = 1.4 × 1024 m3

From the density equation ρ = m⁄V, we get m = ρ × V.

m = 1300 kg/m3 × (1.4 × 1024 m3)

m = 1820 × 1024 kg

m = 1.8 × 1027 kg (or 1.82 × 1027 kg)

Recap

Density (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 51)

Density is a quantity related to how closely packed the particles in a material are, as well as the mass of the particles.

The equation is density (kg/m3) = mass (kg)⁄volume (m3), or ρ = m⁄V.

It is crucial to ensure all units are consistent (e.g., kg/m3 and m3) before multiplying.

(a)

Show that 1 light-year $\approx$ 9.5 × 1015 m. [4]

A light-year is the distance light travels in one year.

1. We use the equation distance = speed × time (or s = vt).

2. The speed of light (c) in a vacuum is 3.0 × 108 m/s.

3. We need the time for one year in seconds: Time = 365 days × 24 hours⁄day × 3600 seconds⁄hour Time = 31,536,000 s (or $\approx$ 3.15 × 107 s)

4. Distance = (3.0 × 108 m/s) × (31,536,000 s) Distance = 9,460,800,000,000,000 m Distance $\approx$ 9.5 × 1015 m

Recap

Light-Year (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 202)

A light-year is a unit of distance, not time. It is the distance that light travels in one year.

The speed of light in a vacuum is 3.0 × 108 m/s. To find the distance, you multiply this speed by the number of seconds in a year (365.25 × 24 × 60 × 60).

(b) (i)

State one measurement that is taken when determining the speed v at which a galaxy is moving away from the Earth. [1]

The change of wavelength (or redshift) of the light from the galaxy.

Recap

Red-Shift (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 204)

Red-shift is the observed increase in the wavelength of light from distant galaxies. This is evidence that the galaxies are moving away from us (the Doppler effect for light).

By measuring the wavelength of light received and comparing it to the known wavelength of that light source, scientists can calculate the red-shift and thus the speed of recession.

(b) (ii)

Write down an equation relating v and the distance d of a far galaxy. [1]

v = H0d (Hubble's Law)

Recap

Hubble's Law (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 204)

Observations show that the recessional speed (v) of a galaxy is directly proportional to its distance (d) from us.

This relationship is called Hubble's Law and is written as v = H0d, where H0 is the Hubble constant.

(b) (iii)

State how the distance d of a far galaxy can be determined other than by using the equation in (ii). [1]

By measuring the brightness of a supernova (of known luminosity, or a 'standard candle') within that galaxy.

Recap

Measuring Distance (Ref: PHYSICSexamsuccess.pdf p. 203)

To measure the distance to far galaxies, astronomers look for a 'standard candle' - an object of known, consistent brightness.

Type 1a supernovae are used as standard candles. Because their peak brightness (luminosity) is known, astronomers can measure how bright they *appear* from Earth. The dimmer they appear, the further away they must be. This allows for the calculation of distance (d).
