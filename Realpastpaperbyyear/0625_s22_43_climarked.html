Question 1

Topic: Mechanics, Electricity

A battery provides energy to an electric car.

(a) The electric car has an acceleration of 2.9 m/s2 when it moves from rest. The combined mass of the car and its driver is 1600 kg.

(i) Calculate the time taken to reach a speed of 28 m/s. [2]

The time taken is calculated using the formula for acceleration, a = Δv⁄t.

Rearranging for time (t), the formula becomes t = Δv⁄a.

Given the change in velocity (Δv) is 28 m/s (from 0 to 28) and the acceleration (a) is 2.9 m/s2:

t = 28 m/s⁄2.9 m/s2 = 9.7 s (rounded to 2 significant figures).

Recap

Acceleration is the increase in velocity per second. It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both size and direction. The standard equation is: a = (v - u)⁄t, where v is the final velocity, u is the initial velocity, and t is the time taken.

(ii) Calculate the force required to produce this acceleration. [2]

Kinetic energy (KE) is calculated using the formula Ek = ½ mv2.

Given the mass (m) is 1600 kg and the velocity (v) is 28 m/s:

Ek = 0.5 x 1600 kg x (28 m/s)2 = 0.5 x 1600 x 784 = 627200 J.

Rounded to two significant figures, this is 630 000 J (or 6.3 x 105 J).

Recap

Kinetic energy is the energy an object has due to its movement. The amount of kinetic energy depends on both the mass of the object and its velocity (speed). Objects with greater mass or higher velocity have more kinetic energy.

(b) The time taken for the car battery to be recharged from zero charge to full charge is 8.3 h. The charge is delivered to the battery by a charger with a current of 32 A.

Calculate the charge supplied by the charger. [3]

First, convert the time from hours into seconds, as the standard unit for this calculation is seconds:

t = 8.3 h x 60 min⁄h x 60 s⁄min = 29880 s.

Next, use the formula relating charge (Q), current (I), and time (t): Q = It.

Q = 32 A x 29880 s = 956160 C.

Rounding to two significant figures (to match 8.3 h and 32 A), the total charge is 960 000 C (or 9.6 x 105 C).

Recap

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge. The relationship is given by the equation I = Q⁄t, where I is the current in amperes (A), Q is the charge in coulombs (C), and t is the time in seconds (s). This can be rearranged to find the charge: Q = I x t.

(c) Under ideal conditions, the car can travel a maximum distance of 390 km when the battery is fully charged.

Suggest why, in normal use, the car needs to be recharged after travelling less than 390 km. [1]

Ideal conditions assume a constant, efficient speed with no energy losses. In normal use, extra energy is required for factors such as:

  • Repeated acceleration and deceleration (using brakes)
  • Motion uphill
  • Overcoming a headwind (increased air resistance)
  • Use of accessories like air conditioning or heaters

Any of these factors will use more energy and reduce the car's range.

Recap

In any real-world process, the useful energy output is less than the total energy input. This is because some energy is always dissipated to the surroundings, often as thermal energy (heat) due to forces like friction and air resistance. Driving in "normal use" involves many more of these dissipative forces than driving in "ideal conditions".

Question 2

Topic: Energy Resources, Efficiency

Water is held behind a dam in a hydroelectric power scheme.

(a) State the main form of energy stored in the water behind the dam. [1]

The main form of energy stored is gravitational potential energy.

Recap

Energy can be stored in different ways. Gravitational potential energy is the energy an object has stored due to its position in a gravitational field. When an object (like a book or water) is lifted to a height, it gains this form of energy.

(b) The water is released from the dam and falls a vertical height of 410 m at a rate of 480 kg/s.

(i) Calculate the rate at which energy is transferred by the falling water. [3]

The "rate at which energy is transferred" is Power.

The energy being transferred is Gravitational Potential Energy (GPE), calculated as ΔEp = mgΔh.

Power = Energy⁄time, so Power = (mgΔh)⁄t. This can be rewritten as (m⁄t) x g x Δh.

We are given the rate of mass flow (m⁄t) = 480 kg⁄s, g = 10 N/kg, and Δh = 410 m.

Power = 480 kg⁄s x 10 N/kg x 410 m = 1,968,000 W.

This is 2.0 x 106 J⁄s (or 2.0 MW).

Recap

The equation for calculating gravitational potential energy is Ep = mgΔh, where m is mass, g is the gravitational field strength, and Δh is the change in height. Power is the rate of energy transfer, measured in watts (W), where 1 W = 1 joule⁄second (J⁄s).

(ii) The power scheme supplies a current of 270 A at a voltage of 6000 V. Calculate the efficiency of the power scheme. [3]

First, calculate the useful electrical power output using the formula P = VI.

Useful Power Output = 6000 V x 270 A = 1,620,000 W.

Next, use the total power input from part (b)(i), which was 1,968,000 W.

Efficiency is calculated using the formula:

Efficiency = (Useful Power Output⁄Total Power Input) x 100%

Efficiency = (1,620,000 W⁄1,968,000 W) x 100 = 82.3... %

The efficiency is 82%.

Recap

The efficiency of any device is a measure of how much of the input energy (or power) is converted into useful output energy (or power). It is often expressed as a percentage. No device is 100% efficient due to the principle of conservation of energy, as some energy is always dissipated (lost) to the surroundings, usually as heat.

(c) Hydroelectric energy is a renewable form of energy.

(i) State one disadvantage of hydroelectric power schemes. [1]

One disadvantage is the damage to habitats caused by flooding a large area to create the reservoir. Other disadvantages include the high construction cost, the risk of flooding if the dam bursts, or reliance on weather (risk of droughts).

Recap

While hydroelectric power is renewable and doesn't produce carbon dioxide during operation, it has significant environmental and social disadvantages. Building the reservoir requires flooding vast areas of land, which destroys farmland and natural habitats, and can displace people's homes.

(ii) State one other renewable source of energy. [1]

One other renewable source of energy is wind power. (Others include solar, tidal, wave, or geothermal).

Recap

An energy resource is renewable if it will not run out (or can be replenished in a short timescale). Examples include solar, wind, wave, tidal, and geothermal. Non-renewable resources, like fossil fuels, will eventually run out.

The resultant force is the vector drawn from the start of the first arrow to the end of the second. When measured, this resultant vector is found to have a magnitude of 130 N (approx) and is directed vertically upwards.

Recap

A vector quantity is a physical quantity that has both size (magnitude) and direction. A scalar quantity has size only. Force is a vector. To find the resultant (or net) effect of two or more vectors, they can be added graphically using a scale diagram by drawing them 'nose to tail'.

Recap

Mass is a measure of the amount of 'stuff' in an object, measured in kg. Weight is the force of gravity acting on that mass, measured in newtons (N). The relationship between them is Weight (N) = mass (kg) x gravitational field strength (N⁄kg) (W = mg). Near the Earth's surface, g is 10 N⁄kg.

Question 4

Topic: Kinetic Theory, Thermal Physics

(a) Fig. 4.1 shows apparatus used to observe the motion of smoke particles (Brownian motion).

The glass cell has light shining on it from the side. The smoke particles are seen as bright specks of light when looking through the microscope.

(i) Draw the path of one of the bright specks of light. [2]

The path drawn should show a zig-zag motion. The line should consist of short, straight paths of random length, with random changes of direction.

Recap

The random motion of smoke particles (or pollen grains in water) is known as Brownian motion. This motion provides evidence for the kinetic theory of matter, which states that fluids are made of tiny, fast-moving particles (atoms or molecules).

(ii) Explain, in terms of forces and the motion of air molecules, the cause of the motion of the smoke particles. [4]

The large, visible smoke particles are continuously bombarded from all sides by invisible air molecules.

These air molecules are small, fast-moving, and travel in random directions.

At any single moment, the smoke particle receives more hits (collisions) on one side than another. This causes an unbalanced force on the smoke particle, forcing it to move. This process repeats, causing the random, zig-zag path.

Recap

This explanation for Brownian motion is a key piece of evidence for the kinetic theory. It demonstrates that even massive particles (like smoke) can be moved by light, fast-moving molecules (like air) if the collisions from these molecules are unbalanced.

(b) The temperature of the air in a sealed glass container is increased.

(i) Explain, in terms of molecules, why the internal energy of the air increases. [1]

When the air is heated, the molecules absorb energy. This causes them to move faster, increasing their average kinetic energy. The internal energy is the total sum of the kinetic (and potential) energies of the molecules, so if the average kinetic energy increases, the internal energy increases.

Recap

The internal energy of a substance is the sum of the potential and kinetic energies of all its particles. Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles. Therefore, increasing the temperature of an object increases the average kinetic energy of its particles, which in turn increases its internal energy.

(ii) Explain, in terms of molecules, why the pressure of the air also increases. [2]

Increasing the temperature makes the air molecules move faster (higher kinetic energy). This has two effects:

  1. The molecules hit the walls of the container more frequently (more often).
  2. Each collision with the wall is harder (a bigger change in momentum).

Both of these factors increase the average force on the walls, and since pressure is force per unit area, the pressure increases.

Recap

Pressure in a gas is caused by the force of its particles colliding and rebounding from the walls of the container. If the temperature of a gas in a fixed volume increases, the particles' speed increases, leading to more frequent and more forceful collisions. This results in an increase in pressure.

Question 5

Topic: Thermal Physics, Changes of State

(a) Define specific heat capacity. [2]

Specific heat capacity is the energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg (or unit mass) of a substance by 1 °C (or unit temperature).

Recap

The specific heat capacity (c) of a material is the energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of the material by 1 °C. For example, water has a high specific heat capacity of 4200 J⁄(kg°C), meaning it takes 4200 J of energy to heat 1 kg of water by 1 °C.

(b) A bowl contains 500 cm3 of water at a temperature of 5.0 °C. The bowl of water is placed in a freezer... it contains ice at a temperature of -18.0 °C. The density of water is 1000 kg/m3.

(i) Calculate the mass of water in the bowl when it is placed in the freezer. [2]

First, convert the volume from cm3 to the standard unit of m3.

1 m3 = 100 cm x 100 cm x 100 cm = 1,000,000 cm3.

So, V = 500⁄1,000,000 m3 = 0.0005 m3.

Now, use the density formula ρ = m⁄V, rearranged to m = ρ x V.

m = 1000 kg⁄m3 x 0.0005 m3 = 0.50 kg.

Recap

Density is a measure of how closely packed the particles in a material are. It is defined as mass⁄unit volume, with the equation ρ = m⁄V. Standard units are mass in kilograms (kg) and volume in cubic metres (m3), so density is in kg⁄m3.

(ii) The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J⁄(kg°C). The specific heat capacity of ice is 2100 J⁄(kg°C). The specific latent heat of fusion of water is 3.3 x 105 J⁄kg. Calculate the energy given out as the water cools from 5.0 °C to ice at -18.0 °C. [5]

This is a three-stage calculation. The total energy given out is the sum of the energy from these three stages:

Stage 1: Cool water (5.0 °C to 0 °C)

Use ΔE = mcΔθ (for water). Δθ = 5.0 °C.

ΔE1 = 0.50 kg x 4200 J⁄(kg°C) x 5.0 °C = 10500 J.

Stage 2: Freeze water (change state at 0 °C)

Use ΔE = mL (latent heat of fusion).

ΔE2 = 0.50 kg x 3.3 x 105 J⁄kg = 165000 J.

Stage 3: Cool ice (0 °C to -18.0 °C)

Use ΔE = mcΔθ (for ice). Δθ = 18.0 °C.

ΔE3 = 0.50 kg x 2100 J⁄(kg°C) x 18.0 °C = 18900 J.

Total Energy:

Total ΔE = ΔE1 + ΔE2 + ΔE3 = 10500 + 165000 + 18900 = 194400 J.

Rounded to two significant figures, this is 190 000 J (or 1.9 x 105 J).

Recap

When calculating thermal energy changes, you must consider two types of energy:
1. Specific Heat Capacity (c): The energy needed to change the temperature. Use ΔE = mcΔθ.
2. Specific Latent Heat (L): The energy needed to change the state (e.g., melt or boil) at a constant temperature. Use ΔE = mL.

Topic: Waves (Diffraction, Refraction, Sound)

(a) (i) Fig. 6.1 shows crests of a plane water wave approaching a barrier with a gap.

On Fig. 6.1, draw three crests of the water wave to the right of the barrier. [2]

The waves will spread out as they pass through the gap. This effect is called diffraction.

The drawing should show semicircular wavefronts spreading out from the gap.

The wavelength (distance between the crests) should remain the same as the wavelength of the incident waves.

Recap

Refraction is the change in direction of a wave as it passes from one medium to another, caused by a change in speed. When wave speed decreases, the wavelength also decreases (since v = fλ and frequency f is constant), and the wave bends towards the normal.

(b) State two ways in which transverse waves differ from longitudinal waves. [2]

A typical value is 1500 m⁄s. (The mark scheme allows any value between 1000 m⁄s and 2000 m⁄s).

Recap

Sound travels at different speeds in different media. It generally travels fastest in solids, slower in liquids, and slowest in gases. For comparison, the speed of sound in air is approximately 330 m⁄s, while in water it is much faster, around 1500 m⁄s.

(c) (ii) Explain why sound travels faster in water than in air. [1]

Sound is a longitudinal wave that travels as a vibration. In water (a liquid), the molecules are much closer together than they are in air (a gas). This allows the vibrations to be passed from one molecule to the next much more quickly and efficiently, resulting in a faster speed.

Recap

The speed of sound depends on the density and compressibility of the medium. In liquids and solids, particles are close together, allowing for rapid transfer of vibrations. In gases, particles are far apart and must travel a greater distance to collide and pass on the vibration, making sound travel slower.

On Fig. 7.1, draw a reflected ray and an incident ray to show the position of the point P. [2]

To find the point P (closest point to A that can be seen), you must use the law of reflection.

The law of reflection states that when a light ray reflects off a plane (flat) surface, the angle of incidence (i) is equal to the angle of reflection (r). Both angles are measured from the normal, which is an imaginary line drawn perpendicular (at 90°) to the surface at the point of incidence.

(b) State two properties of the image formed by the mirror. [2]

Any two from:

  • Virtual (cannot be formed on a screen)
  • Upright (the same way up as the object)
  • Same size as the object

An image in a plane mirror is always virtual, upright, laterally inverted, and the same size as the object. The image also appears to be the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front of it.

Question 8

Topic: Electrical Circuits

(a) Fig. 8.1 shows a circuit.

(i) State the name of component X. [1]

Light-Dependent Resistor (or LDR).

Recap

A Light-Dependent Resistor (LDR) is a component whose resistance changes with light intensity. In bright light, its resistance is low. In dark conditions, its resistance becomes very high.

(ii) The potential difference (p.d.) across component Y is measured with a voltmeter. On Fig. 8.1, draw the symbol for the voltmeter and its connections to the circuit. [1]

The voltmeter symbol (a circle with a 'V' inside) should be drawn in parallel with component Y. This means the voltmeter is connected on either side of component Y.

Recap

In circuits, a voltmeter is always connected in parallel with the component it is measuring. This is because it measures the potential difference (voltage) *across* the component. An ammeter, by contrast, is connected in series to measure the current *through* a component.

(iii) The electromotive force (e.m.f.) of the battery is 12 V. Component Y has a resistance of 400 Ω. In a brightly lit room, the resistance of component X is 350 Ω.

1. Calculate the current in the circuit. [2]

Components X and Y are in a series circuit. The total resistance (Rtotal) is the sum of their individual resistances:

Rtotal = RX + RY = 350 Ω + 400 Ω = 750 Ω.

Using Ohm's Law, V = IR, rearranged to I = V⁄R:

I = 12 V⁄750 Ω = 0.016 A.

Recap

For components in a series circuit, the total resistance is found by adding the individual resistances (Rtotal = R1 + R2 + ...). The current is the same through all components in series.

2. Calculate the p.d. across component Y. [1]

Using Ohm's Law (V = IR) for component Y only.

The current (I) through the whole circuit is 0.016 A (from part 1), and this is the current flowing through Y.

VY = I x RY = 0.016 A x 400 Ω = 6.4 V.

Recap

Ohm's Law, V = IR, can be applied to the entire circuit (using total voltage and total resistance) or to a single component (using the voltage across that component and its individual resistance). In a series circuit, the total voltage is shared between the components.

(iv) In a dark room, the resistance of component X is very large. State the effect this will have on the p.d. across component Y. [1]

In a dark room, the resistance of X (the LDR) increases significantly. This increases the total resistance of the circuit, so the overall circuit current (I) decreases.

The p.d. across Y is VY = I x RY. Since RY is constant (400 Ω) and I has decreased, the p.d. across component Y will decrease.

Recap

This circuit is a potential divider. The total voltage (12 V) is shared between X and Y in proportion to their resistance. In the dark, RX becomes very large, so it takes a larger *share* of the voltage, leaving a smaller share for Y. Therefore, the p.d. across Y decreases.

(b) Suggest a practical use for component X. [1]

A practical use for an LDR is in a circuit that switches on street lights automatically when it gets dark, or a security light.

Recap

An LDR is a sensor. Its property of changing resistance with light level makes it ideal for use in light-sensing circuits, such as automatic night lights, camera light meters, or security systems.

Question 9

Topic: Electromagnetic Induction, Transformers

(a) Fig. 9.1 shows a magnet on the end of a spring and a coil of wire connected to a sensitive centre-zero galvanometer. The magnet can move freely through the coil.

(i) The magnet is pulled down and released. Describe and explain what happens to the needle of the sensitive galvanometer. [4]

Description: The galvanometer needle oscillates (deflects back and forth) about the zero position.

Explanation: As the magnet moves up and down, its magnetic field lines cut through the wires of the coil. This changing magnetic field (or 'flux') induces a voltage (e.m.f.) in the coil. Because the coil is part of a complete circuit, this induced voltage drives an induced current, which the galvanometer detects. The direction of the current (and thus the deflection) opposes the motion of the magnet (Lenz's Law), so it deflects one way as the magnet moves down and the other way as it moves up.

Recap

This process is called electromagnetic induction. A voltage is induced in a conductor (like a coil) whenever it is exposed to a changing magnetic field. This can be achieved by moving a magnet near the coil, moving the coil near a magnet, or changing the strength of a nearby magnetic field. If the conductor is part of a closed circuit, an induced current will flow.

(ii) The magnet is replaced with a stronger magnet. State the effect of using a stronger magnet on what happens to the needle of the galvanometer. [1]

The needle will show a larger (maximum) deflection. The oscillation will be greater.

Recap

The size of the induced voltage depends on the rate of change of the magnetic field. This can be increased by:
1. Using a stronger magnet.
2. Moving the magnet faster.
3. Using a coil with more turns.

(b) A step-up transformer is used to step up the output voltage of a power station from 25 000 V to 400 000 V for transmission along power lines. The number of turns on the secondary coil is 36 000.

Calculate the number of turns on the primary coil. [2]

For an ideal transformer, the ratio of voltages is equal to the ratio of the number of turns on the coils:

Vp⁄Vs = Np⁄Ns

We are given:
Vp (primary voltage) = 25 000 V
Vs (secondary voltage) = 400 000 V
Ns (secondary turns) = 36 000

Rearranging the formula to find Np (primary turns):

Np = Ns x (Vp⁄Vs)

Np = 36 000 x (25 000⁄400 000) = 36 000 x (25⁄400) = 36 000 x 0.0625

Np = 2250 turns.

Recap

A transformer changes an alternating voltage. It consists of two coils, a primary and secondary, wound on a soft iron core. The turns ratio determines the voltage change: Vp⁄Vs = Np⁄Ns. A step-up transformer increases the voltage (Ns > Np), while a step-down transformer decreases it.

Question 10

Topic: Radioactivity, Half-life

A student places a sample of an isotope of protactinium (Pa-234) near a radiation detector. The readings on the detector, taken every 20 s, are recorded in Table 10.1.

Fig. 10.1 shows a graph of the count rate due to this sample against time.

(a) Explain why the readings in Table 10.1 are not the same as those plotted on the graph. [2]

The readings in Table 10.1 are the *total* count rate measured by the detector. This total includes the radiation from the sample *and* the background radiation that is always present.

The graph plots the *corrected* count rate, which is the count rate from the sample only. The graph values are obtained by removing (subtracting) the background radiation count from the total readings in the table.

Recap

Background radiation is low-level ionising radiation that is all around us. It comes from natural sources (like radon gas from rocks, cosmic rays) and man-made sources (like medical fall-out). When measuring a radioactive sample, this background count must be measured and subtracted from the total to find the true count rate of the sample.

(b) Using the graph in Fig. 10.1, determine the half-life of this isotope of protactinium. [2]

The half-life is the time taken for the count rate (or activity) of a sample to decrease to half its initial value.

1. From the graph, the initial count rate (at t=0 s) is 80 counts⁄min.

2. Half of this value is 40 counts⁄min.

3. Find 40 counts⁄min on the y-axis, move across to the curve, and read the corresponding time on the x-axis. This time is approximately 74 s.

(Checking with a second half-life: Half of 40 is 20 counts⁄min. The time for 20 counts⁄min is approx 148 s. The time elapsed is 148 s - 74 s = 74 s. This confirms the half-life.)

Half-life = 74 s (Mark scheme allows 70-76 s).

Recap

Radioactive decay is a random process. The half-life is the time taken for half of the unstable nuclei in a sample to decay (or for the count rate to halve). It is a constant value for a specific isotope and is not affected by temperature or pressure.

(c) The nuclide notation for this isotope of protactinium is 23491Pa. Protactinium-234 decays to an isotope of uranium (U) by β-emission.

Write down the nuclide equation for this decay of protactinium-234. [3]

A beta (β) particle is a high-speed electron, with notation 0-1e (or 0-1β).

In beta decay, a neutron in the nucleus turns into a proton and an electron. The electron is emitted.

The mass number (top) remains the same (234).

The proton number (bottom) increases by 1 (91 + 1 = 92). The element with proton number 92 is Uranium (U).

The balanced equation is:

23491Pa → 23492U + 0-1β

Recap

There are three main types of nuclear radiation:
Alpha (α): A helium nucleus (42He). Mass number decreases by 4, proton number decreases by 2.
Beta (β): An electron (0-1e). Mass number is unchanged, proton number increases by 1.
Gamma (γ): An electromagnetic wave (00γ). No change to mass or proton number.
