Q1 (0625/41/M/J/21) | Motion, Forces

A skydiver of mass 76 kg is falling vertically in still air. At time t = 0, the skydiver opens his parachute.

Fig. 1.1 is the speed-time graph for the skydiver from t = 0.

(a) Using Fig. 1.1, determine:

(i) the deceleration of the skydiver immediately after the parachute opens [2]

(a) (i) Answer

The deceleration is the gradient of the speed-time graph at t=0. To find this, we draw a tangent to the curve at t=0.

A tangent drawn at t=0 passes through approximately (0 s, 58 m/s) and (1.0 s, 18 m/s).

Gradient (Acceleration) = (change in y) / (change in x) = (18 m/s - 58 m/s) / (1.0 s - 0 s) = -40 m/s².

The deceleration is 40 m/s². (Note: Any value between 35 and 43 m/s² is accepted by the mark scheme).

Recap

The gradient of a speed-time graph is equal to the acceleration. A positive gradient means acceleration, a negative gradient means deceleration, and a zero gradient (horizontal line) means constant speed.

(ii) the force due to air resistance acting on the skydiver immediately after the parachute opens. [3]

(a) (ii) Answer

There are two forces: Weight (down) and Air Resistance (up). The resultant force (F_net) causes the deceleration. Let's define the upward direction as positive.

  1. Weight (W) = m x g = 76 kg x 10 N/kg = 760 N (downwards, so -760 N).
  2. Acceleration (a) = +40 m/s² (upwards, as it's a deceleration).
  3. Resultant Force (F_net) = m x a = 76 kg x 40 m/s² = 3040 N (upwards).
  4. F_net = Force (up) + Force (down) = F_air - W.
  5. 3040 N = F_air - 760 N.
  6. F_air = 3040 N + 760 N = 3800 N.

Recap

Newton's Second Law states that the resultant force (F) acting on an object is equal to its mass (m) multiplied by its acceleration (a). The formula is F = ma. The resultant force is the vector sum of all individual forces acting on the object (e.g., air resistance and weight).

(b) Explain, in terms of the forces acting on the skydiver, his motion between t = 0 and t = 6.0 s. [3]

(b) Answer

At t=0, the parachute opens, creating a large upward force of air resistance (3800 N) that is much greater than the downward weight (760 N). This creates a large resultant force upwards, causing the skydiver to decelerate rapidly.

As the skydiver's speed decreases, the air resistance also decreases. This reduces the size of the resultant upward force, so the rate of deceleration becomes smaller (as seen by the gradient becoming less steep).

This continues until, at approximately t = 3.5 s, the upward force of air resistance has decreased so much that it balances the downward force of weight. The resultant force becomes zero, and the skydiver stops decelerating, falling at a constant, slow terminal velocity of about 8 m/s.

Recap

When an object falls, air resistance (drag) acts in the opposite direction to its motion. This force increases with speed. If an object falls for long enough, the force of air resistance will increase until it becomes equal in size to the object's weight. At this point, the forces are balanced, the resultant force is zero, and the object stops accelerating, falling at a constant speed called terminal velocity.

(c) Explain why opening the parachute cannot reduce the speed of the skydiver to zero. [2]

(c) Answer

The force of air resistance depends on speed. If the skydiver's speed were to become zero, the upward force of air resistance would also become zero. However, the skydiver's weight (a constant downward force) would still be acting. This would create a resultant downward force, immediately causing the skydiver to accelerate downwards.

Recap

Drag is a frictional force that occurs due to particles hitting an object as it moves through a liquid or a gas. The faster the object moves, the greater the drag force. If there is no motion, there is no drag.

Q2 (0625/41/M/J/21) | Momentum, Work & Energy

Fig. 2.1 shows a wooden trolley of mass 1.2 kg at rest on the rough surface of a bench.

A ball of mass 0.52 g travels horizontally towards the trolley. The ball embeds itself in the wood of the trolley. The trolley moves with an initial speed of 0.065 m/s.

(a) Calculate:

(i) the impulse exerted on the trolley [2]

(a) (i) Answer

Impulse is defined as the change in momentum. The trolley's mass (m_t) is 1.2 kg. Its initial velocity (u_t) is 0 m/s and its final velocity (v_t) is 0.065 m/s.

Impulse = Δp = m_t x v_t - m_t x u_t

Impulse = (1.2 kg x 0.065 m/s) - (1.2 kg x 0 m/s) = 0.078 N s.

Recap

Impulse is equal to the change in momentum (Δp). The formula is Impulse = FΔt = mv - mu, where v is the final velocity, u is the initial velocity, and m is the mass.

(ii) the speed of the ball as it hits the trolley. [2]

(a) (ii) Answer

We use the principle of conservation of momentum. The total momentum before the collision equals the total momentum after.

Mass of ball (m_b) = 0.52 g = 0.00052 kg. Mass of trolley (m_t) = 1.2 kg.

Momentum before = (m_b x u_b) + (m_t x u_t)

Momentum after = (m_b + m_t) x v_final

(0.00052 kg x u_b) + (1.2 kg x 0) = (0.00052 kg + 1.2 kg) x 0.065 m/s

0.00052 x u_b = 1.20052 x 0.065

0.00052 x u_b = 0.0780338

u_b = 0.0780338 / 0.00052 = 150 m/s.

Recap

The principle of conservation of momentum states that the total momentum before a collision is equal to the total momentum after, provided that no external forces (like friction) act during the collision.

(b) As the trolley moves across the rough surface, it slows down and stops.

Explain, in terms of the work done, the energy change that takes place as the trolley slows down. [3]

(b) Answer

The moving trolley has kinetic energy. The rough surface exerts a frictional force that opposes the motion. The trolley does work done against friction as it moves.

According to the principle of conservation of energy, this work done represents an energy transfer. The kinetic energy of the trolley is transferred into thermal energy (heat) in the trolley and the bench surface. As the trolley's kinetic energy decreases, its speed decreases until it stops.

Recap

Work done is equal to the energy transferred. When a force causes motion, work is done. Friction is a force that opposes motion. Work done against friction transforms kinetic energy into thermal energy (heat).

Q3 (0625/41/M/J/21) | Properties of Matter, Pressure

(a) Explain, in terms of molecules, why liquids are very difficult to compress. [2]

(a) Answer

In a liquid, the molecules are arranged randomly but are already very close together and are touching. Because they are so close, there are very large repulsive forces between them if they are pushed any closer, making the liquid incompressible (very difficult to compress).

Recap

In a liquid, the particles are close together with weak bonds (attractive forces) between them. As the particles are already close together, they cannot be pushed much closer together and so liquids are incompressible. Gases are easy to compress because their particles are very far apart.

(b) Fig. 3.1 shows a device that uses liquid pressure to lift heavy boxes.

The boxes are lifted by pumping oil into the cylinder.

The force upwards on the piston due to the oil, and the force downwards on the piston due to the air above the piston, combine to produce a constant force of 8800 N.

The pressure of the air is 1.0 x 10⁵ Pa and the cross-sectional area of the bottom surface of the piston is 0.016 m².

(i) Calculate the pressure of the oil at the bottom surface of the piston. [3]

(b) (i) Answer

The 8800 N force is the *net* upward force from the oil and air pressures. Let F_oil be the upward force from the oil and F_air be the downward force from the air.

F_net_up = F_oil - F_air

First, calculate the downward force from air pressure: F_air = p_air x A

F_air = (1.0 x 10⁵ Pa) x 0.016 m² = 1600 N.

Now, find the total upward force from the oil:

8800 N = F_oil - 1600 N

F_oil = 8800 N + 1600 N = 10400 N.

Finally, calculate the pressure of the oil: p_oil = F_oil / A

p_oil = 10400 N / 0.016 m² = 650,000 Pa (or 6.5 x 10⁵ Pa).

Recap

The pressure (p) on a surface is defined as the force (F) acting perpendicularly on that surface divided by the area (A) over which the force is distributed. The equation is p = F / A. The unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa), which is equal to one newton per square metre (N/m²).

(ii) As the boxes are lifted, the depth of the oil increases. Explain why the pump must exert an increasing pressure on the oil as the depth of the oil increases. [2]

(b) (ii) Answer

As the piston rises, the depth of the oil in the cylinder increases. The pressure in a liquid increases with depth, due to the weight of the liquid column above. Therefore, the pump must exert an increasingly high pressure to not only support the boxes but also to support the increasing weight of the oil itself.

Recap

The pressure below the surface of a liquid increases directly proportionally with depth (Δh) and the density (ρ) of the liquid. The change in pressure is given by the equation Δp = ρgΔh.

(iii) Suggest one reason why the force of 8800 N in (b) cannot lift boxes of weight 8800 N. [1]

(b) (iii) Answer

The 8800 N is the net upward force on the piston. This force must lift both the boxes *and* the weight of the piston itself. Additionally, there might be frictional forces between the moving piston and the cylinder walls that need to be overcome.

Recap

In real-world systems, forces are needed to overcome friction, which opposes motion. Also, when lifting an object, the applied force must support the weight of all components being moved, not just the load.

Q4 (0625/41/M/J/21) | Thermal Physics

An aluminium saucepan with a plastic handle contains cold water.

Fig. 4.1 shows the saucepan on a hotplate.

(a) State why the pan is made from aluminium but the handle is made from plastic. [1]

(a) Answer

Aluminium is a good thermal conductor, which allows heat to transfer efficiently from the hotplate to the water. Plastic is a poor thermal conductor (an insulator), which prevents heat from reaching the user's hand.

Recap

Materials that allow thermal energy to pass through them easily are called thermal conductors (e.g., metals). Materials that do not are called thermal insulators (e.g., plastic, wood, air).

(b) The hotplate is switched on and, as the temperature of the water increases, the internal energy of the water increases.

(i) State, in terms of molecules, what is meant by an increase in internal energy. [1]

(b) (i) Answer

An increase in internal energy is an increase in the total kinetic energy and potential energy of the molecules.

Recap

The internal energy of a body is the sum of the potential and kinetic energies of its particles. Increasing the temperature of a body increases the average kinetic energy of the particles.

(ii) Explain, in terms of the atomic lattice and electrons, how thermal energy is transferred through the aluminium. [3]

(b) (ii) Answer

Aluminium is a metal. Firstly, the atoms in the atomic lattice at the hot end vibrate more vigorously. They collide with neighbouring atoms, passing on the vibration and energy through the lattice.

Secondly, and more importantly, metals have free electrons. These electrons gain kinetic energy at the hot end, move quickly through the lattice, and collide with atoms and other electrons in cooler parts, transferring energy very efficiently.

Recap

In metals, conduction occurs in two ways. The lattice ions vibrate and pass energy to their neighbours. Metals also have free electrons which gain kinetic energy and move through the metal, colliding with other electrons and ions to transfer energy quickly. Non-metals do not have free electrons and are poor conductors.

(iii) Eventually, the water reaches boiling point. Thermal energy from the hotplate is still being transferred to the water. Explain, in terms of molecules, the effect of this thermal energy on the water. [3]

(b) (iii) Answer

At the boiling point, the thermal energy added does not increase the temperature (kinetic energy) of the molecules. Instead, the energy is used to do work to overcome the attractive forces (bonds) between the liquid molecules. This allows the molecules to escape from the liquid and become a gas (vapour). This process increases the potential energy of the molecules.

Recap

Boiling occurs at a specific, constant temperature. When thermal energy is supplied to a liquid at its boiling point, the temperature does not change. The energy is used to break the bonds between particles, changing the state from liquid to gas. This energy increases the potential energy of the particles.

(iv) The mass of the water decreases by 0.11 kg in 300 s. The specific latent heat of vaporisation of water is 2.3 x 10⁶ J/kg. Calculate the rate at which the water gains thermal energy. [3]

(b) (iv) Answer

First, find the total energy (E) used for vaporisation: E = mass (m) x specific latent heat (L_v)

E = 0.11 kg x (2.3 x 10⁶ J/kg) = 253,000 J.

Next, find the rate of energy gain (Power, P): P = Energy (E) / time (t)

P = 253,000 J / 300 s = 843 W. (Accept 840 W).

Recap

Power is the rate of doing work or the energy transferred per second. The equation is P = ΔE / t. The energy (ΔE) required for a change of state is given by E = m x L, where m is the mass and L is the specific latent heat.

Q5 (0625/41/M/J/21) | Thermal Physics

Fig. 5.1 shows the structure of a liquid-in-glass thermometer.

The bulb of the thermometer is placed into a beaker of warm water. As the liquid expands, it moves along the tube.

(a) Explain, in terms of molecules, why a liquid expands when heated. [2]

(a) Answer

When the liquid is heated, its molecules gain kinetic energy and move faster. This increased movement causes them to push each other further apart, leading to an overall increase in the volume of the liquid.

Recap

When the temperature of a substance (solid, liquid, or gas) is increased, the particles gain kinetic energy and move around faster. This increased movement causes them to take up more space, and the substance expands.

(b) Explain, in terms of molecules, why a liquid expands more than a solid when heated. [2]

(b) Answer

The forces (bonds) between molecules in a liquid are weaker than the forces between particles in a solid's lattice. Therefore, less energy is required to overcome these forces and separate the molecules, resulting in a greater expansion for the same temperature increase.

Recap

The expansion of materials depends on the separation between molecules and their motion. Gases expand the most (largest separation, weakest forces). Solids expand the least (smallest separation, strongest forces). Liquids expand more than solids but less than gases.

(c) A second thermometer has a larger bulb that contains more of the same liquid than the thermometer shown in Fig. 5.1. It has a different scale. In every other way, it is identical.

(i) Explain how the sensitivity of the second thermometer compares with the sensitivity of the thermometer in Fig. 5.1. [2]

(c) (i) Answer

The second thermometer has a greater sensitivity. A larger bulb means there is a greater volume of liquid. For the same temperature increase, this larger volume of liquid will produce a greater increase in volume, pushing the liquid further along the narrow tube.

Recap

The sensitivity of a liquid-in-glass thermometer (how much the liquid moves for a small temperature change) is increased by using a larger bulb or a narrower tube (capillary). This ensures that a small change in volume results in a large change in the length of the liquid thread.

(ii) Explain how the range of the second thermometer compares with the range of the thermometer in Fig. 5.1. [1]

(c) (ii) Answer

The second thermometer has a smaller range. Because it is more sensitive, the liquid will reach the end of the tube with a smaller increase in temperature, assuming the tube length is the same.

Recap

The range of a thermometer is the difference between the maximum and minimum temperatures it can measure. There is often a trade-off: a highly sensitive thermometer (with a large bulb or narrow tube) often has a smaller range.

(d) (i) State one everyday problem that is a result of thermal expansion. [1]

(d) (i) Answer

Railway tracks or bridges buckling (bending) and becoming deformed on a hot day.

Recap

Thermal expansion in large structures like bridges, buildings, and railway tracks can cause significant problems due to large changes in temperature, leading to bending and buckling.

(ii) Suggest and explain one way of solving this problem. [2]

(d) (ii) Answer

The problem is solved by leaving expansion gaps at the ends of bridge sections or between lengths of railway track. When the structure expands on a hot day, the gaps close up, allowing the material to expand safely without bending or breaking.

Recap

To avoid damage from thermal expansion, expansion gaps are left in large structures. If no gap was left, when the structure expanded on a hot day, it would be forced against itself and buckle.

Q6 (0625/41/M/J/21) | Waves

Fig. 6.1 is a full-scale diagram that represents a sound wave travelling in air.

(a) On Fig. 6.1, mark two points, each at the centre of a different compression. Label both of the points C. [1]

(a) Answer

Two points should be marked with 'C' in the middle of two different regions where the lines are closest together (the compressions).

Recap

A sound wave is a longitudinal wave. The particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave travel. This creates a series of compressions (where particles are closer together) and rarefactions (where particles are further apart).

(b) The speed of sound in air is 330 m/s. Measure the diagram and determine the frequency of the sound. [3]

(b) Answer

1. Measure Wavelength (λ): Using a ruler on the full-scale diagram, the distance between the centre of two adjacent compressions (e.g., the two points marked 'C') is measured. This distance is the wavelength. The measurement is approximately 0.052 m (or 5.2 cm). (MS accepts 0.051 to 0.053 m).

2. State Formula: The wave equation is v = f x λ, where v = speed, f = frequency, and λ = wavelength.

3. Calculate Frequency (f): f = v / λ

f = 330 m/s / 0.052 m = 6346 Hz. (MS accepts 6200-6500 Hz).

Recap

The velocity (v) of a wave is related to its frequency (f) and wavelength (λ) by the wave equation: v = f x λ. Wavelength is the distance between adjacent crests or compressions. Frequency is the number of complete waves passing a point per second, measured in hertz (Hz).

(c) The wave reaches a barrier. Fig. 6.2 shows the wave passing through a gap in the barrier.

The frequency of the wave is increased to a value many times greater than the value obtained in (b).

Describe and explain two ways in which a diagram representing the wave with the greater frequency differs from Fig. 6.2. [3]

(c) Answer

1. Change in Wavelength: The speed of sound v is constant. Since v = f x λ, if the frequency (f) is increased, the wavelength (λ) must decrease. Therefore, the diagram would show the compressions (wavefronts) being closer together.

2. Change in Diffraction: The diagram shows the wave diffracting (spreading out) as it passes through the gap. The amount of diffraction depends on the ratio of the wavelength to the gap size. Because the new wavelength is smaller, this ratio (λ / gap size) is smaller, and there will be less diffraction. The waves will spread out less on the other side of the barrier.

Recap

Diffraction is the spreading out of a wave as it passes through a gap or around an edge. Maximum diffraction occurs when the size of the gap is approximately equal to the wavelength of the wave. If the wavelength is much smaller than the gap, little diffraction occurs.

Q7 (0625/41/M/J/21) | Electromagnetic Effects

Fig. 7.1 represents an alternating current (a.c.) generator.

(a) A student rotates the handle H, as shown in Fig. 7.1.

(i) On Fig. 7.2, sketch a graph to show how the electromotive force (e.m.f.) between terminals X and Y varies with time during two complete revolutions of the coil. [3]

(a) (i) Answer

The graph sketched on Fig. 7.2 should be a sinusoidal wave (a smooth, repeating wave). It must show two complete cycles (e.g., two peaks and two troughs). The wave should start at zero, rise to a maximum, fall to zero, drop to a minimum, and return to zero for the first cycle, and then repeat this for the second cycle. The amplitude and period should be constant.

Recap

An a.c. generator induces an alternating e.m.f. (and current). As the coil rotates in the magnetic field, the rate at which it "cuts" magnetic field lines changes, producing a sinusoidal e.m.f. The e.m.f. is maximum when the coil is horizontal (cutting field lines fastest) and zero when the coil is vertical (moving parallel to field lines).

(ii) On Fig. 7.2, mark and label a point P, for the e.m.f. when the coil is horizontal, as shown in Fig. 7.1. [1]

(a) (ii) Answer

Point P should be marked at the first peak (maximum positive e.m.f.) or the first trough (maximum negative e.m.f.). In the horizontal position shown, the coil sides are moving fastest through the magnetic field, inducing the maximum e.m.f.

Recap

In an a.c. generator, the induced e.m.f. is at its maximum value when the coil is horizontal, as the sides of the coil are cutting the magnetic field lines at the fastest rate. The e.m.f. is zero when the coil is vertical.

(iii) The student turns the handle more quickly. State two ways in which the e.m.f. between terminals X and Y changes. [2]

(a) (iii) Answer

1. The amplitude (maximum e.m.f.) increases. (The coil cuts magnetic field lines at a faster rate).

2. The frequency increases. (The coil completes its revolutions in less time, so the e.m.f. changes direction more often).

Recap

Increasing the speed of rotation of an a.c. generator increases both the amplitude (maximum voltage) and the frequency (number of cycles per second) of the induced e.m.f.

(b) Terminals X and Y are connected to the primary coil of a transformer. State and explain what happens in the transformer as the student turns the handle of the a.c. generator. [3]

(b) Answer

Turning the handle creates an alternating current (a.c.) in the primary coil of the transformer. This a.c. produces a continuously changing magnetic field in the iron core. This changing magnetic field passes through (or "cuts") the secondary coil. By the principle of electromagnetic induction, this induces an alternating e.m.f. (voltage) across the secondary coil.

Recap

A transformer works on the principle of electromagnetic induction. An alternating current in the primary coil creates a changing magnetic field in the core. This changing field induces an alternating e.m.f. in the secondary coil. Transformers only work with a.c., not d.c.

(c) Explain why the power losses in transmission cables are lower when electrical energy is transmitted at higher voltages. [2]

(c) Answer

Electrical power transmitted is P = V x I. For the same amount of power (P) to be transmitted, if the voltage (V) is stepped up (increased) to a very high value, the current (I) must be proportionally smaller.

Power loss (as heat) in the cables is given by P_loss = I² x R. Because the current (I) is much smaller, the thermal energy loss in the cables (which have a fixed resistance R) is significantly reduced.

Recap

Electrical energy is transmitted at high voltages and low currents to reduce power loss in the transmission cables. The power lost as heat is calculated using P = I²R. A low current dramatically reduces this heat loss, making transmission more efficient.

Q8 (0625/41/M/J/21) | Electric Circuits

A student sets up a circuit that includes a 12V battery, an 800 Ω resistor, a voltmeter and a thermistor. Fig. 8.1 is an incomplete circuit diagram because the symbol for the thermistor is missing.

The thermistor is connected between terminals P and Q.

(a) Complete Fig. 8.1 by drawing the symbol for a thermistor between terminals P and Q. [1]

(a) Answer

The correct symbol for a thermistor (a rectangle with a diagonal line through it, and a horizontal line 'hockey stick' at the bottom) should be drawn between P and Q.

Recap

A thermistor is a resistor whose resistance changes significantly with temperature. For a standard NTC (Negative Temperature Coefficient) thermistor, as temperature increases, its resistance decreases.

(b) The 12V battery consists of eight identical cells connected in series. Calculate the electromotive force (e.m.f.) of each cell. [1]

(b) Answer

When cells are connected in series, their e.m.f.s add up.

Total e.m.f. = 12 V. Number of cells = 8.

e.m.f. per cell = Total e.m.f. / Number of cells = 12 V / 8 = 1.5 V.

Recap

When voltage sources are connected in series, the total e.m.f. (electromotive force) is the sum of the individual e.m.f.s. When connected in parallel, the total e.m.f. is the same as one of the cells (if they are identical).

(c) The reading on the voltmeter is 8.0 V.

(i) Determine the resistance of the thermistor. [3]

(c) (i) Answer

This is a potential divider circuit. The total voltage (12 V) is shared between the 800 Ω resistor and the thermistor.

1. Voltage across 800 Ω resistor (V_R): The voltmeter reads the voltage across the thermistor (V_Th = 8.0 V).

V_R = V_total - V_Th = 12 V - 8.0 V = 4.0 V.

2. Current (I) in the circuit: The current is the same through both components. We can find it using Ohm's Law (V=IR) on the 800 Ω resistor.

I = V_R / R = 4.0 V / 800 Ω = 0.0050 A.

3. Resistance of thermistor (R_Th): Now use Ohm's Law on the thermistor, using the same current.

R_Th = V_Th / I = 8.0 V / 0.0050 A = 1600 Ω.

Recap

In a series circuit, the current is the same at all points. The total potential difference (voltage) from the supply is shared between the components in proportion to their resistance. This is known as a potential divider.

(ii) A few hours later, the student notices that the reading on the voltmeter is greater. Explain what can be deduced from this observation. [3]

(c) (ii) Answer

The voltmeter reading (V_Th) increased. In this potential divider, the voltage is shared, so a larger voltage across the thermistor means the thermistor's resistance must have increased (it is taking a larger *proportion* of the total voltage).

A thermistor is a component whose resistance depends on temperature. For a standard (NTC) thermistor, resistance increases as temperature decreases.

Therefore, we can deduce that the temperature of the thermistor has decreased.

Recap

A thermistor (NTC type) is a resistor where resistance decreases as temperature increases. This property makes it useful in temperature-sensing circuits, such as thermostats.

Q9 (0625/41/M/J/21) | Nuclear Physics

There are three naturally occurring isotopes of hydrogen: hydrogen-1, hydrogen-2 and hydrogen-3. The nuclide notation for hydrogen-1 is 1₁H.

(a) Write down the symbol, using nuclide notation, for: [1]

hydrogen-2

hydrogen-3.

(a) Answer

Hydrogen-2: 2₁H

Hydrogen-3: 3₁H

Recap

Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same proton number, Z) but with different numbers of neutrons (different mass number, A). Nuclide notation is written as A₂X. All hydrogen isotopes have Z=1.

(b) In a fusion reactor, a nucleus of hydrogen-2 and a nucleus of hydrogen-3 undergo fusion.

(i) State what is meant by nuclear fusion. [2]

(b) (i) Answer

Nuclear fusion is the process where two small (or light) nuclei join together (fuse) to form a single, larger (or heavier) nucleus. This process releases a large amount of energy.

Recap

Nuclear fusion is the joining of lighter nuclei into heavier nuclei with the emission of energy. This process powers the Sun.

(ii) The fusion reaction produces a free neutron and one other particle. Write down, using nuclide notation, the equation that represents this reaction. [3]

(b) (ii) Answer

We start with 2₁H and 3₁H. The products are a neutron (1₀n) and an unknown particle A₂X.

Equation: 2₁H + 3₁H → A₂X + 1₀n

To balance the mass number (top): 2 + 3 = A + 1 &implies; A = 4.

To balance the proton number (bottom): 1 + 1 = Z + 0 &implies; Z = 2.

The particle with Z=2 is Helium (He). The full equation is:

2₁H + 3₁H → 4₂He + 1₀n

Recap

In nuclear equations, both the total mass number (sum of top numbers) and the total proton number (sum of bottom numbers) must be conserved (be equal) on both sides of the reaction arrow.

(c) Nuclear fusion in the Sun is the source of most but not all of the resources that are used to generate electrical energy on Earth. State two resources for which nuclear fusion in the Sun is not the source. [2]

(c) Answer

Two resources that do not get their energy from the Sun are:

  1. Geothermal energy
  2. Tidal energy
  3. (Also correct: Nuclear energy)

Recap

The Sun (powered by nuclear fusion) is the original source for most of our energy resources, including fossil fuels (stored solar energy), wind (from atmospheric heating), and hydroelectric (from the water cycle). The exceptions are Geothermal (from radioactive decay in the Earth), Tidal (from the Moon's gravity), and Nuclear (from elements on Earth).
