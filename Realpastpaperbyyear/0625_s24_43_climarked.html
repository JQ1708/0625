Question 1 | Topics: Kinematics, Forces

(a) Define deceleration.

Deceleration is the rate of decrease in velocity, or a negative rate of change of velocity.

Recap

Deceleration is simply acceleration in the direction opposite to the object's motion. It is measured in the same units, m/s².

(d) Describe and explain the motion of a ball dropped from a tall building, considering air resistance.

Initially, the ball accelerates due to gravity (at approx. 9.8 m/s²). As its speed increases, air resistance increases. This causes the resultant downward force to decrease, so the acceleration decreases. Eventually, the air resistance becomes equal to the ball's weight. At this point, the resultant force is zero, acceleration is zero, and the ball falls at a constant terminal velocity.

Recap

Terminal velocity is reached when the drag force on a falling object equals its weight. The object stops accelerating and moves at a constant velocity. The value depends on the object's shape, area, and the fluid's density.

Question 2 | Topics: Energy, Circuits

(a) Name the energy store in the battery.

The energy is stored as chemical energy.

Recap

Batteries convert stored chemical energy into electrical energy through electrochemical reactions involving different chemicals at the anode and cathode.

(b) State one advantage of a parallel connection in a lighting circuit.

If one lamp breaks, the other one will remain lit because the circuit for the second lamp is still complete.

Recap

In a parallel circuit, components are connected across separate branches. This means they each receive the full supply voltage, and a break in one branch does not affect the others.

(c) The efficiency of the solar cell is 22%. The power supplied to the lights is 15W.

(i) State what is meant by 22% efficiency.

The useful energy/power output from the solar panel is 22% of the total energy/power input.

Recap

Efficiency is a measure of how well a device converts input energy into useful output energy. It is calculated as (Useful Output / Total Input) × 100%. The remaining energy is wasted, usually as heat.

(ii) Calculate the solar power input to the solar cell.

The solar power input is 68 W. This is calculated from the efficiency formula: Input = Useful Output / Efficiency = 15 W / 0.22.

Recap

Rearranging the efficiency formula allows you to calculate the total input power required to achieve a certain useful output, accounting for the energy that will be wasted by the device.

Question 3 | Topics: Moments, Impulse

(a) Child B moves 0.050 m further away from the pivot.

(i) Explain why the seesaw rotates clockwise.

The clockwise moment has increased because the distance for the 900N force increased, while the anti-clockwise moment remained the same.

Recap

The Principle of Moments states that for an object to be in rotational equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any pivot must equal the sum of the anti-clockwise moments. Moment = Force × Perpendicular distance from pivot.

(b) A child falls from the seesaw and experiences an impulse.

(i) Define impulse.

Impulse is the product of force and the time for which it acts, or simply the change in momentum.

Recap

The formula for impulse is I = FΔt = Δ(mv). It is a vector quantity, measured in Newton-seconds (Ns) or kg m/s.

(ii) Explain how a rubber floor reduces injury.

The change in momentum (impulse) is the same for both the rubber and concrete floors. However, the rubber floor deforms, which increases the time of the impact. Since force is the rate of change of momentum (F = Δp / Δt), increasing the time reduces the magnitude of the force on the child, thus reducing injury.

Recap

This principle is used in many safety features, such as car crumple zones, airbags, and climbing ropes. They are all designed to increase the duration of an impact to decrease the force experienced.

Question 4 | Topics: Thermal Physics

(a) State what happens to the water particles as the water temperature increases.

The average kinetic energy of the particles increases, meaning they move faster.

Recap

Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance. Increasing the temperature means supplying energy, which makes the particles vibrate and move more vigorously.

(b)

(ii) Calculate the energy required to increase the water temperature from 20°C to 65°C.

The energy required is 47,000 J. This is calculated using the formula ΔE = mcΔθ, where m=0.25kg, c=4200 J/(kg°C), and Δθ = 65°C - 20°C = 45°C. So, ΔE = 0.25 × 4200 × 45.

Recap

Specific Heat Capacity (c) is the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C. Water has a very high specific heat capacity, meaning it can store a lot of thermal energy.

(c) Explain how using an aluminium saucepan (c=890) vs a steel one (c=500) affects the time taken to heat the water.

The aluminium saucepan will take longer to heat the water. Because aluminium has a higher specific heat capacity, more energy is needed to heat the pan itself to the same temperature. This means less energy is initially available for the water, increasing the overall heating time.

Recap

When heating a substance in a container, the total energy supplied must heat both the container and the substance. A container with a lower specific heat capacity will heat up faster and transfer energy to its contents more quickly, assuming equal mass and power supply.

Question 5 | Topics: Thermal Physics

(a) Explain how electrons transfer thermal energy through the metal of the container.

Metals contain delocalised / free electrons. These electrons gain kinetic energy at the hot end and move through the metal lattice. They then collide with ions/atoms in cooler parts of the metal, transferring their energy and heating those parts.

Recap

Thermal conduction in metals is primarily due to the movement of free electrons, which is much more efficient than lattice vibrations alone. This is why metals are excellent thermal (and electrical) conductors.

(b) Explain why a non-metal conducts thermal energy less well than a metal.

Non-metals do not have no delocalised / free electrons to move and transfer energy.

Recap

In non-metals (insulators), heat is transferred only by lattice vibrations, where particles pass energy to their immediate neighbours. This process is much slower than conduction by free electrons.

(c) Explain, in terms of particles, why gases are poor thermal conductors compared to non-metal solids.

The particles are further apart in gases. This means there are fewer particle collisions to transfer energy, and there are no lattice vibrations.

Recap

The effectiveness of conduction depends on how closely packed the particles are. Solids are the best conductors (especially metals), followed by liquids, while gases are the poorest conductors because of the large distances between particles.

Question 6 | Topics: Optics

(a) Explain the meaning of the terms principal focus and focal length.

Principal focus: The point where rays of light travelling parallel to the principal axis converge after passing through the lens.
Focal length: The distance between the centre of the lens and the principal focus.

Recap

For a converging lens, the principal focus (or focal point) is a real point. For a diverging lens, it is a virtual point from which parallel rays appear to diverge.

(ii) Describe the effect on a negatively charged particle placed at X.

The negatively charged particle experiences a force / attraction towards the positively charged plate.

The direction of an electric field is defined as the direction of the force that would be exerted on a positive test charge. Therefore, a negative charge will experience a force in the opposite direction to the electric field lines.

(b) The charges on the cloud and on the ground are shown in Fig. 7.2.

A lightning strike discharges a current of 28000A for 0.0012s, transferring 1.2 × 10⁸ J of energy.

(i) Calculate the charge that flows from the cloud to the ground.

The charge that flows is 34 C. This is calculated using the formula Charge = Current × Time (Q = It). So, Q = 28000 A × 0.0012 s.

Recap

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge. One Ampere is equivalent to one Coulomb of charge flowing per second.

(ii) Calculate the potential difference between the base of the cloud and the ground.

The potential difference is 3.6 x 10⁶ V (or 3.6 MV). This is calculated using the formula Potential Difference = Energy / Charge (V = E / Q). So, V = (1.2 × 10⁸ J) / 34 C.

Recap

Potential difference (voltage) is the work done (or energy transferred) per unit of charge. One Volt is equivalent to one Joule of energy per Coulomb of charge.

Question 8 | Topics: Medical Physics

(a)

(i) Define ultrasound.

Ultrasound is sound with a frequency higher than 20 kHz, which is above the range of human hearing.

Recap

The range of human hearing is typically from 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz). Sounds below this range are called infrasound, and sounds above it are called ultrasound.

(b) Describe three similarities or differences between the use of ultrasound and X-rays in medical scanning.

1. Both can be used to show internal body parts without surgery.
2. X-rays are better for showing dense structures like bone, while ultrasound is better for showing soft tissues.
3. X-rays are a form of ionising radiation and can be harmful to humans, whereas ultrasound is non-ionising and considered much safer.

Recap

Ultrasound imaging works by detecting reflected sound waves (echoes), while X-ray imaging works by detecting how much X-ray radiation is absorbed by different tissues. This fundamental difference in mechanism leads to their different applications and safety profiles.

Question 9 | Topics: Electromagnetism

(a) Explain how a current is produced in the secondary coil.

The alternating current in the primary coil produces a changing magnetic field. This magnetic field passes through and is 'cut' by the secondary coil. This induces a current in the secondary coil.

Recap

This is the principle of electromagnetic induction. A current is induced in a conductor when it is exposed to a changing magnetic field. This is the basis for transformers, generators, and wireless charging.

(b)

(ii) The charging plate has a useful output power of 15W. Show that the claim that the battery can be charged to 50% capacity (4.3 x 10⁴ J) in less than 30 minutes is true.

First, find the energy required for a 50% charge: E = 0.5 × (4.3 × 10⁴ J) = 2.15 × 10⁴ J. Then, calculate the time it would take to deliver this energy at 15W using Power = Energy / Time. So, t = E / P = (2.15 × 10⁴ J) / 15 W ≈ 1433 seconds. Converting this to minutes: 1433 / 60 ≈ 24 minutes. Since 24 minutes is less than 30 minutes, the claim is true.

Recap

The relationship between Power (P), Energy (E), and Time (t) is given by P = E / t. Power is the rate at which energy is transferred. One Watt is equal to one Joule per second.

Question 10 | Topics: Radioactivity

(a)

(i) Define background radiation.

Background radiation is the radiation always present in the environment from natural sources.

Recap

Sources include cosmic rays from space, radioactive rocks in the ground (which release radon gas), and small amounts in food and drink. It is a low-level, unavoidable form of ionising radiation.

(iii) The average background radiation is 26 counts/minute. The counter shows 382 counts/minute. Determine the count rate due to the tracer.

The count rate due to the tracer is 356 counts/minute. This is found by subtracting the background count from the total count: Corrected count = Count rate - Background count = 382 - 26.

Recap

When measuring the activity of a radioactive source, it is crucial to first measure the background radiation and then subtract this value from the total measurement to find the true activity of the source itself.

(b) Suggest which radioactive emission is suitable for detecting the leak and explain why.

Emission: Beta or Gamma.
Explanation: Alpha radiation would be stopped by the soil and would not reach the detector. Beta has sufficient range to be detected above ground. Gamma would also work, but might pass through the intact pipe as well, making it harder to spot the leak.

Recap

The choice of isotope for a tracer depends on its penetrating power. Alpha is stopped by paper, Beta by a few mm of aluminium, and Gamma requires thick lead. The tracer must be able to escape the pipe and soil to be detected.

(c) Explain why the isotope must not have a very short or very long half-life.

A very short half-life means the activity would drop to undetectable levels before the leak could be found. A very long half-life means the water supply would remain contaminated for a dangerously long time.

Recap

Half-life is the time taken for the radioactivity of a specified isotope to fall to half its original value. The ideal tracer has a half-life long enough to perform the diagnosis but short enough to decay soon after.

Question 11 | Topics: Cosmology

(a) Name the galaxy that contains the Sun.

The Sun is part of the Milky Way galaxy.

Recap

The reference material describes the Milky Way as a barred spiral galaxy containing hundreds of billions of stars, including our Solar System in one of its spiral arms.

(b) Light observed from distant galaxies is redshifted. State the theory of the Universe that this observation supports.

The redshift supports the Big Bang theory, which predicts an expanding Universe.

Recap

Redshifted spectral lines indicate that galaxies are moving away from us. The guide links this observation to the expansion predicted by the Big Bang model.

(c) Cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR) is observed at all points in space.

(i) State when this radiation was produced.

It was produced shortly after the Universe formed, when matter and radiation first decoupled.

Recap

The CMBR dates from the era about 380 000 years after the Big Bang, when electrons and nuclei combined to form atoms and photons could travel freely.

(ii) Explain why this radiation is now in the microwave region of the electromagnetic spectrum.

As the Universe has expanded, the original high-frequency light has been redshifted to the microwave region.

Recap

Expansion stretches wavelengths, reducing photon energy. The once-visible background glow now appears as a microwave signal with a temperature of about 2.7 K.
