(a) Describe how the acceleration of the rocket changes between time = 10 s and time = 30 s.

The acceleration of the rocket increases. This is shown on the speed-time graph by the gradient of the curve, which becomes steeper between 10 s and 30 s.

Recap

On a speed-time graph, the gradient (or slope) of the line represents acceleration. A straight line indicates constant acceleration, while a curve indicates changing acceleration. An upward-curving line with increasing steepness shows that the acceleration is increasing.

(b) By drawing a tangent to the graph, determine the acceleration of the rocket at time = 25 s.

To find the acceleration at t = 25 s, a tangent is drawn to the curve at that point. The gradient of this tangent is then calculated (change in speed / change in time). The acceleration is approximately 80 m/s².

Recap

The gradient of a tangent to a curve at a specific point gives the instantaneous rate of change at that point. For a speed-time graph, this instantaneous rate of change is the instantaneous acceleration.

(c) Determine the distance travelled by the rocket between time = 0 and time = 10 s.

The distance travelled is the area under the speed-time graph. For the interval between 0 and 10 s, this area can be approximated by counting squares or by treating the shape as a triangle. The distance is approximately 2000 m.

Recap

The area under a speed-time graph represents the distance travelled. If the graph is a curve, the area can be estimated by dividing it into simple shapes (rectangles and triangles) or by counting the grid squares underneath the line.

0625/42/O/N/21 Q2

(a) State Hooke's law.

Hooke's law states that the extension of a spring is directly proportional to the load (or force) applied, provided the elastic limit is not exceeded.

Recap

Mathematically, Hooke's Law is expressed as F = kx, where F is the force, x is the extension, and k is the spring constant. The elastic limit is the point beyond which the spring will be permanently deformed and will not return to its original length when the load is removed.

(b) Fig. 2.1 shows the extension–load graph for a spring.

(i) On Fig. 2.1, mark and label the region where the spring obeys Hooke's law.

The region where the spring obeys Hooke's law is the straight-line portion of the graph, starting from the origin up to a load of approximately 20.5 N. This is the region of proportionality.

Recap

An extension-load graph for a material that obeys Hooke's law is a straight line through the origin. The gradient of this line is equal to 1/k (the reciprocal of the spring constant).

(ii) Calculate the spring constant k.

The spring constant k is the gradient of the force-extension graph (or 1/gradient of the extension-force graph). Using a point from the straight-line section, for example, a load of 20 N gives an extension of 140 mm (0.14 m).
k = F / x = 20 N / 0.14 m = 140 N/m.

Recap

The spring constant (k) is a measure of the stiffness of a spring. A stiffer spring has a higher spring constant. The unit of the spring constant is Newtons per meter (N/m).

(iii) The original length of the spring is 120 mm. Calculate the length of the spring when a load of 8.5 N is applied to the spring.

First, find the extension using Hooke's Law: x = F / k = 8.5 N / 140 N/m ≈ 0.061 m or 61 mm.
The new length is the original length plus the extension.
Length = 120 mm + 61 mm = 181 mm (or 0.181 m).

Recap

The total length of a stretched spring is its original length plus the extension. It is important to be consistent with units throughout the calculation.

(c) The weight of an object is 4.0 N on a planet where the acceleration of free fall is 8.7 m/s². Calculate the mass of the object.

Using the formula for weight, W = mg.
Mass, m = W / g = 4.0 N / 8.7 m/s² = 0.46 kg.

Recap

An object's mass is an intrinsic property and is constant everywhere. Its weight, however, is a force that depends on the local gravitational field strength (g). The value of g varies from planet to planet.

0625/42/O/N/21 Q3

(a) After the collision, car A moves at 1.3 m/s. Show that the speed of car B after the collision is approximately 0.4 m/s.

According to the principle of conservation of momentum, the total momentum before the collision equals the total momentum after the collision.
(m_B * u_B) + (m_A * u_A) = (m_B * v_B) + (m_A * v_A)
(800 kg * 2.0 m/s) + (1000 kg * 0 m/s) = (800 kg * v_B) + (1000 kg * 1.3 m/s)
1600 = 800 * v_B + 1300
800 * v_B = 1600 - 1300 = 300
v_B = 300 / 800 = 0.375 m/s, which is approximately 0.4 m/s.

Recap

The principle of conservation of linear momentum states that in the absence of external forces, the total momentum of a closed system remains constant. Momentum (p) is a vector quantity calculated as p = mv.

(b) (i) Calculate the impulse exerted by car A on car B.

Impulse is the change in momentum. For car B:
Impulse = m_B * v_B - m_B * u_B = 800 * 0.375 - 800 * 2.0 = 300 - 1600 = -1300 Ns.
The impulse exerted by car A on car B is 1300 Ns in the opposite direction to B's initial motion.

Recap

Impulse is equal to the change in momentum (Δp) of an object. It is also equal to the product of the force (F) and the time interval (Δt) over which the force acts: Impulse = FΔt = Δp.

(ii) State the impulse exerted by car B on car A.

According to Newton's third law, the impulse exerted by car B on car A is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the impulse exerted by car A on car B. Therefore, the impulse is 1300 Ns in the forward direction.

Recap

Newton's Third Law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. In a collision, the forces the two objects exert on each other are equal and opposite. Since impulse is force multiplied by time, the impulses are also equal and opposite.

0625/42/O/N/21 Q4

(a) A power station uses wind energy to generate electricity. State and explain whether this method of generating electricity is renewable.

Statement: Wind energy is a renewable resource.
Explanation: It is considered renewable because the wind will not run out, or it is naturally replenished. It is an energy source that is effectively infinite.

Recap

Renewable energy sources are those that are naturally replenished on a human timescale, such as sunlight, wind, rain, tides, waves, and geothermal heat. Non-renewable energy sources, like fossil fuels, are finite and will eventually be depleted.

(b) State two energy resources that do not have the Sun as their source.

1. Geothermal energy (comes from the Earth's internal heat).
2. Nuclear energy (comes from the energy stored in atomic nuclei).
(Also acceptable: Tidal energy, which comes from the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun).

Recap

Most energy sources on Earth, including fossil fuels (stored solar energy from ancient plants), wind (caused by uneven heating of the Earth), and hydroelectric power (driven by the water cycle), are ultimately derived from the Sun.

(c) For each energy resource, state the form of energy stored in: fossil fuels, water behind hydroelectric dams.

Fossil fuels: Chemical potential energy.
Water behind hydroelectric dams: Gravitational potential energy.

Recap

Energy can be stored in various forms:
- Chemical energy is stored in the bonds of atoms and molecules.
- Gravitational potential energy is stored in an object due to its position in a gravitational field.
- Elastic potential energy is stored in stretched or compressed objects.
- Nuclear energy is stored in the nucleus of an atom.

(a) A thermocouple thermometer is used to determine the temperature difference between a mixture of ice and water and liquid mercury at approximately 600 °C. Complete Fig. 5.1 with a labelled diagram to show how the thermocouple thermometer can be used in this way.

The diagram should show two wires made of different metals (e.g., copper and constantan) joined at both ends to form two junctions. One junction (the cold junction) is placed in the mixture of ice and water (at 0 °C). The other junction (the hot junction) is placed in the liquid mercury. A voltmeter or galvanometer is connected into one of the wires to measure the electromotive force (e.m.f.) produced.

Recap

A thermocouple works on the Seebeck effect: when two junctions of two different metals are kept at different temperatures, a small voltage (e.m.f.) is produced in the circuit. This voltage is proportional to the temperature difference, allowing it to be used as a thermometer.

(b) State two other physical properties that can be used to measure temperature.

1. The expansion of a liquid (as in a liquid-in-glass thermometer).
2. The electrical resistance of a material (as in a resistance thermometer or thermistor).

Recap

Any physical property that varies consistently and measurably with temperature can be used for thermometry. This property is called a thermometric property. Other examples include the pressure of a fixed mass of gas at constant volume, and the color of light emitted by a hot object.

(c) State two benefits of using a thermocouple thermometer instead of a liquid-in-glass thermometer.

1. It has a much wider range and can measure very high temperatures (like 600 °C).
2. It responds very quickly to changes in temperature due to its low thermal capacity.

Recap

Other advantages of thermocouples include their robustness, small size, and suitability for remote sensing and data logging. Liquid-in-glass thermometers are fragile, slow to respond, and have a limited range (e.g., mercury freezes at -39 °C and boils at 357 °C).

(a) (i) On Fig. 8.1, draw the distribution of charges on object A.

The positively charged rod B attracts the free electrons in the conducting object A. Therefore, there will be an accumulation of negative charges (electrons) on the side of A closer to rod B. This leaves the side of A further from rod B with a net positive charge (a deficit of electrons). The number of induced positive and negative charges should be equal.

Recap

Electrostatic induction is the redistribution of charge within an object, caused by the influence of a nearby charge. In a conductor, free electrons are mobile and will move in response to an external electric field.

(ii) A wire is connected from object A to earth. State and explain any movement of charge.

Statement: Electrons (negative charges) will flow from the earth to object A.
Explanation: The positive rod B attracts electrons. The earth acts as a vast reservoir of electrons. The electrons are attracted from the earth, through the wire, to object A to neutralize the positive charge on the far side of A and accumulate on the side near rod B.

Recap

Earthing (or grounding) is the process of connecting an object to the earth with a conductor. This provides a path for charge to flow to or from the earth, which can be used to neutralize a charged object or as a safety measure.

An OR gate is a digital logic gate that gives a HIGH output (1) if at least one of its inputs is HIGH (1). Its symbol is a curved input side and a pointed output side.

The combination shows a NOT gate followed by an OR gate, with the input I connected to both one input of the OR gate and the input of the NOT gate. The output of the NOT gate is connected to the other input of the OR gate.
- If input I = 0: The top input to the OR gate is 0. The NOT gate inverts the 0 to a 1, so the bottom input to the OR gate is 1. OR(0, 1) = 1. Output O = 1.

(c) An electrical device has a metal case. Explain the benefit of earthing the metal case.

Earthing is a safety feature to prevent electrocution. If a fault causes a live wire to touch the metal case, the case would become live at a high voltage. The earth wire provides a low-resistance path for the current to flow to the ground. This causes a very large current to flow, which blows the fuse or trips the circuit breaker, disconnecting the power supply and making the appliance safe.

Recap

The three wires in a mains plug are the live wire (carries the high potential), the neutral wire (completes the circuit, near earth potential), and the earth wire (safety wire). The earth wire is connected to the metal casing of the appliance.

(d) (i) Explain how a fuse protects a circuit.

A fuse contains a thin piece of wire designed to have a specific melting point. If the current in the circuit becomes too high (due to a fault or surge), the wire in the fuse heats up and melts. This breaks the circuit, stopping the flow of current and protecting the appliance and user.

Recap

A fuse is a safety device consisting of a wire of low melting point. It is always placed in the live wire so that when it blows, the appliance is completely disconnected from the high voltage supply.

0625/42/O/N/21 Q11

(a) Describe the composition and structure of a neutral atom of beryllium-8, which has a proton number of 4 and a nucleon number of 8.

A neutral atom of beryllium-8 has a central, very small nucleus. This nucleus contains protons and neutrons. Specifically, it has a proton number of 4, so it contains 4 protons, and a nucleon number of 8, meaning it has 8 - 4 = 4 neutrons. In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons, so there are 4 electrons surrounding the nucleus in shells or orbits.

Recap

In nuclide notation (AZX), Z is the proton number (or atomic number), which defines the element. A is the nucleon number (or mass number), which is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus. The number of neutrons is A - Z. For a neutral atom, the number of electrons is equal to the proton number.

(b) A radioactive isotope decays by β-emission to form an isotope of barium with nucleon number 135. Use data from Table 11.1 to write down the nuclide equation for this decay.

During β-emission, a neutron in the nucleus decays into a proton and an electron. The proton stays in the nucleus, increasing the proton number by 1, while the electron is emitted. The nucleon number remains unchanged.
The product is barium-135. From the table, barium (Ba) has a proton number of 56.
Since the proton number increased by 1 to become 56, the original decaying isotope must have had a proton number of 55. From the table, the element with proton number 55 is caesium (Cs).
The nuclide equation for the decay is:
13555Cs → 13556Ba + 0-1e

Recap

Beta decay is a type of radioactive decay in which a beta particle (a fast, energetic electron or positron) is emitted from an atomic nucleus. In β⁻ decay, a neutron is converted into a proton, and an electron is emitted. This process increases the proton number by one, changing the element, but leaves the nucleon number unchanged.
