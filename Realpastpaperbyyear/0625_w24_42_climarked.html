Question 1 | 0625/42/O/N/24 Q1 | Topic: General Physics

(a) (i) A rocket has an initial mass of 7.4 × 10⁶ kg. Calculate the initial weight of the rocket.

The initial weight of the rocket is calculated using W = mg, where m = 7.4 × 10⁶ kg and g = 9.8 N/kg. So, W = 7.4 × 10⁶ kg × 9.8 N/kg = 7.3 × 10⁷ N.

Recap

Weight is the force of gravity acting on an object, measured in Newtons (N). It is distinct from mass, which is a measure of the amount of matter in an object.

(a) (ii) Define, in words, the term weight.

Weight is the gravitational force exerted on a mass or object.

Recap

The formula for weight is W = mg, where 'm' is mass and 'g' is the acceleration due to gravity. On Earth, 'g' is approximately 9.8 m/s².

(b) Fig. 1.1 shows part of the speed-time graph for the rocket as it leaves the ground and travels into space.

(i) Describe the motion of the rocket: From O to A; From A to B

From O to A, the rocket experiences increasing acceleration (the gradient of the graph is increasing). From A to B, the rocket experiences constant or uniform acceleration (the gradient is constant).

Recap

On a speed-time graph, the gradient represents acceleration. An increasing gradient means increasing acceleration, while a constant gradient means constant acceleration.

(b) (ii) Draw a tangent to the graph at time = 400s and use this to calculate the acceleration of the rocket at this time. Show your working.

Drawing a tangent at 400s and calculating its gradient (change in speed / change in time) should yield an acceleration between 17 m/s² and 23 m/s².

Recap

The instantaneous acceleration at any point on a speed-time graph can be found by calculating the gradient of the tangent to the curve at that specific point.

(c) Explain in terms of forces why the rocket reaches terminal velocity as it travels through the atmosphere back to the Earth.

As the rocket falls, its speed increases, causing the resistive forces (air resistance/drag) to increase. Eventually, the upward air resistance becomes equal in magnitude to the downward gravitational force (weight). At this point, the resultant force is zero, and the rocket falls at a constant speed called terminal velocity.

Recap

Terminal velocity is achieved when the net force on a falling object is zero, meaning the drag force balances the gravitational force. This results in zero acceleration and constant velocity.

Question 2 | 0625/42/O/N/24 Q2 | Topic: General Physics

Fig. 2.1 shows a golfer about to hit a golf ball with a golf club. The initial momentum of the golf ball is zero.

(a) Define momentum.

Momentum is defined as the product of an object's mass and its velocity (p = mv).

Recap

Momentum is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction. The principle of conservation of momentum states that in a closed system, the total momentum before a collision or explosion is equal to the total momentum after.

(b) The golf club is in contact with the ball for 5.0 × 10⁻⁴s. The velocity of the golf ball as it leaves the golf club is 41 m/s. The golf ball has a mass of 0.046 kg.

(i) Calculate the impulse on the golf ball.

Impulse is the change in momentum. Since initial momentum is zero, impulse = final momentum = mv = 0.046 kg × 41 m/s = 1.9 Ns (or kg m/s).

Recap

Impulse is also equal to the force applied multiplied by the time over which it acts (Impulse = FΔt). It is a measure of the effect of a force acting over time.

(ii) Calculate the force applied to the ball by the golf club.

Using Impulse = FΔt, we can find the force: F = Impulse / Δt = 1.9 Ns / (5.0 × 10⁻⁴ s) = 3800 N.

Recap

This calculation demonstrates the relationship between force, impulse, and change in momentum. A large force acting for a short time can produce a significant change in momentum.

Question 3 | 0625/42/O/N/24 Q3 | Topic: Energy, Work and Power

(a) State two energy resources for which radiation from the Sun is the main source of energy.

Two energy resources for which radiation from the Sun is the main source are solar panels (or solar cells) and wind energy (or wind turbines).

Recap

Other solar-derived energy resources include hydroelectric power (driven by the water cycle, powered by the sun), biofuels (from plants that photosynthesize), and fossil fuels (formed from ancient organic matter).

(b) A wind turbine is used to generate electricity. The useful output from the turbine in 1.0s is 6000 J. The kinetic energy of the wind hitting the turbine in 1.0 s is 11000 J. The velocity of the wind hitting the turbine is 6.3m/s.

(i) Show that the mass of air hitting the turbine each second is approximately 550 kg.

Using the kinetic energy formula E_k = 1/2 mv², we can rearrange to find mass: m = 2E_k / v². So, m = (2 × 11000 J) / (6.3 m/s)² = 22000 / 39.69 ≈ 554 kg, which is approximately 550 kg.

Recap

Kinetic energy is the energy an object possesses due to its motion. The formula E_k = 1/2 mv² shows that kinetic energy is directly proportional to mass and the square of velocity.

(ii) Calculate the efficiency of the turbines. You may assume that all the kinetic energy stored in the wind is transferred to the turbine.

Efficiency is calculated as (Useful energy output / Total energy input) × 100%. So, Efficiency = (6000 J / 11000 J) × 100% = 54.5% (or 55% to 2 s.f.).

Recap

Efficiency is a measure of how much useful energy is produced from the total energy input. No energy conversion process is 100% efficient due to energy losses, often as heat.

(c) Tidal energy and wind energy are both renewable energy resources. Suggest one reason why tidal energy is a more useful energy resource than wind energy. Ignore the costs of construction and maintenance.

Tidal energy is more useful because tides follow a regular and predictable pattern, unlike wind, which is intermittent and less predictable.

Recap

Renewable energy resources are naturally replenished on a human timescale. Predictability is a key advantage for energy sources, as it allows for more stable power generation and grid management.

Question 4 | 0625/42/O/N/24 Q4 | Topic: Thermal Physics

Fig. 4.1 shows a pressure cooker on an electric heating element. The cooker has a tight-fitting lid.

(a) The pressure cooker is half-full of water. As the water is heated some water evaporates before the water boils. Describe two differences between evaporation and boiling of the water in the cooker.

1. Evaporation occurs at any temperature below the boiling point, while boiling occurs at a specific temperature (the boiling point).

2. Evaporation occurs only at the surface of the liquid, whereas boiling occurs throughout the liquid.

Recap

Both evaporation and boiling are processes of vaporization, but they differ in their conditions and mechanisms. Evaporation is a slower, cooling process, while boiling is a rapid process that requires a continuous supply of heat.

(b) As the water is heated, the pressure of the gas inside the cooker increases. Explain this increase in pressure in terms of particles.

As the water is heated, the water particles gain kinetic energy and move faster. This leads to a greater number of collisions per unit time with the walls of the cooker. Each collision also exerts a greater force on the walls, resulting in an overall increase in pressure.

Recap

This explanation is based on the kinetic theory of gases, which describes gases as collections of particles in constant, random motion. Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of these particles.

Question 5 | 0625/42/O/N/24 Q5 | Topic: Thermal Physics

On a sunny day, the temperatures of a black tarmac road and the air above the road increase.

(a) Explain why the surface temperature of the tarmac increases.

The surface temperature of the tarmac increases because it absorbs infrared radiation (or radiation) emitted from the Sun.

Recap

Infrared radiation is a form of electromagnetic radiation that transfers heat. Dark, dull surfaces are good absorbers of radiation, while light, shiny surfaces are good reflectors.

(b) State the method of thermal energy transfer from the tarmac to the air immediately above the road.

The method of thermal energy transfer from the tarmac to the air immediately above the road is conduction.

Recap

Conduction is the transfer of thermal energy through direct contact between particles. It is the primary mode of heat transfer in solids.

(c) State the main method of thermal energy transfer from the air immediately above the road to the rest of the air.

The main method of thermal energy transfer from the air immediately above the road to the rest of the air is convection.

Recap

Convection is the transfer of thermal energy through the movement of fluids (liquids or gases). Hotter, less dense fluid rises, and cooler, denser fluid sinks, creating convection currents.

(d) Explain why the surface temperature of the tarmac is higher than the surrounding air temperature.

The tarmac is a better absorber of radiation than air. Additionally, air is a poor emitter of radiation at low temperatures, and the tarmac forms a thin layer with a large surface area exposed to radiation.

Recap

Different materials have different thermal properties. Good absorbers of radiation tend to heat up more quickly when exposed to a heat source. The specific heat capacity of a material also plays a role in how much its temperature changes for a given amount of absorbed energy.

Question 6 | 0625/42/O/N/24 Q6 | Topic: Waves

A student plays the violin near the doorway to a large room. Fig. 6.1 shows a young teacher standing where he can hear the sound but cannot see the student.

(a) (i) State the wave effect that allows the young teacher to hear sounds from the violin at the position he is standing in Fig. 6.1.

The wave effect that allows the teacher to hear the sound is diffraction.

Recap

Diffraction is the spreading of waves as they pass through an opening or around an obstacle. Sound waves, having relatively long wavelengths, diffract easily around corners and through doorways.

(a) (ii) Calculate the frequency of sound with a wavelength of 0.75 m. The speed of sound in air is 340m/s.

Using the wave equation v = fλ, the frequency f = v / λ = 340 m/s / 0.75 m = 453 Hz (or 450 Hz to 2 s.f.).

Recap

The wave equation is a fundamental relationship in wave physics. It applies to all types of waves, including sound waves and electromagnetic waves.

(a) (iii) A violin produces sounds in the frequency range 200 Hz-3800 Hz. The width of the open doorway is 0.75 m. Explain why the young teacher hears the frequency calculated in (a)(ii) clearly but finds a frequency of 3500 Hz much harder to hear.

The frequency of 453 Hz has a wavelength (0.75 m) that is similar to the width of the doorway (0.75 m), leading to large diffraction. The 3500 Hz frequency has a much shorter wavelength, resulting in less diffraction and making it harder to hear around the corner.

Recap

The extent of diffraction is inversely proportional to the ratio of the obstacle size to the wavelength. Significant diffraction occurs when the wavelength is comparable to or larger than the obstacle/aperture size.

(b) A plane mirror is placed at point X so that the teacher can see the student. On Fig. 6.1: draw a light ray from the violin to point X and from point X to the teacher; draw and label the mirror; add an arrow to the ray to show how the teacher sees the student.

The diagram should show a ray drawn from the violin to point X and then from point X to the teacher. A mirror should be drawn and labelled at point X. An arrow on the ray should indicate the direction of light from the violin to the teacher, with the angle of incidence equal to the angle of reflection.

Recap

The Law of Reflection states that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. This principle is crucial for understanding how mirrors work and how light travels.

Question 7 | 0625/42/O/N/24 Q7 | Topic: Electricity and Magnetism

A washing machine has an electric motor and an electric heater. Fig. 7.1 shows a simplified circuit diagram for the washing machine.

The heater has a resistance of 25Ω and the power supply has an electromotive force (e.m.f.) of 230 V.

(a) State the meaning of electromotive force.

Electromotive force (e.m.f.) is the electrical work done by a source in moving a unit charge around a complete circuit.

Recap

E.m.f. is measured in Volts (V) and represents the energy provided by the source per unit charge. It is the driving force that pushes current through a circuit.

(b) State the potential difference (p.d.) across the heater.

Assuming the heater is the only component in the circuit or is in parallel with other components, the potential difference across the heater is equal to the supply e.m.f., which is 230 V.

Recap

In a simple series circuit, the potential difference across a component is a fraction of the total e.m.f. In a parallel circuit, the potential difference across each branch is equal to the total e.m.f. (assuming ideal wires).

(c) Calculate the current in the heater.

Using Ohm's Law, I = V / R. So, I = 230 V / 25 Ω = 9.2 A.

Recap

Ohm's Law states that the current through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage across the two points. The constant of proportionality is the resistance.

(d) The current in the motor is 1.6A. Determine the reading on the ammeter in Fig. 7.1. Explain your answer.

The ammeter reading will be the sum of the current in the heater and the current in the motor, assuming they are in parallel. So, 9.2 A + 1.6 A = 10.8 A. This is because the sum of currents into a junction equals the sum of currents out of the junction (Kirchhoff's First Law).

Recap

Kirchhoff's Current Law (or Junction Rule) is based on the conservation of charge. It states that the total current entering a junction must equal the total current leaving it.

Question 8 | 0625/42/O/N/24 Q8 | Topic: Waves

A fisherman uses high frequency sound waves to locate fish in the sea. Fig. 8.1 shows the sound waves emitted from the boat.

(a) State the name of sound waves which have a frequency greater than 20 kHz.

Sound waves with a frequency greater than 20 kHz are called ultrasound.

Recap

Ultrasound has many applications, including medical imaging, industrial testing, and sonar (as in this question). Its high frequency allows for better resolution in imaging.

(b) High frequency sound waves travel from the boat through the sea water. The speed of sound in water is 1500m/s. The seabed is 22m below the boat. Calculate the time taken for the boat to receive the reflected wave from the seabed after the sound is emitted.

The total distance travelled by the sound wave is 2 × 22 m = 44 m. Using time = distance / speed, time = 44 m / 1500 m/s = 0.029 s.

Recap

This is an application of sonar (Sound Navigation and Ranging). The time taken for an echo to return can be used to calculate distances, which is crucial for navigation and mapping underwater features.

(c) Fig. 8.2 shows a fish below the boat. Describe and explain how the reflected sound wave received by the boat from the fish differs from the reflected sound wave received from the seabed.

The reflected sound wave from the fish will be weaker and have a smaller amplitude compared to the reflection from the seabed. This is because the fish is a smaller object, so only a small part of the sound wave reflects off it, with most of the sound continuing to the seabed.

Recap

The amplitude of a wave is related to its energy. When a wave reflects off a smaller or less dense object, less energy is reflected, resulting in a smaller amplitude reflected wave.

Fig. 9.1 shows a wireless charging plate used to charge the battery in a mobile phone (cell phone). The coil of wire is part of an electric circuit.

The charging plate is connected to an a.c. power supply. The power supply is turned on.

(a) Describe the magnetic field around the charging plate in terms of its magnitude and direction.

The magnetic field around the charging plate has a constantly changing magnitude and constantly changing direction. It is strongest closer to the coil of wire and is perpendicular to the alternating current producing it.

Recap

An alternating current (a.c.) produces a magnetic field that continuously changes in both strength and direction. This changing magnetic field is essential for electromagnetic induction, which is the principle behind wireless charging.

(b) A mobile phone is placed on the charging plate as shown in Fig. 9.2. The coil in the mobile phone is part of a separate circuit that charges the battery.

The coil in the charging plate and the coil in the mobile phone act like a transformer.

(i) Explain why there is a current in the secondary coil shown in Fig. 9.2.

There is a current in the secondary coil because the changing magnetic field produced by the primary coil (in the charging plate) passes through the secondary coil (in the phone). This induces a voltage and thus a current in the secondary coil, a process known as electromagnetic induction.

Recap

Faraday's Law of Induction states that a changing magnetic flux through a coil induces an electromotive force (e.m.f.). This induced e.m.f. drives the induced current.

(ii) Suggest why the transformer made from the charging plate and mobile phone is not 100% efficient.

The transformer is not 100% efficient because there is no iron core to strengthen the magnetic field, and some energy is transferred to thermal energy in the phone and surroundings due to resistance and eddy currents.

Recap

Ideal transformers are 100% efficient, but real transformers always have energy losses due to factors like resistance in the coils, eddy currents in the core, and magnetic flux leakage.

Question 10 | 0625/42/O/N/24 Q10 | Topic: Atomic Physics

Carbon-14 (¹⁴₆C) is a radioactive isotope of carbon. Carbon-12 (¹²₆C) is not radioactive.

(a) Explain how an atom of carbon-14 (¹⁴₆C) differs from an atom of carbon-12 (¹²₆C).

An atom of carbon-14 differs from carbon-12 by having two more neutrons in its nucleus. Both have 6 protons and 6 electrons, but carbon-14 has 8 neutrons (14-6) while carbon-12 has 6 neutrons (12-6).

Recap

Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same number of protons) that have different numbers of neutrons. This difference in neutron count leads to different mass numbers.

(b) All living organisms contain both carbon-12 atoms and carbon-14 atoms. The ratio of carbon-14 to carbon-12 is 1:1 x 10¹². Carbon-14 has a half-life of 5700 years.

(i) When an organism dies no new carbon is absorbed. The amount of carbon-12 in the dead organism remains fixed. Describe how the amount of carbon-14 in the dead organism decreases with time.

The amount of carbon-14 in the dead organism decreases by half every 5700 years (its half-life). This decrease occurs at a decreasing rate over time, as there are fewer radioactive nuclei remaining to decay.

Recap

Radioactive decay is a random process where unstable atomic nuclei spontaneously transform into more stable forms, emitting radiation. The half-life is a characteristic property of each radioactive isotope.

(ii) A sample of wood contains carbon-14 to carbon-12 atoms in the ratio 1:4 × 10¹². Calculate how many years ago the tree died.

The initial ratio was 1:1 × 10¹². The current ratio is 1:4 × 10¹². This means the amount of C-14 has reduced to 1/4 of its original amount. Since 1/4 = (1/2)², two half-lives have passed. So, the time passed is 2 × 5700 years = 11400 years.

Recap

Carbon dating uses the known half-life of carbon-14 to determine the age of organic materials. By comparing the remaining C-14 to the initial amount, the number of half-lives that have passed can be calculated.

(c) Other radioactive isotopes have different half-lives. Suggest a use of a radioactive isotope with a half-life of one hour. Explain why a short half-life is suitable for this use.

A radioactive isotope with a half-life of one hour could be used as a medical tracer or for medical imaging. A short half-life is suitable because it ensures the isotope does not stay in the body too long, minimizing the radiation dose and potential harm to the patient.

Recap

Medical tracers are radioactive isotopes introduced into the body to track physiological processes or diagnose diseases. Their short half-life is crucial for patient safety, as it reduces long-term exposure to radiation.

Question 11 | 0625/42/O/N/24 Q11 | Topic: Astrophysics

(a) (i) State the name of one planet that has an orbit further away from the Sun than Venus.

One planet that has an orbit further away from the Sun than Venus is Earth (or Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune).

Recap

The Solar System consists of the Sun and all the celestial bodies gravitationally bound to it. The planets orbit the Sun in elliptical paths, with their distances from the Sun determining their orbital periods.

(a) (ii) State the name of one planet that has an orbit closer to the Sun than Venus.

One planet that has an orbit closer to the Sun than Venus is Mercury.

Recap

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun and has the shortest orbital period. Its proximity to the Sun results in extreme temperature variations between day and night.

(b) Venus has an average radius of orbit of 1.1 × 10¹¹ m and an orbital period of 220 Earth days. Calculate the average orbital speed of Venus. Give your answer in m/s.

First, convert the orbital period to seconds: 220 days × 24 hours/day × 60 minutes/hour × 60 seconds/minute = 19008000 s. The circumference of the orbit is 2πr = 2π × 1.1 × 10¹¹ m = 6.91 × 10¹¹ m. Average orbital speed = distance / time = 6.91 × 10¹¹ m / 19008000 s ≈ 3.6 × 10⁴ m/s.

Recap

The orbital speed of a planet can be calculated by dividing the circumference of its orbit by its orbital period. This assumes a circular orbit, which is a good approximation for most planets.

(c) State the relationship between the orbital speeds of the planets and their distances from the Sun.

Planets closer to the Sun have greater orbital speeds, while planets further away have smaller orbital speeds.

Recap

This relationship is a consequence of Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion and Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation. Planets closer to the Sun experience a stronger gravitational pull, requiring higher speeds to maintain their orbits.

(d) Comets are balls of ice and dust. Some comets orbit the Sun. State how the speed of a comet changes as it orbits the Sun. Explain your answer using ideas about the conservation of energy. You may include a labelled diagram in your answer.

As a comet approaches the Sun, its speed increases, and as it moves away, its speed decreases. This is due to the conservation of energy. As the comet gets closer to the Sun, its gravitational potential energy decreases, which is converted into an increase in kinetic energy, thus increasing its speed. Conversely, as it moves away, kinetic energy is converted back into gravitational potential energy.

Recap

The conservation of mechanical energy (kinetic + potential) applies to objects in orbit. In an elliptical orbit, the speed of the orbiting body changes as its distance from the central body changes.
