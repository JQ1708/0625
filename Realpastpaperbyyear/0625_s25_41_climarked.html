Question Number: 1 | Paper Code: 0625/41/M/J/25 Q1 | Topic(s): Kinematics

1 (a) Circle the vector quantities in the list.

acceleration mass speed time velocity

The vector quantities in the list are acceleration and velocity.

Recap

A vector quantity is a physical quantity that has both magnitude and direction. A scalar quantity only has magnitude.

1 (b) Fig. 1.1 shows the speed-time graph for a train travelling from station A to station B.

(i) State the maximum speed of the train.

The maximum speed of the train is 56 m/s.

Recap

On a speed-time graph, the maximum speed is the highest point reached on the y-axis.

(ii) Describe the motion of the train between station A and station B.

The train first accelerates, then moves at a constant speed, and finally decelerates.

Recap

On a speed-time graph, the gradient represents acceleration. A positive gradient means acceleration, a zero gradient means constant speed, and a negative gradient means deceleration.

(iii) Calculate the distance between station A and station B.

The distance is 26 000 m (or 26 km). This is calculated by finding the area under the speed-time graph.

Recap

The area under a speed-time graph represents the distance travelled.

(iv) On a different day, the train takes 650s to travel between station A and station B. Suggest one change to the motion of the train that leads to this longer journey time.

A possible change is a lower maximum speed, lower acceleration, or lower deceleration.

Recap

Any factor that reduces the overall average speed will increase the journey time for the same distance.

Question Number: 2 | Paper Code: 0625/41/M/J/25 Q2 | Topic(s): Forces

2 (a) (i) State two different changes to the motion that the resultant force may cause.

A resultant force can cause a change in the direction of motion or a change in the speed of the object.

Recap

A resultant force causes a change in velocity (acceleration), which can be a change in speed, direction, or both.

(ii) State one other way that forces may change a stationary object.

A force can change the shape or size of a stationary object.

Recap

Forces can cause deformation. This can be elastic (temporary) or inelastic (permanent).

2 (b) Describe how a uniform metre ruler, a pivot and a selection of masses can be used to demonstrate that there is no resultant moment on an object in equilibrium.

First, place the metre ruler on the pivot at its center. Then, add a mass on one side and another mass on the other side to balance it. The moment is the force × perpendicular distance from the pivot. For equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments must equal the sum of anticlockwise moments.

Recap

The Principle of Moments is a key concept for understanding rotational equilibrium.

Question Number: 3 | Paper Code: 0625/41/M/J/25 Q3 | Topic(s): Energy, Work, Power

3 (a) A skateboarder is initially at rest at point A. The skateboarder then travels through point B and comes to rest at point C.

Describe the transfer of energy as the skateboarder travels from A to B to C along the concrete track.

From A to B, gravitational potential energy is converted to kinetic energy. From B to C, kinetic energy is converted back to gravitational potential energy and also to thermal energy.

Recap

The conservation of energy principle states that energy is transformed, not lost. Here, some mechanical energy is lost to heat due to friction.

3 (b) (i) B is at ground level and C is at 2.8 m above ground level. The mass of the skateboarder is 65 kg. Calculate the work done on the skateboarder as she travels from B to C.

The work done is 1800 J. It is calculated as Work Done = mass × g × height = 65 kg × 9.8 N/kg × 2.8 m.

Recap

Work done against gravity is equal to the gain in gravitational potential energy (GPE).

(ii) The skateboarder falls off the skateboard at B. She hits the track and comes to rest after a few milliseconds. State the equation that defines the force F with which the skateboarder hits the track. State the meaning of any symbols you use.

The equation is F = Δp / Δt. F is the force, Δp is the change in momentum, and Δt is the time taken.

Recap

This is from Newton's Second Law. A large force results from a rapid change in momentum.

Question Number: 4 | Paper Code: 0625/41/M/J/25 Q4 | Topic(s): Thermal Physics

Fig. 4.1 shows a heater used to warm the air in a room.

4 (a) (i) State the main method of thermal energy transfer throughout the air in the room.

The main method of thermal energy transfer is convection.

Recap

Convection is the transfer of heat through fluids (liquids or gases) by the movement of the heated particles.

(ii) Explain how the heater warms all the air in the room.

The heater warms the air, making it less dense, so it rises. Cooler, denser air sinks, creating a convection current that circulates warm air.

Recap

A convection current is a continuous cycle of fluid movement driven by temperature differences.

4 (b) (i) The power of the heater is 2.0kW when it is connected to the mains supply with an e.m.f. of 230V. Show that the current in the heater is approximately 8.7A.

Using P = IV, I = P/V = 2000W / 230V = 8.7A.

Recap

Electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is converted to other forms of energy.

(ii) The plug connecting the heater to the mains supply is fitted with a fuse. Fuse ratings of 3A, 5A, 10A and 13A are available. State which fuse is used. Explain your answer.

A 10A fuse is used. It is slightly higher than the operating current (8.7A) to prevent nuisance blowing but low enough to protect the circuit.

Recap

A fuse is a safety device that melts and breaks the circuit if the current exceeds a safe level.

Question Number: 10 | Paper Code: 0625/41/M/J/25 Q10 | Topic(s): Gravitation, Astronomy

Jupiter and the Earth are planets in our Solar System.

10 (a) Describe the composition of Jupiter and the Earth.

Jupiter is a gaseous planet made mostly of hydrogen and helium, whereas the Earth is a rocky planet formed from silicate rocks and metallic cores.

Recap

The reference guide explains that outer gas giants retain light gases like hydrogen, while inner terrestrial planets are dominated by rock and iron, giving them contrasting densities.

(b) The gravitational field strength at the surface of the Earth is approximately 9.8 N / kg. The gravitational field strength at the surface of Jupiter is approximately 23 N / kg.

(i) Define gravitational field strength.

Gravitational field strength is the force per unit mass experienced by a small test mass placed in the field (g = F / m).

Recap

The textbook defines gravitational field strength as the weight of 1 kg at that location, so values are larger for stronger gravitational forces.

(ii) State one factor which causes the difference between the gravitational field strength at the surface of Jupiter and the gravitational field strength at the surface of the Earth.

statement ..................................................................................................................................

explanation ...............................................................................................................................

Statement: Jupiter has a far greater mass than the Earth.

Explanation: The larger mass produces a stronger gravitational field at its surface, so each kilogram experiences a larger weight.

Recap

According to the gravitation chapter, surface g increases with planetary mass (and decreases with radius), so giants like Jupiter exert stronger pulls than smaller terrestrial planets.

(c) State and explain the difference between the orbital speed of Jupiter and the orbital speed of the Earth.

Jupiter has a lower orbital speed than Earth because it orbits further from the Sun, where the Sun’s gravitational pull is weaker and the required centripetal speed is smaller.

Recap

The Solar System section notes that planetary orbital speed decreases with increasing orbital radius, following the relationship described by Kepler’s laws and circular motion.
