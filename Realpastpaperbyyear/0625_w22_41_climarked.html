Question 1 | 0625/41/O/N/22 | Topics: Mechanics, Energy, Momentum

Two blocks, A and B, are joined by a thin thread that passes over a frictionless pulley. Block A is at rest on a rough horizontal surface and block B is held at rest, just below the pulley.

Block B is released and it falls vertically. The thread remains loose until block B has fallen a distance of 0.45 m.

The mass of block B is 0.50 kg.

(a) Calculate the change in the gravitational potential energy (g.p.e.) of block B as it falls through 0.45 m.

The change in gravitational potential energy is calculated using the formula ΔG.P.E = mgh. Given the mass of block B (0.50 kg), the acceleration of free fall (10 m/s²), and the height (0.45 m), the change in g.p.e. is 2.3 J. The calculation is (0.50 kg) x (10 N/kg) x (0.45 m).

Recap

Gravitational Potential Energy (G.P.E.) is the energy stored in an object due to its position in a gravitational field. It is the work done to lift the object to that height. The formula is G.P.E. = mgh, where 'm' is mass, 'g' is the gravitational field strength, and 'h' is the vertical height.

(b) The mass of block A is 2.0 kg.

(i) When the thread tightens, it pulls on block A which moves to the right at a speed of 0.60 m/s. Calculate the impulse exerted on block A as it accelerates from rest to 0.60 m/s.

The impulse is the change in momentum. Using the formula I = Δp = m(v-u), for block A with a mass of 2.0 kg accelerating from rest (0 m/s) to 0.60 m/s, the impulse is 1.2 Ns. The calculation is (2.0 kg) x (0.60 m/s - 0 m/s).

Recap

Impulse is defined as the product of a force and the time for which it acts (Impulse = F x t). It is also equal to the change in momentum of the object. This relationship is fundamental in understanding collisions and interactions.

(ii) Both of the blocks now move at a constant speed of 0.60 m/s until block B hits the ground and the thread becomes loose. Explain the energy change that takes place in block A after block B stops moving.

After block B stops, the kinetic energy of block A decreases as it slows down. This is because work is done against the frictional force between the block and the rough surface. The kinetic energy is converted into thermal energy (heat), causing the temperature of the block and the surface to increase slightly.

Recap

The principle of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or transformed. When friction acts, it does negative work, converting kinetic energy into internal or thermal energy, which is dissipated as heat.

Question 2 | 0625/41/O/N/22 | Topics: Mechanics, Forces, Hooke's Law

A force is a vector quantity.

(a) (i) State two features of a vector quantity.

A vector quantity has both magnitude (or size) and direction.

Recap

Physical quantities can be classified as scalars or vectors. A scalar quantity has only magnitude (size), for example, distance, speed, and mass. A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction. Examples include displacement, velocity, and force.

(a) (ii) State the names of two other quantities that are vectors.

Two other vector quantities are velocity and acceleration. Others include momentum, impulse, and weight.

(b) A student suspends a spring from a clamp stand and measures the length l₀ of the spring. Fig. 2.1 shows the apparatus. The student then suspends loads of different weights from the spring and measures the length of the spring for each load. He then plots a graph of the length of the spring against weight. Fig. 2.2 is the graph that the student plots.

(i) Using Fig. 2.2, determine the initial length l₀ of the spring.

The initial length of the spring (with no load) is the value on the y-axis when the weight (x-axis) is zero. From the graph, this is 0.12 m.

(ii) State what is meant by the limit of proportionality and, using Fig. 2.2, determine the weight of the load that causes this spring just to reach the limit of proportionality.

The limit of proportionality is the point beyond which the extension of the spring is no longer directly proportional to the applied load. On the graph, this is the point where the straight line begins to curve. This occurs at a weight of approximately 10.6 N.

(iii) Using Fig. 2.2, determine the spring constant of this spring.

The spring constant (k) is the gradient of the straight-line portion of the graph (k = ΔF/Δx). Using a point from the linear region, for example, a load of 8.0 N, the length is 0.54 m. The extension is 0.54 m - 0.12 m = 0.42 m. The spring constant is k = 8.0 N / 0.42 m ≈ 19 N/m. (Note: The mark scheme allows a range, this is one valid calculation).

Recap

Hooke's Law states that the extension of a spring is directly proportional to the load or force applied, as long as the limit of proportionality is not exceeded. The formula is F = kx, where 'F' is the force, 'x' is the extension, and 'k' is the spring constant, a measure of the spring's stiffness.

Question 3 | 0625/41/O/N/22 | Topics: Mechanics, Forces, Moments, Equilibrium

A rock climber, of total mass 62 kg, holds herself in horizontal equilibrium against a vertical cliff. She pulls on a rope that is fixed at the top of the cliff and presses her feet against the cliff. Fig. 3.1 shows her position.

(a) Calculate the total weight of the climber.

Weight is calculated using the formula W = mg. Given the climber's mass of 62 kg and taking g as 10 N/kg, her weight is 620 N.

(b) State the two conditions needed for equilibrium.

For an object to be in equilibrium, two conditions must be met:

  1. There must be no resultant force acting on the object.
  2. There must be no resultant moment about any pivot.

Recap

Mass is the amount of matter in an object (in kg). Weight is the force of gravity on that mass (in N). For an object to be in equilibrium, all forces must be balanced, resulting in no change in motion (no translational or rotational acceleration).

(c) (i) The climber’s centre of mass is 0.90 m from the cliff. Calculate the moment about her feet due to her weight.

A moment is the turning effect of a force. It is calculated as Moment = Force × perpendicular distance from the pivot. The climber's weight (620 N) acts at a perpendicular distance of 0.90 m from the pivot (her feet). The moment is 620 N × 0.90 m = 558 Nm.

(c) (ii) The line of the rope meets the horizontal line through her centre of mass at a distance of 1.2 m from the cliff, as shown in Fig. 3.1. The rope is at an angle of 60° to the horizontal. Determine the tension in the rope.

According to the Principle of Moments, for the climber to be in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments must equal the sum of anticlockwise moments. The weight creates a clockwise moment. The tension in the rope creates an anticlockwise moment. The perpendicular distance for the tension is 1.2 m * sin(60°).
Clockwise moment = Anticlockwise moment
558 Nm = Tension × (1.2 m × sin 60°)
Tension = 558 / (1.2 × 0.866) ≈ 538 N.

Recap

A moment is the turning effect of a force, calculated as Moment = Force × perpendicular distance from the pivot. The Principle of Moments states that for an object to be in rotational equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point must equal the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point.

Question 4 | 0625/41/O/N/22 | Topics: Thermal Physics, Pressure, Work Done, Kinetic Theory

A quantity of gas is trapped by a piston in a cylinder with thin metal walls. The piston is free to move without friction within the cylinder. Fig. 4.1 shows the cylinder and piston. The cylinder is placed inside a freezer.

(a) (i) The air in the freezer is at atmospheric pressure, which is 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. The area of the piston in contact with the air in the freezer is 2.4 × 10⁻³ m². Calculate the force exerted on the piston by the air in the freezer.

Force is calculated from pressure and area using the formula F = P × A. The force is (1.0 × 10⁵ Pa) × (2.4 × 10⁻³ m²) = 240 N.

Recap

Pressure is defined as the normal force exerted per unit area (P = F/A). The SI unit for pressure is the Pascal (Pa), which is equivalent to one newton per square metre (N/m²).

(a) (ii) When the cylinder is first placed into the freezer, the temperature of the gas in the cylinder decreases and the air pushes the piston into the cylinder. Calculate the work done on the piston by the air in the freezer as the air pushes the piston a distance of 0.021 m into the cylinder.

Work done is calculated using the formula W = F × d. The work done is 240 N × 0.021 m = 5.04 J.

Recap

Work is done when a force causes displacement. It is a measure of energy transfer. The unit of work is the Joule (J), where 1 Joule of work is done when a force of 1 Newton moves an object by 1 metre.

(b) The initial temperature of the cylinder and the gas is 21 °C and, in the freezer, the temperature of the cylinder decreases to –18 °C. The thermal capacity of the cylinder is 89 J / °C. Calculate the change in the internal energy of the cylinder.

The change in internal energy is calculated using the formula ΔE = CΔθ. The temperature change is 21 °C - (–18 °C) = 39 °C. The change in internal energy is 89 J/°C × 39 °C = 3471 J (or 3.5 × 10³ J).

Recap

Thermal capacity (or heat capacity) is the heat energy required to change the temperature of a substance by one degree. Internal energy refers to the total energy contained within a system, associated with the random, disordered motion of molecules.

(c) When the temperature reaches –18 °C, the pressure of the gas in the cylinder is still equal to that of the atmosphere. Explain, in terms of the particles of the gas, how the pressure remains equal to its original value.

The pressure is a result of collisions of gas particles with the container walls. As the temperature decreases, the particles move slower and collide with the walls less violently and less often. However, the volume of the gas has also decreased, so the particles are closer together and collide with the walls more frequently. These two effects—less forceful collisions and more frequent collisions—cancel each other out, allowing the pressure to remain constant.

(d) As the temperature of the metal cylinder decreases, the volume of the metal decreases. The decrease in the volume of the metal is much less than the decrease in the volume of the gas. Explain, in terms of the particles of the metal, why the decrease in the volume of the metal is less than that of the gas.

In a solid metal, the particles are held closely together in a fixed lattice by strong intermolecular forces. They can only vibrate about their fixed positions. In a gas, the particles are far apart with negligible forces between them. Therefore, a change in temperature has a much smaller effect on the volume of the solid compared to the gas.

Recap

The Kinetic Theory of Matter explains the properties of solids, liquids, and gases based on the arrangement and movement of their particles. Gas particles are far apart and move randomly. Solid particles are tightly packed in a regular structure and vibrate in fixed positions due to strong bonds.

Question 5 | 0625/41/O/N/22 | Topics: Thermal Physics, Electromagnetic Spectrum, Radiation

Fig. 5.1 shows a heater in a bathroom. The heater is at a very high temperature and it glows red. The manufacturer states: "The heater emits light and radiation and it transfers thermal energy by radiation."

(a) State the part of the electromagnetic spectrum that transfers thermal energy.

The part of the electromagnetic spectrum primarily responsible for transferring thermal energy is infrared radiation.

(b) (i) State one way in which visible light and the radiation identified in (a) are similar.

Both visible light and infrared radiation are types of electromagnetic waves. They are also both transverse waves and travel at the same high speed in a vacuum (the speed of light).

(b) (ii) State one way in which visible light differs from the radiation identified in (a).

Visible light has a shorter wavelength and a higher frequency than infrared radiation.

Recap

The electromagnetic spectrum is a continuous range of waves, from radio waves to gamma rays. All electromagnetic waves are transverse, travel at the speed of light in a vacuum, and can travel through a vacuum. They differ in wavelength and frequency.

(c) (i) Some surfaces are better at emitting radiation than others. Describe an experiment to show whether a black surface or a white surface is the better emitter of radiation. You may draw a diagram.

An experiment can be done using two identical containers, one with a dull black surface and the other with a shiny white surface. Both are filled with the same amount of hot water at the same initial temperature. A thermometer is placed in each. The temperature of each container is recorded at regular time intervals. The container that cools down faster is the better emitter of thermal radiation. The results will show the black container cools faster.

(c) (ii) To ensure that the conclusion reached in the experiment in (c)(i) is correct, several details of the experiment must be identical when testing the two different surfaces. State two quantities in the experiment that you described that must be identical during the test.

To ensure a fair test, several variables must be controlled. Two such quantities are:

  1. The initial temperature of the water in both containers.
  2. The volume (or mass) of water in both containers.

Other valid quantities include the surface area and shape of the containers.

Recap

The rate at which an object radiates thermal energy depends on its surface colour and texture. Dull, black surfaces are good emitters and good absorbers of thermal radiation. Shiny, white surfaces are poor emitters and poor absorbers (and good reflectors). A Leslie's cube is a piece of apparatus specifically designed to demonstrate this principle.

Question 6 | 0625/41/O/N/22 | Topics: Light, Optics, Refraction, Total Internal Reflection

The red light produced by a laser is monochromatic.

(a) State what is meant by monochromatic.

Monochromatic light is light of a single frequency and therefore a single wavelength (and colour).

(b) The red light from the laser hits the curved surface of a semicircular transparent plastic block at point P and passes into the plastic. The red light travels through the plastic and hits the straight edge of the block at its midpoint M. Fig. 6.1 shows that some of the light is reflected and that some light travels in the air along the straight edge of the plastic block.

(i) Explain why the red light does not change direction as it enters the plastic block.

The light ray strikes the curved surface at a right angle (90°) to the tangent at that point. This means it is travelling along the normal, so its angle of incidence is 0°. When the angle of incidence is zero, light does not refract (change direction).

(ii) At M, the angle between the red light in the plastic and the normal is 37°. The speed of light in air is 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Calculate the speed of the red light in the plastic.

The light travels along the straight edge, which means the angle of refraction is 90°. This means the angle of incidence in the plastic (37°) is the critical angle (c). The refractive index (n) is n = 1 / sin(c) = 1 / sin(37°) ≈ 1.66. The speed of light in the plastic is v = (speed in air) / n = (3.0 × 10⁸ m/s) / 1.66 ≈ 1.8 × 10⁸ m/s.

(iii) In the plastic, blue light travels slightly slower than red light and so the critical angle for blue light is smaller than the critical angle for red light. The laser that emits red light is replaced by one that emits blue light. Now blue light enters the block at P and hits the straight edge at M. Explain what happens to the blue light after it hits the straight edge at M.

The blue light hits the boundary at the same angle of incidence, 37°. We are told the critical angle for blue light is smaller than for red light (c_blue < 37°). Because the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle for blue light, the light will undergo total internal reflection. It will reflect back into the plastic block at an angle of reflection of 37°.

Recap

Refraction is the bending of light as it passes from one medium to another due to a change in speed. The refractive index (n) is a measure of how much a substance slows down light. When light travels from a denser to a less dense medium, if the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle (c), the light undergoes total internal reflection, where all the light is reflected back into the denser medium. The critical angle is related to the refractive index by the formula n = 1/sin(c).

Question 7 | 0625/41/O/N/22 | Topics: Electricity, Electromagnetism, DC Circuits, Relays

The electric starter motor in a car is switched on and off using a relay. The relay consists of a plastic case and two flexible springy strips, X and Y, which are made of soft iron. These iron strips act as the switch when a circuit is connected between the terminals W and Z. Fig. 7.1 shows X, Y and the plastic case. Fig. 7.2 shows the equipment from Fig. 7.1 inside a magnetising coil. The magnetising coil is in series with the 12V car battery and switch S, which is open.

(a) Switch S is now closed. Explain what happens to the springy iron strips X and Y.

When switch S is closed, a current flows through the magnetising coil, turning it into an electromagnet. This induces magnetism in the soft iron strips X and Y. The strips are magnetised with opposite poles facing each other, so they attract. This attraction pulls them together, causing them to touch and close the switch for the starter motor circuit.

Recap

A relay is an electrically operated switch. It uses a small current in one circuit (the coil circuit) to switch on or off a much larger current in another circuit (the contact circuit). When the small current flows through a coil, it creates an electromagnet which attracts an iron armature, closing the contacts in the high-current circuit. This provides safety and control.

(b) (i) The power of the starter motor is 1.8 kW and it is also operated by the car battery. Calculate the current in the starter motor when it is used.

Using the formula for electrical power, P = VI, the current is I = P / V. The power is 1.8 kW = 1800 W, and the voltage is 12 V. The current is 1800 W / 12 V = 150 A.

(b) (ii) The starter motor circuit is connected between terminals W and Z. Explain why copper wires with a large cross-sectional area are used for this circuit.

The starter motor draws a very large current (150 A). A wire with a large cross-sectional area has a low resistance. This is important because a lower resistance minimises the amount of heat generated (power loss = I²R) in the wires, preventing them from overheating or melting.

Recap

Electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred, calculated as P = VI (Power = Voltage × Current). Resistance is a measure of the opposition to current flow. A thick wire has a lower resistance than a thin wire of the same material, allowing a large current to flow without overheating, as less heat energy (P = I²R) is dissipated.

(c) Fig. 7.3 shows the relay and the symbols for the car battery and the starter motor. The springy iron strips X and Y act as the switch for the starter motor circuit. Complete the circuit diagram for the motor circuit.

To complete the circuit, a wire should be drawn from terminal W to one side of the starter motor (M). Another wire should be drawn from the other side of the starter motor to terminal Z, placing the motor in series with the relay switch.

Question 8 | 0625/41/O/N/22 | Topics: Electricity, DC Circuits, EMF, Potential Difference, Resistance

The unit of the two electrical quantities electromotive force (e.m.f.) and potential difference (p.d.) is the volt (V).

(a) State one other similarity between e.m.f. and p.d.

Both e.m.f. and p.d. are measures of energy per unit charge.

(b) State one difference between e.m.f. and p.d.

E.m.f. refers to the energy being supplied to a circuit by a source (e.g., a battery), whereas p.d. refers to the energy being used or converted by a component in the circuit.

Recap

Electromotive force (e.m.f.) is the energy supplied by a source (like a battery) to each unit of charge that passes through it. It is the work done by the source on the charge. Potential difference (p.d.) is the energy converted by a unit of charge as it passes through a component. It is the work done by the charge on the component. Both are measured in Volts (V), which represents Joules per Coulomb.

(c) A battery consists of four cells, each of e.m.f. 1.2 V, in series.

(i) Calculate the e.m.f. of the battery.

For cells in series, the total e.m.f. is the sum of the individual e.m.f.s. Total e.m.f. = 4 × 1.2 V = 4.8 V.

(ii) The battery is connected in a circuit with four 12 Ω resistors. Fig. 8.1 is the circuit diagram. Calculate the total resistance of this arrangement of resistors.

The top branch has two 12 Ω resistors in series, so its resistance is 12 + 12 = 24 Ω. This branch is in parallel with the middle 12 Ω resistor. The equivalent resistance (R_p) of this parallel combination is 1/R_p = 1/24 + 1/12 = 3/24, so R_p = 8 Ω. This combination is in series with the bottom 12 Ω resistor. Therefore, the total resistance of the circuit is R_total = 8 Ω + 12 Ω = 20 Ω.

(iii) Calculate the reading on the voltmeter in Fig. 8.1.

First, calculate the total current flowing from the battery using Ohm's Law: I = V / R_total = 4.8 V / 20 Ω = 0.24 A. The voltmeter is connected across the series 12 Ω resistor at the bottom. The potential difference across this resistor is V = I × R = 0.24 A × 12 Ω = 2.88 V (which is approximately 2.9 V).

Recap

For resistors in series, the total resistance is the sum of individual resistances (R_total = R₁ + R₂ + ...). For resistors in Ohm's Law (V=IR) is fundamental to all circuit calculations.

Question 9 | 0625/41/O/N/22 | Topics: Atomic Physics, Radioactivity, Isotopes, Half-life

Only one isotope of gold occurs naturally on Earth.

(a) State what this indicates about the nuclear structure of all the naturally occurring atoms of gold on Earth.

This indicates that all naturally occurring gold atoms have the same number of protons and the same number of neutrons in their nucleus.

(b) There are several artificially produced isotopes of gold. Gold-198 (¹⁹⁸₇₉Au) is an artificial isotope which is used in medicine and in scientific research. Gold-198 decays by β (beta)-emission to a stable isotope of mercury.

(i) Determine the number of protons and the number of neutrons in a nucleus of this isotope of mercury.

In beta decay, a neutron becomes a proton and emits an electron. Therefore, the proton number increases by 1 and the mass number is unchanged. The new element has 79 + 1 = 80 protons (which is mercury). The number of neutrons is the mass number minus the proton number: 198 - 80 = 118 neutrons.
Number of protons: 80
Number of neutrons: 118

(ii) A sample of gold-198 is placed near to a radiation detector in a research laboratory. The count rate is recorded at the same time every day for 32 days. The results are used to plot the graph shown in Fig. 9.1. Using Fig. 9.1, determine the background count rate in the research laboratory.

The background count rate is the constant rate that the detector records after the sample has almost completely decayed. From the graph, the curve flattens out at a constant value of 20 counts/min.

(iii) Using Fig. 9.1, determine the half-life of gold-198.

First, the initial count rate from the source must be corrected by subtracting the background radiation. Corrected initial rate = 400 - 20 = 380 counts/min. Next, find half of this corrected rate: 380 / 2 = 190 counts/min. To find the half-life on the graph, we must add the background rate back to this value: 190 + 20 = 210 counts/min. Looking at the graph, the time it takes for the count rate to fall to 210 counts/min is approximately 2.7 days.

Recap

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons. Some isotopes are unstable and undergo radioactive decay. In beta decay, a neutron in the nucleus turns into a proton, and a high-energy electron (the beta particle) is emitted. This increases the proton number by 1. The half-life of a radioactive isotope is the time taken for half of the unstable nuclei in a sample to decay, or the time for the count rate from the sample to fall to half its initial level (after correcting for background radiation).
