Recap

On a speed-time graph, the gradient represents acceleration. A steeper gradient means a greater acceleration. It's important to convert units (e.g., minutes to seconds) to get the acceleration in standard units (m/s²).

(b) Calculate the total distance travelled by the ship between time = 42 min and time = 100 min.

The distance travelled is the area under the graph. We can split the area from t=42 min to t=100 min into a trapezium and a rectangle.
Area 1 (trapezium, 42-60 min): ½ * (7.5 + 15) * (60-42)*60 = 12150 m
Area 2 (rectangle, 60-100 min): 15 * (100-60)*60 = 36000 m
Total distance = 12150 + 36000 = 48150 m, or approximately 48 km.

Recap

The area under a speed-time graph represents the total distance travelled. The area can be calculated by dividing the graph into regular shapes like rectangles, triangles, and trapeziums.

Recap

Newton's Second Law states that the resultant force on an object is equal to the product of its mass and acceleration. The force will be in the same direction as the acceleration.

The calculated force is the resultant or net force. The ship's engine must produce a forward thrust that is greater than this resultant force in order to overcome resistive forces like drag or water resistance.

Resultant Force = Forward Thrust - Resistive Forces. Therefore, the forward thrust from the engine must be larger than the resultant force to account for and overcome the backward resistive forces.

0625/43/O/N/21 Q3

(a) A gas bubble is released at the bottom of a lake. Atmospheric pressure is 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. The density of water is 1000 kg/m³. The temperature of the water in the lake is constant.

(i) Explain why the volume of the bubble increases.

As the bubble rises, the depth of the water above it decreases. This causes the pressure exerted by the water to decrease. Since the total pressure on the bubble decreases and the temperature is constant, the volume of the gas bubble increases according to Boyle's Law.

Recap

The pressure in a liquid increases with depth, given by the formula p = hρg. The total pressure on the bubble is the sum of the atmospheric pressure and the water pressure. Boyle's Law (p₁V₁ = p₂V₂) states that for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, pressure and volume are inversely proportional.

(ii) The volume of the gas bubble is 0.40 cm³ when it is 3.0 m below the surface of the lake. Calculate the volume of the gas bubble when it is 0.50 m below the surface of the lake.

1. Calculate pressure at 3.0 m: P₁ = P_atm + p_water = 1.0×10⁵ + (3.0 * 1000 * 10) = 1.3 × 10⁵ Pa.
2. Calculate pressure at 0.50 m: P₂ = P_atm + p_water = 1.0×10⁵ + (0.50 * 1000 * 10) = 1.05 × 10⁵ Pa.
3. Use Boyle's Law (P₁V₁ = P₂V₂): (1.3 × 10⁵) * 0.40 = (1.05 × 10⁵) * V₂.
V₂ = (1.3 × 10⁵ * 0.40) / (1.05 × 10⁵) ≈ 0.495 cm³, or 0.50 cm³.

Recap

This problem combines the formula for pressure in a fluid with Boyle's law. It is crucial to calculate the total (absolute) pressure at each depth by adding the atmospheric pressure to the gauge pressure from the water column.

(b) Fig. 3.1 shows a diagram of a hydraulic press. A small quantity of air leaks into the oil. Suggest and explain the effect the air has on the operation of the hydraulic press.

The press will be less efficient. When force is applied to piston A, some of the energy will be used to compress the air bubbles instead of transmitting the pressure to piston B. This means that for the same input force, the output force on piston B will be less, and the paper will not be compressed as much.

Recap

Hydraulic systems work based on Pascal's principle, which states that pressure applied to an enclosed, incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished to every portion of the fluid. Liquids are nearly incompressible, making them ideal. Gases, however, are compressible, so their presence in a hydraulic system reduces its effectiveness.

0625/43/O/N/21 Q4

Explain what happens to the pressure of a constant volume of air when the temperature of the air increases. Use ideas of momentum of molecules in your explanation.

When the temperature of the air increases, the air molecules gain kinetic energy and move faster. This means they collide with the walls of the container more frequently and more forcefully. Each collision involves a greater change in momentum. The increased rate and force of collisions result in a greater average force on the walls, and therefore a higher pressure, since pressure is force per unit area.

Recap

This is explained by the Pressure Law (or Gay-Lussac's Law), which states that for a fixed mass of gas at constant volume, the pressure is directly proportional to the absolute temperature (in Kelvin). The kinetic theory of gases provides the microscopic explanation for this macroscopic law.

(b) Explain why metals are better thermal conductors than non-metals.

Metals have free electrons (or delocalised electrons) that are able to move throughout the metal structure. These electrons gain kinetic energy in hotter parts of the metal and move quickly to colder parts, transferring energy efficiently through collisions. Non-metals do not have free electrons and can only transfer heat slowly via lattice vibrations.

Recap

Thermal conduction in solids occurs through two main mechanisms: lattice vibrations (phonons) and the movement of free electrons. In metals, the electron mechanism is much more effective, making them excellent thermal conductors.

0625/43/O/N/21 Q6

(a) Describe an experiment to determine the speed of sound in air.

A simple method is the echo method.
1. Stand a large, known distance (d) away from a tall, flat wall (e.g., 50 m or more).
2. Use two wooden blocks to make a sharp clap. Start a stopwatch at the exact moment you hear the clap.
3. Listen for the echo and stop the stopwatch the instant you hear it. This measures the time (t) for the sound to travel to the wall and back.
4. The total distance travelled by the sound is 2d.
5. Calculate the speed of sound using the formula: speed = 2d / t.
To improve accuracy, measure the time for multiple claps (e.g., 50) and find the average time for one clap.

Recap

Another method involves two microphones connected to a timer. A sound is made at a known distance from the first microphone. The timer starts when the sound reaches the first microphone and stops when it reaches the second. The speed is then distance / time. This method is more accurate as it eliminates human reaction time error.

(b) Suggest why light waves and sound waves behave differently in this situation.

Diffraction is most significant when the wavelength of the wave is similar to the size of the gap. The wavelength of sound waves is roughly comparable to the size of a doorway, so they diffract significantly. The wavelength of light waves is extremely small, much smaller than a doorway, so very little diffraction occurs.

Recap

Diffraction is the spreading out of waves as they pass through a gap or around an obstacle. The amount of diffraction depends on the ratio of the wavelength to the size of the gap. Significant diffraction occurs when this ratio is close to 1.

(a) A transformer has 500 turns on the primary coil and 25 turns on the secondary coil. The input voltage is 120 V.

(i) Calculate the output voltage.

Using the transformer turns ratio equation: Vp / Vs = Np / Ns.
120 V / Vs = 500 / 25
Vs = 120 V * (25 / 500) = 6.0 V.

Recap

For an ideal transformer, the ratio of the voltages across the primary (p) and secondary (s) coils is equal to the ratio of the number of turns on the coils: Vp / Vs = Np / Ns. This is a step-down transformer as it decreases the voltage.

(ii) The current in the primary coil is 125 mA. The transformer is 100% efficient. Calculate the output current.

For a 100% efficient transformer, input power = output power (Pp = Ps).
Vp * Ip = Vs * Is
120 V * 0.125 A = 6.0 V * Is
Is = (120 * 0.125) / 6.0 = 2.5 A.

Recap

For a 100% efficient transformer, the power is conserved. The relationship is VpIp = VsIs. Note that for a step-down transformer, the voltage decreases but the current increases proportionally.

The function of the split-ring commutator in a d.c. motor is crucial. It acts as a reversing switch to ensure the torque on the coil always acts in the same direction, thus producing continuous rotation.

0625/43/O/N/21 Q11

(a) A detector of radioactivity is placed in a laboratory where there are no radioactive samples. A student notices that the detector shows a count rate that varies between 20 counts/min and 24 counts/min.

(i) Suggest a source of these readings.

This is due to background radiation. Sources could include radon gas from the ground, radioactive materials in rocks or building materials, cosmic rays from space, or radiation from food and drink.

Recap

Background radiation is the low level of ionising radiation that is present all around us at all times. It is important to subtract the background count rate from any measurements made on a radioactive source to get the true count rate from the source itself.

(ii) Explain why these readings are not constant.

Radioactive decay is a random and spontaneous process. It is not possible to predict which nucleus will decay or exactly when it will decay, so the number of decays per minute will fluctuate.

Recap

The random nature of radioactive decay means that count rates are always subject to statistical fluctuations. To get a more reliable value for the average count rate, counts should be taken over a long period of time.

(b) A nucleus of uranium (U) contains 92 protons and 146 neutrons. It decays by emitting an α-particle to become a nucleus of thorium (Th). Complete the nuclide equation for this radioactive decay.

The uranium nucleus has a nucleon number A = 92 + 146 = 238, and a proton number Z = 92.
An alpha particle (α) is a helium nucleus, ⁴₂He.
In alpha decay, A decreases by 4 and Z decreases by 2.
The equation is: 23892U → 23490Th + 42α

Recap

In all nuclear equations, both the total nucleon number (top number) and the total proton number (bottom number) must be conserved (i.e., they must be equal on both sides of the equation).

(c) An isotope of radon has a half-life of 3.8 days. It decays by emitting α-radiation. Calculate the time taken for 16 mg of this isotope to decay to 2 mg of this isotope.

1. Determine the number of half-lives. The mass halves with each half-life.
16 mg → 8 mg (1 half-life)
8 mg → 4 mg (2 half-lives)
4 mg → 2 mg (3 half-lives)
2. It takes 3 half-lives for the mass to decay from 16 mg to 2 mg.
3. Calculate the total time: Time = number of half-lives × half-life duration.
Time = 3 × 3.8 days = 11.4 days.

Recap

The half-life of a radioactive isotope is the time taken for half of the unstable nuclei in a sample to decay, or for the activity of the sample to halve. It is a constant value for a given isotope.
