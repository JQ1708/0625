Question 1 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q1 | Topic: Motion, Forces and Energy

(a) Fig. 1.1 shows a trolley travelling down a ramp.

The trolley has a piece of paper tape attached to it. The tape passes through a machine which makes a dot on the tape every 0.02 s.

Fig. 1.2 shows a section of the tape.

(i) State how the dots on the tape show that the trolley was moving with constant speed. [1]

The dots on the tape are equally spaced, meaning the trolley travels the same distance in the same time interval (0.02 s), which indicates constant speed.

Recap

A ticker-tape timer is a device used to study motion. The spacing of the dots on the tape provides information about the object's speed and acceleration. Equal spacing indicates constant speed, while increasing spacing indicates acceleration.

(ii) When the trolley reaches the point P, the ramp is tilted so that the angle x is greater. Describe and explain the change in motion of the trolley. [2]

Description: The trolley will accelerate or its speed/velocity will increase.

Explanation: Tilting the ramp to a greater angle means that a larger component of the trolley's weight acts down the slope. This results in a larger resultant force acting on the trolley, causing it to accelerate according to Newton's second law (F=ma).

Recap

Newton's second law of motion states that the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass (F=ma). A resultant force is the single force that represents the vector sum of all forces acting on an object.

(b) Another trolley is released from the top of the ramp.

Fig. 1.3 shows the speed-time graph for this trolley.

Using Fig. 1.3, calculate the distance travelled by the trolley in the first 0.5s. [2]

The distance travelled is represented by the area under the speed-time graph.

For the first 0.5 s, the graph is a triangle with base = 0.5 s and height = 0.75 m/s (from the graph).

Distance = 0.5 × base × height = 0.5 × 0.5 s × 0.75 m/s = 0.1875 m.

Rounding to two significant figures, the distance travelled is 0.19 m.

Recap

On a speed-time graph, the gradient represents acceleration, and the area under the graph represents distance travelled. This is a key concept for analysing motion.

(c) Fig. 1.4 shows a metal ball at rest in a tube of liquid.

The ball is released and reaches terminal velocity at point X. Explain the motion of the ball as it falls from rest until it reaches point X. Use ideas of force and acceleration in your answer. [3]

Initially, when the ball is released, its velocity increases (it accelerates) because the downwards resultant force (weight minus upthrust and drag) is acting on it. As the ball's velocity increases, the resistance of the liquid (drag) also increases. This causes the resultant downwards force to decrease, and consequently, the acceleration decreases.

Eventually, the downwards force (weight) becomes equal to the upwards forces (upthrust + drag) at point X. At this point, the resultant force is zero, and the ball travels at a constant velocity/speed, known as terminal velocity.

Recap

Terminal velocity is reached when the forces acting on a falling object are balanced, resulting in zero acceleration. This concept applies to objects falling through fluids (liquids or gases) where drag forces are significant.

Question 2 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q2 | Topic: Motion, Forces and Energy (Kinematics & Momentum)

Fig. 2.1 shows a cliff edge with water below it.

A ball falls over the edge of the cliff. The mass of the ball is 160g. The height of the cliff is 115m.

(a) Calculate the vertical speed of the ball as it hits the water. Air resistance can be ignored. [3]

We can use the principle of conservation of energy. The gravitational potential energy (GPE) lost by the ball is converted into kinetic energy (KE) gained.

GPE = mgh, where m = 0.160 kg, g = 10 m/s², h = 115 m.

GPE = 0.160 kg × 10 m/s² × 115 m = 184 J.

KE = 0.5 × mv².

Equating GPE lost to KE gained: 184 J = 0.5 × 0.160 kg × v².

184 = 0.080 × v²

v² = 184 / 0.080 = 2300

v = √2300 ≈ 48 m/s (to 2 significant figures).

Recap

The principle of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another. In free fall (ignoring air resistance), potential energy is converted entirely into kinetic energy.

(b) Calculate the vertical momentum of the ball as it hits the water. [2]

Momentum (p) is calculated as the product of mass (m) and velocity (v).

p = mv = 0.160 kg × 47.96 m/s ≈ 7.7 kg m/s (to 2 significant figures).

Recap

Momentum is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction. It is a measure of the "quantity of motion" an object has. The unit of momentum is kg m/s or N s.

Question 3 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q3 | Topic: Motion, Forces and Energy (Scalars, Vectors & Circular Motion)

(a) (i) Speed is a scalar quantity. State one other scalar quantity. [1]

Other scalar quantities include distance, mass, time, energy, power, temperature, or density.

Recap

A scalar quantity is defined by its magnitude only, while a vector quantity has both magnitude and direction.

(a) (ii) Velocity is a vector quantity. State one other vector quantity. [1]

Other vector quantities include displacement, acceleration, force, momentum, or weight.

Recap

Understanding the distinction between scalar and vector quantities is crucial in physics, as it dictates how these quantities are added, subtracted, and used in calculations.

(b) Fig. 3.1 shows a model car travelling at constant speed on a flat circular track.

The speed of the car is 0.30m/s. In one complete revolution around the track, the car travels 3.9m.

(i) Calculate the time taken for the car to complete one revolution around the track. [2]

Time taken (t) can be calculated using the formula: time = distance / speed.

t = 3.9 m / 0.30 m/s = 13 s.

Recap

For an object moving at constant speed, the relationship between distance, speed, and time is straightforward: distance = speed × time.

(ii) On Fig. 3.1, draw and label with the letter F an arrow to show the resultant force acting on the car. [1]

The resultant force (F) acting on the car in circular motion is the centripetal force. This force always acts inwards towards the centre of the circle, perpendicular to the direction of motion. An arrow labelled 'F' should be drawn from the car pointing towards the centre of the circular track.

Recap

Centripetal force is the force that keeps an object moving in a circular path. Without this inward force, the object would move in a straight line tangent to the circle.

(iii) The speed of the car increases and at point P on Fig. 3.2 the car does not stay on the track.

1. Suggest, in terms of the force acting on the car, why the car does not stay on the track at point P. [1]

When the speed of the car increases, the required centripetal force to keep it on the circular track also increases. If the frictional force (which provides the centripetal force) between the tires and the track is insufficient to provide this increased centripetal force, the car will not stay on the track.

Recap

The magnitude of centripetal force is given by F = mv²/r. This shows that centripetal force is directly proportional to the square of the velocity. Therefore, as velocity increases, a significantly larger centripetal force is required.

2. On Fig. 3.2, draw and label an arrow with the letter S to show the direction of motion of the car as it leaves the track at point P. [1]

As the car leaves the track at point P, it will move in a straight line tangential to the circular path at that point. An arrow labelled 'S' should be drawn starting from point P and pointing tangentially outwards from the circle, in the direction of the car's instantaneous velocity.

Recap

Inertia causes an object in circular motion to tend to move in a straight line tangent to its path if the centripetal force is removed or becomes insufficient.

Question 4 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q4 | Topic: Thermal Physics (States of Matter & Gas Laws)

In Fig. 4.1, the circles represent molecules in different states of matter.

(a) Identify the states A, B and C. [2]

  • A: Liquid (molecules are close together but randomly arranged and can move past each other)
  • B: Solid (molecules are closely packed in a regular arrangement and vibrate about fixed positions)
  • C: Gas (molecules are far apart, randomly arranged, and move rapidly and randomly)

Recap

The kinetic theory of matter describes the properties of solids, liquids, and gases based on the arrangement and motion of their constituent particles (atoms or molecules).

(b) Explain, in terms of forces between molecules, why gases expand more than liquids when they have the same rise in temperature. Assume that the pressure remains constant. [2]

In gases, the average distance between molecules is much greater than in liquids, and the attractive forces between molecules are negligible or very low. Therefore, when gases gain thermal energy, the molecules move faster and further apart, leading to a significant expansion. In liquids, the molecules are still relatively close, and there are stronger attractive forces, which restrict their expansion even with the same temperature rise.

Recap

The strength of intermolecular forces and the average separation of molecules are key factors determining the physical properties and behaviour of substances in different states.

(c) Fig. 4.2 shows a cylinder and piston.

The volume of gas in the cylinder is 3400cm³. The pressure of the gas in the cylinder is 0.90 × 10⁵ Pa.

(i) The piston is moved to the left and fixed in a new position. The pressure of the gas in the cylinder increases to 2.5 × 10⁵ Pa. Assume that the temperature of the gas does not change. Calculate the new volume of the gas. [3]

Since the temperature of the gas does not change, we can use Boyle's Law: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂.

Given: P₁ = 0.90 × 10⁵ Pa, V₁ = 3400 cm³, P₂ = 2.5 × 10⁵ Pa.

V₂ = (P₁ × V₁) / P₂ = (0.90 × 10⁵ Pa × 3400 cm³) / (2.5 × 10⁵ Pa)

V₂ = (0.90 × 3400) / 2.5 = 3060 / 2.5 = 1224 cm³.

Rounding to two significant figures, the new volume is 1200 cm³.

Recap

Boyle's Law states that for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, the pressure is inversely proportional to the volume. This means that if the pressure increases, the volume decreases proportionally.

(ii) The gas in the cylinder is now heated. The piston remains fixed in the same position as in (c)(i). State and explain, in terms of molecules, any change in the pressure of the gas. [3]

Statement: The pressure of the gas increases.

Explanation: When the gas is heated, the molecules gain kinetic energy and move faster. Since the volume is fixed, the molecules will collide with the walls of the cylinder more frequently and with greater momentum. This increased rate of change of momentum of the molecules colliding with the walls results in a larger force over the same area, hence an increase in pressure.

Recap

The relationship between pressure, volume, and temperature of a gas is described by the ideal gas law. For a fixed volume, increasing the temperature increases the pressure, a concept known as Gay-Lussac's Law or Amontons's Law.

Question 5 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q5 | Topic: Thermal Physics (Heat Transfer)

Fig. 5.1 shows two metal plates A and B with a radiant heater placed midway between them.

Metal plate A is shiny. Metal plate B is dull black. A piece of cork is attached to each plate using wax. The wax is a solid at room temperature and has a melting point of 37 °C.

(a) State and explain what happens to the pieces of cork a few minutes after the heater is switched on. [4]

The cork on the dull black plate (B) will fall off first. This is because dull black surfaces are better absorbers of thermal radiation (infrared radiation) compared to shiny surfaces. Therefore, plate B absorbs more heat from the radiant heater, causing the wax holding the cork to melt faster than the wax on the shiny plate A.

Recap

The colour and texture of a surface affect its ability to absorb and emit thermal radiation. Dull, dark surfaces are good absorbers and emitters, while shiny, light surfaces are poor absorbers and emitters (good reflectors).

(b) Give the name of the method of transfer of thermal energy in solid metals. [1]

The method of transfer of thermal energy in solid metals is conduction.

Recap

Conduction is the transfer of thermal energy through direct contact, primarily by the vibration of particles and the movement of free electrons in metals. Convection involves the movement of fluids, and radiation involves electromagnetic waves.

Question 6 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q6 | Topic: Waves (Sound)

(a) Sound waves consist of compressions and rarefactions. Explain the terms compression and rarefaction. Give your explanation in terms of the spacing of molecules and the pressure for sound waves in air. [3]

A compression is a region in a sound wave where the molecules are closer together than normal, resulting in a higher pressure than the surrounding air.

A rarefaction is a region in a sound wave where the molecules are further apart than normal, resulting in a lower pressure than the surrounding air.

Recap

Sound waves are longitudinal waves, meaning the oscillations of the particles are parallel to the direction of wave propagation. These oscillations create alternating regions of high pressure (compressions) and low pressure (rarefactions).

(b) A musical instrument emits a sound with a frequency of 4.4kHz. The speed of sound in air is 340m/s.

(i) Calculate the wavelength of the sound. [3]

Given: frequency (f) = 4.4 kHz = 4400 Hz, speed (v) = 340 m/s.

Using the wave equation: v = f × λ, so λ = v / f.

λ = 340 m/s / 4400 Hz ≈ 0.077 m (to 2 significant figures).

Recap

The wave equation (v = fλ) is a fundamental relationship in wave physics, connecting wave speed, frequency, and wavelength. It applies to all types of waves, including sound and light.

(ii) The frequency of the sound emitted by the instrument is changed to 5.1kHz and the amplitude of the sound is increased. Without calculation, state what happens to

1. the speed of the sound [1]

The speed of sound stays the same. The speed of sound in a medium depends only on the properties of the medium (e.g., temperature, density), not on the frequency or amplitude of the sound wave.

Recap

The speed of a wave is an intrinsic property of the medium through which it travels. Changes in the source (frequency or amplitude) do not alter the wave's speed in a uniform medium.

2. the wavelength of the sound [1]

The wavelength decreases. Since v = fλ and the speed (v) remains constant, if the frequency (f) increases (from 4.4 kHz to 5.1 kHz), the wavelength (λ) must decrease proportionally.

Recap

For a constant wave speed, frequency and wavelength are inversely proportional. This means that higher frequency waves have shorter wavelengths, and lower frequency waves have longer wavelengths.

Question 7 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q7 | Topic: Waves (Electromagnetic Spectrum & Ultrasound)

(a) State two uses for infrared radiation. [2]

Two uses for infrared radiation are:

  1. Remote controls (e.g., for televisions, air conditioners).
  2. Thermal imaging (e.g., night vision cameras, heat sensors).
  3. Infrared sensors/alarms.
  4. Specific electrical appliances (e.g., toasters, grills).

Recap

Infrared radiation is part of the electromagnetic spectrum, with wavelengths longer than visible light but shorter than microwaves. It is primarily associated with heat transfer.

(b) X-rays are used in hospitals to help treat patients. Suggest and explain three precautions for the safe use of X-rays. [3]

Three precautions for the safe use of X-rays are:

  1. Shielding: Operators should be shielded behind a screen or wear a lead apron to absorb radiation. Other parts of the patient not being X-rayed should also be shielded with lead.
  2. Distance: Maintain a safe distance from the X-ray source, as the intensity of radiation decreases with the square of the distance.
  3. Limit exposure: Limit the time of exposure to X-rays and avoid frequent or unnecessary X-ray examinations to minimise the total radiation dose.
  4. Limit beam strength/intensity: Use the minimum necessary strength or intensity of the X-ray beam required for the diagnostic or therapeutic purpose.

Recap

X-rays are a form of ionising radiation, which can cause damage to living cells and DNA. Therefore, strict safety protocols are necessary to minimise exposure for both patients and medical staff.

(c) (i) State the speed in a vacuum of

1. microwaves [1]

The speed of microwaves in a vacuum is 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

All electromagnetic waves, including microwaves, travel at the same speed in a vacuum, which is the speed of light (c).

2. X-rays [1]

The speed of X-rays in a vacuum is 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

The electromagnetic spectrum encompasses a wide range of wavelengths and frequencies, but all its components share the same speed in a vacuum.

(ii) State a possible frequency for an ultrasound wave. [1]

A possible frequency for an ultrasound wave is greater than 20 000 Hz (or 20 kHz).

Ultrasound refers to sound waves with frequencies higher than the upper audible limit of human hearing. It is widely used in medical imaging and industrial applications.

Question 8 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q8 | Topic: Electricity and Magnetism (Circuits & Logic Gates)

(a) (i) Fig. 8.1 shows an electrical circuit. The resistor has a resistance of 4.0Ω. The reading on the voltmeter is 3.0V.

Calculate the current in the resistor. [2]

Using Ohm's Law, V = IR, where V is voltage, I is current, and R is resistance.

Given: V = 3.0 V, R = 4.0 Ω.

I = V / R = 3.0 V / 4.0 Ω = 0.75 A.

Recap

Ohm's Law is a fundamental principle in electrical circuits, stating that the current through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage across the two points. The constant of proportionality is the resistance.

(ii) Fig. 8.2 shows the same circuit with one component reversed.

State the reading on the voltmeter and explain your answer. [2]

The reading on the voltmeter will be 0 V.

This is because the diode has been reversed. A diode only allows current to flow in one direction (forward bias). When reversed, it is in reverse bias and acts as an open circuit, preventing current from flowing through the resistor. Without current, there is no potential difference across the resistor.

Recap

A diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only. In reverse bias, its resistance is extremely high, effectively blocking the current and causing the voltage drop across any series component to be zero.

Question 9 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q9 | Topic: Electricity and Magnetism (Power Transmission & Transformers)

(a) Electrical power is produced in a power station by an alternating current (a.c.) generator. The output of the generator has a voltage of 22000V. The electrical power is transmitted at a voltage of 400 000 V. Explain why electrical power is transmitted at a voltage of 400000 V and not 22000V. [3]

Electrical power is transmitted at a very high voltage (400 000 V) to reduce the current for the same amount of power (since P = VI). This is done to minimise energy/power loss as heat during transmission through the cables (since heat loss = I²R). A lower current means less heat is generated, making transmission more efficient. It also allows for the use of thinner wires, which are cheaper.

Recap

Power transmission over long distances is a critical application of transformers. Step-up transformers increase voltage and decrease current for efficient transmission, while step-down transformers reduce voltage for safe use by consumers.

(b) A computer contains a transformer. The input voltage to the transformer is 240V. The output voltage from the transformer is 20V and the output current is 2.3A. The efficiency of the transformer is 90%. Calculate the input current to the transformer. [5]

First, calculate the output power (P_out):

P_out = V_out × I_out = 20 V × 2.3 A = 46 W.

The efficiency (η) of the transformer is 90% (0.9). Efficiency is given by η = P_out / P_in.

So, calculate the input power (P_in):

P_in = P_out / η = 46 W / 0.9 ≈ 51.11 W.

Finally, calculate the input current (I_in) using P_in = V_in × I_in:

I_in = P_in / V_in = 51.11 W / 240 V ≈ 0.21 A (to 2 significant figures).

Recap

Transformers are devices that change alternating voltages. Their efficiency is a measure of how much input power is converted into useful output power, with some energy always lost as heat due to factors like resistance in windings and eddy currents in the core.

Question 10 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q10 | Topic: Electricity and Magnetism (Electromagnets & Relays)

Fig. 10.1 shows a relay.

(a) The switch in circuit A is closed. Describe how this operates the motor in circuit B. [3]

When the switch in circuit A is closed, current flows through the coil, which creates a magnetic field around the iron core, making it an electromagnet. This electromagnet then attracts the soft-iron armature. The armature pivots, causing the contacts to touch, which completes circuit B and allows current to flow to operate the motor.

Recap

A relay is an electrically operated switch. It uses an electromagnet to open or close a switch, allowing a small current in one circuit to control a larger current in another circuit, often used for safety or remote control.

(b) The switch in circuit A is opened. The soft-iron armature is replaced with a steel armature. The switch in circuit A is closed. Explain what happens when the switch in circuit A is then opened. [2]

When the switch in circuit A is opened, the current in circuit B will not stop. This is because when the soft-iron armature is replaced with a steel armature, the steel becomes a permanent magnet when magnetised by the coil. Even after the current in circuit A is switched off, the steel armature remains magnetised and continues to hold the contacts in circuit B closed, so the motor continues to operate.

Recap

Soft iron is a magnetically soft material, meaning it is easily magnetised and demagnetised, making it suitable for electromagnets. Steel is a magnetically hard material, meaning it retains its magnetism after the magnetising field is removed, making it suitable for permanent magnets.

Question 11 | 0625/43/O/N/20 Q11 | Topic: Atomic Physics (Radioactivity)

(a) Fig. 11.1 shows a beam of α-particles, β-particles and γ-rays directed between two metal plates P and Q.

The metal plates are parallel and there is a large potential difference (p.d.) between them. Plate P is positive and plate Q is negative.

On Fig. 11.1, draw the paths of each of the radiations between the plates and after leaving the plates. Label the paths α, β and γ. [5]

The paths of the radiations are as follows:

  • α-particles (alpha particles) are positively charged, so they will be deflected towards the negative plate (Q). Their deflection will be less than beta particles due to their larger mass.
  • β-particles (beta particles) are negatively charged, so they will be deflected towards the positive plate (P). Their deflection will be greater than alpha particles due to their smaller mass.
  • γ-rays (gamma rays) are uncharged electromagnetic radiation, so they will pass undeflected through the electric field.

The paths should be curved within the plates for alpha and beta, and straight for gamma. After leaving the plates, the deflected particles will continue in a straight line in their new direction.

Recap

The behaviour of alpha, beta, and gamma radiations in electric and magnetic fields is a key characteristic used to distinguish between them. This is due to their differing charges and masses.

(b) State and explain one practical application of γ-rays. [2]

One practical application of γ-rays (gamma rays) is sterilisation of medical equipment or food products. Gamma rays are highly penetrating and can kill bacteria and other microorganisms without significantly heating the material, making them ideal for sterilising heat-sensitive items.

Another application is in radiotherapy for treating cancer. Gamma rays can be directed at cancerous tumours to destroy the cancerous cells, as they are highly energetic and can penetrate deep into tissues.

Recap

Gamma rays are the most energetic and penetrating form of electromagnetic radiation. Their high energy makes them useful for applications requiring deep penetration or the destruction of biological material.
