Question 9 (0625/41_Summer_2018_Q7) - Electricity

(a) State, in terms of their structure, why metals are good conductors of electricity. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Metals contain free/mobile electrons (or delocalised electrons) that are able to move through the structure and carry charge.

(b) A cylindrical metal wire W₁ of length l and cross-sectional area A, has a resistance of 16Ω. A second cylindrical wire W₂ having length l⁄2 and cross-sectional area 2A, is made from the same metal. Determine

(i) the resistance of W₂, [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Resistance is proportional to length and inversely proportional to area. Halving the length halves the resistance (16Ω → 8Ω). Doubling the area also halves the resistance (8Ω → 4Ω).
So, the new resistance is 16Ω × (1⁄2) × (1⁄2) = 4.0 Ω.

(ii) the effective resistance of W₁ and W₂ when connected in parallel. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using the formula for parallel resistors: 1⁄Rp = 1⁄R₁ + 1⁄R₂ = 1⁄16Ω + 1⁄4Ω = 1+4⁄16Ω = 5⁄16Ω.
Rp = 16⁄5 = 3.2 Ω.

(c) The parallel pair of resistors in (b)(ii) is connected to a battery that is made from three cells in series, each of electromotive force (e.m.f.) E. There is a current in each resistor.

(i) State the e.m.f. of the battery. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The e.m.f. of the battery is 3E.

(ii) The current in the battery is IB, the current in W₁ is I₁ and the current in W₂ is I₂. Place a tick (✓) in one box to indicate how these three currents are related. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The correct box is IB > I₂ > I₁.

Question 11 (0625/43_Summer_2018_Q9) - Electricity

Fig. 9.1 shows a circuit that includes a battery of electromotive force (e.m.f.) 12V.

The reading on the ammeter is 0.15A.

(a) Calculate the resistance of the circuit. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using Ohm's Law for the whole circuit, R = V⁄I = 12V⁄0.15A = 80 Ω.

(b) The variable resistor is adjusted so that its resistance decreases.

(i) State what happens to the reading on the ammeter. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The reading on the ammeter increases.

(ii) State and explain what happens to the reading on the voltmeter. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The reading on the voltmeter decreases. The voltmeter is measuring the p.d. across the variable resistor. Since its resistance has decreased and the current has increased, a larger share of the total voltage is now across the fixed 20Ω resistor, leaving less for the variable resistor.

(c) The battery is formed from cells of electromotive force (e.m.f.) 1.5 V.

(i) Explain, in terms of electrical energy, what is meant by an electromotive force (e.m.f.) of 1.5 V. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
An e.m.f. of 1.5V means that the cell supplies 1.5 Joules of electrical energy for every coulomb of charge that passes through it.

(ii) State how many 1.5 V cells are connected in series to form the battery. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Number of cells = Total e.m.f.⁄e.m.f. per cell = 12V⁄1.5V = 8 cells.
Question 12 (0625/42_Summer_2018_Q10) - Electricity

(a) An uncharged conducting metal plate rests on insulating supports. Fig. 10.1 shows the plate and a positively charged insulating plastic sheet placed on top of the metal plate.

(i) Describe any flow of charge that takes place when the plastic sheet is placed onto the metal plate. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Electrons (negative charges) within the metal plate are attracted to the positive plastic sheet and move towards the top surface of the plate.

(ii) On Fig. 10.1, draw how charges are now arranged within the metal plate. [1]

(iii) State and explain if this arrangement of charge helps to keep the plastic sheet in place. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Yes, it helps keep the plastic sheet in place. The accumulation of negative charges on the top surface of the metal plate and positive charges on the bottom creates an electrostatic attraction. The unlike charges attract, so there is an attractive force between the metal plate and the plastic sheet.

(b) Fig. 10.2 shows two uncharged conducting spheres suspended on insulating threads.

1. The spheres are now both given positive charges. On Fig. 10.2, draw a possible position of each sphere and thread.

2. Explain the positions you have drawn. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The spheres will move apart from each other, with the threads angled outwards. This is because both spheres have positive charges, and like charges repel.

Tap to reveal answer
Using P = VI, the current I = P⁄V = 60W⁄110V = 0.55 A.

The lamps are in parallel, so the total current is the sum of the currents in each branch.

Question 14 (0625/43_Winter_2018_Q7) - Electricity

A defibrillator is a machine that sends an electrical charge through the heart of a patient whose heart is not beating correctly. Doctors learn to use a defibrillator by practising on a medical dummy. Fig. 7.1 shows the two contacts of a defibrillator attached to a medical dummy.

The contacts that touch the dummy are made from metal, and when the defibrillator is being used, one contact becomes strongly negatively charged and the other contact becomes strongly positively charged. The handles of the contacts are made from plastic, which is an electrical insulator.

(a) (i) State how the structure of an electrical insulator differs from the structure of a conductor. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
An insulator has no free/delocalised electrons, whereas a conductor does.

(ii) Suggest why the handles are made from an electrical insulator. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The handles are made from an insulator to prevent charge from flowing through the doctor, ensuring the doctor does not get an electric shock which could be fatal or cause injury.

(b) Explain, in terms of the particles involved, how one contact becomes negatively charged and how the other contact becomes comes positively charged. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Electrons move from one contact to the other. The contact that gains electrons becomes negatively charged, and the contact that loses electrons becomes positively charged.

(c) The defibrillator passes a charge of 9.1 × 10⁻³ C through the medical dummy in 6.5 × 10⁻⁴ s. Calculate the average current in the dummy. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using the formula I = Q⁄t = 9.1 × 10⁻³ C⁄6.5 × 10⁻⁴ s = 14 A.

Question 16 (0625/43_Winter_2018_Q8) - Electricity

A 9.0V battery is connected to a 120Ω resistor in series with wire P. Fig. 8.1 shows a voltmeter connected across the 120Ω resistor.

(a) State the energy changes that are taking place in the circuit. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Chemical energy in the battery is converted to electrical energy, which is then converted to thermal energy (heat) in the resistors.

(b) The reading on the voltmeter is 2.4 V. Calculate:

(i) the current in the 120Ω resistor [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using Ohm's Law, I = V⁄R = 2.4V⁄120Ω = 0.020 A.

(ii) the potential difference (p.d.) across wire P [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The total p.d. is 9.0V. The p.d. across P is 9.0V - 2.4V = 6.6 V.

(iii) the resistance of wire P [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The current is the same (0.020A). Using Ohm's Law, R = V⁄I = 6.6V⁄0.020A = 330 Ω.

(c) Wire P has a diameter d and a length L. A second piece of wire Q is made of the same material. The diameter of wire Q is 0.50 × d and its length is 5.0 × L. Calculate the resistance of wire Q. [4]

Tap to reveal answer
Resistance is proportional to length (L) and inversely proportional to area (A), and A is proportional to d².
Effect of length: 5.0 × L means resistance is multiplied by 5.
Effect of diameter: 0.50 × d means area is multiplied by (0.5)² = 0.25. Since R ∝ 1/A, resistance is multiplied by 1/0.25 = 4.
New resistance = 330Ω × 5 × 4 = 6600 Ω.
