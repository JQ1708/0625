(c) Fig. 8.1 shows a power supply of e.m.f. 10.5V connected in series with a lamp and a heater. The p.d. across the lamp is 2.1V and the current in the lamp is 1.5A.

Calculate:

(i) the resistance of the heater [2]

Tap to reveal answer
In a series circuit, the total voltage is shared. The voltage across the heater is 10.5V - 2.1V = 8.4 V.
The current is the same throughout the series circuit (1.5A).
Using Ohm's Law for the heater, R = V⁄I = 8.4 V⁄1.5 A = 5.6 Ω.

(ii) the power of the heater. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using the formula P = VI for the heater.
Power = 8.4 V × 1.5 A = 12.6 W.
Question 34 (0625/43_Summer_2020_Q8) - Electricity

(a) (i) Describe what is meant by an electric field. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
An electric field is a region where an electric charge experiences a force.

(ii) State what is meant by the direction of an electric field. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The direction of an electric field at a point is the direction of the force that would be exerted on a positive charge placed at that point.

(b) Fig. 8.1 shows a polystyrene ball covered with aluminium paint. The polystyrene ball is suspended between two charged metal plates by an insulated thread. The ball oscillates between the two charged plates. Explain why the ball oscillates. [4]

Space for diagram Fig. 8.1
Tap to reveal answer
1. The uncharged conducting ball is initially attracted to one of the plates (e.g., the positive plate).
2. When it touches the positive plate, it transfers electrons to the plate and becomes positively charged itself.
3. Now being positively charged, it is repelled by the positive plate and attracted to the negative plate.
4. It moves across and touches the negative plate, where it gains electrons and becomes negatively charged.
5. Now negatively charged, it is repelled by the negative plate and attracted back to the positive plate. This process repeats, causing the oscillation.

(c) There is a current of 0.29A in an electrical circuit. Calculate the time taken for a charge of 15C to flow through the electrical circuit. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
Using the formula Q = It, we can rearrange to find time: t = Q⁄I.
Time = 15 C⁄0.29 A = 52 s.
Question 35 (0625/41_Winter_2020_Q8) - Electricity

(a) Explain what is meant by electromotive force (e.m.f.). [2]

Tap to reveal answer
E.m.f. is the energy supplied by a source (like a battery) per unit charge to drive that charge around a complete circuit.

(b) An electric heater contains two heating elements R₁ and R₂. An electric motor operates a fan. The fan blows cool air over the heating elements. Fig. 8.1 shows the circuit. The heater is powered by a mains supply of e.m.f. 240V. Switches S₁ and S₂ are closed. Heating element R₁ gets hot. The resistance of R₁ is 30Ω.

(i) Calculate the current in heating element R₁. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Using Ohm's Law, I = V⁄R. The voltage across the parallel branches is 240V.
Current in R₁ = 240 V⁄30 Ω = 8.0 A.

(ii) Calculate the power produced in heating element R₁. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using P = VI.
Power in R₁ = 240 V × 8.0 A = 1900 W.

The resistance of heating element R₂ is 60 Ω. Switches S₁, S₂ and S₃ are closed.

1. State and explain how the current in R₂ compares with the current in R₁. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The current in R₂ is half the current in R₁.
Explanation: Both resistors have the same voltage across them. Since the resistance of R₂ (60Ω) is twice the resistance of R₁ (30Ω), and current is inversely proportional to resistance (I = V⁄R), the current in R₂ will be half that in R₁.

2. The current in the motor is 0.10A. The cable from the electric heater to the plug for the mains socket is safe when the current in it is less than 20A. Suggest and explain a suitable fuse rating for this circuit. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Suggested Rating: 13 A.
Explanation: The total current is the sum of the currents in the parallel branches.
Current in R₂ = 8.0 A / 2 = 4.0 A.
Total current = I(motor) + I(R₁) + I(R₂) = 0.10 A + 8.0 A + 4.0 A = 12.1 A.
The fuse rating must be just above the normal operating current, so 13A is the next suitable standard value.
Question 36 (0625/42_Winter_2020_Q8) - Electricity

(a) State and explain why electrical sockets and plugs used outside in a garden need to be different from those that can be used safely in a room inside a house. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Outdoor conditions are often damp or wet. Since water conducts electricity, outdoor sockets and plugs must be waterproof to prevent short circuits and the risk of electric shock.

(b) State and explain why fuses and circuit breakers are installed in electrical circuits connected to the mains supply. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Fuses and circuit breakers are safety devices that protect appliances and users. If the current in a circuit becomes dangerously high (due to a fault or overload), the fuse melts or the circuit breaker opens, breaking the circuit and stopping the flow of current. This prevents overheating, fire, and electric shock.

(ii) A voltmeter is connected to measure the potential difference (p.d.) across the 4.0Ω resistor. On Fig. 10.1, draw a voltmeter symbol connected in the correct position. [1]

(iii) With the circuit completed, the current in the 2.0Ω resistor is 2.5A. Calculate the current in the 6.0Ω resistor. [4]

Tap to reveal answer
The 2.0Ω and 4.0Ω resistors are in parallel, so they have the same p.d. across them.
p.d. across parallel section = I × R = 2.5 A × 2.0 Ω = 5.0 V.
Current in 4.0Ω resistor = V⁄R = 5.0 V⁄4.0 Ω = 1.25 A.
The total current leaving the parallel section is the sum of the currents in the branches: 2.5 A + 1.25 A = 3.75 A.
This total current flows through the 6.0Ω resistor, so the current is 3.8 A (to 2 s.f.).

The alternating current in the primary coil produces a continuously changing magnetic field in the soft iron core. This changing magnetic field cuts through the secondary coil, inducing an alternating e.m.f. (and current if connected) in the secondary coil.

For a given amount of power to be transmitted (P = VI), a higher voltage means a lower current is required. Power loss in cables is due to heating (Ploss = I²R). A lower current significantly reduces this power loss, making transmission more efficient.
Question 40 (0625/43_Summer_2021_Q7) - Electricity

(a) Define electromotive force (e.m.f.). [2]

Tap to reveal answer
E.m.f. is the energy supplied by a source to drive a unit charge (1 Coulomb) around a complete circuit.

(b) Fig. 7.1 shows a circuit.

The two lamps shown are identical. Each lamp has a potential difference (p.d.) of 3.0 V across it and a current of 2.0A in it. PQ is a length of uniform metal wire. The resistance of PQ is R.

(i) Calculate the value of R. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
The lamps are in parallel, so the p.d. across the parallel combination is 3.0V. The total current is the sum of the currents in the lamps: 2.0A + 2.0A = 4.0 A.
This total current flows through wire PQ. The p.d. across PQ is the total e.m.f. minus the p.d. across the lamps: 12V - 3.0V = 9.0 V.
Using Ohm's Law for wire PQ: R = V⁄I = 9.0 V⁄4.0 A = 2.3 Ω.

(ii) Another piece of wire is made of the same metal as PQ. The length of the new piece of wire is twice the length of PQ. The diameter of the new piece of wire is twice the diameter of PQ. Calculate the resistance of the new piece of wire. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
Resistance R is proportional to length L (R ∝ L) and inversely proportional to cross-sectional area A (R ∝ 1⁄A). Area is proportional to the square of the diameter (A ∝ d²).
The new wire has twice the length, which doubles the resistance.
It also has twice the diameter, which means the area is 2² = 4 times larger. This decreases the resistance by a factor of 4.
New Resistance = R × 2 × (1⁄4) = R × 0.5 = 2.3 Ω × 0.5 = 1.1 Ω.
