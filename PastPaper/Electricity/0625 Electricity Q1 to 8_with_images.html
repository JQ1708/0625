Question 1 (0625/42_Summer_2017_Q5) - Thermal Effects, Electricity

(a) (i) An electric kettle contains 600g of water at 20°C. The heater in the kettle operates at 240 V. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200J/(kg°C). The current in the heater is 12 A. Calculate the time taken for the temperature of the water to rise to 100°C. [4]

Tap to reveal answer
First, calculate the thermal energy required: E = mcΔT.
E = 0.6 kg × 4200 J/(kg°C) × (100°C - 20°C) = 201600 J.
Next, calculate the electrical energy supplied per second (power): P = VI.
P = 240 V × 12 A = 2880 W.
Finally, calculate the time: time = E⁄P = 201600 J⁄2880 W = 70 s.

(ii) State one assumption you made in your calculation in calculation in (a)(i). [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The assumption made is that there are no thermal energy losses to the surroundings.

(b) Using the apparatus shown in Fig. 5.1, describe an experiment to demonstrate good and bad emitters of thermal radiation. Include the expected results and the conclusion. You may use a diagram. [4]

Tap to reveal answer
First, fill the metal bottle with hot water. Then, place the two thermometers at equal distances from the black and white sides of the bottle. Record the temperature readings from both thermometers at regular intervals.
Expected result: The thermometer near the black side will show a higher temperature reading or its temperature will rise faster.
Conclusion: This demonstrates that black surfaces are better emitters of thermal radiation than white surfaces.
Question 2 (0625/42_Summer_2017_Q8) - Electricity, Electron And Electronics

Fig. 8.1 shows a 12.0V power supply connected in a circuit.

The circuit includes a lamp and a resistance wire AB of constant cross-sectional area. There is a sliding contact that can be moved between A and B.

(a) The rating of the lamp at normal brightness is 6.0V, 9.0W. Calculate

(i) the current in the lamp at normal brightness, [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using the formula P = VI, the current I = P⁄V = 9.0W⁄6.0V = 1.5 A.

(ii) the resistance of the lamp at norma normal brightness. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using Ohm's Law, R = V⁄I = 6.0V⁄1.5A = 4.0 Ω.

(b) AB is 1.00 m long and has a resistance of 5.0Ω. The lamp has normal brightness when the sliding contact is at X.

(i) The sliding contact is moved to B. Explain, without a calculation, why the lamp becomes dimmer.

Tap to reveal answer
When the contact is moved to B, the length of the resistance wire in the circuit increases. This means the total resistance of the circuit is greater, which causes the current to decrease and the lamp to become dimmer.

(ii) Calculate the distance AX for the lamp to have normal brightness. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
For the lamp to have normal brightness, the p.d. across it must be 6.0V. The total p.d. is 12.0V, so the p.d. across the wire section AX must also be 6.0V.
The current at normal brightness is 1.5A. The resistance of wire AX is R = V⁄I = 6.0V⁄1.5A = 4.0 Ω.
Since the 1.00m wire has a total resistance of 5.0Ω, the distance AX is (4.0Ω⁄5.0Ω) × 1.00 m = 0.80 m.

(b) Fig. 9.1 shows a 6.0V battery connected in series with a 1.2kΩ resistor and a thermistor.

(i) At a certain temperature, the resistance of the thermistor is 2.4 kΩ. Calculate the reading on the voltmeter. [4]

Tap to reveal answer
This is a potential divider circuit. The total resistance is 1.2 kΩ + 2.4 kΩ = 3.6 kΩ.
The voltmeter reads the p.d. across the thermistor.
Vout = (Rthermistor⁄Rtotal) × Vin = (2.4 kΩ⁄3.6 kΩ) × 6.0 V = 4.0 V.

Question 4 (0625/43_Summer_2017_Q9) - Electricity, Electron And Electronics

A 12V battery is connected in series to a 24W lamp and to a parallel pair of identical resistors X and Y. Fig. 9.1 is the circuit diagram.

The 24 W lamp lights at normal brightness when the potential difference (p.d.) across it is 6.0V. The lamp is at normal brightness.

(a) Calculate the resistance of the lamp. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
First, find the current: I = P⁄V = 24W⁄6.0V = 4.0 A.
Then, find the resistance: R = V⁄I = 6.0V⁄4.0A = 1.5 Ω.

(b) Determine

(i) the p.d. between A and B, [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The total voltage is 12V and the lamp uses 6.0V, so the remaining voltage is across the parallel pair AB.
p.d. = 12V - 6.0V = 6.0 V.

(ii) the combined resistance of the parallel pair of identical resistors X and Y, [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The total current of 4.0A flows through the parallel pair. The p.d. is 6.0V.
R = V⁄I = 6.0V⁄4.0A = 1.5 Ω.

(iii) the resistance of X. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
For two identical resistors in parallel, the combined resistance is half the individual resistance.
So, the resistance of X is 1.5 Ω × 2 = 3.0 Ω.

(c) Resistor X is removed from the circuit in Fig 9.1. Explain why the lamp becomes dimmer.

Tap to reveal answer
When X is removed, the resistance of that part of the circuit increases from 1.5Ω to 3.0Ω. This increases the total resistance of the circuit. A higher total resistance causes the current in the lamp to decrease, making it dimmer.
Question 5 (0625/41_Summer_2017_Q10) - Electricity

(a) Describe the movement of charge that causes an object to become positively charged. [1]

Electrons / negative charges are removed from the object.

(b) Fig. 10.1 shows a negatively charged rod held over an uncharged metal sphere.

(i) On Fig. 10.1, add + and - signs to represent the results of the movement of charge within the sphere. [2]

(ii) Describe the actions that must be taken to obtain an even distribution of positive charge on the surface of the sphere. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
While the negatively charged rod is held nearby, connect an earth wire to the sphere. This allows electrons to be repelled from the sphere and flow to the earth. Then, remove the earth wire first, and finally, remove the charged rod. The excess positive charge will then distribute evenly over the sphere.

Fig. 8.1 is a circuit diagram.

Calculate

(a) the resistance of the circuit, [4]

Question 7 (0625/43_Winter_2017_Q8) - Electricity

(a) Fig. 8.1 shows an uncharged conducting sphere on an insulating stand placed close to a positively charged rod. The rod and the sphere are not moved.

Describe how to charge the sphere using a wire connected to earth and explain whether the sphere becomes positively charged or negatively charged. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
First, touch the sphere with the earth wire. Since the rod is positive, electrons are attracted by the positive rod and flow from the earth to the sphere. Then, remove the earth wire before removing the rod. The sphere becomes negatively charged.

(b) Fig. 8.2 shows a small black circle that represents a positive charge.

On Fig. 8.2. draw the pattern and the direction of the electric field in the region around the charge. [2]

(c) A charge of 7.0C flows along a wire in 5.0 minutes. Calculate the current in the wire. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Time in seconds = 5.0 min × 60 s/min = 300 s.
Current I = Q⁄t = 7.0C⁄300s = 0.023 A.
