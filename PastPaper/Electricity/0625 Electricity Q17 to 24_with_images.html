Question 17 (0625/41_Winter_2018_Q9) - Electricity

Fig. 9.1 shows the symbol for a 12 V battery.

(a) Two lamps are connected in parallel with the battery. On Fig. 9.1, using the correct symbols, complete the circuit diagram. [1]

(b) One of these lamps has a resistance of 6.0Ω. Calculate, for this lamp:

(i) the current [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Using Ohm's Law, I = V⁄R = 12 V⁄6.0 Ω = 2.0 A.

(ii) the power. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using the formula P = VI = 12 V × 2.0 A = 24 W.

(c) The power of the other lamp is 36 W. Calculate the total energy delivered to this lamp in 20 hours. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
First, convert time to seconds: 20 hours × 3600 s/hour = 72000 s.
Using the formula E = Pt = 36 W × 72000 s = 2.6 × 10⁶ J.
Question 18 (0625/43_Winter_2018_Q9) - Electricity, Magnets And Currents

(a) Describe how a direct current (d.c.) differs from an alternating current (a.c.). [1]

Tap to reveal answer
A direct current (d.c.) flows in only one direction and has a constant value, while an alternating current (a.c.) continuously changes direction.

(b) Fig. 9.1 shows how the voltage output of an a.c. generator varies with time.

A heater is connected directly to the a.c. generator and the maximum current in the heater is 0.75A.

(i) On Fig. 9.2, sketch a graph to indicate how the current in the heater varies with time. [1]

(ii) Calculate the power produced by the heater when the current is 0.75A. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
From the voltage graph (Fig 9.1), the maximum voltage is 7.2V. When the current is at its maximum of 0.75A, the voltage is also at its maximum.
Using P = VI = 7.2 V × 0.75 A = 5.4 W.

(c) Fig. 9.3 shows the coil ABCD of the a.c. generator between two magnetic poles.

(i) On Fig. 9.3, draw a straight arrow to indicate the direction in which side AB of the coil is moving. Label this arrow M. [1]

(ii) Deduce the direction of the current induced in side AB of the coil and explain your reasoning. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The current is from A to B. This can be determined using Fleming's Right-Hand Rule, where the thumb points in the direction of motion (upwards), the first finger points in the direction of the magnetic field (N to S), and the second finger indicates the direction of the induced current.

(iii) The rate at which the coil of the a.c. generator rotates increases. State two ways in which the alternating voltage changes. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
1. The maximum voltage (amplitude) becomes greater.
2. The frequency increases (or the time period decreases).

Light energy.

(b) Consider the generation of electrical energy by a large number of solar cells, as shown in Fig. 3.1.

(i) State one environmental advantage and one environmental disadvantage. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Advantage: It produces no air pollution or greenhouse gases during operation.
Disadvantage: It can cause visual pollution or requires a large area of land.

(ii) State and explain whether this source of electrical energy is renewable. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Yes, it is renewable because the energy source (the Sun) is not used up in the process.

(c) Each group of solar cells is arranged in a rectangle 1.2 m × 2.8 m. The solar cells are situated in a region where 260W of solar energy is received per square metre of the cells. The electrical output of each group of solar cells is a current of 2.5 A with a potential difference of 86 V. Calculate the efficiency of the solar cells. [4]

Tap to reveal answer
First, calculate the power input: Area = 1.2m × 2.8m = 3.36 m².
Power Input = 260 W/m² × 3.36 m² = 874 W.
Next, calculate the power output: P = VI = 86 V × 2.5 A = 215 W.
Efficiency = (Power Output⁄Power Input) × 100% = (215W⁄874W) × 100% = 25%.
Question 20 (0625/41_Summer_2019_Q7) - Electricity

Fig. 7.1 shows a circuit diagram that includes component X.

(a) State the name of component X. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Component X is a thermistor.

(b) The electromotive force (e.m.f.) of the battery is E. The switch is closed. The potential difference (p.d.) across the 30Ω resistor is V₃₀. The p.d. across the 20Ω resistor is V₂₀. The p.d. across component X is Vₓ. State an equation that relates Vₓ to:

(i) V₃₀ [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Vₓ = V₃₀

(ii) E and V₂₀. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Vₓ = E - V₂₀

(c) The e.m.f. of the battery is 6.0V and the resistance of component X is 15Ω. Calculate:

(i) the total resistance of the circuit [3]

Tap to reveal answer
First find the resistance of the parallel part: 1⁄Rp = 1⁄15Ω + 1⁄30Ω = 2+1⁄30Ω. So, Rp = 10Ω.
Total resistance = Rp + 20Ω = 10Ω + 20Ω = 30 Ω.

(ii) the ammeter reading. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using Ohm's Law, I = V⁄Rtotal = 6.0V⁄30Ω = 0.20 A.

(d) The temperature of component X increases. State and explain what happens to the ammeter reading. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
When the temperature of the thermistor (X) increases, its resistance decreases. This causes the total resistance of the circuit to decrease. As a result, the ammeter reading (total current) increases.

(a) A conducting sphere is mounted on an insulating stand. Explain how you would use a positively charged rod of insulating material to charge the sphere by induction. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
1. Bring the positively charged rod close to the sphere, but do not touch it.
2. Earth the sphere by touching it with your finger or a wire connected to the ground. Electrons will be attracted from the earth onto the sphere.

Question 22 (0625/43_Summer_2019_Q8) - Forces And Energy, Electricity

Fig. 8.1 shows a 240 V mains supply connected to an air-conditioning unit and a freezer. A fuse X is placed in the circuit as shown.

The freezer has an operating power of 700 W.

(a) Calculate the current in the freezer. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Using P = VI, the current I = P⁄V = 700W⁄240V = 2.9 A.

(b) The maximum operating current of the air-conditioning unit is 7.5A. Fuses of current rating 1A, 3A, 5A, 10A, 13A are available. Suggest a suitable rating for fuse X. Give two reasons for your answer. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
Fuse rating: 13 A.
Reason 1: The total operating current is 2.9 A + 7.5 A = 10.4 A. The fuse rating must be higher than this to avoid blowing during normal operation.
Reason 2: The rating should be as close as possible above the operating current to provide protection. A fuse with too high a rating would not blow until the current was dangerously high.

(c) A fuse is made out of a short length of wire. Explain why fuses of a higher rating are made of thicker wire. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
A thicker wire has a lower resistance. For a given current, a lower resistance means less heating effect (P=I²R). Therefore, a thicker wire can carry a higher current before it gets hot enough to melt.

(d) Electrical energy can be obtained from renewable and non-renewable sources of energy.

(i) State two renewable sources of energy. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Source 1: Solar. Source 2: Wind. (Other examples: hydroelectric, geothermal, tidal).

(ii) State one social, economic or environmental disadvantage of one of your answers to (d)(i). [1]

Tap to reveal answer
A disadvantage of solar farms is that they require a large area of land, which could otherwise be used for agriculture or housing.
Question 23 (0625/42_Summer_2019_Q10) - Electricity

Fig. 10.1 shows a circuit containing a filament lamp of resistance 0.30Ω and two resistors, each of resistance 0.20 Ω.

(a) Calculate the combined resistance of the lamp and the two resistors. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
The lamp (0.30Ω) is in parallel with one resistor (0.20Ω). Their combined resistance is:
1⁄Rp = 1⁄0.30Ω + 1⁄0.20Ω = 2+3⁄0.60Ω. So, Rp = 0.12Ω.
This parallel part is in series with the other 0.20Ω resistor.
Total resistance = 0.12Ω + 0.20Ω = 0.32 Ω.

(b) The potential difference (p.d.) of the supply is increased so that the current in the lamp increases. State and explain any change in the resistance of the lamp. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Statement: The resistance of the lamp increases.
Explanation: As the current increases, the filament in the lamp gets hotter. The temperature of the lamp increases, which causes its resistance to increase.
Question 24 (0625/41_Winter_2019_Q5) - Electricity

Fig. 5.1 shows a sphere that is negatively charged. The sphere is attached to a plastic stand.

(a) On Fig. 5.1, draw arrows to indicate the pattern and direction of the electric field in the region surrounding the sphere. [2]

(b) A smaller, uncharged metal sphere S is suspended by a plastic thread near the negatively charged sphere. Fig. 5.2 shows the two spheres.

(i) By drawing on Fig. 5.2, indicate the distribution of charge on S. [2]

(ii) Describe what happens to S and why. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Sphere S is attracted and moves towards the negatively charged sphere. This is because the negative sphere induces a separation of charge in S, with positive charges moving closer, resulting in a net attractive force.

(iii) The sphere S is now connected to the Earth by a wire. Describe what happens in the wire and state how this affects the charge on S. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Electrons (negative charges) are repelled by the large negative sphere and move along the wire to the Earth. This leaves sphere S with a net positive charge.

(c) The metal sphere S is an electrical conductor. The plastic thread is an electrical insulator. Explain this difference by referring to the structures of the two materials. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The metal conductor has free or mobile electrons that are able to move and carry charge. In the plastic insulator, the electrons are fixed in position and cannot move freely.
