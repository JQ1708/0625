Question 25

(0625/42_Winter_2022_Q10) - Electron And Electronics

(a) The magnitude of the charge on a β-particle is 1.6 × 10-19C.

(i) State the proton number and nucleon number of an α-particle. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

proton number: 2

nucleon number: 4

(ii) Determine the magnitude of the charge of an α-particle. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

An α-particle contains 2 protons. The charge is twice the elementary charge.

Charge = 2 × 1.6 × 10-19 C = 3.2 × 10-19 C.

(b) A nucleus of radium-230 consists of 88 protons and 142 neutrons. Radium-230 is radioactive and decays by β-emission to an isotope of actinium. The symbol for radium is Ra and the symbol for actinium is Ac. Write down the nuclide equation for this decay. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

23088Ra → 23089Ac + 0-1β

The nucleon number (230) remains the same on both sides. The proton number on the left (88) equals the sum of the proton numbers on the right (89 + -1).

(c) The half-life of radium-230 is 93 min. A sample contains 9.6 × 10-12g of radium-230. Calculate the mass of radium in the sample after 279 min. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Number of half-lives = Total time⁄Half-life = 279 min⁄93 min = 3.

After 3 half-lives, the mass will have halved three times.

Mass remaining = Initial mass⁄23 = (9.6 × 10-12 g)⁄8 = 1.2 × 10-12 g.

Question 26

(0625/43_Winter_2022_Q10) - Electron And Electronics

(a) A cloud chamber can be used to detect α-particles and β-particles. Alcohol in the cloud chamber exists as a vapour and condenses on ions produced in the air. This forms visible tracks. Fig. 10.1 shows the tracks when a source of α-particles and β-particles is present in the cloud chamber. Some of the tracks are short and thick. Other tracks are longer and thinner. State and explain which tracks are produced by α-particles and which tracks are produced by β-particles. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

α-particles produce the short, thick tracks. This is because they are more ionising, creating a dense trail of ions, but they are less penetrating and have a shorter range.

β-particles produce the long, thin tracks. This is because they are less ionising, creating a sparse trail, but they are more penetrating and have a longer range.

(b) A radioactive isotope of sodium (Na) is used to detect leaks from water pipes. A nucleus of this isotope of sodium contains 11 protons and 13 neutrons. This nucleus decays by emitting a β-particle to form a nucleus of magnesium (Mg).

(i) Describe what is meant by an isotope. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons in their nucleus.

(ii) Write down the nuclide equation for the decay of this isotope of sodium to magnesium. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The isotope is Sodium-24 (11 protons + 13 neutrons).

2411Na → 2412Mg + 0-1β

The nuclide equation is balanced with Na on the left, and Mg and β on the right with correct proton and nucleon numbers.

(iii) This isotope of sodium has a half-life of 15 hours. The isotope of magnesium is stable and does not undergo radioactive decay. Suggest why these properties of the isotope of sodium and the isotope of magnesium make this isotope of sodium suitable to detect leaks from water pipes. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The half-life is long enough to allow time for the leak to be detected. However, it is also short enough that the radioactivity will decay to a negligible amount after a few days, making the water safe. Also, the decay product (magnesium) is stable, so it is not hazardous.
