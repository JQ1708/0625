A student sets up a circuit that includes a 12V battery, an 800Ω resistor, a voltmeter and a thermistor. Fig. 8.1 is an incomplete circuit diagram because the symbol for the thermistor is missing. The thermistor is connected between terminals P and Q.

(a) Complete Fig. 8.1 by drawing the symbol for a thermistor between terminals P and Q. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

(b) The 12V battery consists of eight identical cells connected in series. Calculate the electromotive force (e.m.f.) of each cell. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

e.m.f. per cell = Total e.m.f.⁄Number of cells = 12 V⁄8 = 1.5 V.

(c) The reading on the voltmeter is 8.0V.

(i) Determine the resistance of the thermistor. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The voltmeter measures the p.d. across the thermistor, which is 8.0 V. The p.d. across the 800Ω resistor is 12 V - 8.0 V = 4.0 V.

The current in the circuit is I = V⁄R = 4.0 V⁄800 Ω = 0.0050 A.

The resistance of the thermistor is RTh = VTh⁄I = 8.0 V⁄0.0050 A = 1600 Ω.

(ii) A few hours later, the student notices that the reading on the voltmeter is greater. Explain what can be deduced from this observation. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

A greater voltmeter reading means a larger proportion of the e.m.f. is across the thermistor. This implies that the resistance of the thermistor has increased. For a thermistor, resistance increases when the temperature decreases. Therefore, the room has become cooler.

(c) An electrical device has a metal case. Explain the benefit of earthing the metal case. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Earthing prevents electrocution. If a live wire touches the metal case, the earth wire provides a low resistance path for the current to flow to the ground. This causes a large surge of current that blows the fuse or trips the circuit breaker, disconnecting the supply and making the appliance safe.

(d) (i) Explain how a fuse protects a circuit. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

A fuse contains a thin wire designed to melt at a specific current. If the current becomes too high due to a fault, the wire heats up and melts. This breaks the circuit and stops the flow of current, protecting the appliance from damage.

(ii) The current in an electric kettle connected to the mains through a fuse is 10A. Fuses with the following ratings are available. Circle the correct fuse rating for this appliance and explain your answer. 3A 9A 10A 13A 30A

Tap to reveal answer

Correct fuse rating: 13 A.

Explanation: The fuse rating must be slightly higher than the normal operating current. A 10A fuse might blow during normal operation, while 9A and 3A are too low. 30A is too high and would not protect the appliance.

Question 24

(0625/41_Winter_2022_Q9) - Electron And Electronics

Only one isotope of gold occurs naturally on Earth.

(a) State what this indicates about the nuclear structure of all the naturally occurring atoms of gold on Earth. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

This indicates that all naturally occurring gold atoms have the same number of neutrons (as well as the same number of protons).

(b) There are several artificially produced isotopes of gold. Gold-198 decays by beta-emission to a stable isotope of mercury.

(i) Determine the number of protons and the number of neutrons in a nucleus of this isotope of mercury. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Gold-198 is 19879Au. In beta decay, a neutron becomes a proton and an electron is emitted. The proton number increases by 1, and the nucleon number is unchanged.

Number of protons = 79 + 1 = 80.

Number of neutrons = 198 - 80 = 118.

(ii) A sample of gold-198 is placed near to a radiation detector in a research laboratory. The count rate is recorded at the same time every day for 32 days. The results are used to plot the graph shown in Fig. 9.1. Using Fig. 9.1, determine the background count rate in the research laboratory. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The background count rate is the reading after the source has fully decayed. From the graph, the count rate flattens out at 20 counts/min.

(iii) Using Fig. 9.1, determine the half-life of gold-198. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

Initial count rate from the source = Initial total count - background count = 390 - 20 = 370 counts/min.

Half of this count rate is 370 / 2 = 185 counts/min.

The total count rate on the graph to look for is 185 + background = 185 + 20 = 205 counts/min.

From the graph, a count rate of 205 counts/min corresponds to a time of approximately 2.7 days. (Accept range 2.4-2.9 days).
