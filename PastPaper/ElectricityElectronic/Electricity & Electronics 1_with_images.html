Question 1

(0625/42_Summer_2017_Q8) - Electricity, Electron And Electronics

Fig. 8.1 shows a 12.0V power supply connected in a circuit.

The circuit includes a lamp and a resistance wire AB of constant cross-sectional area. There is a sliding contact that can be moved between A and B.

(a) The rating of the lamp at normal brightness is 6.0V, 9.0W.

Calculate

(i) the current in the lamp at normal brightness, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Using the formula P = V × I, we can find the current I.

I = P⁄V = 9.0 W⁄6.0 V = 1.5 A

(ii) the resistance of the lamp at normal brightness. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Using Ohm's Law, V = I × R, we can find the resistance R.

R = V⁄I = 6.0 V⁄1.5 A = 4.0 Ω

(b) AB is 1.00 m long and has a resistance of 5.0Ω. The lamp has normal brightness when the sliding contact is at X.

(i) The sliding contact is moved to B. Explain, without a calculation, why the lamp becomes dimmer. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

When the contact is moved to B, the length of the resistance wire in the circuit increases. This increases the total resistance of the circuit. A higher total resistance leads to a lower current flowing through the lamp, and a smaller p.d. across the lamp, making it dimmer.

(ii) Calculate the distance AX for the lamp to have normal brightness. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

For normal brightness, current is 1.5 A. The total resistance of the circuit is Rtotal = Vsupply⁄I = 12.0 V⁄1.5 A = 8.0 Ω.

The total resistance is the sum of the lamp's resistance and the resistance of the wire section AX. Rtotal = Rlamp + RAX.

So, RAX = 8.0 Ω - 4.0 Ω = 4.0 Ω.

The wire's resistance is proportional to its length. The full length (1.00 m) has 5.0 Ω. The distance AX is (4.0 Ω⁄5.0 Ω) × 1.00 m = 0.80 m.

Question 3

(0625/43_Summer_2017_Q9) - Electricity, Electron And Electronics

A 12V battery is connected in series to a 24W lamp and to a parallel pair of identical resistors X and Y. Fig. 9.1 is the circuit diagram.

The 24 W lamp lights at normal brightness when the potential difference (p.d.) across it is 6.0V. The lamp is at normal brightness.

(a) Calculate the resistance of the lamp. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

First, find the current at normal brightness: I = P⁄V = 24 W⁄6.0 V = 4.0 A.

Then, calculate resistance: R = V⁄I = 6.0 V⁄4.0 A = 1.5 Ω.

(b) Determine

(i) the p.d. between A and B, [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The p.d. across the parallel pair is the supply voltage minus the p.d. across the lamp: 12 V - 6.0 V = 6.0 V.

(ii) the combined resistance of the parallel pair of identical resistors X and Y, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The current through the parallel pair is the same as the current through the lamp, 4.0 A. The combined resistance is Rcombined = VAB⁄I = 6.0 V⁄4.0 A = 1.5 Ω.

(iii) the resistance of X. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

For two identical resistors in parallel, Rcombined = RX⁄2.

Therefore, RX = 2 × Rcombined = 2 × 1.5 Ω = 3.0 Ω.

(c) Resistor X is removed from the circuit in Fig 9.1. Explain why the lamp becomes dimmer. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

When resistor X is removed, the total resistance of the parallel part of the circuit increases (from 1.5 Ω to 3.0 Ω). This increases the total resistance of the entire circuit. As a result, the total current decreases, causing the lamp to become dimmer.

(a) A conducting sphere is mounted on an insulating stand. Explain how you would use a positively charged rod of insulating material to charge the sphere by induction. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

1. Bring the positively charged rod close to, but not touching, the conducting sphere.

2. Earth the sphere by touching it with your finger. Electrons will be attracted from the earth onto the sphere.

3. Remove the earth connection (your finger) while the rod is still held close.

4. Finally, remove the rod. The sphere will be left with a net negative charge.
