Question 9

(0625/41_Winter_2017_Q1) - Forces And Motion

Fig. 1.1 shows the speed-time graph for the motion of a car.

The mass of the car is 1200 kg.

(a) Calculate, for the first 20s of the motion,

(i) the distance travelled by the car, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The distance is the area under the graph. For the first 20s, this is a triangle with area ½ × base × height = ½ × 20 s × 13 m/s = 130 m.

(ii) the acceleration of the car, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Acceleration is the gradient of the graph. a = (v-u) ⁄ t = (13 m/s - 0 m/s) ⁄ 20 s = 0.65 m/s².

(iii) the resultant force acting on the car. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Resultant force is calculated using F = ma. So, 1200 kg × 0.65 m/s² = 780 N.

(b) Describe the motion of the car in the period of time from 25s to 40s. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The car is accelerating, but the rate of acceleration is decreasing. The speed is increasing at a lower rate.

Question 10

(0625/43_Winter_2017_Q1) - Forces And Motion

A truck accelerates uniformly along a straight, horizontal road. The mass of the truck is 2.0 x 104 kg.

(a) The speed of the truck increases from rest to 12m/s in 30 s. Calculate

(i) the distance travelled by the truck during this time, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Distance can be calculated using s = average speed × time = ((u+v) ⁄ 2) × t. So, ((0+12) ⁄ 2) × 30 = 180 m.

(ii) the resultant force on the truck. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

First, calculate acceleration: a = Δv ⁄ t = 12 m/s ⁄ 30 s = 0.40 m/s². Then, calculate force: F = ma = (2.0 × 104 kg) × 0.40 m/s² = 8000 N.

(b) To maintain a uniform acceleration, the forward force on the truck must change. Explain why. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

As the speed of the truck increases, the resistive force of air resistance (or drag) increases. To maintain a constant resultant force (and thus constant acceleration), the forward engine force must increase to counteract the growing resistive force.

Question 11

(0625/42_Winter_2017_Q2) - Forces And Motion

(a) An object is moving in a straight line at constant speed. A resultant force begins to act upon the object. State the ways in which the force may change the motion of the object. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The force can cause the object to accelerate (increase speed), decelerate (decrease speed), stop, or change direction.

(b) State one other effect a force could have on the object. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

A force could also cause a change of shape or size.

(c) The mass of a car is 1400kg. The car, initially at rest, is moved along a level road by a resultant force of 3500 N. The car reaches a speed of 30 m/s.

(i) Calculate the average acceleration of the car. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Using F=ma, the acceleration is a = F ⁄ m = 3500 N ⁄ 1400 kg = 2.5 m/s².

(ii) Calculate the time for which the force is applied. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Using a = (v-u) ⁄ t, the time is t = (v-u) ⁄ a = (30 m/s - 0) ⁄ 2.5 m/s² = 12 s.

(iii) State the name of a force which opposes the motion of the car. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

A force opposing the motion is friction or air resistance (drag).

Question 12

(0625/41_Winter_2017_Q4) - Forces And Motion, Forces And Pressure

Fig. 4.1 shows a balloon filled with helium that is used to lift measuring instruments to a great height above the Earth's surface.

(a) Explain, in terms of momentum, how the atoms of helium produce a force on the wall of the balloon. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Helium atoms collide with the wall of the balloon and rebound. This causes a change of momentum for the atoms. The force on the wall is equal to the total rate of change of momentum of the atoms.

(b) At ground level, the pressure of the helium in the balloon is 1.0 x 105 Pa. The volume occupied by the helium is 9.6m³. The balloon is released and it rises quickly through the atmosphere. The volume occupied by the helium increases, but the temperature of the helium may be assumed to stay constant.

(i) Explain, in terms of the helium atoms in the balloon, why the pressure in the balloon is smaller than at ground level. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

As the volume increases, there are fewer atoms per unit volume. This leads to a lower rate of collision of atoms with the walls of the balloon, resulting in lower pressure.

(ii) Calculate the pressure of the helium when it occupies a volume of 12m³. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Assuming constant temperature, Boyle's Law applies: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂. So, P₂ = P₁V₁ ⁄ V₂ = (1.0 × 105 Pa × 9.6 m³) ⁄ 12 m³ = 8.0 x 104 Pa.

Question 13

(0625/41_Summer_2018_Q1) - Forces And Motion

Fig. 1.1 shows the speed-time graph for a vehicle accelerating from rest.

(a) Calculate the acceleration of the vehicle at time t = 30s. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The acceleration is the gradient of the graph at that point. By drawing a tangent to the curve at t = 30s and calculating its gradient (change in speed ⁄ change in time), the acceleration is found to be in the range of 0.30 to 0.45 m/s².

(b) Without further calculation, state how the acceleration at time t = 100s compares to the acceleration at time t = 10s. Suggest, in terms of force, a reason why any change has taken place. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The acceleration at t=100s is less than at t=10s. This is because as speed increases, resistive forces like air resistance increase. This causes the resultant force on the vehicle to decrease, leading to a smaller acceleration.

(c) Determine the distance travelled by the vehicle between time t = 120s and time t = 160s. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The distance is the area under the graph. This section is a trapezium. Area = ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × height = ½ × (30 m/s + 20 m/s) × 40 s = 1000 m.

(a) Fig. 1.1 shows the axes of a distance-time graph for an object moving in a straight line.

(i) 1. On Fig. 1.1, draw between time = 0 and time = 10s, the graph for an object moving with a constant speed of 5.0 m/s. Start your graph at distance = 0m. [2]

2. State the property of the graph that represents speed.

Tap to reveal answer

1. The graph is a straight line from (0,0) to (10s, 50m).

2. The property representing speed is the gradient (or slope) of the graph.

(ii) Between time = 10s and time = 20s the object accelerates. The speed at time = 20s is 9.0 m/s. Calculate the average acceleration between time = 10s and time = 20 s. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Average acceleration is a = (v-u) ⁄ t. The initial speed at 10s was 5.0 m/s. So, a = (9.0 m/s - 5.0 m/s) ⁄ 10 s = 0.40 m/s².

(b) Fig. 1.2 shows the axes of a speed-time graph for a different object.

The graph should be a straight line starting from (0s, 50m/s) and ending at (100s, 15m/s), since the speed decrease is 0.35 m/s² × 100 s = 35 m/s.

(ii) Calculate the distance travelled by the object from time = 0 to time = 100s. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The distance is the area under the graph. Area of trapezium = ½ × (50 + 15) × 100 = 3250 m (The mark scheme gives 3300m, likely a slight calculation variance or rounding).

Question 15

(0625/43_Summer_2018_Q1) - Forces And Motion

There is no atmosphere on the Moon. A space probe is launched from the surface of the Moon. Fig. 1.1 shows the speed-time graph of the space probe.

(a) Determine the acceleration of the space probe at time = 0. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The acceleration at t=0 is the initial gradient of the curve. By drawing a tangent at the origin and calculating its slope, the value is found to be in the range of 13 to 14 m/s².

(b) Between time = 0 and time = 150s, the acceleration of the space probe changes.

(i) Without calculation, state how the graph shows this. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The graph shows the acceleration is changing because the gradient changes; the graph is curved.

(ii) During this time, the thrust exerted on the space probe by the motor remains constant. State one possible reason why the acceleration changes in the way shown by Fig. 1.1. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The acceleration increases because the mass of the space rocket decreases as it burns and ejects fuel. A constant thrust on a decreasing mass results in increasing acceleration (from F=ma).

(c) Calculate the distance travelled by the space probe from time = 200 s to time = 300 s. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The distance is the area under the graph. This section is a trapezium. Area = ½ × (4100 m/s + 5000 m/s) × 100 s = 4.55 x 105 m.

Question 16

(0625/41_Winter_2018_Q1) - Forces And Motion

A train of mass 5.6 x 105 kg is at rest in a station. At time t = 0 s, a resultant force acts on the train and it starts to accelerate forwards. Fig. 1.1 is the distance-time graph for the train for the first 120s.

(a) (i) Use Fig. 1.1 to determine:

1. the average speed of the train during the 120s [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Average speed = total distance ⁄ total time = 4800 m ⁄ 120 s = 40 m/s.

2. the speed of the train at time t = 100s. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The speed at t=100s is the gradient of the tangent to the curve at that point. The graph is a straight line after approx 80s, so we can take the gradient of this line. The value is between 50 and 60 m/s.

(ii) Describe how the acceleration of the train at time t = 100s differs from the acceleration at time t = 20s. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

At t = 20s, the graph is curving, so the speed is changing and there is a positive acceleration. At t = 100s, the graph is a straight line, meaning the speed is constant and the acceleration is zero.

(b) (i) The initial acceleration of the train is 0.75 m/s². Calculate the resultant force that acts on the train at this time. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Resultant force is F = ma = (5.6 × 105 kg) × 0.75 m/s² = 4.2 x 105 N.

(ii) At time t = 120s, the train begins to decelerate. State what is meant by deceleration. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Deceleration is a decrease in speed/velocity over time, or a negative acceleration.
