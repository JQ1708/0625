Question 25

(0625/43_Summer_2019_Q2) - Forces And Motion, Forces And Energy

Fig. 2.1 is the top view of a small ship of mass 1.2 x 106 kg. The ship is moving slowly sideways at 0.040 m/s as it comes in to dock.

The ship hits the wooden pillars which move towards the dock wall.

(a) Calculate the kinetic energy of the ship before it hits the pillars. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Kinetic energy is calculated using the formula KE = ½mv². So, KE = ½ × (1.2 × 106 kg) × (0.040 m/s)², which equals 960 J.

(b) The ship is in contact with the pillars for 0.30s as it comes to rest. Calculate the average force exerted on the side of the ship. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

First, calculate the change in momentum (impulse): Δp = mv = (1.2 × 106 kg) × (0.040 m/s) = 4.8 × 104 kg m/s. Then, the average force is F = Δp ⁄ t = (4.8 × 104 kg m/s) ⁄ 0.30 s, which equals 1.6 x 105 N.

(c) Assume that the kinetic energy calculated in (a) is used to do work moving the pillars. Calculate the distance moved by the pillars. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Work done is equal to the force multiplied by the distance moved (Work Done = Fd). The work done is equal to the initial kinetic energy of the ship, 960 J. The distance is d = Work ⁄ F = 960 J ⁄ (1.6 × 105 N) = 0.0060 m or 6.0 mm.

(d) Dock walls sometimes have the pillars replaced with rubber car tyres. Explain how this reduces the possibility of damage when a boat docks. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The rubber tyres are compressible, which increases the time of collision. Since the change in momentum is the same, increasing the time over which the force acts results in a smaller average force exerted on both the ship and the dock wall, reducing the chance of damage.

Question 26

(0625/41_Winter_2019_Q1) - Forces And Motion

A car accelerates from rest at time t=0 to its maximum speed.

Fig. 1.1 is the speed-time graph for the first 25 s of its motion.

(a) The mass of the car is 2300 kg. For the time between t=0 and t=5.0 s, determine:

(i) the acceleration of the car [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Acceleration is the gradient of the speed-time graph. Between 0 and 5.0 s, the graph is a straight line. The acceleration is a = Δv ⁄ Δt = (15 m/s) ⁄ (5.0 s) = 3.0 m/s².

(ii) the resultant force acting on the car. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Resultant force is calculated using F = ma. So, F = 2300 kg × 3.0 m/s², which equals 6900 N.

(b) Describe the motion of the car between t=10s and t=15s. Explain how Fig. 1.1 shows this. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Between 10s and 15s, the car is still accelerating but at a decreasing rate. The graph shows this because the speed is increasing, but the gradient of the graph is positive and decreasing.

(c) Between t=10s and t=15 s, the force exerted on the car due to the engine remains constant. Suggest and explain why the car moves in the way shown by Fig. 1.1. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

As the car's speed increases, the resistive force of air resistance also increases. With a constant engine force, the increasing resistive force causes the resultant force to decrease, which in turn causes the acceleration to decrease.

Question 27

(0625/41_Winter_2019_Q3) - Forces And Motion

Fig. 3.1 shows a shooting competition, where air rifles fire soft metal pellets at distant targets.

When an air rifle is fired, it exerts an impulse of 0.019 Ns on the pellet.

(a) Define impulse. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Impulse is the product of the force and the time for which it acts.

(b) The pellet has a mass of 1.1 x 10-4 kg. Determine:

(i) the speed with which the pellet leaves the air rifle [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Impulse is equal to the change in momentum (I = mv, since it starts from rest). So, the speed is v = I ⁄ m = 0.019 Ns ⁄ (1.1 × 10-4 kg), which is approximately 170 m/s.

(ii) the kinetic energy of the pellet as it leaves the rifle. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Kinetic energy is calculated as KE = ½mv². So, KE = ½ × (1.1 × 10-4 kg) × (170 m/s)², which is approximately 1.6 J.

(c) The pellet melts when it strikes the target. Describe how the molecular structure of the liquid metal differs from that of the solid metal. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

In a liquid, the molecules have an irregular or random arrangement, are further apart on average, and are not fixed in place, allowing them to move past each other. In a solid, molecules are typically in a fixed, regular pattern.

Question 28

(0625/41_Summer_2020_Q1) - Forces And Motion

An aeroplane of mass 2.5 x 105 kg lands with a speed of 62 m/s, on a horizontal runway at time t=0. The aeroplane decelerates uniformly as it travels along the runway in a straight line until it reaches a speed of 6.0 m/s at t=35 s.

(a) Calculate:

(i) the deceleration of the aeroplane in the 35 s after it lands [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Deceleration is calculated using a = (v-u) ⁄ t. So, a = (6.0 m/s - 62 m/s) ⁄ 35 s = -1.6 m/s². The deceleration is 1.6 m/s².

(ii) the resultant force acting on the aeroplane as it decelerates [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Resultant force is F = ma = (2.5 × 105 kg) × 1.6 m/s², which equals 4.0 x 105 N.

(iii) the momentum of the aeroplane when its speed is 6.0 m/s [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Momentum is p = mv = (2.5 × 105 kg) × 6.0 m/s, which equals 1.5 x 106 kg m/s.

(b) At t=35 s, the aeroplane stops decelerating and moves along the runway at a constant speed of 6.0 m/s for a further 15s. On Fig. 1.1, sketch the shape of the graph for the distance travelled by the aeroplane along the runway between t=0 and t=50 s. You are not required to calculate distance values. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The distance-time graph from t=0 to t=35s should be a curve with a decreasing gradient, starting from the origin. From t=35s to t=50s, the speed is constant, so the graph should be a straight line with a positive gradient.

(c) As the aeroplane decelerates, its kinetic energy decreases. Suggest what happens to this energy. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The kinetic energy is converted primarily into thermal energy in the brakes, tyres, and the surrounding air due to work done against friction.

Question 29

Fig. 1.1 shows the speed-time graph of a person on a journey. On the journey, he walks and then waits for a bus. He then travels by bus. He gets off the bus and waits for two minutes. He then walks again. His journey takes 74 minutes.

(a) For the whole journey calculate:

(i) the distance travelled [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The total distance is the total area under the speed-time graph. By calculating the area of each section (walking, bus travel, final walk) and adding them together, the total distance is found to be 17 km.

(ii) the average speed. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Average speed is total distance ⁄ total time. The total time is 74 minutes. So, the average speed is 17 km ⁄ (74 ⁄ 60) h, which is approximately 14 km/h.

(b) State and explain which feature of a speed-time graph shows acceleration. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The gradient of the graph shows acceleration. The gradient is calculated as the change of speed divided by time.

(c) State and explain the acceleration of the person at time = 40 minutes. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The acceleration is zero. This is because at 40 minutes, the graph is a horizontal line, meaning its gradient is zero and the speed is constant.

Question 31

(0625/42_Summer_2020_Q2) - Forces And Motion

Fig. 2.1 shows a train. The total mass of the train and its passengers is 750000 kg. The train is travelling at a speed of 84 m/s. The driver applies the brakes and the train takes 80s to slow down to a speed of 42 m/s.

(a) Calculate the impulse applied to the train as it slows down. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Impulse is the change in momentum, calculated as impulse = m(v-u). The change in momentum is 750000 kg × (42 m/s - 84 m/s), which equals a magnitude of 3.2 x 107 Ns.

(b) Calculate the average resultant force applied to the train as it slows down. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The average resultant force is the impulse divided by time (F = Impulse ⁄ t). So, (3.2 × 107 Ns) ⁄ 80 s gives a force of 3.9 x 105 N.

(c) Suggest how the shape of the train helps it to travel at high speeds. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

A streamlined or aerodynamic shape reduces air resistance (drag), allowing it to maintain high speeds more efficiently.

(d) The train took 80s to reduce its speed from 84 m/s to 42m/s. Explain why, with the same braking force, the train takes more than 80s to reduce its speed from 42 m/s to zero. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The total resistive force is the braking force plus air resistance. At lower speeds, there is less air resistance. With the same braking force, the total resultant decelerating force is smaller, so it takes a longer time to produce the same change in speed.

(e) On a wet day, the train travels a greater distance before it stops along the same track. The train has the same speed of 84 m/s before the brakes are applied. Suggest a reason for this. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

On a wet day, the friction between the train's wheels and the rails is reduced. This reduces the maximum possible braking force, leading to a smaller deceleration and a longer stopping distance.

Question 32

(0625/43_Summer_2020_Q3) - Forces And Pressure, Forces And Motion

In a double-decker bus there are two passenger compartments, one above the other.

(a) Fig. 3.1 shows a double-decker bus on a tilted platform. The platform is used to test the stability of the bus. The angle the bus makes with the horizontal is gradually increased until the bus begins to topple to the left. Explain why the bus begins to topple. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The bus begins to topple when the vertical line of action of its weight, acting through the centre of mass, falls outside the base of the bus. This creates an unbalanced moment that causes it to rotate and fall over.

(b) There are 30 passengers in the upper compartment of the bus and 2 passengers in the bottom compartment of the bus. State how this affects the stability of the bus and the reason for this. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

This makes the bus less stable. The reason is that having more mass in the upper compartment raises the overall centre of mass of the bus, making it easier to topple.

(c) A bus is travelling along a straight road. The bus and the driver have a combined mass of 16000kg when there are no passengers in it. The bus has 73 passengers. The average mass of each of the passengers is 65kg.

(i) Calculate the total mass of the bus, the driver and the 73 passengers. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The total mass is the sum of the bus's mass and the passengers' mass. Mass of passengers = 73 × 65 kg = 4745 kg. Total mass = 16000 kg + 4745 kg = 20745 kg (rounded to 21000 kg in the scheme).

(ii) The fully loaded bus accelerates uniformly from rest to a speed of 14 m/s. The time taken to reach a speed of 14 m/s is 20s. Calculate the resultant force on the bus during the acceleration. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

First, calculate acceleration: a = Δv ⁄ t = 14 m/s ⁄ 20 s = 0.70 m/s². Then, using F = ma with the total mass, F = 21000 kg × 0.70 m/s², the resultant force is 15000 N (rounded).
