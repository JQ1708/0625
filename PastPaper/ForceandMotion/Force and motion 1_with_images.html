Question 1

(0625/41_Summer_2017_Q1) - Forces And Motion

Fig. 1.1 is the speed-time graph for an ice skater.

(a) Explain what is meant by deceleration. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Deceleration is the rate of decrease of velocity or speed. It means the object is slowing down.

(b) Use Fig. 1.1 to determine

(i) the distance travelled between times t = 3.0s and t = 6.0s, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The distance travelled is the area under the graph between the given times. This area can be calculated as a trapezium: Area = ½ × (a+b) × h = ½ × (11 m/s + 5 m/s) × 3.0 s = 24 m.

(ii) the deceleration between times t = 3.0s and t = 6.0s. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Deceleration is the gradient of the graph in this section. a = (change in velocity) ⁄ (time taken) = (5 m/s - 11 m/s) ⁄ (6.0 s - 3.0 s) = -6 m/s ⁄ 3.0 s. The deceleration is 2.0 m/s².

(c) (i) State what happens to the size of the deceleration after time t = 6.0 s. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

After t = 6.0 s, the size of the deceleration decreases.

(ii) State what happens to the resultant force on the skater after time t = 6.0 s. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Since the deceleration is decreasing, the resultant force on the skater also decreases.

Question 4

(0625/41_Summer_2017_Q2) - Forces And Motion, Forces And Energy

A footballer kicks a ball vertically upwards. Initially, the ball is stationary.

(a) His boot is in contact with the ball for 0.050s. The average resultant force on the ball during this time is 180N. The ball leaves his foot at 20 m/s.

Calculate

(i) the impulse of the force acting on the ball, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Impulse is calculated as Force × time. So, 180 N × 0.050 s gives an impulse of 9.0 Ns.

(ii) the mass of the ball, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Impulse equals the change in momentum (Ft = mv). Mass is m = Ft ⁄ v. So, 9.0 Ns ⁄ 20 m/s = 0.45 kg.

(iii) the height to which the ball rises. Ignore air resistance. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The initial kinetic energy is converted to gravitational potential energy (½mv² = mgh). The height is h = v² ⁄ (2g) = (20 m/s)² ⁄ (2 × 10 N/kg) = 20 m.

(b) While the boot is in contact with the ball, the ball is no longer spherical. State the word used to describe the energy stored in the ball. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The energy stored is elastic potential energy or strain energy.

Question 5

(0625/42_Summer_2017_Q2) - Forces And Motion, Forces And Pressure

Fig. 2.1 shows a vehicle designed to be used on the Moon.

The brakes of the vehicle are tested on Earth.

(a) The acceleration of free fall on the Moon is one sixth (1⁄6) of its value on Earth. Tick one box in each column of the table to predict the value of that quantity when the vehicle is used on the Moon, compared to the test on Earth. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Mass of vehicle on Moon: same as value on Earth.

Weight of vehicle on Moon: 1⁄6 × value on Earth.

Deceleration of vehicle on Moon with same braking force: same as value on Earth.

(b) Fig. 2.2 shows the brake pedal of the vehicle. The driver exerts a force on the pedal, which increases the pressure in the oil to operate the brakes. The area of the piston in the cylinder is 6.5 x 10-4 m² (0.00065 m²). The pressure increase in the oil is 5.0 x 105 Pa (500000 Pa). Calculate the force exerted by the driver on the brake pedal. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

First, find the force on the piston: F = P × A = 5.0 × 105 Pa × 6.5 × 10-4 m² = 325 N. Then, using the principle of moments about the pivot, Driver's Force × 24 cm = 325 N × 7.0 cm. The driver's force is (325 × 7.0) ⁄ 24 = 95 N.

Question 6

(0625/43_Summer_2017_Q2) - Forces And Motion, Forces And Energy

(a) State the word equation that defines momentum. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

momentum = mass × velocity

(b) A metal block A, travelling in a straight line at 4.0 m/s on a smooth surface, collides with a second metal block B which is at rest. Fig. 2.1 shows the two metal blocks A and B before and after the collision. The mass of A is 3.2 kg. The mass of B is 1.6 kg. After the collision, the velocity of A is 1.5 m/s.

Calculate

(i) the momentum of A before the collision, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Momentum is p = mv. So, 3.2 kg × 4.0 m/s = 12.8 kg m/s (rounded to 13 in mark scheme).

(ii) the velocity v of B after the collision. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

By conservation of momentum, total momentum before = total momentum after. 12.8 = (3.2 × 1.5) + (1.6 × v). This gives 12.8 = 4.8 + 1.6v. So, 1.6v = 8.0, and v = 5.0 m/s.

(c) In the collision that occurred in (b), block A and block B are in contact for 0.050 s. Calculate the average force that is exerted on B during the collision. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The force is the rate of change of momentum of block B. F = Δp ⁄ Δt = (1.6 kg × 5.0 m/s) ⁄ 0.050 s = 8.0 ⁄ 0.050 = 160 N.

(d) After the collision in (b), the total kinetic energy of the two blocks is less than the kinetic energy of block A before the collision. Suggest one reason for this. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Some of the kinetic energy was converted into other forms, such as thermal energy (internal energy) or sound energy during the collision.

Question 7

(0625/41_Summer_2017_Q3) - Forces And Motion

Fig. 3.1 shows remote sensing equipment on the surface of a distant planet.

(a) The mass of the equipment is 350kg. The acceleration of free fall on the surface of this planet is 7.5 m/s².

(i) State what is meant by the term weight. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Weight is the force of gravity acting on an object.

(ii) Calculate the weight of the equipment on the planet. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Weight is calculated as W = mg. So, 350 kg × 7.5 m/s² = 2625 N (rounded to 2600 N in mark scheme).

(b) The equipment releases a balloon from a point that is a small distance above the surface of the planet. The atmosphere at the surface of this planet has a density of 0.35 kg/m³. The inflated balloon has a mass of 80 g and a volume of 0.30 m³. Make an appropriate calculation and then predict and explain the direction of any motion of the balloon. Show your working. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

Calculation: The density of the balloon is ρ = m ⁄ V = 0.080 kg ⁄ 0.30 m³ = 0.27 kg/m³.

Prediction: The balloon will move up.

Explanation: The balloon moves up because its average density (0.27 kg/m³) is less than the density of the surrounding atmosphere (0.35 kg/m³).

Question 8

(0625/42_Summer_2017_Q3) - Forces And Motion, Forces And Energy

(a) Underline the pair of quantities which must be multiplied together to calculate impulse. [1]

force and mass     time and velocity     force and velocity     weight and velocity     mass and time     force and time

Tap to reveal answer

The correct pair is force and time.

(b) Fig. 3.1 shows a collision between two blocks A and B on a smooth, horizontal surface. Before the collision, block A, of mass 2.4kg, is moving at 3.0 m/s. Block B, of mass 1.2kg, is at rest. After the collision, blocks A and B stick together and move with velocity v.

(i) Calculate

1. the momentum of block A before the collision, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Momentum is p = mv. So, 2.4kg × 3.0m/s = 7.2 kg m/s.

2. the velocity v, [2]

Tap to reveal answer

By conservation of momentum, mₐuₐ = (mₐ+mₑ)v. So, 7.2 = (2.4 + 1.2)v. This gives v = 7.2 ⁄ 3.6 = 2.0 m/s.

3. the impulse experienced by block B during the collision. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Impulse is the change in momentum of block B. Impulse = m(v-u) = 1.2 kg × (2.0 - 0) m/s = 2.4 Ns.

(ii) Suggest why the total kinetic energy of blocks A and B after the collision is less than the kinetic energy of block A before the collision. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Energy was converted into other forms, such as thermal energy or sound energy, during the inelastic collision.
