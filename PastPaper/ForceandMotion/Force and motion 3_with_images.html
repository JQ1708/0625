Question 17

(0625/42_Winter_2018_Q1) - Forces And Motion

A lorry is travelling along a straight, horizontal road.

Fig. 1.1 is the distance-time graph for the lorry.

(a) Using Fig. 1.1, determine:

(i) the speed of the lorry at time t = 30 s [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The speed is the gradient of the distance-time graph. In the first 60 seconds, the graph is a straight line. The gradient is calculated as (change in distance) ⁄ (change in time) = 1800 m ⁄ 60 s, which gives a speed of 30 m/s.

(ii) the average speed of the lorry between time t = 60 s and time t = 120s. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Average speed is calculated as (total distance travelled) ⁄ (total time taken). Between 60s and 120s, the distance travelled is 2700 m - 1800 m = 900 m. The time taken is 60s. So, the average speed is 900 m ⁄ 60 s = 15 m/s.

(b) At time t = 30s, the total resistive force acting on the lorry is 1.4 x 104 N.

(i) Using Fig. 1.1, determine the magnitude of the acceleration of the lorry at time t = 30 s. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

At t = 30 s, the lorry is moving at a constant speed (the graph is a straight line). Therefore, the magnitude of the acceleration is 0 m/s².

(ii) Determine the forward force on the lorry due to its engine at time t = 30 s. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Since the acceleration is zero, the resultant force is zero. This means the forward force from the engine is equal and opposite to the total resistive force. The forward force is 1.4 x 104 N.

(c) Describe the motion of the lorry between time t = 60s and time t = 130s. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Between 60s and approximately 110s, the lorry's speed is decreasing (it is decelerating). After that, the distance-time graph becomes horizontal, indicating that the lorry is stationary (speed is zero).

Question 18

(0625/43_Winter_2018_Q1) - Forces And Motion

Fig. 1.1 is the distance-time graph for a moving car.

(a) On Fig. 1.1, mark a point P where the acceleration of the car is zero. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Point P should be marked on the straight-line section of the graph (between t=0 and t=30s), as a constant gradient on a distance-time graph indicates constant speed and therefore zero acceleration.

(b) Determine:

(i) the speed of the car at time t = 15s [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The speed is the gradient of the graph. For the first 30s, the gradient is constant: (150 m) ⁄ (30 s) = 5.0 m/s.

(ii) the average speed of the car between time t = 30s and time t = 45s. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Average speed is calculated as (change in distance) ⁄ (change in time). This is (300 m - 150 m) ⁄ (45 s - 30 s) = 150 m ⁄ 15 s = 10 m/s.

(c) At time t = 45 s, the car starts to decelerate. At time t = 55 s and at a distance of 400 m from the starting point, the car stops. It then remains stationary for 5.0s. On Fig. 1.1, draw a possible continuation of the distance-time graph. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The graph should show a curve with a decreasing gradient from (45s, 300m) to (55s, 400m). Then, a horizontal line should be drawn from (55s, 400m) to (60s, 400m) to show the car is stationary.

(a) Complete Fig. 2.1 by writing in the right-hand column the name of the quantity given by the product in the left-hand column. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

(b) Fig. 2.2 shows a man hitting a ball with a golf club.

The ball has a mass of 0.046kg. The golf club is in contact with the ball for 5.0 x 10-4s and the ball leaves the golf club at a speed of 65 m/s.

(i) Calculate:

1. the momentum of the ball as it leaves the golf club [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Momentum is calculated as p = mv. So, 0.046 kg × 65 m/s gives a momentum of 3.0 kg m/s.

2. the average resultant force acting on the ball while it is in contact with the golf club. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The average force is the change in momentum divided by time, F = Δp ⁄ t. So, 3.0 kg m/s ⁄ (5.0 × 10-4 s) gives an average force of 6000 N.

(ii) While the golf club is in contact with the ball, the ball becomes compressed and changes shape. State the type of energy stored in the ball during its contact with the golf club. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The energy stored due to the compression is elastic potential energy or strain energy.

Question 20

(0625/42_Winter_2018_Q3) - Forces And Motion, Forces And Energy

(a) The velocity of an object of mass m increases from u to v. State, in terms of m, u and v, the change of momentum of the object. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The change in momentum is given by the expression mv - mu.

(b) In a game of tennis, a player hits a stationary ball with his racquet.

(i) The racquet is in contact with the ball for 6.0ms. The average force on the ball during this time is 400N. Calculate the impulse on the tennis ball. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Impulse is calculated as force × time. So, 400 N × 0.0060 s gives an impulse of 2.4 Ns.

(ii) The mass of the ball is 0.056kg. Calculate the speed with which the ball leaves the racquet. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Since impulse equals the change in momentum (Ft = mv - mu) and the ball starts from rest (u=0), the final speed is v = Ft ⁄ m. This is 2.4 Ns ⁄ 0.056 kg, which is 43 m/s.

(iii) State the energy transfer that takes place:

1. as the ball changes shape during the contact between the racquet and the ball

Tap to reveal answer

The energy transfer is from kinetic energy of the racquet to elastic potential energy in the ball and strings.

2. as the ball leaves the racquet.

Tap to reveal answer

The energy transfer is from elastic potential energy back into kinetic energy of the ball.

Question 21

(0625/41_Summer_2019_Q1) - Forces And Motion

A rocket is stationary on the launchpad. At time t = 0, the rocket engines are switched on and exhaust gases are ejected from the nozzles of the engines. The rocket accelerates upwards.

Fig. 1.1 shows how the acceleration of the rocket varies between time t = 0 and time t = tf.

(a) Define acceleration. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, or the change of velocity ⁄ unit time.

(b) On Fig. 1.2, sketch a graph to show how the speed of the rocket varies between time t = 0 and time t = tf. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The graph should start at the origin. Since the acceleration is initially increasing, the speed graph will be a curve with an increasing gradient. When the acceleration becomes constant, the speed graph will become a straight line with a constant positive gradient.

(c) Some time later, the rocket is far from the Earth. The effect of the Earth's gravity on the motion of the rocket is insignificant. As the rocket accelerates, its momentum increases.

(i) State the principle of the conservation of momentum. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

In an isolated system where no external forces act, the total momentum remains constant.

(ii) Explain how the principle of the conservation of momentum applies to the accelerating rocket and the exhaust gases. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The total momentum of the rocket-gas system is initially zero. As the rocket ejects gas, the gas gains momentum in one direction (downwards). To conserve momentum, the rocket gains an equal amount of momentum in the opposite direction (upwards), causing it to accelerate.

A bus is travelling between points A and D. There are bus stops at A, B, C and D but the bus does not stop at B and C. Fig. 1.1 is a speed-time graph for the bus.

(a) Describe the motion of the bus between each of the bus stops. Select the appropriate description from the list below. [3]

constant acceleration, increasing acceleration, decreasing acceleration, moving backwards at constant speed, moving forwards at constant speed, stationary

Tap to reveal answer

1. between A and B: decreasing acceleration

2. between B and C: moving forwards at constant speed

3. between C and D: constant deceleration (negative acceleration)

(b) The average speed of the bus between A and D is 23 km/h. Calculate the distance between A and D. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Distance is calculated as average speed × time. The total time from A to D is 2.0 minutes, which is 2.0 ⁄ 60 hours. So, the distance is 23 km/h × (2.0 ⁄ 60) h = 0.77 km.

(c) The bus stops at D for 1 min and then travels at a constant acceleration for 30 seconds. On Fig. 1.1, sketch a possible graph for this additional motion. Label X when the bus starts to accelerate and label Y for 30 seconds later. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

A horizontal line should be drawn at speed = 0 from t = 2.0 min to t = 3.0 min. Point X is at (3.0, 0). From X, a straight line with a constant positive gradient should be drawn for 30 seconds (to t = 3.5 min). Point Y is at the end of this line.

Question 24

(0625/42_Summer_2019_Q2) - Forces And Motion

Fig. 2.1 shows a model fire engine. Its brakes are applied. 0.80 kg of water is emitted in the jet every 6.0s at a velocity of 0.72 m/s relative to the engine.

(a) Calculate the change in momentum of the water that is ejected in 6.0s. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The change in momentum is calculated as Δp = mΔv. So, 0.80 kg × 0.72 m/s gives a change in momentum of 0.58 kg m/s.

(b) Calculate the magnitude of the force acting on the model because of the jet of water. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Force is the rate of change of momentum, F = Δp ⁄ t. So, 0.58 kg m/s ⁄ 6.0 s gives a force of 0.096 N.

(c) The brakes of the model are released. State and explain the direction of the acceleration of the model. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Statement: The model accelerates to the right (backwards).

Explanation: A force is exerted on the water to eject it to the left. By Newton's third law, the water exerts an equal and opposite force on the model, pushing it to the right. Acceleration is in the same direction as the resultant force.

(d) In (c) the model contains a water tank, which is initially full. State and explain any change in the magnitude of the initial acceleration if the brakes are first released when the tank is nearly empty. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Statement: The acceleration will be more when the tank is empty.

Explanation: The force from the water jet remains constant. When the tank is empty, the total mass of the model is less. According to Newton's second law (F=ma), for a constant force, a smaller mass results in a greater acceleration.
