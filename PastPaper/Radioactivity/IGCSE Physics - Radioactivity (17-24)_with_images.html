Question 18 (0625/43_Winter_2019_Q11) - Atoms And Radioactivity

(a) The circles shown in Fig. 11.1 represent three gold nuclei. Three α-particles are approaching the gold nuclei. On Fig. 11.1, complete the path of each α-particle. [3]

(b) A detector of radioactivity in a laboratory indicates an average of 16 counts/min when no radioactive samples are present. A radioactive sample of half-life 1.5 days is placed close to the detector, which indicates a count rate of 208 counts/min. Calculate the count rate that is indicated 6 days later. [4]

(c) The waste from nuclear power stations includes the isotopes technetium-99, tin-126 and selenium-79. These isotopes are radioactive with half-lives of many thousands of years. State three economic and environmental consequences of producing this waste. [3]

(a) The top α-particle is deflected back to the left. The middle α-particle is deflected on an upward curve. The bottom α-particle is deflected on a downward curve.

(b) Initial count rate from the source = 208 - 16 = 192 counts/min. 6 days is 4 half-lives (6 / 1.5 = 4). After 4 half-lives, the source count rate will be 192 / 16 = 12 counts/min. The final detector reading will be 12 + 16 (background) = 28 counts/min.

(c) 1. The waste must be stored securely with shielding, which is expensive. 2. Transporting the waste is also expensive and carries risk. 3. If there is an accident or leak, it can cause long-term contamination of the environment, making land uninhabitable for thousands of years.

Question 19 (0625/41_Summer_2020_Q10) - Atoms And Radioactivity

Fig. 10.1 represents a neutral atom of an isotope of element X.

(a) State one similarity between this atom and a neutral atom of a different isotope of element X. [1]

(b) The isotope of element X is radioactive. It decays to form an isotope of element Y by emitting a β-particle.

(i) Using Fig. 10.1 deduce the nuclide notation for the isotope of Y produced by this decay. [3]

(ii) β-particles ionise the air they pass through less strongly than the same number of α-particles. Suggest why this is so. [3]

(a) Both isotopes have the same number of protons (and also the same number of electrons).

(b)(i) From the diagram, the atom X has 5 protons and 7 neutrons, so it is 125X. In β-decay, a neutron becomes a proton, so Y will have 6 protons and 6 neutrons. The nuclide notation for Y is 126Y.

(b)(ii) This is because β-particles have a smaller charge (-1e) and smaller mass than α-particles (+2e). They also travel at a higher speed, meaning they spend less time near any given air molecule, reducing the probability of causing ionisation.

Question 20 (0625/42_Summer_2020_Q10) - Atoms And Radioactivity

Fig. 10.1 shows a vacuum tube with a radioactive source. The radioactive source emits α-particles, β-particles and γ-rays. There is a very strong magnetic field between the N pole and the S pole of the magnet.

(a) The lead cylinder has a narrow central hole. State and explain the effect of the lead cylinder. [2]

(b) Describe the paths of the α-particles, β-particles and γ-rays as they pass through the magnetic field. Explain your answers.

(i) α-particles [2]

(ii) β-particles [2]

(iii) γ-rays [2]

(a) The lead cylinder absorbs radiation travelling in other directions, producing a narrow, straight beam (collimating it).

(b)(i) α-particles are deflected out of the page. This is because they are positively charged, and applying Fleming's Left-Hand Rule predicts the force direction.

(b)(iii) γ-rays are undeflected because they are uncharged and therefore do not experience a force in a magnetic field.

Question 21 (0625/43_Summer_2020_Q10) - Atoms And Radioactivity

(a) A radioactive nucleus of carbon decays to a nucleus of nitrogen by emitting a particle. Complete the nuclide equation and state the name of the particle. [3]

146C → 147N + ...X

(b) A radiation detector in a laboratory records a reading of 10 counts/min. There are no radioactive samples in the laboratory.

(i) Explain why the radiation detector records a reading and suggest a possible source. [2]

(ii) Carbon-14 has a half-life of 5700 years. An archaeologist digs up some ancient wood. In the same laboratory as in (b)(i), a sample of this ancient wood gives a reading of 20 counts/min. An equivalent sample of living wood gives a reading of 80 counts/min. It is suggested that the age of the ancient sample is 11400 years. Do a calculation to check whether this suggestion is correct. [4]

(a) The equation is 146C → 147N + 0-1X. The particle X is a beta particle (or electron).

(b)(i) Explanation: The detector records a reading due to background radiation. Source: Cosmic rays or rocks/soil.

(b)(ii) The suggested age of 11400 years is two half-lives. The corrected count rate for living wood is 80 - 10 = 70 counts/min. After two half-lives, the expected count rate would be 70 / 4 = 17.5 counts/min. The expected detector reading would be 17.5 + 10 = 27.5 counts/min. The actual reading is 20 counts/min, which is lower than expected. This means more time has passed, so the age is greater than 11400 years. The suggestion is incorrect.

(i) Describe what is meant by the direction of a magnetic field. State the direction of the magnetic field between the two poles in Fig. 7.1. [1]

(ii) Describe and explain what happens to the beam of electrons in the magnetic field between the poles of the magnet in Fig. 7.1. [3]

Question 23 (0625/41_Winter_2020_Q10) - Atoms And Radioactivity

A radiation detector is placed on the bench in a laboratory. It detects a background count rate of 40 counts/minute.

(a) State what is meant by background radiation. Suggest one source for it. [2]

(b) A sample containing atoms of the radioactive isotope polonium-208 is removed from a lead container and brought close to the detector. The average count rate increases to 890 counts/minute. When two sheets of paper are inserted between the sample and the detector, the average count rate returns to 40 counts/minute. Polonium-208 is represented by the symbol 20884Po. It decays to an isotope of lead (Pb).

(i) Deduce the type of radiation emitted by polonium-208. Explain your answer. [2]

(ii) Write down the nuclide equation for the decay of polonium-208. [3]

(a) Background radiation is the ionising radiation that is always present in our environment. One source is cosmic rays from space.

(b)(i) The radiation emitted is alpha-particles. This is because the radiation was completely stopped by paper, which is a characteristic of alpha radiation.

(b)(ii) The decay is alpha decay. The equation is: 20884Po → 20482Pb + 42α.

Question 24 (0625/42_Winter_2020_Q11) - Atoms And Radioactivity

(a) State two differences between nuclear fission and nuclear fusion. [2]

(b) Radioactive tracers emitting γ-rays can be used in medicine. The half-life of the source of these γ-rays is 6 hours.

(i) Explain why a source of γ-rays used in this way should not have a half-life shorter or longer than about 6 hours. [2]

(ii) Technetium-99 is a source of γ-rays often used as a radioactive tracer. It is produced from molybdenum-99 which emits β-particles. The symbol for technetium is Tc and the symbol for molybdenum is Mo. Complete the nuclide equation for this decay. [3]

...42Mo → ......Tc + ......β

(iii) State another use of radioactive nuclides in medicine. [1]

(a) 1. Fission is the splitting of a large nucleus, while fusion is the joining of small nuclei. 2. Fission produces long-lasting radioactive waste, while fusion produces much less.

(b)(i) A shorter half-life might mean the tracer decays too quickly before the scan is complete. A longer half-life would mean the patient remains radioactive for an unnecessarily long time, increasing their radiation dose.

(b)(ii) The completed equation is: 9942Mo → 9943Tc + 0-1β.

(b)(iii) Another use is in radiotherapy for treating cancer.
