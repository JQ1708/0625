Question 33 (0625/41_Summer_2022_Q10) - Atoms And Radioactivity

Two of the isotopes of hydrogen are hydrogen-2 (2H) and hydrogen-3 (3H).

(a) (i) State one similarity in the composition of their nuclei. [1]

(ii) Describe how a nucleus of hydrogen-3 differs from a nucleus of hydrogen-2. [1]

(b) In a nuclear fusion reactor, a nucleus of hydrogen-2 fuses with a nucleus of hydrogen-3 at an extremely high temperature. This fusion reaction produces an isotope of element X and releases a neutron.

(i) Explain why an extremely high temperature is needed when forcing the nuclei together. [3]

(ii) Using nuclide notation, complete the equation for this reaction. [2]

21H + 31H → ...X + ...n

(a)(i) Both nuclei have the same number of protons (one proton).

(a)(ii) A nucleus of hydrogen-3 has one more neutron than a nucleus of hydrogen-2.

(b)(i) An extremely high temperature is needed to give the nuclei very high kinetic energy. This is required to overcome the strong electrostatic repulsion between the positively charged nuclei so they can get close enough to fuse.

(b)(ii) The completed equation is: 21H + 31H → 42He + 10n

Question 36 (0625/41_Summer_2023_Q9) - Atoms And Radioactivity

Fig. 9.1 represents all the particles in a neutral atom of a radioactive isotope X1.

(a) Determine the number of neutrons in this atom and explain how the answer is obtained. [2]

(b) The isotope X1 is a beta emitter that decays to the stable isotope X2.

(i) Describe how a nucleus of X2 differs from a nucleus of X1. [2]

(ii) Suggest why isotope X2 is stable whereas X1 is not stable. [1]

(c) The half-life of X1 is approximately 20 ms.

(i) Define the term half-life. [2]

(ii) Suggest one reason why isotopes with very short half-lives are especially hazardous. [1]

(a) Number of neutrons = 7. Explanation: The nucleus contains 12 particles (nucleons) in total. 5 of these are protons (e.g., white circles), so the number of neutrons is the total number of nucleons minus the number of protons (12 - 5 = 7).

(b)(i) In beta decay, a neutron changes into a proton. Therefore, a nucleus of X2 has one more proton and one fewer neutron than a nucleus of X1.

(b)(ii) Isotope X2 is stable because it has a more stable ratio of neutrons to protons than X1, which has an excess of neutrons.

(c)(i) Half-life is the average time taken for the number of radioactive nuclei in a sample (or the rate of decay) to halve.

(c)(ii) They are hazardous because they have a very high initial activity (rate of decay), releasing a large amount of radiation in a short time.

Question 39 (0625/41_Winter_2023_Q9) - Atoms And Radioactivity

Many household smoke alarms contain a sample of the radioactive isotope americium-241 (Am).

(a) Americium-241 is the isotope of the element americium that has the nucleon number (mass number) 241.

(i) State how the composition of a nucleus of americium-241 differs from that of a nucleus of americium-242. [1]

(ii) An atom of a different element has a nucleon number of 241. State two differences between the composition of a nucleus of this atom and a nucleus of americium-241. [2]

(b) Americium-241 decays to an isotope of neptunium (Np) by alpha-particle (α-particle) emission.

(i) Complete the equation for this decay. [3]

241...Am → ...93Np + ......α

(ii) Explain why α-particles are more strongly ionising than β-particles. [2]

(iii) The isotope of neptunium produced by americium-241 is also radioactive. The half-life of neptunium is longer than two million years. Using this information, explain the advantage of this long half-life for the use and safe disposal of a household smoke alarm. [2]

(a)(i) A nucleus of americium-241 has one fewer neutron than a nucleus of americium-242.

(a)(ii) It would have a different number of protons and a different number of neutrons.

(b)(i) The completed equation is: 24195Am → 23793Np + 42α.

(b)(ii) α-particles are more strongly ionising because they have a larger charge (+2e compared to -1e for β-particles) and they have a larger mass.

(b)(iii) The very long half-life means the neptunium decays extremely slowly, so its activity is very low. This makes the smoke alarm safe during its operational lifetime and means it does not pose a significant radioactive hazard upon disposal, making it cheap/easy.

Question 40 (0625/42_Winter_2023_Q8) - Atoms And Radioactivity

The isotope uranium-235 is represented by 23592U.

(a) State what the numbers 92 and 235 represent in this symbol. [2]

(b) Uranium-235 is a fuel used in nuclear reactors.

(i) State the process by which energy is released from uranium-235 in a nuclear reactor. [1]

(ii) A nuclide equation for this process is 23592U + 10n → 14054Xe + 9438Sr + 2 10n. Describe the mass and energy changes that take place during this process in a nuclear reactor. [2]

(c) (i) Describe how thermal energy from nuclear reactions is used to generate electricity in a power station. [3]

(ii) State one advantage and one disadvantage of using nuclear fuels in a power station instead of using fossil fuels. [2]

(a) 92 is the proton number (or atomic number). 235 is the nucleon number (or mass number).

(b)(i) The process is nuclear fission.

(b)(ii) The total mass of the products is slightly less than the mass of the initial nucleus and neutron. This missing mass is converted into a large amount of energy (thermal and kinetic).

(c)(i) The thermal energy is used to boil water to create high-pressure steam. This steam then turns a turbine, which is connected to a generator that produces electricity.

(c)(ii) Advantage: It produces no greenhouse gases. Disadvantage: It produces dangerous radioactive waste that is difficult to store safely.

Question 41 (0625/43_Winter_2023_Q9) - Atoms And Radioactivity

(a) For each application of radioactive isotopes, state and explain which type of radioactive emission is suitable and suggest an appropriate half-life for the isotope.

(i) household smoke alarm [3]

(ii) measuring the thickness of aluminium strips produced in a factory [3]

(b) Lead-208 (208Pb) has the highest nucleon number of the stable isotopes of lead. Explain why lead-214 (214Pb) is radioactive. [2]

(c) State two different sources of background radiation. [2]

(a)(i) Household smoke alarm: The type of emission is alpha. This is because it is highly ionising and not very penetrating. The half-life should be long (e.g., hundreds of years) so the alarm functions for many years.

(a)(ii) Measuring thickness: The type of emission is beta. This is because its penetration is sensitive to changes in the thickness of aluminium. The half-life should be long so the equipment gives consistent readings over many years.

(b) Lead-214 is radioactive because its nucleus contains too many neutrons for the number of protons, making it unstable. It will decay to reduce the number of neutrons and reach a more stable configuration.

(c) Two sources are rocks and buildings and radon gas (in the air).
