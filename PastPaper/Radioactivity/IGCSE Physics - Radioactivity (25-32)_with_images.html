Question 25 (0625/43_Winter_2020_Q11) - Atoms And Radioactivity

(a) Fig. 11.1 shows a beam of α-particles, β-particles and γ-rays directed between two metal plates P and Q. The metal plates are parallel and there is a large potential difference (p.d.) between them. Plate P is positive and plate Q is negative.

On Fig. 11.1, draw the paths of each of the radiations between the plates and after leaving the plates. Label the paths α, β and γ. [5]

(b) State and explain one practical application of γ-rays. [2]

(a) The α-particles, being positively charged, are deflected in a smooth curve towards the negative plate Q. The β-particles, being negatively charged, are deflected towards the positive plate P and are deflected more than α-particles due to their much smaller mass. The γ-rays are uncharged and pass straight through without deviation. After leaving the plates, all paths continue in a straight line.

(b) Application: Sterilisation of medical equipment. Explanation: γ-rays are highly penetrating and can kill bacteria and other microbes on the equipment, even through its packaging.

Question 26 (0625/42_Summer_2021_Q8) - Atoms And Radioactivity, Magnets And Currents

(a) Two identical radioactive sources emit α-particles and γ-rays into two vacuum tubes.

(i) Fig. 8.1 shows two electrically charged plates on either side of one of the vacuum tubes. Write the symbol α once in Table 8.1 to indicate any deflection of the α-particles. Write the symbol γ once in Table 8.1 to indicate any deflection of the γ-rays. [2]

(ii) Fig. 8.2 shows the poles of a very strong magnet on either side of the other vacuum tube. Write the symbol α once in Table 8.2 to indicate any deflection of the α-particles. Write the symbol γ once in Table 8.2 to indicate any deflection of the γ-rays. [2]

(b) Fig. 8.3 shows a simple direct current (d.c.) electric motor with a split-ring commutator.

(i) State and explain the direction of rotation of the coil as seen from point X. [3]

(ii) The coil rotates through 90° from the position shown. State what happens to the moment in this position. [1]

(iii) The coil is rotated through 180° from the position shown. By considering the forces on the coil, explain how the split-ring commutator enables the motor to turn continuously. [2]

(a)(i) In Table 8.1: α-particles are positive and are attracted to the negative plate (-2500V), so they deflect towards the bottom of the page. γ-rays are uncharged and have no deflection.

(a)(ii) In Table 8.2: Using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule for the positive α-particles (current), the force is directed into the page. γ-rays are uncharged and have no deflection.

(b)(i) Direction: Clockwise. Explanation: Using Fleming's Left-Hand rule, the force on the left-hand wire is up and the force on the right-hand wire is down, creating a turning effect.

(b)(ii) In the vertical position (90° rotation), the moment becomes zero.

(b)(iii) The split-ring commutator reverses the direction of the current in the coil every half turn. This ensures that the force on the wire that is now on the left is always up, and the force on the wire that is now on the right is always down, allowing the coil to rotate continuously in the same direction.

Question 28 (0625/43_Summer_2021_Q10) - Atoms And Radioactivity

(a) Fig. 10.1 shows a beam of radiation in a vacuum. The beam contains α-particles, β-particles and γ-rays. The beam enters a region where there is a strong, uniform magnetic field. The direction of the magnetic field is out of the page.

On Fig. 10.1, mark and label the paths through the magnetic field of:

(i) α-particles (label this path α) [1]

(ii) β-particles (label this path β) [2]

(iii) γ-rays (label this path γ). [1]

(b) Radioactive sources have many uses in medicine. State two safety precautions which hospital staff take when working with γ-ray sources. [2]

(c) The radioactive isotope iodine-131 is used as a tracer in medical diagnosis. A nucleus of iodine-131 contains 53 protons and 78 neutrons. The symbol for iodine is I.

(i) Use nuclide notation to show this isotope of iodine. [1]

(ii) Iodine-131 emits γ-radiation. It has a half-life of 8 hours. Explain why this emission and this half-life make iodine-131 a suitable material for use as a tracer in medical diagnosis. [2]

(a) The α-particles (positive) deflect in a downward curve. The β-particles (negative) deflect in the opposite direction (upward curve) and with a greater curvature due to their smaller mass. The γ-rays (neutral) pass straight through undeflected.

(b) Two precautions are: 1. Store the source in lead-lined boxes when not in use. 2. Limit the time of exposure and maintain a safe distance (or stand behind shielding).

(c)(i) The nuclide notation is: 13153I

(c)(ii) It is suitable because γ-rays can penetrate body tissues and be detected outside the body. The half-life is long enough for the diagnostic procedure but short enough that the patient's radioactivity decays quickly to a safe level, and it is weakly ionising.

Question 29 (0625/42_Summer_2021_Q11) - Atoms And Radioactivity

(a) A student investigates a radioactive substance in a laboratory. Fig. 11.1 is a graph showing the count rate detected as the substance decays for 7.5 minutes.

The background radiation is 20 counts/min.

(i) Determine the half-life of the substance. [3]

(ii) Calculate the count rate detected at time = 9.6 minutes. [2]

(b) The substance emits α-particles and γ-rays. The student suggests that it is safe to store the substance in a plastic container of thickness 2 mm. State and explain whether the student's suggestion is correct. [3]

(a)(i) The initial total count rate is 220 counts/min. Subtracting the background (20 counts/min) gives an initial source count rate of 200 counts/min. After one half-life, the source count rate will be 100 counts/min. The total detected rate would be 100 + 20 = 120 counts/min. Reading from the graph, the time taken to reach 120 counts/min is 2.4 min.

(a)(ii) The time elapsed is 9.6 minutes. The number of half-lives is 9.6 / 2.4 = 4. The source's count rate after 4 half-lives will be 200 / 24 = 200 / 16 = 12.5 counts/min. The total detected count rate will be the source rate plus the background rate: 12.5 + 20 = 32.5 counts/min (or 32/33).

(b) Statement: The suggestion is incorrect. Explanation: While the 2 mm plastic container will absorb the α-particles, it will not stop the highly penetrating γ-rays, which will pass through the container and still be dangerous.

Question 32 (0625/43_Winter_2021_Q11) - Atoms And Radioactivity

(a) A detector of radioactivity is placed in a laboratory where there are no radioactive samples. A student notices that the detector shows a count rate that varies between 20 counts/min and 24 counts/min.

(i) Suggest a source of these readings. [1]

(ii) Explain why these readings are not constant. [1]

(b) A nucleus of uranium (U) contains 92 protons and 146 neutrons. It decays by emitting an α-particle to become a nucleus of thorium (Th). Complete the nuclide equation for this radioactive decay. [3]

...U → ...Th + ...α

(c) An isotope of radon has a half-life of 3.8 days. It decays by emitting α-radiation. Calculate the time taken for 16 mg of this isotope to decay to 2 mg of this isotope. [2]

(a)(i) The source is background radiation (e.g., from cosmic rays or building materials).

(a)(ii) The readings are not constant because radioactive decay is a random process.

(b) The uranium isotope is 23892U. The completed equation is: 23892U → 23490Th + 42α

(c) The mass of the isotope reduces from 16 mg to 2 mg. This reduction sequence is 16 → 8 → 4 → 2, which represents 3 half-lives. The total time taken is 3 × 3.8 days = 11.4 days.
