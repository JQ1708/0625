Question 33 (0625/41_Summer_2023_Q8) - Magnets And Currents

The electricity supplied to a town is transmitted using a high-voltage cable. A transformer in the town has a soft-iron core.

(a) Explain the principle of operation of a simple iron-cored transformer. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

An alternating current in the primary coil generates a changing magnetic field in the iron core. The soft iron core concentrates and transfers this magnetic field to the secondary coil. As the secondary coil cuts this changing magnetic field, an e.m.f. is induced across it.

(b) The transformer steps the supply voltage down from 220000 V to 33000 V.

(i) There are 450 turns on the secondary coil. Calculate the number of turns on the primary coil. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Np ⁄ Ns = Vp ⁄ Vs

Np = Ns × (Vp ⁄ Vs) = 450 × (220000 ⁄ 33000)

Number of turns = 3000.

(ii) The electrical power transferred to the transformer by the high-voltage cable is 77MW. Calculate the current in the primary coil. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

P = I × V

I = P ⁄ V = 77,000,000 W ⁄ 220,000 V

Current = 350 A.

Question 34 (0625/42_Summer_2023_Q8) - Electricity, Magnets And Currents

(a) (i) State what is meant by a magnetic field. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

A magnetic field is a region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force.

(ii) Define the direction of a magnetic field. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The direction of a magnetic field is defined as the direction of the force on a North pole.

(b) Fig. 8.1 shows a negatively charged metal sphere. On Fig. 8.1, draw four lines to show the electric field and its direction. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The drawing should show four radial lines pointing towards the sphere, equally spaced around it.

(c) Fig. 8.2 shows a circuit.

The three cells are identical and have zero resistance. The resistors R₁, R₂, and R₃ are identical. The reading on the voltmeter is 6.0V. When the diode is conducting, it has zero resistance and zero potential difference (p.d.) across it.

(i) Determine the e.m.f. of one cell. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The voltmeter reads the total e.m.f. of the three cells. The e.m.f. of one cell is 6.0 V ⁄ 3 = 2.0 V.

(ii) Determine the ratio of the p.d. across R₂ to the p.d. across R₃. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The ratio of the potential difference is 1:2.

(iii) All the cells are reversed.

1. State the change in current in R₁. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The diode is now in the wrong direction and will not allow current to pass. Therefore, the current in R₁ becomes zero.

2. Determine the new value of the ratio of the p.d. across R₂ to the p.d. across R₃. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

With no current in the R₁ branch, R₂ and R₃ are in series. Since they are identical, they will have the same potential difference across them. The new ratio is 1:1.

Question 35 (0625/43_Summer_2023_Q7) - Magnets And Currents

The voltage across the primary coil of a 100% efficient transformer is 220 V and the voltage across the secondary coil is 12V.

(a) The current in the secondary coil is 2.5A. Calculate the current in the primary coil. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Vp × Ip = Vs × Is

Ip = (Vs × Is) ⁄ Vp = (12 V × 2.5 A) ⁄ 220 V

Current = 0.14 A.

(b) Calculate the ratio of the number of turns on the primary coil to the number of turns on the secondary coil of the transformer. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Np ⁄ Ns = Vp ⁄ Vs = 220 V ⁄ 12 V

Turns ratio ≈ 18.33, which is 18 (to 2 s.f.).

Question 36 (0625/43_Winter_2023_Q8) - Magnets And Currents

(a) Fig. 8.1 shows the single turn coil of a simple direct current (d.c.) motor.

(i) Explain the direction of the turning effect as seen by an observer at O. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The turning effect is clockwise. Using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, the force on the left side of the coil is upwards, while the force on the right side is downwards, creating a clockwise moment.

(ii) The coil is replaced by an otherwise identical new coil with three turns and the same current in the coil. State how the turning effect compares with the turning effect in (i). [1]

Tap to reveal answer

With more turns, the turning effect is greater.

(iii) A third coil is identical to the coil in (i) except that its resistance is three times greater. The potential difference (p.d.) across the coil is the same as the p.d. in (i). State how the turning effect compares with the turning effect in (i). [1]

Tap to reveal answer

With three times the resistance, the current (I=V⁄R) will be one-third of the original. Since the force is proportional to the current, the turning effect is less.

(b) Fig. 8.2 is a voltage-time graph showing the output of a simple alternating current (a.c.) generator at times t₀, t₁, t₂, and t₃.

Fig. 8.3 is an end view of the plane of the coil of the generator at time t₀. The coil is rotating clockwise.

(i) Draw an end view of the position of the plane of the coil at time t₁. Include the labels A and B. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

At t₁, the coil has rotated 90° clockwise and is horizontal. The drawing should show a horizontal line, with B on the left and A on the right.

(ii) Draw an end view of the position of the plane of the coil at time t₂. Include the labels A and B. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

At t₂, the coil has rotated 180° and is vertical again. The drawing should show a vertical line, with A at the top and B at the bottom.

(iii) Draw an end view of the position of the plane of the coil at time t₃. Include the labels A and B. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

At t₃, the coil has rotated 270° and is horizontal again. The drawing should show a horizontal line, with A on the left and B on the right.
