Question 9 (0625/41_Summer_2018_Q10) - Magnets And Currents

(a) Explain why the voltage of the supply to the primary coil of a transformer must be alternating. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

An alternating voltage is required to produce an alternating current, which in turn creates a changing magnetic field. This changing field is necessary to induce a voltage in the secondary coil.

(b) Fig. 10.1 shows a transformer.

There are 8000 turns in the primary coil of the transformer. The primary coil is connected to a 240 V mains supply. A 6.0V lamp connected to the secondary coil operates at full brightness.

(i) Calculate the number of turns in the secondary coil. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Ns ⁄ Np = Vs ⁄ Vp

Ns = Np × (Vs ⁄ Vp) = 8000 × (6.0 ⁄ 240)

Number of turns = 200.

(ii) The current in the lamp is 2.0A. The transformer operates with 100% efficiency. Calculate the current in the primary circuit. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

IpVp = IsVs

Ip = (IsVs) ⁄ Vp = (2.0A × 6.0V) ⁄ 240V

Current = 0.050 A.

(iii) The primary circuit contains a 2A fuse. Calculate the maximum number of lamps, identical to the lamp in (ii), that can be connected in parallel in the secondary circuit without blowing the fuse. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The maximum primary current is 2A. Each lamp draws 0.050A from the primary.

Number of lamps = Total current ⁄ Current per lamp = 2A ⁄ 0.050A

Number of lamps = 40.

Question 10 (0625/43_Summer_2018_Q10) - Magnets And Currents

(a) A bar magnet is held with its N-pole just inside one end of a coil. Fig. 10.1 shows the coil connected to a galvanometer that has the zero mark in the centre of the scale.

The magnet is pulled horizontally to the right at a constant speed through a large distance.

(i) State what happens to the galvanometer reading as time passes. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The galvanometer shows a reading as the magnet is pulled out, and then the reading returns to zero once the magnet is far away.

(ii) As the magnet moves, an N-pole is produced at the left-hand end L of the coil. Explain why the pole at L is an N-pole. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

According to Lenz's law, the induced magnetic field opposes the change that causes it. As the S-pole of the magnet moves away from end L, an N-pole is induced to attract the S-pole and oppose its removal.

(b) A transformer has NP turns on the primary coil and NS turns on the secondary coil. The transformer is used in a school laboratory as a 12V alternating current (a.c.) supply. The transformer is powered from the 240 V a.c. mains supply.

(i) Determine the turns ratio NP⁄NS of the transformer. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

NP ⁄ NS = VP ⁄ VS = 240V ⁄ 12V

Turns ratio = 20 or 20:1.

(ii) The laboratory 12 V a.c. supply is rectified to produce a direct current (d.c.) supply. Underline the component that the rectification circuit must include. [1]

AND gate     diode     NOT gate     potentiometer     thermistor

Tap to reveal answer

A rectification circuit must include a diode.

Question 11 (0625/42_Winter_2018_Q9) - Magnets And Currents

Fig. 9.1 shows a permanent bar magnet next to a circuit that contains a coil and a galvanometer.

(a) Suggest a metal from which the magnet is made. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The magnet is made from a hard magnetic material like steel.

(b) The magnet is moved to the left and inserted a small distance into the coil. The galvanometer deflects briefly and shows that there is a current in the coil.

(i) Explain why there is a current in the coil. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

As the magnet moves, its magnetic field lines cut the coil. This induces an e.m.f. (voltage) which drives a current through the complete circuit.

(ii) As the magnet is moving near to the coil, end Q of the coil behaves as a magnetic pole. State the polarity of end Q and explain why it has this polarity. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

End Q becomes an N-pole. This is because the induced magnetic field repels or opposes the approaching N-pole of the magnet, according to Lenz's law.

(c) Suggest two ways in which the deflection of the galvanometer can be reversed. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
  1. Withdraw the N-pole from end Q (move the magnet to the right).
  2. Insert the S-pole into end Q.
Question 12 (0625/43_Winter_2018_Q9) - Electricity, Magnets And Currents

(a) Describe how a direct current (d.c.) differs from an alternating current (a.c.). [1]

Tap to reveal answer

A direct current (d.c.) has a constant value and flows in only one direction, whereas an alternating current (a.c.) continuously changes direction.

(b) Fig. 9.1 shows how the voltage output of an a.c. generator varies with time.

A heater is connected directly to the a.c. generator and the maximum current in the heater is 0.75A.

(i) On Fig. 9.2, sketch a graph to indicate how the current in the heater varies with time. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The graph should be a sinusoidal curve that is in phase with the voltage graph, having the same frequency and reaching a maximum value of 0.75A.

(ii) Calculate the power produced by the heater when the current is 0.75A. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

From Fig 9.1, when the current is max (0.75A), the voltage is max (7.2V).

P = V × I = 7.2V × 0.75A

Power = 5.4 W.

(c) Fig. 9.3 shows the coil ABCD of the a.c. generator between two magnetic poles.

(i) On Fig. 9.3, draw a straight arrow to indicate the direction in which side AB of the coil is moving. Label this arrow M. [1]

(ii) Deduce the direction of the current induced in side AB of the coil and explain your reasoning. [2]

(iii) The rate at which the coil of the a.c. generator rotates increases. State two ways in which the alternating voltage changes. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

(i) Arrow M on side AB should be pointing vertically upwards.

(ii) The current is induced from A to B. This is determined using Fleming's Right-Hand Rule, where the thumb (Motion) points up, the first finger (Field) points from N to S, and the second finger (Current) points from A to B.

(iii) 1. The maximum voltage (amplitude) becomes greater. 2. The frequency becomes greater (or the time period becomes smaller).

Question 13 (0625/41_Winter_2018_Q10) - Magnets And Currents

A transformer consists of two coils of wire wound on a metal core. Fig. 10.1 represents the transformer.

(a) State the name of the metal from which the core is made. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The core is made of soft iron.

(b) The primary coil of the transformer is connected to the output voltage of an a.c. generator which supplies an alternating current.

(i) Explain why there is a voltage between the two terminals of the secondary coil. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The alternating magnetic field from the primary coil is linked to the secondary coil via the core. This changing magnetic field cuts the secondary coil, inducing an e.m.f. (voltage).

(ii) There are 560 turns on the primary coil and 910 turns on the secondary coil of the transformer. The voltage between the two terminals of the secondary coil is 78 V. Calculate the voltage of the a.c. generator. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Vp ⁄ Vs = Np ⁄ Ns

Vp = Vs × (Np ⁄ Ns) = 78V × (560 ⁄ 910)

Generator voltage = 48 V.

(c) Transformers are used to increase the voltage when electrical energy is transmitted in cables across long distances. Explain why power losses in the cables are lower when the voltage is high. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

For the same power, a higher voltage means a lower current. Since power loss in cables is given by I2R, a lower current means less power loss as heat.

Question 14 (0625/41_Summer_2019_Q8) - Magnets And Currents

A student turns the handle of an alternating current (a.c.) generator and the coil rotates. Fig. 8.1 represents the structure of the a.c. generator.

(a) There is an alternating voltage output between the two terminals.

(i) Explain why rotating the coil produces an output voltage. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

As the coil rotates in the magnetic field, the wires of the coil cut the magnetic field lines. This induces an e.m.f. (voltage) across the ends of the coil by electromagnetic induction.

(ii) State the position of the rotating coil when the alternating output voltage is at a maximum value and explain why the maximum output occurs at this position. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The output voltage is maximum when the plane of the coil is horizontal. In this position, the sides of the coil are moving vertically and cutting the magnetic field at the fastest rate.

(b) A lamp and an open switch are connected in series to the output terminals of the a.c. generator. The switch is closed and the lamp lights up. The student has to apply a greater force on the handle. Explain why a greater force is needed to keep the lamp lit. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

When the lamp is lit, there is a current in the coil. This current flowing in the magnetic field experiences a force that opposes the rotation (Lenz's Law). Therefore, the student must apply a greater force to do more work and supply the energy for the lamp.

Question 15 (0625/42_Summer_2019_Q9) - Magnets And Currents

(a) Describe how to demagnetise a bar magnet using alternating current (a.c.) in a coil. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

First, place the magnet inside the coil. Then, switch on the a.c. power supply. Finally, with the power still on, gradually withdraw the magnet from the coil to a large distance.

(b) Fig. 9.1 shows a simple direct current (d.c.) motor.

(i) Explain the purpose of the split-ring commutator. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The purpose is to keep the coil rotating in the same direction by changing the direction of the current in the coil every half cycle (180 degrees).

(ii) The voltage of the power supply is increased. State the effect this has on the motor. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The coil will rotate faster.

Question 16 (0625/43_Summer_2019_Q10) - Magnets And Currents

Fig. 10.1 shows a simple alternating current generator.

(a) On Fig. 10.2, sketch a graph to show how the electromotive force (e.m.f.) induced varies with time for one revolution of the coil. Assume that the coil starts in the horizontal position, as shown in Fig. 10.1. Label the points on the time axis where the coil has completed 1/4 revolution and 3/4 revolution. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The graph should show a correct sine/cosine shape starting from a maximum voltage. It should pass through zero at the points labelled 1/4 revolution and 3/4 revolution.

(b) Explain why an e.m.f. is induced only when the coil is turning. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

An e.m.f. is induced only when the coil is cutting magnetic field lines, which only happens when it is turning.

(c) State the name of the components labelled P and Q and state their purpose. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Name: Slip rings.

Purpose: To provide a continuous connection to the external circuit while the coil is rotating.

(d) State two possible changes that cause a larger e.m.f. to be induced. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
  1. Increase the strength of the magnetic field.
  2. Increase the speed of rotation of the coil.
