Question 17 (0625/42_Winter_2019_Q7) - Electricity, Magnets And Currents

(a) Fig. 7.1 shows a coil of wire wound on a thin plastic cylinder. The plastic has no effect on any magnetic field. The galvanometer is extremely sensitive.

A magnet is fixed to a small trolley that runs without friction on a track through the cylinder and coil.

(i) The trolley is released from point A so it runs through the coil from right to left. State and explain what is observed on the galvanometer. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The galvanometer shows a deflection and then a reverse deflection. This is because a voltage is induced as the magnet enters the coil, and then an opposite voltage is induced as it leaves the coil, due to the change of magnetic flux.

(ii) The trolley is now released from point B so it runs through the coil from right to left again. State what is observed on the galvanometer and explain why it is different to your answer in (a)(i). [2]

Tap to reveal answer

A larger deflection is observed because the trolley moves at a higher speed. This causes a larger rate of change of magnetic flux, inducing a larger voltage.

(b) Fig. 7.2 shows an extension lead used to supply power to a 3kW electric heater on a cool evening.

State and explain three dangers with this arrangement. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

Danger 1: The extension lead is designed for a 25W lamp but is used for a 3kW heater. This will draw too much current, causing the lead to overheat and potentially cause a fire.

Danger 2: There is a cut in the outer insulation, which could expose live wires and lead to an electric shock.

Danger 3: The plug and socket are lying on damp grass. Water can conduct electricity, creating a risk of a short circuit or electrocution.

Question 18 (0625/43_Winter_2019_Q10) - Magnets And Currents

(a) A magnet and a coil are attached separately to a door and a door frame as shown in Fig. 10.1.

The purpose of the arrangement is to activate a circuit connected to an LED indicator when the door is opening or closing. This will provide a visual indication that the door is being used. Initially, the door is closed and then it is opened.

(i) Explain why the indicator comes on and then goes off when the door is opened. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

As the door opens, the movement of the magnet relative to the coil induces a current, causing the indicator to light up. The light goes off when the magnet stops moving relative to the coil.

(ii) The door shuts. The indicator comes on more brightly but for a shorter time than it did in (i). Suggest and explain why this happens. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The door closes more quickly than it was opened, resulting in a larger rate of change of magnetic flux. This induces a higher current, making the indicator brighter. The movement occurs over a shorter length of time.

(b) A circuit breaker is recommended for use with an electric lawnmower. State two reasons for this recommendation. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Reason 1: It provides quick response to a surge in current, protecting against electric shock if the cable is cut.

Reason 2: It is easily re-settable, unlike a fuse which needs to be replaced.

Question 19 (0625/41_Summer_2020_Q7) - Magnets And Currents

An electromagnet consists of a solenoid X that is made of copper wire. The solenoid contains an iron core.

(a) Explain why:

(i) the structure of copper makes it a suitable material for the wire [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Copper contains free electrons that are able to move, making it a good electrical conductor.

(ii) iron is a suitable material for the core of an electromagnet. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Iron is a magnetic material that is easily magnetised and demagnetised, making it a good temporary magnet.

(b) Fig. 7.1 shows the electromagnet inside a second solenoid Y.

(i) Describe and explain what happens in solenoid Y when solenoid X is connected to an alternating current (a.c.) power supply. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The a.c. in solenoid X produces an alternating magnetic field. This changing field induces an alternating electromotive force (e.m.f.) across the terminals of solenoid Y through electromagnetic induction.

(ii) A switch and a lamp are connected in series with the terminals of solenoid Y. When the switch is closed, the lamp lights up at normal brightness. Describe and explain what happens to the current in solenoid X when the switch is closed. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The current in solenoid X increases. This is because it must now supply the power that is being used by the lamp in solenoid Y.

(a) A permanent magnet is made from only one material. Underline the material from which it is possible to make a permanent magnet. [1]

aluminium    copper    soft iron    mercury    plastic    steel    uranium

Tap to reveal answer

A permanent magnet is made from steel.

(b) An electron source produces a narrow beam of electrons that all travel at the same speed. The electron source is placed in a vacuum and the beam of electrons travels vertically downwards. Fig. 7.1 shows the beam of electrons before it passes between the N-pole and the S-pole of a magnet.

(i) Describe what is meant by the direction of a magnetic field. State the direction of the magnetic field between the two poles in Fig. 7.1. [1]

(ii) Describe and explain what happens to the beam of electrons in the magnetic field between the poles of the magnet in Fig. 7.1. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

(i) The direction of a magnetic field is the direction of the force on a N-pole. The direction is from left to right (N to S).

(ii) The beam deflects into the page. Moving electrons constitute a current, and a current in a magnetic field experiences a force, as described by Fleming's Left-Hand Rule.

(c) A beam consists of α-particles, β-particles and γ-rays. Explain how a uniform magnetic field may be used to separate the α-particles, the β-particles and the γ-rays. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

When the beam passes through the magnetic field, the α-particles and β-particles deflect in opposite directions because they have opposite charges. The α-particles deflect less due to their greater mass. The γ-rays are uncharged and therefore do not deflect.

Question 24 (0625/43_Winter_2020_Q9) - Magnets And Currents

(a) Electrical power is produced in a power station by an alternating current (a.c.) generator. The output of the generator has a voltage of 22000 V. The electrical power is transmitted at a voltage of 400 000 V. Explain why electrical power is transmitted at a voltage of 400 000 V and not 22 000 V. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Transmitting at a high voltage results in a low current for the same power. Since heat loss depends on the current squared (P=I2R), this leads to less power loss in the transmission lines.

(b) A computer contains a transformer. The input voltage to the transformer is 240V. The output voltage from the transformer is 20V and the output current is 2.3A. The efficiency of the transformer is 90%. Calculate the input current to the transformer. [5]

Tap to reveal answer

Power output = Vs × Is = 20V × 2.3A = 46W

Efficiency = (Power output ⁄ Power input) × 100%

Power input = Power output ⁄ Efficiency = 46W ⁄ 0.90 = 51.1W

Input current = Power input ⁄ Vp = 51.1W ⁄ 240V

Input current = 0.21 A.
