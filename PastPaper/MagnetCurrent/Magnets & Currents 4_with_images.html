(a) Fig. 7.1 shows two magnets and the gap between the N pole of one magnet and the S pole of the other magnet.

On Fig. 7.1, draw three lines to show the pattern and direction of the magnetic field in the gap. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The magnetic field lines should be drawn from the N pole to the S pole. The line in the middle should be straight, with the other two lines showing a slight curve outwards. Arrows on all lines must point from N to S.

(b) (i) Fig. 7.2 is a repeat of Fig. 7.1 showing the two magnets. On Fig. 7.2, draw the position of a plotting compass needle when it comes to rest in the gap between the N pole and the S pole. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The plotting compass needle will align with the magnetic field. The north end of the compass needle will point towards the S pole of the magnet.

(ii) Explain why the needle comes to rest in this position. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The compass needle itself is a small magnet, and it aligns with the direction of the magnetic field it is in. The North pole of the needle is attracted to the South pole of the main magnet because unlike poles attract.

(c) Describe a method of demagnetising a bar magnet. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Place the magnet inside a solenoid connected to an alternating current (a.c.) supply. With the power on, slowly withdraw the magnet from the coil to a large distance before switching off the current.

(ii) Fig. 8.2 shows the poles of a very strong magnet on either side of the other vacuum tube.

(b) Fig. 8.3 shows a simple direct current (d.c.) electric motor with a split-ring commutator.

(i) State and explain the direction of rotation of the coil as seen from point X. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Statement: The coil will rotate clockwise.

Explanation: Using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, the force on the left-hand wire of the coil is upwards, and the force on the right-hand wire is downwards. This pair of forces creates a clockwise turning moment.

(ii) The coil rotates through 90° from the position shown. State what happens to the moment in this position. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

When the coil is vertical, the moment becomes zero.

(iii) The coil is rotated through 180° from the position shown. By considering the forces on the coil, explain how the split-ring commutator enables the motor to turn continuously. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

After 180° rotation, the split-ring commutator reverses the direction of the current in the coil. This ensures that the wire that is now on the left still experiences an upward force and the wire on the right experiences a downward force, allowing the coil to continue rotating in the same direction.

Question 27 (0625/43_Summer_2021_Q9) - Magnets And Currents

(a) An X-ray machine requires a supply of 110kV. The mains electricity supply is 230V. A transformer is used to supply the correct voltage to the X-ray machine. There are 50 turns on the primary coil of the transformer. Calculate the number of turns on the secondary coil. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Ns ⁄ Np = Vs ⁄ Vp

Ns = Np × (Vs ⁄ Vp) = 50 × (110000 V ⁄ 230 V) ≈ 23913

Number of turns = 24000 (rounded).

(b) Draw a labelled diagram of a step-down transformer. On the labels, state a suitable material for each of the components. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The diagram should show:

  • A soft iron core (typically a rectangular loop).
  • A primary coil made of copper wire with a larger number of turns.
  • A secondary coil made of copper wire with a fewer number of turns than the primary.

(c) Explain how a transformer operates. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

An alternating voltage in the primary coil drives an alternating current, which produces a changing magnetic field in the soft iron core. The core guides this changing field to the secondary coil. The changing magnetic flux cutting through the secondary coil induces an alternating voltage across it.

Question 28 (0625/43_Winter_2021_Q10) - Magnets And Currents

(a) A transformer has 500 turns on the primary coil and 25 turns on the secondary coil. The input voltage is 120V.

(i) Calculate the output voltage. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Vs = Vp × (Ns ⁄ Np) = 120 V × (25 ⁄ 500)

Output voltage = 6.0 V.

(ii) The current in the primary coil is 125 mA. The transformer is 100% efficient. Calculate the output current. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

IsVs = IpVp

Is = (IpVp) ⁄ Vs = (0.125 A × 120 V) ⁄ 6.0 V

Output current = 2.5 A.

(b) Fig. 10.1 shows a loose wire connected in a circuit with a d.c. (direct current) power supply and a switch. The length of the wire between the two supports is in the magnetic field of a horseshoe magnet. The power supply is switched on and the wire moves down.

(i) On Fig. 10.1, draw an arrow on the wire to show the direction of the current. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, with the Force downwards and the Field from N to S, the current must flow from right to left across the loose part of the wire.

(ii) The power supply is switched off and the wire returns to its original position. The power supply is then switched on so that the current is in the opposite direction. State and explain what happens to the wire. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The wire moves up. Reversing the direction of the current reverses the direction of the force on the wire.

(c) A split-ring commutator is an important feature of a d.c. motor. Suggest one reason why the d.c. motor cannot operate without a split-ring commutator. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Without it, the motor would not continue to rotate in the same direction after completing a half turn, as the direction of the turning force would reverse.

Question 29 (0625/42_Summer_2022_Q7) - Magnets And Currents

Fig. 7.1 shows a small plotting compass which is aligned with the magnetic field between magnetic poles A and B of a U-shaped magnet.

(a) State the polarity of the poles. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The N of the compass points towards the S pole. Therefore, pole A is N and pole B is S.

(b) Fig. 7.2 shows a wire, placed between two poles, carrying a current in the direction of the arrow. On Fig. 7.2, draw an arrow to show the direction of the force on the wire due to the magnetic field. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, with the magnetic field from N to S (left to right) and the current flowing into the page, the force on the wire is vertically upwards.

(c) Fig. 7.3 shows a β-particle moving in the direction of the arrow between the same two poles. On Fig. 7.3, draw an arrow to show the direction of the force on the β-particle due to the magnetic field. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

A β-particle is negatively charged, so its motion to the right is equivalent to a conventional current to the left. Using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, with the field from N to S and the current to the left, the force is vertically downwards.

Question 30 (0625/43_Summer_2022_Q9) - Magnets And Currents

(a) Fig. 9.1 shows a magnet on the end of a spring and a coil of wire connected to a sensitive centre-zero galvanometer. The magnet can move freely through the coil.

(i) The magnet is pulled down and released. Describe and explain what happens to the needle of the sensitive galvanometer. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

The needle of the galvanometer oscillates back and forth. As the magnet moves up and down, the coil cuts the magnetic field lines. This induces a changing voltage and hence a changing current in the coil. The induced current flows in one direction as the magnet moves down and in the opposite direction as it moves up.

(ii) The magnet is replaced with a stronger magnet. State the effect of using a stronger magnet on what happens to the needle of the galvanometer. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Using a stronger magnet will produce a larger (maximum) deflection of the needle.

(b) A step-up transformer is used to step up the output voltage of a power station from 25000 V to 400000V for transmission along power lines. The number of turns on the secondary coil is 36000. Calculate the number of turns on the primary coil. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Np ⁄ Ns = Vp ⁄ Vs

Np = Ns × (Vp ⁄ Vs) = 36000 × (25000 V ⁄ 400000 V)

Number of turns = 2250.

Question 32 (0625/43_Winter_2022_Q11) - Magnets And Currents

(a) Fig. 11.1 shows a solenoid connected to a battery. On Fig. 11.1, draw the pattern of the magnetic field inside and around the solenoid. Indicate the direction of the magnetic field with an arrow. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The magnetic field pattern should resemble that of a bar magnet. Inside the solenoid, the field lines should be straight, parallel lines. Outside, they should loop around from one end to the other. Using the right-hand grip rule, the top of the solenoid is a N pole, so the arrows should point upwards inside the solenoid and emerge from the top, looping around to enter the bottom.

(b) Electrical power is transmitted at a voltage of 400kV. A transformer reduces the voltage to 33kV for use by heavy industry in large factories. The number of turns on the primary coil of the transformer is 11000. Calculate the number of turns on the secondary coil of the transformer. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Ns ⁄ Np = Vs ⁄ Vp

Ns = Np × (Vs ⁄ Vp) = 11000 × (33000 V ⁄ 400000 V) = 907.5

Number of turns = 910 (rounded to a whole number).
